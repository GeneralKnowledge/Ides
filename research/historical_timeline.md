# Master Historical Timeline (c. 46–42 BC)

**Scope:** Core window 46 BC → aftermath of Philippi (42 BC), with selected earlier context and later anchors for foreshadowing.

**Event classes:**
- **MAJOR FIXED** — must occur; do not shift for plot convenience
- **MINOR DOCUMENTED** — should occur; slight timing flex OK for fiction
- **GAP** — historical silence; primary D-space

**Certainty:** A/B/C/D on claims. See `historical_sources.md`.

**Info columns legend (per major event):**
- **Public:** street / Forum belief
- **Elite:** senators, equestrians, major households
- **Faction:** specific network knowledge
- **Private:** 1–2 people
- **Unknown:** nobody knows (or only later historians claim to)

## 46 BC

### Early–mid 46 — African aftermath & return to Rome
- **What happened:** Caesar returns from Africa after Thapsus; celebrates triumphs (Gaul, Egypt, Pontus, Africa — exact sequencing **B/C** in display detail). **[MAJOR FIXED — triumphs]**
- **Location:** Rome
- **People:** Caesar; Cleopatra’s later presence in Rome **B/C** timing; Cicero; Antony (political position evolving)
- **Political:** Caesar’s dominance visible; *clementia* toward defeated **A** as policy brand
- **Military:** Veterans expecting land; further Spanish unfinished business
- **Economic:** Debt crisis, land settlement pressure, grain politics continue
- **Consequences:** Pompeian hard core regroups in Spain under Gnaeus and Sextus Pompeius
- **Info:** Provinces learn of African victory weeks/months later depending on season and sea

### Late 46 — Calendar reform begins / dictatorships
- **Caesar’s dictatorships and consulships:** Exact titulature sequence **A/B** via Fasti; popular memory simplifies to “Caesar rules alone”
- **Julian calendar reform:** Introduced effectively from 45 (**A**); 46 as “year of confusion” with intercalation (**A/B**)
- **GAP:** Private elite dinners assessing whether monarchy is inevitable

## 45 BC

### Early 45 — Spanish campaign (Munda)
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Battle of Munda (17 March 45 BC traditional date — **A/B** on exact day). Caesar’s hardest fight of the civil wars (**B** from sources).
- **Location:** Near Munda, Hispania
- **People:** Caesar; Gnaeus Pompeius (killed afterward **A**); Sextus Pompeius escapes (**A**)
- **Military:** Civil war seemingly decided; Sextus remains a long-term threat
- **Consequences:** Young Octavius (Octavian) present in theatre of war traditions (**B/C** on exact role); veterans’ expectations intensify
- **Info asymmetry:** Rome hears delayed, filtered victory news; rumours of Caesar’s death circulate then die (**B** pattern of wartime rumour)

### Mid–late 45 — Return; honours escalate
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Flood of honours: statues, temple associations, calendar month *Iulius*, oaths, seat in theatre, etc. Exact package piecemeal (**A** cumulative; **C** on some individual decrees’ dates)
- **Political significance:** Optics of kingship without the title *rex*
- **Antony:** Close associate; marriage to Fulvia (timing around this period **B**)
- **Brutus:** Pardoned earlier; praetorship path; marriage to Porcia (Cato’s daughter) — timing **B** (~45)
- **Cassius:** Also reconciled; deep personal/political resentment traditions (**C** motives)
- **GAP:** Private conversations among nobles about “when enough is enough”
- **GAP:** Client networks negotiating land, priesthoods, provincial posts under Caesar’s favour

### Late 45 — Octavian’s position
- **B:** Caesar shows favour to Octavius (great-nephew); adoption formally revealed later in will (**A** for will contents as later published)
- **C:** How widely adoption intention was known before Ides
- **Foreshadow:** Introduce Octavian as peripheral youth, not heir-apparent

## 44 BC — The hinge year

### January–February 44 — Kingship crisis atmosphere
- **MINOR/MAJOR mix:**
  - Lupercalia (15 February): Antony offers diadem to Caesar; Caesar refuses (**A** as event tradition; **C** on staging — sincere refusal vs political theatre)
  - Sibylline rumour that only a king can defeat Parthia (**C** on who floated it)
  - Title debates: *dictator perpetuo* (**A** by early 44); *rex* refused
- **People:** Caesar planning Parthian war; magistrates designated years ahead (**A**)
- **Political:** Liberators crystallise; recruitment spreads (**B** process; **C** exact dates of joining)
- **Info:** Street talks of crowns; elite letters anxious; many believe Caesar will leave for East soon

### Early March 44 — Final days before Ides
- **MINOR DOCUMENTED / C-heavy:**
  - Omens, sacrificial failures, Spurinna’s warning tradition (**C** details; **A** that omen culture was real)
  - Calpurnia’s dream (**C** content variants)
  - Artemidorus warning-note tradition (**C**)
  - Decimus Brutus persuades Caesar to attend Senate (**A** as common narrative; **C** exact words)
- **GAP:** Conspirator meetings in houses, porticoes, gardens; final assignment of roles
- **GAP:** Who sleeps where night of 14–15 March

### 15 March 44 BC — Ides of March
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Caesar assassinated at a Senate meeting in the Curia of Pompey (Theatre of Pompey complex, Campus Martius) (**A**)
- **Location:** Porticus / Curia Pompeia — **not** the later Curia Julia
- **People:** Conspirators including M. Brutus, C. Cassius, Decimus Brutus, Trebonius, Casca(s), Tillius Cimber, et al. (**A** core; **C** full roster)
- **Sequence uncertainties (C):** Order of blows; Caesar’s resistance; last words; Antony delayed by Trebonius (**tradition B/C**); Lepidus’ forces nearby
- **Immediate aftermath:** Conspirators go to Capitol; Antony flees then negotiates; city panic (**A/B**)
- **Info explosion:** Within hours — partial truths, body location rumours, “liberty” slogans, fear of proscriptions

### 16–17 March 44 — Negotiation
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Political settlement talks; amnesty trajectory (**A**)
- **People:** Antony (consul), Lepidus (MAG), Cicero (influence without office), conspirators on Capitol
- **C:** Exact terms hour-by-hour
- **GAP:** Household reactions (Calpurnia; Caesar’s staff); bribes; threats

### ~18–20 March 44 — Will and funeral
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Caesar’s will: Octavian principal heir / adopted; substantial bequests to people; gardens to public (**A** on main points)
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Public funeral; Antony’s speech; riot; burning of Caesar’s body in Forum; attack on conspirators’ properties (**A** core narrative; **C** speech wording)
- **Consequences:** Conspirators’ position in Rome collapses; Decimus eventually goes to Cisalpine Gaul command (**A**)
- **Info:** Will’s adoption clause transforms Octavian from peripheral youth to claimant (**A** effect)

### April–May 44 — Octavian arrives; Antony–Octavian tension begins
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Octavian returns to Italy from Greece/Apollonia area; accepts inheritance politically (**A**)
- **People:** Octavian; Philippus / Atia counsel; Cicero watches; Antony controls war chest and politics initially (**A/B**)
- **Military:** Caesar’s veterans as prize constituency
- **Economic:** Contests over funds (Temple of Ops etc. — **B/C** details)
- **GAP:** Private assessments of Octavian as “boy”

### Summer–autumn 44 — Polarisation
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Cicero’s *Philippics* begin (2nd Philippic circulated as pamphlet **A**); Antony denounced
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Brutus and Cassius leave Italy for East (timing of departures **B**; provincial commands contested **A**)
- **Decimus** holds Cisalpine Gaul; Antony seeks to exchange provinces (**A**)
- **Cleopatra** leaves Rome / Italy after Ides (timing **B/C**); Caesarion’s status awkward
- **Sextus Pompey** consolidates naval power in West (**A** trajectory)

### December 44 – January 43 — Mutina crisis forms
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Antony besieges Decimus at Mutina; Hirtius & Pansa (consuls 43) and Octavian move against Antony (**A**)
- **Political:** Senate tries to use Octavian against Antony — classic miscalculation foreshadow
- **Info:** Letters fly; Cicero believes republic can be restored with young Caesar’s sword (**A** from Philippics/letters)

## 42 BC

### 42 — Liberators in the East
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Brutus and Cassius dominate East; tax/exaction from cities (**A**); republican coinage (*EID MAR* — **A**)
- **MAJOR FIXED:** Battles of Philippi (first and second engagements, Oct 42) (**A**)
- **Outcome:** Cassius dies (after first battle, suicide amid confusion — **A/B**); Brutus dies after second (**A**); Antony’s military prestige high; Octavian ill but survivors’ share of credit contested (**C** on exact credit)
- **Consequences:** Republican cause as senatorial restoration effectively ends; Mediterranean contest becomes Antony vs Octavian (with Sextus, Lepidus as complicating factors)

### Immediate post-Philippi
- **Settlement:** Provinces divided; veterans to be settled; Italy’s land crisis worsens (**A**)
- **GAP:** Countless private reckonings among survivors
- **Foreshadow frame:** Perusine War (41–40), Treaty of Brundisium (40), Actium (31) exist as *later* consequence horizon — do not force into Book 1 structure

## Per-major-event deep cards

### CARD: Munda (17 Mar 45)
- **Know:** Caesar wins last pitched battle of Caesar–Pompeian civil war phase.
- **Sources:** Appian, Dio, Plutarch, Spanish War monograph (authorship **C**).
- **Uncertain:** Casualties; Caesar’s near-defeat degree; Octavian’s precise activity.
- **Before/after fiction:** Desertion rumours; messengers racing to Rome; Sextus’ escape corridor.

### CARD: Lupercalia diadem (15 Feb 44)
- **Know:** Antony offers crown-like diadem; Caesar refuses; public episode.
- **Uncertain:** Scripted or spontaneous; crowd composition; conspirators’ reading of it.
- **Before/after fiction:** Who encouraged Antony; whether Caesar expected it; graffiti afterward.

### CARD: Ides assassination (15 Mar 44)
- **Know:** Murder in Pompey’s Curia by senatorial conspirators; Caesar dead that day.
- **Uncertain:** Full roster; exact choreography; Caesar’s foreknowledge; Antony’s knowledge.
- **Before/after fiction:** 14 Mar dinners; slave gossip; which senator skipped; where Calpurnia was hour by hour.

### CARD: Funeral & will (~mid–late Mar 44)
- **Know:** Will elevates Octavian; funeral inflames people; conspirators unsafe in Rome.
- **Uncertain:** Exact speech; who organised crowd; when will first privately read.
- **Before/after fiction:** Forgery fears; competing copies; which clauses leak first.

### CARD: Lex Titia / Proscriptions (Nov–Dec 43)
- **Know:** Legal triumviral power; mass political murder including Cicero.
- **Uncertain:** Exact list formation process; private bargains (e.g. trading victims).
- **Before/after fiction:** Who sells whom; forged names; survivors’ guilt.

### CARD: Philippi (Oct 42)
- **Know:** Two engagements; Liberators destroyed; Brutus & Cassius dead.
- **Uncertain:** Octavian’s battlefield role; messages between camps; exact suicide circumstances’ psychology.
- **Before/after fiction:** Omens in camp; pay disputes; Eastern city hostages.
