# Historical Sources Register

**Purpose:** Provenance discipline. Prefer primary ancient testimony; treat late biographies as shaped narratives; never invent citations.

**Certainty reminders:** A/B/C/D apply to *claims*, not to the mere existence of a source.

---

## 1. Contemporary / near-contemporary witnesses

### Marcus Tullius Cicero (106–43 BC) — **A** as documentary survival
- **What survives:** Letters (*Ad Atticum*, *Ad Familiares*, *Ad Brutum*), speeches (*Philippics*, *Pro Marcello*, etc.), philosophical works.
- **Value:** Best window into elite psychology, rumour, day-to-day politics 63–43 BC; unmatched for post-Ides months of 44–43.
- **Limits:** Partisan; self-dramatising; often absent from Rome; later editions/arrangement of letters involve editorial history (**C** on some dates/order).
- **Use for:** Information states, elite gossip speed, Antony/Octavian framing, provincial news lag.

### Gaius Julius Caesar (100–44 BC)
- **What survives:** *Commentarii* on Gallic and Civil Wars (authorship of later books partly **C**); fragments/quotations of speeches and laws.
- **Value:** Self-presentation, military logistics, provincial geography.
- **Limits:** Propaganda; ends before Ides; does not explain private motives in 45–44.
- **Use for:** Army composition, Spanish/African campaigns, rhetoric of *clementia*.

### Sallust (c. 86–35 BC)
- **What survives:** *Bellum Catilinae*, *Bellum Jugurthinum*, *Historiae* (fragments).
- **Value:** Late-Republican moral vocabulary; Catilinarian memory still live in 60s–40s politics.
- **Limits:** Not a continuous narrative of 46–42; highly rhetorical.
- **Use for:** Ideological language of corruption, *nobilitas*, populares rhetoric.

### Cornelius Nepos (c. 110–25 BC)
- **What survives:** Short biographies (e.g. Atticus).
- **Value:** Social circle of Atticus; glimpses of private friendship across factions.
- **Limits:** Brief; moralising.

### Asinius Pollio (76 BC–AD 4) — lost *Historiae*
- **Status:** Major contemporary history **lost**; known via later use (Appian, Plutarch, etc.).
- **Value:** Often behind the best Civil War narratives (**B** for many battle details).
- **Limits:** Cannot cite directly; reconstruct via dependents.

### Nicolaus of Damascus (c. 64 BC–after AD 14)
- **What survives:** *Life of Augustus* fragments (esp. on assassination and Octavian’s arrival).
- **Value:** Close to Augustan court tradition; detailed Ides narrative.
- **Limits:** Pro-Augustus bias; writing decades later; some scenes may be dramatised (**C**).
- **Use for:** Octavian’s early months; Decimus’ role; sequence around the murder.

## 2. Later ancient narratives (use with caution)

### Plutarch (c. AD 46–120)
- **Lives:** Caesar, Brutus, Antony, Cicero, Cato, Pompey, Crassus, etc.
- **Value:** Character psychology, anecdote density, comparative moral frames.
- **Limits:** Greek moralist; chronological compression; theatrical scenes; sources mixed (**C** on many private dialogues).
- **Rule:** Treat vivid private conversations as **C/D** unless corroborated.

### Suetonius (c. AD 69–after 122)
- **What survives:** *Divus Iulius*, *Divus Augustus*.
- **Value:** Anecdotes, omens, will, physical description, administrative detail.
- **Limits:** Thematic not chronological; court gossip tradition; omens shaped for biography (**C**).

### Appian (2nd c. AD)
- **What survives:** *Civil Wars* (esp. Books 2–5 for our period).
- **Value:** Best continuous military-political narrative 49–31 BC; lists of conspirators; Philippi detail.
- **Limits:** Late; numbers often rounded; speeches invented in Thucydidean manner (**C**).

### Cassius Dio (c. AD 155–235)
- **What survives:** Roman History Books 40–48 (various completeness).
- **Value:** Institutional framing; year-by-year structure; senatorial perspective.
- **Limits:** Very late; anachronistic constitutional language; speeches rhetorical (**C**).

### Velleius Paterculus (wrote c. AD 30)
- Brief Augustan-leaning summary; useful for chronology checks; thin on private detail.

### Livy (59 BC–AD 17)
- Relevant books largely **lost** for this period; *Periochae* (summaries) preserve outlines (**B/C**).

### Other
- **Valerius Maximus:** Exempla; unreliable for precise chronology.
- **Macrobius, Servius, scholiasts:** Occasional antiquarian scraps (**C**).
- **Inscriptions / coins / Fasti:** Strong **A** for offices, dates, propaganda imagery (e.g. coin legends of conspirators, Antony, Octavian).

## 3. Modern scholarship (orientation, not gospel)

Use for structure, demography, topography, and source criticism. Prefer:

- Standard reference works (e.g. *Cambridge Ancient History* vols. IX–X; *Oxford Classical Dictionary*).
- Prosopography: Broughton, *Magistrates of the Roman Republic*; Syme, *The Roman Revolution* (interpretive, still foundational).
- Topography: Platner–Ashby / LTUR tradition; Claridge; Coarelli for Rome.
- Military: Brunt; Keppie; Goldsworthy (popular but careful).
- Economy: Hopkins; Harris; Kay (debt, money supply debates are **C** in detail).
- Assassination studies: Tempest; Strauss; Osgood (*Caesar’s Legacy*); Welch on Pompeians/Sextus.

**Rule:** Modern historians disagree. When they do, mark **C** and record both sides in `continuity/contradictions.md`.

---

## 4. Source conflict protocol

When ancient accounts disagree:

1. State each account with author.
2. Note chronological distance from events.
3. Note known bias (Caesarian / republican / Augustan).
4. Label claim **C**.
5. Do **not** pick a winner for fiction unless `continuity/canon.md` explicitly locks a choice.
6. Prefer leaving reader-facing ambiguity where history is silent or split.

### Known high-conflict clusters (see also `contradictions.md`)

| Topic | Conflict type |
|-------|----------------|
| Exact list of conspirators | Names vary by Appian / Dio / Plutarch / Nicolaus |
| Caesar’s last words | Greek/Latin variants; Suetonius vs others |
| Calpurnia’s dream content | Multiple versions |
| Whether Caesar received a written warning | Suetonius anecdote **C** |
| Antony’s exact role morning of Ides | Trebonius delay traditions **C** |
| Timing of will’s publicity | Sequence of reading vs funeral **C** in detail |
| Number of wounds | 23 is traditional (**A** as tradition; medical reality **C**) |
| Presence/absence of bodyguards | Policy of dismissing lictors/guards **C** in motive |

## 5. Citation style for this project

In research notes, prefer:

```
[A] Cicero, Att. 14.1 (c. April 44): …
[C] Plutarch, Caesar 63 (dream versions diverge from Suet. Iul. 81)
[B] Travel Rome↔Brundisium ~1–2 weeks by courier under good conditions (model; see locations.md)
[D] Private conversation between X and Y on night of 14 March — inventable if consistent with A/B
```

Do not invent book/chapter numbers. If unsure of a citation, mark **VERIFY** and add to `future_research.md`.

## 6. What sources cannot give us (automatic D-space)

- Verbatim private conversations without a contemporary letter
- Interior monologue
- Exact slave/freedman names in most households
- Most women’s private political opinions (except where letters/speeches survive)
- Precise hour-by-hour Rome street movements on most days
- Full membership lists of informal dinner factions
- Exact balances of most personal debts
- What Caesar “really believed” about kingship

These silences are the novel’s oxygen — not failures of research.
