# Economics & Power

How someone without the “right” office can still matter — and how office without money/soldiers can mean nothing.

---

## 1. Power ingredients (stackable)

| Resource | What it buys | Example 46–42 |
|----------|--------------|---------------|
| **Legions** | Ultimate veto | Antony, Octavian, Liberators in East |
| **Land** | Status, veterans, grain, clients | Confiscations; colonies |
| **Cash / bullion** | Donatives, bribes, ships | Temple of Ops fights; proscription spoils |
| **Credit** | Time and allies | Moneylenders; Atticus |
| **Offices** | Legality theatre, agenda control | Consulship 44–43 |
| **Priesthoods** | Blocking power via ritual | Augury; pontificate |
| **Name / ancestry** | Soft legitimacy | Brutus’ tyrannicide brand; Caesar’s name for Octavian |
| **Oratory** | Senate & court steering | Cicero |
| **Clientela** | Crowds, votes (memory), muscle | House networks |
| **Provincial revenue** | War chests | Asia under Liberators |
| **Grain** | Urban peace | Egypt, Sicily, Africa |
| **Marriage** | Binding houses | Brutus–Porcia; later Octavia–Antony |
| **Information** | Timing | Freedmen agents, letters |

**Rule:** Formal magistracy is one instrument. After 49 BC, **armed force + money** dominate.

---

## 2. Why Cicero is powerful and powerless at once

- **Has:** Voice, networks, moral capital, letter web.  
- **Lacks:** Reliable legions.  
- **Result:** Shapes debate 44–43; dies when triumvirs prioritise guns (**A**).

## 3. Why Octavian jumps power levels

- **Has initially:** Name in will, some money claims, audacity, Atia/Philippus counsel.  
- **Gains:** Veterans who love the *name* Caesar; then armies; then consulship.  
- **Lesson for story:** Inheritance is a resource only if converted into MIL + FIN.

## 4. Why Decimus can kill Caesar and still lose

- **Has:** Trust, province, some troops.  
- **Lacks:** Urban popular legitimacy after funeral; lasting coalition.  
- **Dies fleeing** (**A**) — consequence chain exemplar.

## 5. Land & veterans

- Civil war promise engine: settle soldiers on Italian/provincial land.
- Italian landowners resist → future Perusine rage (41–40 foreshadow).
- Each donative resets loyalty auctions between Antony/Octavian.

## 6. Debt

- Personal: binds nobles to patrons; shame and urgency.
- Public: cities owe Roman financiers; Brutus’ lending in East documented in Cicero (**A**).
- Political murder sometimes cheaper than repayment (theme, not monocausal Ides explanation).

## 7. Grain & urban dependence

- Rome feeds on provincial sea lanes.
- Cleopatra’s Egypt = strategic calorie reserve.
- Grain scarcity → riot → elite panic. Keep as offstage pressure gauge.

## 8. Taxation & publicani

- Equites’ companies farm taxes; hate instability that breaks contracts.
- Extortion literature trains audiences to see provinces as extractive.
- Liberators’ emergency fundraising = republican branding crisis.

## 9. Booty & spectacular wealth

- Triumphs display conquest economics.
- Confiscations 43 recycle elite wealth into war.
- Proscription is fiscal policy with knives.

## 10. Patronage mechanics (daily)

1. Dawn *salutatio*: clients greet patron.  
2. Patron delivers legal help, gifts, meals, recommendations.  
3. Client delivers attendance, applause, votes (when relevant), violence.  
4. Great men are nodes; freedmen are routers.

## 12. Economic consequence buttons (for plotting)

- Delay a grain fleet → street mood shift  
- Intercept a donative → legion wavers  
- Forge a will clause rumour → house panic  
- Call in loans after Ides → fence-sitters pick sides  
- Promise Campanian farms → Italian notables hate you

## 13. Uncertainties (C)

- Exact size of Caesar’s fortune liquid vs land  
- Precise Ops funds Antony seized  
- Quantitative models of Italian land available for settlement  
- Relative wealth ranking of mid-tier conspirators  

Mark numbers in fiction as character beliefs, not narrator statistics, unless sourced.
