# Roman Daily Life — Reference Layer (c. 50–40 BC)

Practical texture for fiction. Prefer messy reality over marble tourism. All generalisations are **B** unless noted; local exceptions abound.

---

## Food & drink
- **Staples:** Bread/wheat, olive oil, wine, pulses, cheese, vegetables, fish sauce (*garum*).
- **Elite dining:** Multicourse *convivium*; politics happens between courses; seating hierarchy matters.
- **Meat:** Less everyday than modern expectation for poor; elite display includes exotic.
- **Drink:** Wine mixed with water; neat wine = excess stereotype.
- **Street food:** *Popinae* / cookshops — smell, smoke, class mixing, rumour.

## Clothing & appearance
- **Toga:** Citizen male public garment — cumbersome; political costume more than daily workwear.
- **Tunics:** Actual daily wear; quality signals class.
- **Women:** Stola/palla ideals for matrons; cosmetics & jewellery as status; moralising male sources distort (**C**).
- **Purple:** Border (*clavus*) and full purple = rank signals; kingship panic around diadems/crowns.
- **Hairstyles/beards:** Period fashion shifts; Caesar’s grooming anecdotes in Suetonius (**C** colour).

## Housing
- **Domus:** Atrium house; *tablinum* for business; peristyle; slaves everywhere.
- **Insulae:** Multi-storey rentals — fire, collapse, noise; most urban Romans.
- **Villas:** Otium + agribusiness; Cicero’s villa letters = gold for rhythm of elite life.
- **Privacy:** Minimal by modern standards; walls have ears (literal slave ears).

## Slavery & freedpeople
- **Pervasive:** Urban households, farms, mines, sex labour, education, accounting.
- **Violence:** Legal near-absolute master power; practice varies.
- **Manumission:** Common pathway; freedmen as agents of great houses (**A** pattern).
- **Tiro-type literate slaves:** Possible and attested; not universal.
- **Narrative:** Do not sentimentalise or flatten into one “slave opinion.”

## Marriage, sex, household
- **Marriage:** Property, alliance, dowry; affection possible but not the legal point.
- **Divorce:** Relatively available to elites compared with later Christian norms.
- **Concubinage & adultery laws:** Political weapons (remember Caesar as alleged endless adulterer in rhetoric).
- **Same-sex relations:** Elite male norms ≠ modern categories; status/penetration ideologies; treat carefully historically.
- **Children:** High mortality; adoption politically huge (Octavian).

## Religion in daily life
- Morning household rites; doorways; ancestral masks in atria (*imagines*).
- Vows for safe travel; illness explained as divine + medical simultaneously.
- See `religion.md`.

## Funerals
- Elite: procession, mask parade of ancestors, eulogy, pyre.
- Caesar’s funeral breaks norms into riot (**A**).
- Mourning as politics.

## Medicine
- Greek doctors in elite houses; herbs, diet, surgery limited; religion parallel.
- Wounds from Ides: medical detail can be researched; ancient counts rhetorical.

## Bathing & sanitation
- Baths as social world; oil, scrapers, gossip.
- Sewers (*Cloaca Maxima*) exist; streets still filthy; chamber pots; refuse.
- Do not depict ancient Rome as sterile monumental CGI.

## Travel
- Roads excellent relative to era; still slow, dusty, inn-dependent, bandit-risk.
- Litters for elite urban move; horses/mules; wagons restricted in city hours historically (rules evolve).
- Sea: cheaper/faster when lucky; terrifying when not.

## Weapons & armour (civilian Rome)
- Senators not normally armed in Senate — hence daggers as shock (**A**).
- Street violence via clients/gladiators historically (50s memory).
- Soldiers in *sagum* etc. when present; city uneasy with swords.

## Money
- **Denarius** silver backbone; sesterces in accounting rhetoric; gold less everyday.
- Cash + land + loans; liquidity crises political.
- Bribery endemic in late Republic memory.
- See `economics.md`.

## Debt
- Social death risk; political dependency; Caesar’s early debts vs later supremacy story.
- Provincial cities crushed by interest (Brutus in East — Cicero evidence **A**).

## Taxation & provinces
- Tithes, harbour dues, publicani; extortion trials as political genre.
- Liberators’ emergency exactions 43–42 feel like conquest to locals.

## Elections & offices
- By 44, free competitive elections hollowed by Caesar’s designations (**A** grievance).
- Memory of *ambitus* (bribery) laws and street gangs still shapes behaviour.
- *Cursus honorum* ideals vs reality.

## Courts
- Public spectacle; advocates as stars (Cicero).
- Politics by prosecution.

## Festivals & calendar
- **Lupercalia** (15 Feb), **Ides** of each month as religious markers, triumphs, games.
- Caesar’s calendar reform 46/45 (**A**) — time itself politicised.
- Market days (*nundinae*) structure weeks.

## Timekeeping
- Hours seasonal; water clocks; public events by daylight.
- “Dawn,” “fourth hour,” “before the Senate met” > digital clock feel.

## Etiquette & address
- *Nomen* systems: praenomen + nomen + cognomen; care with who uses familiar forms.
- Greeting hierarchy clients at *salutatio* morning — core patronage ritual (**A**).
- Insults land harder in honour culture.

## Literacy & documents
- Elite male literacy high; female literacy variable but real among aristocracy.
- Tablets, papyrus rolls, seals, witnesses.
- Forgery fear around wills — plot engine.

## Letters
- Dictated; copied; commonplaces of friendship; political dynamite if leaked.
- Cicero corpus shows speed expectations among elites in Italy.

---

## Atmosphere do / don’t

**Do:** Smoke, mud, animals, shouting, ritual blood, patronage queues at dawn, damp cold in March Senate, multilingual slaves, bad teeth, perfume covering rot.

**Don’t:** Spotless white togas as daily realism; modern police; modern privacy; atheist elites as default; egalitarian banter across status without cost.
