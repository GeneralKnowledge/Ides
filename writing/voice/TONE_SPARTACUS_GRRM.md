# TONE-1 — Spartacus Bite + GRRM Weight (Soft Lock)

**Status:** Soft lock for first-pass dialogue/character energy. Does **not** override VOICE-1, anchors, or Roman texture.  
**Steal craft, not costumes:** no dragons, no “m’lord,” no arena soap replacing Senate politics.

---

## 1. What we want

| Source | Take | Leave |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Spartacus (esp. Batiatus)** | Elevated, slightly formal spoken English; status as performance; **humour from ego that does not know it is funny**; patronage as theatre; cruelty said as business | Full stylised “Latin cosplay” every line; camp drowning dread; gladiator plot transplant |
| **GRRM / ASOIAF** | Moral grey; dialogue that means two things; dark jokes under threat; food/body/debt texture; people who are competent and awful; wit as weapon; aftermath that stays messy | Fantasy heraldry; modern prestige-TV quip storms; turning Rome into Westeros with togas |

**Blend in one sentence:** People speak a little larger than naturalism, joke while bargaining over blood, and the joke does not cancel the bill.

## 2. Batiatus rule (unintentional comedy)

**Batiatus energy** = a character who:
1. Narrates their own virtue while doing something petty or lethal  
2. Treats human damage as **housekeeping / ledger / honour of the house**  
3. Is sincere — the laugh is in the gap between self-image and act  
4. Never delivers a modern punchline or winks at camera  

**Primary carriers (see maps):** F4 Hordeonius · L. Cornelius Balbus · selective Dolabella.  
**Secondary seasoning only:** Cicero (vanity), Antony (swagger), never Brutus as sitcom.

### Mini-pattern (do / don’t)

**Do (Batiatus-adjacent, Roman):**
> “I am a friend to the Republic in the only way that matters—I keep its ledgers honest. The man was careless with seals. Seals are sacred.”

**Don’t (modern sitcom):**
> “Well, that escalated quickly.” / “I’m not saying we kill him, but…”

**Don’t (Spartacus parody spam):**
> “Would that it were so simple, Dominus!” every beat.

## 3. GRRM dialogue habits (Romanised)

1. **Subtext over speech:** what is not said is the knife.  
2. **Hospitality & food as threat** — dinner, wine, *salutatio* queues.  
3. **Lists and debts** — names owed, land promised, who smiled too late.  
4. **Children / heirs / names** as horror (Octavian’s name; Caesarion rumour; proscription lists).  
5. **Gallows humour** from soldiers and clerks, not from moral centres mid-vow.  
6. **Competence without niceness** — Cassius, Balbus, Fulvia.  
7. After violence: **cleanup, smell, argument**, not orchestral catharsis.

POV craft we already share with GRRM: ensemble close-third, asymmetric info, nobody gets a clean win.
