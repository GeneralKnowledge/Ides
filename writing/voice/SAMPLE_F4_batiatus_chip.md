# SAMPLE — F4 Batiatus Chip (tone only)

**Purpose:** Calibrate unintentional ego comedy. Not a full scene card replacement.  
**POV:** L. Hordeonius Felix (F4) · **Beat:** early SIDE-1 / debt talk · **Tone:** TONE-1

The tablets still smelled of the room where Visellius had died—cheap oil, old wine, the metal tang men pretend is only coins.

Hordeonius washed his hands anyway. A banker who carried another man’s death on his fingers invited rumour, and rumour was interest you paid without a contract.

“He was,” Hordeonius told the silent shelf of pigeonholes, “a credit to his father’s manumission. Paid on the Kalends. Do you know how rare that is in this city of philosophers?”

Nobody answered. The shelf never did. That was why he liked it.

Basilus’s name sat in wax like a fly. An Antonian agent’s mark sat beside it like a second fly. Hordeonius did not swat either. He weighed them.

“I am not a man of factions,” he said, because saying it made it feel like an account entered. “I am a man of seals. Seals are older than liberty. Seals will be here when liberty is a word sold by the jug.”

He almost smiled at his own seriousness, then did not. Smiling at seriousness was how clerks became funny in taverns.

Outside, someone shouted about bread. Hordeonius blotted the wax, gentle as a father closing a child’s eyes, and entered *Visellius, A. — doubtful* in a hand so neat it looked like virtue.
