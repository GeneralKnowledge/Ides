# Mind sheets — Book 1

**Status:** In use for the full-book voice pass, 2026-11-11 (the three-scene pilot was accepted). Descriptive, not prescriptive.
**Why:** An outside reader of Ch 1–6 found that the viewpoint characters have different vocabularies but one way of thinking: each sees a thing, sorts it and states what it means. [`POV_DICTION.md`](POV_DICTION.md) lists what each person says. These sheets describe how each person’s attention works, so that a scene can be told from inside one particular head.

**How to use them.** Read the sheet before writing or revising a scene, then put it away. Nothing here is a rule or a ban. People generalise; the sheets say when this person does and where the thought comes from. If a sentence could be moved into another character’s scene without anyone noticing, it probably belongs to the author and not to the character.

**Three tests for a rewrite** (Reuben, 2026-11-11, after the pilot). A rewrite is not judged by whether it resembles the pilot scenes.

1. **Action.** What actually happens in this scene, and what changes because of it?
2. **Point of view.** What would this particular person notice, misunderstand, conceal or care about?
3. **Explanation.** Is the narration telling the reader something the scene has already shown?

The Nysa scene in Ch 2 is the benchmark for one thing only: whether a rewrite has moved the reader closer to the character. It is not a pattern for sentence shape or for length. No kind of line is banned. Hordeonius keeps his memorable lines and Cicero stays eloquent. The question is whether the line is this person’s, said to someone, with a consequence. Characters should not be made less articulate. They should be given more to do, more to conceal, and people on whom their words have an effect. A homely comparison from a character’s own world (Nysa’s big jar going over) works because it is rare.

**Two tests for a scene’s ending** (from the outside reader, 2026-11-11):

1. Take away the last sentence. Does the scene lose anything it needs? If not, the sentence may be an explanation.
2. End on the physical action instead. What does the reader understand without being told?

Neither test is an order to cut endings. The Ligarius letter and the shared bread stay because they carry weight without explaining it.

**The aim** is people who are more particular. It is not plainer prose. Caesar talking about a leaking cistern and a lame horse (Ch 6) is the model: he is not making a statement about power, he is having a conversation, and the reader learns him from it.

## Nysa

**Notices first:** hands, feet, what is being carried, who is standing apart from whom, which door. She sees a person as a set of things she could describe to the steward later: a boil, a writing case, a clean toga. She counts, in Greek, because it is quicker.

**How a thought moves:** in order of events. This happened, then this. She keeps what she saw and does not turn it over to see what it means, because meaning is for the people upstairs. When she does reach a conclusion it is practical and about the next hour.

**When she generalises:** when she is frightened, and then it is a memory of what happened to a particular person (Chreste, who repeated a fishmonger’s joke and was sent to Labici). She has cases where other people have sayings.

**What she cannot or will not say:** anything that would make her the source. She answers the question asked and stops. Her omissions are choices and she knows which ones she has made.

**What she misunderstands:** she thinks politics is something that happens to senators and can be kept out of a kitchen by not carrying it in. She thinks that if she reports only objects she has reported nothing. She does not see that the count on the steps is politics. She did not know who the man on the painted board was.

**In talk:** short, literal, a beat behind when someone is being clever at her. She is sometimes funny by accident, because she answers exactly what was asked (“So I wasn’t here”).

**Body:** the weight of what she carries, a knotted sandal strap, a hip. She is about twenty-eight, Caesar’s freedwoman, and nobody’s property.

**Sounds like her:** “Two had nothing in their hands at all and stood a little apart from each other in very clean togas, not talking.”
**Does not:** “Cheers were loud. Spit lasted.” (A verdict. She would keep the spit and not rule on it.)

## Cicero

**Notices first:** sentences. Who made one, whether it was good, whether it was his. Then audiences: who is listening, who will repeat it, what Atticus will think of the version he gets.

**How a thought moves:** backwards into history and precedent, in long periods with the verb held back, and then round to himself. He cannot hear a title without knowing who last held it. This is the one mind in the book that properly thinks in built sentences, and the narration inside his head can be longer and more ornate than anywhere else.

**When he generalises:** constantly, and with pleasure, but as an orator does: a claim with a precedent attached, aimed at a listener. He does not produce the two-beat maxim. His generalisations are often about himself.

**What he cannot or will not say:** in public, anything. In private he says all of it, to Tiro and to Atticus, and takes the saying for a kind of action.

**What he misunderstands:** almost everything about force. He believes events must pass through a speech before they happen, that his private disgust is a form of resistance, that the men who compliment his voice still need it. He forecasts with confidence and is wrong (three years in Parthia will take care of “for ever”). He does not notice that Tiro runs him.

**In talk:** the best in the room and aware of it. Kind in small ways when it costs nothing (“I heard nothing at all”). Vain about style even when being servile: he will not send Caesar a compliment Caesar has already had.

**Body:** he walks when he dictates and cannot sit when he is pleased or ashamed.

**Sounds like him:** “Sulla had kept his until he was bored. Caesar had taken a year, and then ten years, the way a guest takes a second cup and then the jug.”
**Does not:** “Friendship as cipher: the only legion he trusted.” (A caption. He would give that thought a subject, a verb and a subordinate clause.)

## Hordeonius

**Notices first:** the term on the paper. Dates, seals, security, who is standing behind a borrower. He reads faces for whether they will pay.

**How a thought moves:** from the instrument outwards. He is the one character who really does think in rates and entries, and he is allowed his maxims, but they are a banker’s proverbs said aloud to a client across a board, solemnly, as moral instruction. He means every word. He is funny only because he is sincere: he mourns a ruined bribery market as other men mourn the Republic.

**When he generalises:** when he is teaching someone younger what paper is, or defending his own decency. Once or twice in a scene, in speech. The narration around him does not need to make ledger jokes on his behalf.

**What he cannot or will not say:** that he is afraid, or that he enjoyed something. He does his real business last and presents it as a kindness.

**What he misunderstands:** he thinks every grievance is a request for credit. He can tell exactly who will repay and has no idea who will kill. He believes he stands outside politics because he lends to all sides.

**In talk:** grave, slow, courteous to the people he is squeezing. He lets a silence work. Sosia gets the quick lines and Hordeonius reproves him for them.

**Body:** ink on two fingers, a ring, a cloth he wipes his hands on when a matter is closed. He acts before he explains: turns the tablet, moves the list aside, draws the wax to him.

**Sounds like him:** “I have buried clients who paid me out of the province they were robbing to do it, and I spoke well of them at the pyre.”
**Does not:** “A house aiming at a horizon with a name was a rate change in laundry’s clothes.” (The narrator performing him.)

## Laberius

**Notices first:** feet, boots, straps, what a man is carrying and whether he has eaten. Ground: where it is soft, where it rises, how far to the next dry place. Who is on his left and who is behind him.

**How a thought moves:** from the body outwards and no further than the century. He knows a thing by having done it twenty times. He does not keep accounts in his head and does not “file” anything. He remembers days by what was wrong with his feet.

**When he generalises:** after the twentieth time he has watched the same thing go wrong, and then it comes out as a joke about pay, said to Vibius or Gaius and not thought privately. “To whoever’s standing on your left” is the one saying he owns, and he is teased for it.

**What he cannot or will not say:** that he is frightened, or that he loves the men. He checks their kit instead.

**What he misunderstands:** why the gentlemen do anything. He thinks a promise with Caesar’s name on it is as good as a date. He thinks the land is empty. He does not need to know what a battle was for, and he distrusts anyone who explains it to him.

**In talk:** short, flat, a beat late with the joke and better for it. Gaius does the talking. Vibius does the arithmetic and the cruelty. Laberius is the one they both look at.

**Body:** forty-two at Munda. Knees. A shield arm that aches before rain. He eats when there is food because he has been where there was none.

**Sounds like him:** “Laberius believed the fifth one.”
**Does not:** “He filed *donative dated* beside *colony rumour* the way he filed water: needed, not guaranteed.” (A clerk’s mind. He would ask the clerk for the day and get no answer.)

## Brutus

**Notices first:** what is fitting. How a thing will look, to the household, to the dead, to a reader a hundred years on. Faces on a wall. Who has been kept waiting and whether it was rude. He sees his own conduct from outside, as if it were already written down.

**How a thought moves:** towards a sentence. He composes. He begins the philosophical period and the world interrupts it with something small and physical, a cup, a slave’s question, wax on his hand, and he attends to the small thing with great courtesy and never gets back to the sentence. In the best of his scenes the thought arrives later, on its own, as a memory of somebody’s hand.

**When he generalises:** he starts to and cannot finish. When he does finish, in speech, it is noble, quite sincere, and it costs somebody else something.

**What he cannot or will not say:** that he liked Caesar. That he needs money. That his mother arranges his life and he lets her.

**What he misunderstands:** he believes an act can be kept clean by intending it cleanly, and that refusing a thing politely is the same as refusing it. He does not see who pays for his scruples: the freedman who carries the lie, the clients turned back at the gate, the cities, Cassius. He thinks the city will come to love the vocabulary.

**In talk:** slow, exact, formal even with his wife. Almost no humour, and when he tries a joke it is about lawsuits. Porcia, Servilia and Cassius all finish his sentences for him, differently.

**Body:** hands. He looks at them, washes them, does not know what to do with them. He weeps easily and is not ashamed of it.

**Sounds like him:** “He found he had no feeling about that at all, and was a little frightened by how easily he had none.”
**Does not:** “Clemency sat in the room like a soft chain.” (The author’s image, handed to him. He would see Caesar’s hand on his shoulder and be unable to put a name to it.)

## Cassius

**Notices first:** who was given what, and what he was given instead. Hands: what a man does with a cup, a ring, a seal. Exits. He tests people and watches how they take it. His eyes are poor at distance and he has spent twenty years making other men’s reports do instead.

**How a thought moves:** by counting, quickly, to a conclusion about a person, and then he trusts the conclusion more than the person. He is usually right about what a man is. He has Epicurus for the rest: a cool, tidy doctrine he really does hold and cannot always live in.

**When he generalises:** aloud, as a blade, when he is cornered at a table. Rarely in his own head, where he is counting. Carrhae is his case for everything, and he does not tell it.

**What he cannot or will not say:** that he wanted the thing he was not given. That he is fond of Brutus, or of Trebonius. He goes to see a friend off because a note told him not to.

**What he misunderstands:** he thinks being right about a man is the same as knowing what he will do. He thinks contempt can stand in for the part of the report he does not have. He believes he is the one who is not fooled.

**In talk:** dry, exact, courteous in a way that is meant to be felt. One good line and then he stops. He does not win every exchange, and the ones he loses he replays.

**Body:** he makes a fist under the couch and opens it. He does not drink the wine he calls for. He counts with a thumb.

**Sounds like him:** “Caesar had been kind about that too. That, Cassius thought, was what was wrong with him.”
**Does not:** “Mercy made leftover Pompeians sound edible.” (A caption on the room. He would count which of them had been asked to Spain.)

## Decimus

**Notices first:** measurements. Widths, hours, loads, the fall of a road, how long a bridge takes and what it will carry after rain. Doors and which way they open. He likes a thing to be the right size.

**How a thought moves:** it does not move much. He likes a thing or dislikes it and gets on. A worry stays in his body as a tightness and he walks it off or washes his hands. He remembers ten years with Caesar as a list of works: the ships against the Veneti, Massilia, bridges.

**When he generalises:** almost never. Once in a scene at most, about roads or about what gets men killed, and he means it literally.

**What he cannot or will not say:** that he needs to be chosen. He says “useful” where another man would say “loved”.

**What he misunderstands:** he thinks being useful to Caesar and being his friend are the same thing, and that because neither of them mentions what he is owed, nothing is. He thinks Caesar’s notice of him is private, when half the portico is trading in it. He thinks logistics can outrun politics, and that men who praise his letters will name a day.

**In talk:** blunt, cheerful, numbers before compliments. He is easy company, and the ease is real, which is the trouble. Other men have the wit, and he gives them the measurements.

**Body:** hands open and visible. He touches columns, hinges, a horse’s mouth. He puts a hand out before the step, half a breath early.

**Sounds like him:** “He counted them as they went, from habit, and when the number stopped being a number he went to look at the maps.”
**Does not:** “Decimus filed them without crowning one.” (Two borrowed minds at once. He would know how far each story had travelled and not care which was true.)

## Octavian

**Notices first:** who was looked at and who was skipped. Where the reserved place went. What older men do with their hands and how they stand, which he copies without meaning to and then catches himself at. Lists, and his own name on them or not.

**How a thought moves:** coldly, to a use, and then he is ashamed of the coldness, or sick. At seventeen and eighteen the shame is quick. By the consulship it is slower. The body tells on him: he shakes, he vomits, he gets a fever, and he treats each of these as information somebody else might read.

**When he generalises:** to himself, as a rule for next time, and he dislikes how young it sounds. One such rule in a scene is him. One in a paragraph is the author.

**What he cannot or will not say:** thank you. *Son.* That he wants his mother’s hand on his forehead.

**What he misunderstands:** at seventeen, how much of a room’s indifference is about him. He thinks the old men are slighting him when they have not noticed him. He thinks getting the names right is the same as knowing what happened. Later he understands rooms very well and people a little less.

**In talk:** stiff, correct, too polished, and he hears it. Agrippa is the one person he is short with, which is how the reader knows he trusts him.

**Body:** thin shoes. A ring he twists. Palms flat on a table until the shaking stops.

**Sounds like him:** “Octavian found that he was standing with his weight on one hip and a hand loose at his side, as the consul stood when he stood, and he set both feet square again.”
**Does not:** “Weakness spoken aloud became other men’s tool.” (A maxim he has not earned yet. He would hide the weakness and be seen hiding it.)

## Antony

**Notices first:** bodies and noise. Who is sweating, who is drunk, who has gone quiet. Whether a crowd is with him, which he feels on his skin before he could say why. What there is to eat.

**How a thought moves:** late. He acts, and the meaning of what he did arrives a beat afterwards, often from somebody else’s face. He is not stupid; he is quick with his body and slow with consequences, and he knows it, and covers it by laughing first.

**When he generalises:** loudly, for a room, as a toast or a joke. He does not believe his own sayings and would be surprised to have one quoted back to him. Fulvia quotes them back.

**What he cannot or will not say:** that he was used. That he does not know who is running a thing. That he is afraid of his wife’s opinion, which he is.

**What he misunderstands:** he thinks being liked is the same as being followed. He thinks the boy is a name with a child attached. He takes a silence for agreement because he would never keep one himself.

**In talk:** warm, coarse, generous, physical. He clasps people. He repeats a word he likes. With Fulvia he gets shorter and more truthful as the door shuts.

**Body:** a soldier’s, going heavy. Goat-reek, wine, sweat. He misses the axes in the fasces. He drinks when he has been told something he does not want to think about.

**Sounds like him:** “Antony heard the word go up and understood, a beat late, that he had been the fool.”
**Does not:** “He stored her sentence where he stored unpaid cohorts: not denied, not paid tonight.” (A ledger again. He would drink, and remember it at the wrong moment next day.)

## Calpurnia

**Notices first:** the house. Sounds and their hours: a latch, a dog, a courier’s step at the wrong time. Who is up. What is out of its place. Lamps trimmed too short. Her husband’s breathing.

**How a thought moves:** it does not become an argument. It gathers in her body, behind the eyes, in her hands, and she knows a thing for days before she could give a reason a man would accept. She does not add the signs up. She would say only that the house is wrong.

**When she generalises:** she does not. She gives orders about cloth and doors.

**What she cannot or will not say:** that there has been no child. That she knows about the queen across the river. That she is frightened for herself as well as for him.

**What she misunderstands:** she thinks that being right will be enough to be heard. She thinks a man who is kind to her fear has listened to it. She trusts Decimus because he is easy in the house.

**In talk:** brief, about practical things. With servants she is exact and not unkind, and she does not ask them what they think; they tell her anyway, plainly, about stains and doors. With Caesar she has one sentence and repeats it.

**Body:** nails in her palm in her sleep. A taste of metal before dawn. She counts his breaths.

**Sounds like her:** “She woke and lay counting his breaths until the counting itself had become a thing she was afraid to stop.”
**Does not:** “She stacked them beside the lesser nightmares and found the stack heavier than any one omen.” (An accountant’s sum. She would get up and see whether the street door was barred.)

## Caesar (two short scenes only)

He is seen from inside twice, briefly, and the book’s point is that he does not see it coming. Keep him to work: ships, grain, maps, who is waiting. He is quick, courteous, amused, tired. He answers fear with a small joke and a date. The narration must not know more than he does, and must not tell the reader that he is wrong.

**Sounds like him:** “I am always worried. That is what the work is.”
**Does not:** “If someone had handed him a clean list of knives, he might have read it. Nobody did.” (The narrator looking over his shoulder.)
