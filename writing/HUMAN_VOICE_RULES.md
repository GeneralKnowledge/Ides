# Human-voice rules — Book 1

**Status:** Standing craft law after the 2026-10-19 human-voice work; evidence, voice-survival and stopping rules for any final-stage change added 2026-10-22; revision vs development split (§1a) added 2026-10-23; protection narrowed to named cores, pass caps confined to style-only passes and repetition cuts made lighter 2026-10-24 (`archive/pass_logs/rules_review_2026-10-24.md`); habit I (caption fragments) added 2026-10-29 (`archive/pass_logs/one_word_sentence_pass_2026-10-29.md`).

## 0. One-line law

**Prefer a cold read that feels human over a scrub that scores better.**  
If a second or third pass on the same chapter is chasing detector language, stop. Revert to the last version that still had presence.

## 1. Evidence before edit

Applies to every substantive manuscript change (one that alters a fact, a sequence, who knows what, a relationship or what a line means; `LLM_WRITING_BRIEF.md` §12), not only voice work. No change is accepted because an audit, a model, a checklist, a detector or this file recommends it.

**Cutting repetition is lighter.** Removing a sentence or paragraph that restates what the reader has just been shown (a recap, a thesis line, an overheard verdict on a scene already played, a repeated motif) needs one line in the pass log saying what it repeats. It still keeps any plant a later chapter pays.

A substantive recommendation names:

1. **Where:** chapter and exact passage.
2. **What the reader experiences:** the stumble, confusion, false fact or flatness, in reader terms.
3. **Why it fails:** what is wrong with the passage, not how it differs from a preference.
4. **The smallest fix** that solves that problem.
5. **Side effects** on character voice, ambiguity, continuity and later payoffs.

**Acceptance:** a reviewer can explain the problem without saying that a detector flagged it or that a rule says to change it. If they cannot, leave the passage alone.

**Protected material needs a demonstrated defect.** Protection covers beats, not word counts. The protected core of the Ides chapters (Ch 14–19) is: Caesar dies at the Curia of Pompey on the Ides; Trebonius holds Antony outside, and what follows from it; the disputed last words stay disputed; the continuous, dreamlike quality of the killing; the wrong Cinna; Antony spared. With the held beats and load-bearing brands in §3.4, the named cores of the settled chapters in §4.6 and every deliberate ambiguity (`LLM_WRITING_BRIEF.md` §8), these are not revised for improvement or a reviewer’s taste. They change only to fix a defect shown under this rule, and the fix keeps what made them work.

The prose around that core is ordinary text (`continuity/lock_architecture_audit.md` §4.1–4.2). Restatements, corridor verdicts, thesis lines and recaps in Ch 14–19 are cut to the same standard as anywhere else, and continuity fixes and weak lines are fixed. Protection guards against polishing and against evening out the Ides peaks, not against story. When development work (below) needs a protected passage, open it: say what the new material needs from it, change only that, and record what was kept.

### 1a. Revision and development are different work

The rules above govern **revision**: changing prose that already exists to make it better. **Development** is adding story: new scenes, new tracks, new chapters, or moving material so a turning point happens on the page instead of in summary. Development is allowed whenever it answers a story need, and it follows its own test instead of evidence-before-edit:

1. **Story need.** Name what the reader currently misses: a choice made offstage, a relationship with no scene at its crisis, a real event reported in a clause, a character who only observes.
2. **A scene, not a report.** The addition has a viewpoint character who wants something, meets resistance and leaves changed, or makes a decision that alters what comes next.
3. **It pays or plants.** It pays off something already planted, or plants something a later chapter pays. A new track is logged in [`../continuity/expansion/subplot_tracks.md`](../continuity/expansion/subplot_tracks.md) first.
4. **Invariants hold.** Historical anchors, knowledge bans, deliberate ambiguities and the series scope stay intact (`LLM_WRITING_BRIEF.md` §8, `continuity/canon.md`).
5. **No padding.** Length is never the reason. Battle and war sequences may grow only where a character's choice happens inside them, and stay within the fog rules.

Development is not a licence to revise surrounding prose for taste. Where an addition touches existing scenes, those touches are limited to what the addition needs (a stitch, a callback, a cut summary paragraph the new scene replaces), and they are reviewed under §1.

## 2. What “synthetic” actually sounds like here

Fix these when they cluster. Leave scattered singles alone, except habit I, which is fixed wherever it appears.

| Habit | Sound | Fix |
|-------|--------|-----|
| **A — Shared ledger dialect** | Several mouths use *price / weather / file / furniture / Mark me* as clever ops talk | Keep only when the POV owns that job (Hordeonius stool, Laberius water/Capua, Octavian inventory). Else revoice plain. |
| **B — Corridor stamp** | Polite face → step-aside “true” line, or an overheard verdict on the scene just played | Cut it unless the step-aside changes what someone does next, and then only within the POV’s hearing. |
| **C — Broken parentheticals** | `(` … dialogue starts … `)` closes mid-thought | Open into breaths or colon lists. |
| **D — Thesis wrappers** | Proverb that captions a cost already enacted | Cut the proverb; keep the body cost. |
| **E — Ornate stacked *as if*** | One simile doing three jobs; multi-*as if* in a paragraph | One max per beat if needed; prefer behaviour. |
| **F — Multi-clause AI cadence** | Semicolon cascades; long uniform mid-paragraph stretch | Break into shorter breaths. Not every sentence must be short. |
| **G — Hedged polish** | “as if by accident,” “station warning,” “exit-complaint,” “announced need” | Soften to ordinary cause and effect. |
| **H — Content-word catalogues** | Sculpted lists of nouns/metaphors in one line | Split, or cut the cleverest item. |
| **I — Caption fragments** | In narration, a full sentence followed by one-word or verbless fragments that label it: “He tapped the tablet once. Soft. Final.” / “Antony laughed. Not mirth. Recognition.” / “Not hurrying. That was worse.” | Fold it into the sentence (comma, colon, *rather than*), or give it a subject and verb. Fix singles too, not only clusters (author’s ruling, 2026-10-29). |

## 3. Do

1. **Pilot one chapter, cold-read, then expand.** Ch 1 → bookwide is the pattern. Do not launch a bookwide scrub from a detector paste alone.
2. **Break breath, not voice.** Shorter cadence is good; sanding every POV into the same plainness is not.
3. **Protect character-true diction.** Hordeonius invoices; Laberius boots/Capua; Trebonius market-day; Nysa withhold; Decimus ditches; Cassius salt; Octavian furniture ban (dialogue); soft-chain / digest.
4. **Keep load-bearing brands** even if a detector flags them: the soft chain of clemency; Cassius’s “I digest”; Octavian banning the “hopeful shadow” nickname and taking the name; spare Antony; the unread note at the Curia and Nysa’s separate fragment; the wrong Cinna; Decimus taken at the ford; Cassius as the man who reads information (and dies of bad information). A brand is kept, not repeated: one strong use beats four.
5. **Surgical on gold spine (Ch 14–19).** Cut padding there as anywhere (§1), but do not flatten Ides propulsion or touch the protected core.
6. **Stop after one successful feel-pass** on a chapter unless a human cold read names a concrete leftover cluster.
7. **Hard cap: two style-only passes per chapter** (voice, cadence, detector-led). A third is a revert signal (see Ch 5). The cap does not apply to fixes for a reader’s note, continuity or factual fixes, cutting repetition (§1), or development (§1a); those are judged on their evidence, however many times the chapter has been opened.
8. **No em dashes** in scenic prose (author voice).
9. **No modern weekdays** (Monday–Sunday). Rome runs on Kalends / Nones / Ides and the eight-day market cycle (*nundinae*). Trebonius’s boring-business leash is **market day**, not Monday.
10. **Make the voice survive the improvement.** A line is not better because it is shorter, clearer, more vivid or more elegant. Before changing dialogue or close-third narration, check that it keeps the speaker’s vocabulary and professional habits, their evasions and blind spots, what they know, their status and immediate aim, and their own rhythm and degree of polish.
11. **Read the neighbours.** After the change, reread the voices around it. A line that shines alone but makes Cicero, Decimus and Hordeonius sound like one authorial intelligence is a regression. **Acceptance:** the revision fixes a demonstrated weakness without flattening a deliberate distinction or adding a new synthetic pattern (§2).

## 4. Don’t

1. **Don’t optimise for AI detectors.** Length averages, function-word ratios, and 3-gram stats will keep flagging literary historical fiction. Gaming them flattens the book.
2. **Don’t scrub owner metaphors.** Removing Hordeonius’s ledger religion or Laberius’s filing because “ledger dialect = AI” destroys POV.
3. **Don’t delete every short punch** (`Cassius ate.` / `He sat.` / `Ditch timing.`). A short sentence with a subject and verb, or a held brand, is human, not the problem. The one-word caption fragment is not a short punch: it is habit I (§2).
   **One-word sentences in narration** are kept only where the stop itself is the event: a recognition (Cicero seeing Philologus, Laberius seeing Rufrius), broken news rendered as heard (Ch 20 “Knives. Senate.”), or a word written on the page (*Clean.*). About one per chapter at most; any new one is named in the pass log. Speech may clip as people do (“Yes.” “Next.” “Later.”); the rule covers narration.
4. **Don’t stack aphorism replacements.** Swapping one thesis wrapper for another (“That would have shown need”) is still caption.
5. **Don’t run chapter-by-chapter detector loops.** If Ch N still “fails” after two feel-passes, leave it and trust cold read — or revert.
6. **Don’t run polish wars on the settled chapters.** Each has a named core that changes only for a demonstrated defect (§1); the prose around it is ordinary text:
   - **Prologue and Ch 1–3:** Cato refuses the pardon at Utica; the clemency dinner where nobody says so; Cicero wins Ligarius’s pardon from Caesar (Ch 3).
   - **Ch 20–23:** the Liberators leave Rome; Octavian takes the name; Nysa’s house is emptied by kind stewards; Statilius keeps his name off the petition.
   - **Philippi (Ch 35–36):** Cassius dies on bad information, Pindarus holding the blade; Brutus dies on his own sword with Strato holding it; credit for Octavian’s survival stays contested.
7. **Don’t treat the watch-list as a hit queue.** Notice ≠ revise.
8. **Don’t impose quotas or bans** on sentence length, fragments (habit I aside), adverbs, metaphors, humour, sensory detail, aphorisms or any construction beyond the author-voice rules above (§3.8–9).
9. **Don’t require every scene to contain** conflict, humour, a physical action or a reversal, and don’t expand a landed chapter to reach a word count.
10. **Don’t rewrite a flagged passage that works.** A flag is a reason to look, not a reason to change.

## 5. Stopping rule and change budget

Every final-stage pass (voice, copyedit, continuity, history) has a stated purpose and scope before it starts.

1. **Confirmed defects before preferences.** Optional improvements wait until confirmed defects are fixed or deferred.
2. **No scope creep.** Unrelated imperfections noticed along the way go on the pass record as open items; they do not widen the pass.
3. **No repeat audits** of the same kind (the same checklist over the same chapters) without a material manuscript change or a demonstrated gap in the earlier audit’s coverage. A new question about the book is not a repeat audit.
4. **Check what changed.** After an edit, verify the revised passage and the dependencies it touches (see [`LLM_WRITING_BRIEF.md`](LLM_WRITING_BRIEF.md) §12), not the whole manuscript by default.
5. **Keep a short record:** accepted fixes, rejected recommendations (one line on why) and unresolved issues.

**A pass ends** when its defined defects are resolved or explicitly deferred. It does not create an automatic successor pass.

A development phase (§1a) ends when the story needs it was opened for are met: its tracks are paid or deliberately left open. Revision passes then resume under the rules above.

## 6. Process checklist (any future voice work)

```
[ ] Purpose and scope written down before starting (§5)
[ ] Each change has the five-part evidence (§1)
[ ] Is there a human cold-read complaint (stumble, sameness, thinness)?
[ ] Or only a detector paste?  → if only detector, prefer leave alone or one light pilot chapter
[ ] Named chapters + named habits (A–I), not “make it less AI”
[ ] No new one-word or verbless caption sentence in narration (habit I)
[ ] One pass → author cold read → stop / selective expand / revert
[ ] Pass log: held beats + cut samples + non-goals + rejected recommendations + open items
[ ] If pass 2 still feels wrong to the author, revert toward last good tip
```

---

## 7. Success test (human)

A cold reader can:

- feel a **room with bodies**, not a caption explaining the room  
- tell **who is speaking** without the name tag more often than not  
- keep reading through Ides and Philippi without inventing a missing single hero  

A cold reader should **not** need the prose to win a detector score.
