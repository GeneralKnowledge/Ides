# Human-voice rules — Book 1

**Status:** Standing craft law after the 2026-10-19 human-voice work.  
**Purpose:** Keep the novel feeling like rooms with bodies, not polished caption.  
**Not:** A detector-optimisation programme. Detector flags are clues, not success metrics.

**Evidence:** Ch 1 pilot (#64) worked; bookwide selective pass (#65) worked; Ch 2 / Ch 4 deepenings worked; Ch 5 detector-led passes #69–#71 thinned past the useful point and should be reverted (#72).

Read with [`STYLE_GLOSSARY.md`](STYLE_GLOSSARY.md), [`voice/POV_DICTION.md`](voice/POV_DICTION.md), and the watch-list in [`../archive/reports/whole_book_editorial_assessment.md`](../archive/reports/whole_book_editorial_assessment.md).

---

## 0. One-line law

**Prefer a cold read that feels human over a scrub that scores better.**  
If a second or third pass on the same chapter is chasing detector language, stop. Revert to the last version that still had presence.

---

## 1. What “synthetic” actually sounds like here

Fix these when they cluster. Leave scattered singles alone.

| Habit | Sound | Fix |
|-------|--------|-----|
| **A — Shared ledger dialect** | Several mouths use *price / weather / file / furniture / Mark me* as clever ops talk | Keep only when the POV owns that job (Hordeonius stool, Laberius water/Capua, Octavian inventory). Else revoice plain. |
| **B — Corridor stamp** | Polite face → step-aside “true” line, every room | Keep highest-stakes (Cassius Curia/Capitol; Pacuvius). Cut or vary restatements. |
| **C — Broken parentheticals** | `(` … dialogue starts … `)` closes mid-thought | Open into breaths or colon lists. |
| **D — Thesis wrappers** | Proverb that captions a cost already enacted | Cut the proverb; keep the body cost. |
| **E — Ornate stacked *as if*** | One simile doing three jobs; multi-*as if* in a paragraph | One max per beat if needed; prefer behaviour. |
| **F — Multi-clause AI cadence** | Semicolon cascades; long uniform mid-paragraph stretch | Break into shorter breaths. Not every sentence must be short. |
| **G — Hedged polish** | “as if by accident,” “station warning,” “exit-complaint,” “announced need” | Soften to ordinary cause and effect. |
| **H — Content-word catalogues** | Sculpted lists of nouns/metaphors in one line | Split, or cut the cleverest item. |

## 2. Do

1. **Pilot one chapter, cold-read, then expand.** Ch 1 → bookwide is the pattern. Do not launch a bookwide scrub from a detector paste alone.
2. **Break breath, not voice.** Shorter cadence is good; sanding every POV into the same plainness is not.
3. **Protect character-true diction.** Hordeonius invoices; Laberius boots/Capua; Trebonius market-day; Nysa withhold; Decimus ditches; Cassius salt; Octavian furniture ban (dialogue); soft-chain / digest.
4. **Keep load-bearing brands** even if a detector flags them: soft chain, “I digest,” hopeful-shadow ban, name-take, spare Antony, NOTE-1 mud, wrong Cinna, Seius mule, Decimus ford, Cassius INF.
5. **Surgical on gold spine (Ch 14–19).** Prefer fewer, higher-value cuts. Do not flatten Ides propulsion.
6. **Stop after one successful feel-pass** on a chapter unless a human cold read names a concrete leftover cluster.
7. **Hard cap: two targeted passes per chapter** without a new whole-book reason. A third detector-led pass is a revert signal (see Ch 5).
8. **No em dashes** in scenic prose (author voice).
9. **No modern weekdays** (Monday–Sunday). Rome runs on Kalends / Nones / Ides and the eight-day market cycle (*nundinae*). Trebonius’s boring-business leash is **market day**, not Monday.

---

## 3. Don’t

1. **Don’t optimise for AI detectors.** Length averages, function-word ratios, and 3-gram stats will keep flagging literary historical fiction. Gaming them flattens the book.
2. **Don’t scrub owner metaphors.** Removing Hordeonius’s ledger religion or Laberius’s filing because “ledger dialect = AI” destroys POV.
3. **Don’t delete every short punch** (`Cassius ate.` / `Glory.` / `Ditch timing.`) — those are human, not the problem.
4. **Don’t stack aphorism replacements.** Swapping one thesis wrapper for another (“That would have shown need”) is still caption.
5. **Don’t run chapter-by-chapter detector loops.** If Ch N still “fails” after two feel-passes, leave it and trust cold read — or revert.
6. **Don’t reopen Ch 1–3 / Ch 20–23 / Philippi** for polish wars without a new whole-book reason (settled non-goals).
7. **Don’t treat the watch-list as a hit queue.** Notice ≠ revise.

---

## 4. Process checklist (any future voice work)

```
[ ] Is there a human cold-read complaint (stumble, sameness, thinness)?
[ ] Or only a detector paste?  → if only detector, prefer leave alone or one light pilot chapter
[ ] Named chapters + named habits (A–H), not “make it less AI”
[ ] One pass → author cold read → stop / selective expand / revert
[ ] Pass log: held beats + cut samples + non-goals
[ ] If pass 2 still feels wrong to the author, revert toward last good tip
```

---

## 5. Success test (human)

A cold reader can:

- feel a **room with bodies**, not a caption explaining the room  
- tell **who is speaking** without the name tag more often than not  
- keep reading through Ides and Philippi without inventing a missing single hero  

A cold reader should **not** need the prose to win a detector score.
