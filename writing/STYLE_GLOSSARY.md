# Style Glossary — First-Pass Guardrails

**Also read:** [`HUMAN_VOICE_RULES.md`](HUMAN_VOICE_RULES.md) for anti-synthetic cadence, shared ledger dialect, and stop/revert caps after the 2026-10 human-voice work.

## Forms of address (approximate; prefer restraint)

| Situation | Prefer | Avoid |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Elite men in public | Cognomen / full tria nomina on first tag | Modern “Mr.” |
| Client to patron | Deference; “you” with hierarchical behaviour | Buddy banter |
| Master to slave | Short imperatives; name or “boy/girl” carefully | Soft modern HR tone |
| Between peers | Cognomen; occasional praenomen if intimacy earned | First-name American casual |
| Women of rank | Proper names; respect matron status | Anachronistic “Lady X” spam |

When unsure: **show hierarchy in space and interruption patterns**, not in fancy titles.

## Time & calendar texture

- Prefer “at the second hour,” “before the Senate met,” “on the eve of the Ides.”  
- Month *Quintilis* becoming *Iulius* is political (Act 4/5 texture).  
- Market days / festivals as rhythm (Lupercalia must land).

## Sensory defaults (use; don’t inventory every room)

Smoke, urine alleys, garum, blood from sacrifice, oil in baths, horse sweat, wet wool togas, seal wax, dust on Campania roads, cold March marble.

## Dialogue guardrails

- Short. Status-shaped.  
- Indirect threats > villain monologues.  
- No modern idioms (“okay,” “focus,” “optics” as buzzword — you may show optics as behaviour).  
- Letters may be quoted in part; they are stylised.

### Exposition: reminders allowed, briefings banned

- The narrator still does not stop for a “X was the …” history sentence.
- A fact may sit in dialogue when **the listener does not know it**, when **one person reminds another** the way people do (“You forget he governed Gaul for Caesar”), or when the fact is **leverage**: wonder, accusation or a jab (“How in Hades did you get Syria?”).
- A POV character may hold a fact in one clause of memory.
- Banned: a speaker briefing a listener on what both already know (“As you know, Bob”). The tell is “everyone knows”, or a speaker reciting the listener’s own province, office or career to them.
- Introduce each real person **once** with traits; later scenes show them, they do not re-list the same traits.

### TONE-1 seasoning (see `voice/TONE_SPARTACUS_GRRM.md`)

- Slight elevation OK (Spartacus-adjacent); not every line inverted.  
- **Batiatus rule:** humour from ego that does not know it is funny (F4 primary; Cicero vanity; Balbus).  
- **GRRM habits:** subtext, debt/food/body texture, dark asides beside real cost — not Westeros cosplay.  
- Dial humour **down** for Ides stabbing and Cicero’s death.

## Violence

- Ides: chaotic, close, ugly; not ballet.  
- Proscriptions: bureaucratic terror + street greed.  
- Avoid gore tourism; keep moral weight.

---

## Sex

- Present when it is power/alliance/vulnerability (Servilia history; Cleopatra alliance; Fulvia partnership).  
- Not modern romance-novel default.  
- Consent and coercion are historically asymmetric — handle without exploitation chic.

---

## VOICE-1 (locked for first pass)

- Close third limited  
- Past tense  
- No chapter epigraphs from future historians  
- No “Little did he know”
