# Voice pilot, 2026-11-11

Three scenes rewritten in full to test one question: can the book stop explaining itself without losing its voice?

An outside reader of Ch 1–6 found that the narration sees a thing and then says what it means, that every viewpoint does this in the same way, that everyone wins every exchange, and that scenes end by summing themselves up. Line edits would not reach that, so these three scenes were rewritten from the new mind sheets (`writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md`) and not trimmed.

| File | Replaces | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| `sc02_nysa_triumph_weather.md` | Ch 2, SC-02 | Can a viewpoint perceive the world in its own way? |
| `sc12_cicero_forever.md` | Ch 6, SC-12 | Can talk and narration stop making the same point several times? |
| `sc14_hordeonius_closed_market.md` | Ch 6, SC-14 | Can a witty character stay himself without a maxim in every paragraph? |

**The book is not changed.** `drafts/book1/` and the tracked DOCX are as on main (225cefa). If the pilot is accepted, the scenes are spliced into their chapters, the chapter notes are updated as each file’s Notes section says, and the DOCX is regenerated.

**How it is judged.** A reading copy with both versions of each scene, unlabelled and in mixed order, is in the project files (`exports_2026-11-11_voice_pilot/`), with a separate key. The question is whether the scene feels more like something happening to people. Fewer quotable lines is not the measure.

**What the pilot was asked to do** (Reuben, 2026-11-11): do not make the prose plainer; make the people more particular. Keep the book’s intelligence. A line that is good and earned stays (each scene keeps its opening or its best line).

**One thing to decide from the result: length.** The rewrites are longer because an event takes more room than a verdict on it.

| Scene | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Nysa | 672 | 1,397 |
| Cicero | 754 | 1,186 |
| Hordeonius | 424 | 842 |

If a third of the book’s scenes were rewritten at this rate the book would grow by roughly 15,000 to 20,000 words. That may be right, or the rewrites may need to be tighter. The reading copy asks the reader whether the length earned its place.

**Rules suspended for this work** (Reuben, 2026-11-11): owner diction (§3.3), load-bearing brands (§3.4), the cap on passes (§3.7) and the preference for cuts over rewrites, all in `writing/HUMAN_VOICE_RULES.md`. The history, the Ides events, the ending, no em dashes and no explaining narrator all still hold.
