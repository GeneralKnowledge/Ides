# SC-31 — Hold the Consul

POV: Trebonius / Decimus · Portico of Pompey  
Date / place: 13–14 March · colonnade shade  
Canon refs: CHOREO-1 · M6 · spare decision  
Info ENTER: Spare decision known to few · EXIT: Trebonius tasked to delay Antony  
Overhear risk: Medium–Low

---

“Keep him talking,” Decimus said.

They stood where Pompey’s stone still watched the living arrange Monday—the same Monday Trebonius had priced as thirst in a festival cup, now grown teeth. Trebonius nodded like a man accepting a boring errand while his eyes did the opposite. His face had the mildness of someone who had been consul under Caesar and still knew how to make business smell like friendship.

“Gaul talk,” Decimus went on. “Debt talk. The sort of talk that makes a consul lean in. Hold him in the portico until the room inside has finished being a Senate.”

“And if he smells it?” Trebonius asked.

“Then you invent a longer Monday,” Decimus said. “You are good at Monday.”

Glaucia, Trebonius’s attendant, waited just inside polite earshot with the patience of men who misread shouts for sport—and who would later sell whatever soft version of this huddle paid best. Decimus did not look at him. Looking would have made the true errand a performance.

“Cover,” Trebonius murmured, for Glaucia’s benefit as much as Decimus’s. “I lingered for provincial papers. I lingered because Antony likes to be lingered with. I lingered because the morning was cold.”

“Any of those,” Decimus said. “None of the true one.”

They parted with the clasp of men confirming a hunt—station warmth, festival-brother hands. Ten paces on, Trebonius’s mildness thinned for Glaucia alone. “If anyone asks, I am a bore with Gaul on my tongue. If anyone listens well, I am already tired of being the polite half of other men’s courage.”

Glaucia’s mouth twitched. “Bores survive Mondays.”

“Bores,” Trebonius said, “also hold consuls while better men invent their knives.” He sipped nothing from an empty hand and went to practice longer Mondays. The spare-Antony mercy now had a body attached: Trebonius’s voice as a leash.

# SC-32 — Blind Calm

POV: Cicero · villa letter desk  
Date / place: 13–14 March · afternoon  
Canon refs: digest pattern · C7 · B3  
Info ENTER: Elite rumour only · EXIT: Still not ops  
Overhear risk: N/A

---

Cicero wrote to Atticus about games.

Not only games—also a cousin’s lawsuit, a bookish slight, the eternal question of whether Rome still deserved his lungs—but games sat in the ink like a man pretending the city was still a holiday problem. Elite rumour had brought him kingship talk, Sibylline trial balloons, Calpurnia’s household nerves as gossip with dinner. None of it arrived as a roster. None of it named a morning in Pompey’s Curia.

“The city is theatrical,” he wrote, then sharper: “Theatre is what remains when men of standing refuse to stage a Republic.”

He considered a sentence about Caesar’s departure east and the offices stacked like bricks ahead of lesser men. He considered—briefly—whether Brutus looked tired at last dinner. Tired men were everywhere in March; that proved nothing.

Sphaerus had brought a note from Hirtius’s orbit about nothing that mattered. Cicero answered with courtesy that congratulated itself. He didn't know Trebonius was learning how to hold a consul with talk. He didn't know Decimus had become a key. He believed, with the confidence that had always been half his talent, that if blades were coming they would first have to pass through a speech he had not yet written.

He sealed the letter. Sealing felt like action. In the garden a thrush made a sound like a hinge. Cicero took it for spring.

# SC-33 — Land Powder

POV: F3 Laberius · Italian winter quarter talk  
Date / place: Mid-March-ish · colony rumour around a brazier  
Canon refs: FS10 · B4  
Info ENTER: Love of Caesar + unpaid expectation · EXIT: Soft powder for post-Ides rage  
Overhear risk: High among soldiers

---

Sex. Laberius Cordus loved Caesar the paymaster more than Caesar the god.

Around the brazier the talk was colonies and delays—who had been promised land near Capua, who had heard the promise thin out into “after Parthia,” who still had a father’s farm seized in someone else’s victory. Spain’s mud still lived in Laberius’s boots as memory: mopping finished, donative not yet dated. Vibius, his optio, spat and said philosophers in Rome were weighing crowns while veterans weighed empty hands. Gaius of the split sandal held his palms to the heat and asked whether Parthia paid in dirt or in postponement.

“He pays,” Laberius said. “When the eagle eats, we eat.”

“When the eagle flies east,” Vibius answered, “we count dirt.”

Gaius did not grin. “Capua was a date once. Now it’s after Parthia. After Parthia is not a place.”

Vibius poked the brazier with a stick until ash rose. “Dirt without coin is still a grave with opinions. Write that on a promise and see who salutes.”

Laberius didn't hate the Senate as an idea. He hated unpaid gaps. Anger lived in unpaid gaps the way fire lived in dry grass—waiting for a match that would look, later, like grief. He didn't know a funeral would be that match. He only knew the powder was soft underfoot already, and soft powder still burned.

Someone sang a dirty song about kings that borrowed the Forum’s February panic and made it tavern-shaped. Laberius did not laugh. Capua unpaid was not a joke tonight—only powder waiting for a name he still trusted on the wind.

# SC-34 — GO

POV: Brutus / Cassius · closed room (D)  
Date / place: Night of 13–14 March  
Canon refs: App. 2.113 energy · matrix · hour map  
Info ENTER: Final roster · EXIT: GO; daggers ready · overhear near-zero  
Overhear risk: Must be near-zero

---

Names checked. Roles set—almost.

Tillius for the petition—brother’s exile on the tongue, hands ready to insult with touch. Casca for first ugly courage. Decimus for the door of trust. Trebonius for Antony. The Cascas, Cimber, the Appian edges who had said yes without making speeches: present as weight, not as a hymn.

Cassius’s finger stopped on one blank place in the list he kept only in his head. “Ligarius’s cousin,” he said. “The one who swore he would stand at the door with a message if the Forum thickened—Ligarius’s pride was supposed to buy us that body. He sent fever instead. Or a man who smelled like fever.”

Brutus waited. Missing men made rooms louder. Outside the door a creditor’s soft cough advertised itself—polite, exact, the Kalends still smiling in a throat. The cough wanted an answer as much as the blank line did. Brutus gave neither.

“We do not wait for cousins,” Cassius said. “We redistribute the door. If he talks before dawn, fever takes him and we take the door. We do not invent another postponement for a man who already chose not to stand.”

Brutus’s money sat outside the door like a creditor not invited. A note had arrived that afternoon—polite, exact, a lender who still smiled when he named the Kalends. Brutus had not answered. He could still walk out of this room, pay the man, dine as a spared philosopher, and keep the masks as decoration instead of law.

“If anyone talks between now and dawn,” Cassius said, “we are not liberators. We are a joke that bleeds.”

Brutus looked at his own hands. They were clean. He rubbed a thumb across the palm as if dirt might appear and explain him. “For the Republic,” he said, and the sentence landed thinner than the unpaid note outside the door.

Cassius did not soften. “Enough delay. Parthia will not wait for our conscience. Neither will your lender. Choose which fear runs you.”

The room was small enough that fear had to stand close and pretend it was resolve. Brutus's hesitation lasted a breath past comfort, then released.

“GO,” Brutus said.

Not inventory. Choice. The word tasted like metal and unpaid interest.

Cassius’s nod was small. Small nods moved mornings. He named the redistributed door once, checked Trebonius’s leash in principle, and stopped talking before talk became a way to unmake the lock.

Brutus nodded once. The ancestral gesture. He didn't sleep well after. Sleep was for mornings that weren't knives. Cassius slept worse and called it planning.

They left the room separately. Outside, a baker’s boy argued with a customer about ordinary March prices, and neither man looked twice at the two who had just spent a breath on knives.
