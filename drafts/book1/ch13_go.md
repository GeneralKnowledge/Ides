# SC-31 — Hold the Consul

POV: Trebonius / Decimus · Portico of Pompey  
Date / place: 13–14 March · colonnade shade  
Canon refs: CHOREO-1 · M6 · spare decision  
Info ENTER: Spare decision known to few · EXIT: Trebonius tasked to delay Antony  
Overhear risk: Medium–Low

---

“Keep him talking,” Decimus said.

They stood where Pompey’s stone still watched the living arrange Monday. Trebonius nodded like a man accepting a boring errand that was not boring. His face had the mildness of someone who had been consul under Caesar and still knew how to make business smell like friendship.

“Gaul talk,” Decimus went on. “Debt talk. The sort of talk that makes a consul lean in. Hold him in the portico until the room inside has finished being a Senate.”

“And if he smells it?” Trebonius asked.

“Then you invent a longer Monday,” Decimus said. “You are good at Monday.”

Glaucia, Trebonius’s attendant, waited out of polite earshot with the patience of men who misread shouts for sport. Decimus glanced once toward the Curia’s future mouth and felt the key of himself turn in a lock he could not unturn.

“Cover,” Trebonius murmured. “I lingered for provincial papers. I lingered because Antony likes to be lingered with. I lingered because the morning was cold.”

“Any of those,” Decimus said. “None of the true one.”

They parted with the clasp of men confirming a hunt. The spare-Antony mercy now had a body attached: Trebonius’s voice as a leash.

# SC-32 — Blind Calm

POV: Cicero · villa letter desk  
Date / place: 13–14 March · afternoon  
Canon refs: digest pattern · C7 · B3  
Info ENTER: Elite rumour only · EXIT: Still not ops  
Overhear risk: N/A

---

Cicero wrote to Atticus about games.

Not only games—also a cousin’s lawsuit, a bookish slight, the eternal question of whether Rome still deserved his lungs—but games sat in the ink like a man pretending the city was still a holiday problem. Elite rumour had brought him kingship weather, Sibylline trial balloons, Calpurnia’s household nerves as gossip with dinner. None of it arrived as a roster. None of it named a morning in Pompey’s Curia.

“The city is theatrical,” he wrote, then sharper: “Theatre is what remains when men of standing refuse to stage a Republic.”

He considered a sentence about Caesar’s departure east and the offices stacked like bricks ahead of lesser men. He considered—briefly—whether Brutus looked tired at last dinner. Tired was not a conspiracy. Tired was March.

Sphaerus had brought a note from Hirtius’s orbit about nothing that mattered. Cicero answered with courtesy that congratulated itself. He did not know Trebonius was learning how to hold a consul with talk. He did not know Decimus had become a key. He believed, with the confidence that had always been half his talent, that if blades were coming they would first have to pass through a speech he had not yet written.

He sealed the letter. Sealing felt like action. In the garden a thrush made a sound like a hinge. Cicero took it for spring.

# SC-33 — Land Powder

POV: F3 Laberius · Italian winter quarter talk  
Date / place: Mid-March-ish · colony rumour around a brazier  
Canon refs: FS10 · B4  
Info ENTER: Love of Caesar + unpaid expectation · EXIT: Soft powder for post-Ides rage  
Overhear risk: High among soldiers

---

Sex. Laberius Cordus loved Caesar the paymaster more than Caesar the god.

Around the brazier the talk was colonies and delays—who had been promised land near Capua, who had heard the promise thin out into “after Parthia,” who still had a father’s farm seized in someone else’s victory. Vibius, his optio, spat and said philosophers in Rome were weighing crowns while veterans weighed empty hands.

“He pays,” Laberius said. “When the eagle eats, we eat.”

“When the eagle flies east,” Vibius answered, “we count dirt.”

Laberius did not hate the Senate as an idea. He hated unpaid gaps. Anger lived in unpaid gaps the way fire lived in dry grass—waiting for a match that would look, later, like grief. He did not know a funeral would be that match. He only knew the powder was soft underfoot already, and soft powder still burned.

Someone sang a dirty song about kings. Laberius laughed once, short. Laughter was not loyalty. Loyalty was the next donative arriving with a name he trusted on the wind.

# SC-34 — GO

POV: Brutus / Cassius · closed room (D)  
Date / place: Night of 13–14 March  
Canon refs: App. 2.113 energy · matrix · hour map  
Info ENTER: Final roster · EXIT: GO; daggers ready · overhear near-zero  
Overhear risk: Must be near-zero

---

Names checked. Roles set.

Tillius for the petition—brother’s exile on the tongue, hands ready to insult with touch. Casca for first ugly courage. Decimus for the door of trust. Trebonius for Antony. The Cascas, Cimber, the Appian edges who had said yes without making speeches: present as weight, not as a hymn.

Cassius said, “Enough delay. Parthia will not wait for our conscience.”

Brutus said, “For the Republic.”

Neither speech was enough. They both knew it. The room was small enough that fear had to stand close and pretend it was resolve. Brutus’s money worries sat somewhere outside the door like a creditor not invited; Cassius’s contempt for hesitation sat inside like a second lamp.

“If anyone talks between now and dawn,” Cassius said, “we are not liberators. We are a joke that bleeds.”

Brutus nodded once. The ancestral gesture. He did not sleep well after. Sleep was for mornings that were not knives. Cassius slept worse and called it planning.

They left the room separately. The city outside still believed in ordinary March. That belief was the conspiracy’s last luck.
