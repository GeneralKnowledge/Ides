# SC-26 — Kitchen Prodigies

POV: F1 Nysa · Domus Publica service court  
Date / place: Late winter morning · kitchen / service court  
Canon refs: Suet. 81 gossip · E1 · Dama  
Info ENTER: Routine piety · EXIT: Hears king-bird / Spurinna talk; cannot parse  
Overhear risk: Extreme (staff)

---

Staff traded omens like recipes.

Nysa carried a basin past Dama, the kitchen boy, who swore the sacred chickens had been sullen again. Someone older said sullen chickens were a morning habit, not a prophecy. A washerwoman from the next court leaned on the doorpost with a story she had bought cheap in the Forum: a king-bird torn apart in Pompey’s hall, other birds watching like a jury that already knew the verdict.

“And Spurinna,” the washerwoman added, pleased with the name, “told him to beware the Ides. Beware is a priest’s whole trade.”

Nysa set the basin down more carefully than the water required. When the washerwoman said *beware*, two kitchen girls glanced at the service door and one checked the bar with her thumb—habit, not theology. Nysa noted which doors got the thumb. Doors that stayed careful meant fewer beatings for incomplete messages. Senate and gods could keep their own names.

Pallas, dressing Calpurnia upstairs, had said the mistress slept like someone running. Nysa put that beside the bird and the chickens and did not decide which was true.

Phileros passed through the court and sniffed. “If you have time for birds,” he said, “you have time for the vestibule mats.”

Nysa moved. In her tunic, against the ribs, there was not yet a wax fragment—only the habit of listening. Listening kept you fed.

Later she told Medullina, in the Subura, that the house was full of birds and priests. Medullina wanted names of senators. Nysa gave her chickens. Chickens were safer currency.

# SC-27 — Only a King

POV: Cicero · villa fringe of Rome  
Date / place: Same season · letter desk, afternoon  
Canon refs: OMEN-1 manufactured · C7 · Parthia clock  
Info ENTER: Parthia chatter · EXIT: Hears “only a king can win Parthia” as trial balloon; wrong floater guess  
Overhear risk: Elite

---

Cicero sniffed the Sibylline talking-point the way he sniffed a bad period in someone else’s oration.

They said—someone always said—that the books implied only a *king* could conquer Parthia. The word arrived at dinner wrapped in piety and ambition, like a fish in vine leaves. Caesarian flatterers had been testing kingship talk for weeks; Cicero could taste the test. Heaven, when it spoke this clearly in a politician’s interest, was usually a man with a throat.

“Balbus,” he told the empty room, because Atticus was not there and the room would not contradict him, “or one of Balbus’s softer knives. They float crowns to see who salutes.”

He was wrong about the floater. Wrong guesses were still useful; they proved the air was combustible. He set down a line for Atticus about games and omens and the city’s appetite for theatre, then scratched half of it out. Letters were oxygen. Oxygen could also burn.

He did not imagine knives in a Curia. He imagined speeches, decrees, a Republic that could still be talked back into shape if the right man—himself, confessedly—timed the Philippics of the soul correctly. Parthia was a clock for Caesar. For Cicero it was still a topic.

He pictured Balbus smiling the soft smile of a man who never needed a dagger because he owned calendars. The picture comforted him. Intelligent men stayed blind that way and still felt busy.

Outside, a courier waited—Hirtius’s orbit again, a short note asking whether Cicero would dine that week and “speak plainly about the Parthia talk before it grew teeth.” Cicero read it twice. Plain speech would cost him a dinner invitation from men who still floated crowns as piety; silence would cost him nothing he could invoice today.

He told the courier, “Tell Hirtius the books are being misused by flatterers, and that I am occupied with letters that keep the Republic’s throat clear.” Occupied. Clear. The words sounded like action and were a refusal to leave the desk.

The courier hesitated. “He asked for you in the room, not on the tablet.”

“Then he may read my tablet in the room,” Cicero said, and sealed nothing that committed his body to anyone’s test balloon.

“Only a king,” he murmured afterward, tasting the phrase like a bad period, and returned to the desk before the courier’s dust had settled in the courtyard.
