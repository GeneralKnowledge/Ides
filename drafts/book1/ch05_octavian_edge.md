# SC-10: Nobody Watches

POV: Octavian (sole) · Forum / temple steps
Date / place: Public ceremony edge, Rome · late 45
Canon refs: FS01–03 · C5
Info ENTER: Kin nobody watches · EXIT: Wrong chest inventory; packing lists
Overhear risk: Low–N/A
**G1:** One primary POV; Apollonia is dwell continuation, not mosaic cut

On the temple steps above the Forum, heat came off the stone into Octavian’s thin shoes. He stood where important men’s shadows fell and practised being invisible on purpose.

He took his own inventory while he waited, because no one else would. Gaius Octavius. Eighteen, and nobody yet had a reason to remember it. Caesar’s sister’s grandson, which made him a great-nephew: near enough to be invited, far enough to be stood at the edge. On his father’s side, a respectable house with a modest fortune and no armies. On his great-uncle’s, a great many armies. The gap between those two columns was the whole of his position.

Priests moved with the boredom of men who handled gods professionally. Senators performed importance for one another. A woman selling wreaths named three wrong officials and sold the wreaths anyway. He listened more than he spoke. A boy without legions borrowed maps with his ears.

Caesar’s glance found him once, brief as a list check, not unkind, not enough. No prophecy in it. Octavian’s shoulders stayed still until the glance moved on. Only then did he let out the breath he had been holding. He didn’t look at Agrippa to confirm he had been seen. Looking would have spent the moment.

Marcus Agrippa at his shoulder said nothing. Same age. Farm-bred, a soldier’s forearms, no family name worth the price of a seat. He had attached himself to Octavian the way some dogs attach themselves to the one person in the house who forgets to be afraid of them. Apollodorus, his Greek, hired to turn rhetoric into a statesman and so far the maker of a very good cloak-holder, fidgeted somewhere behind with the cloaks like a man who wanted his principal larger than the hour allowed.

“They don’t know your name yet,” Agrippa murmured.

“Good,” Octavian said. “Names that arrive early get spent.”

“That sounds wise. Where did you read it?”

“I made it up on the steps.”

“It’s very good.”

“It’s very hot,” said Octavian, “and I would trade it for a seat in the shade.”

A senator’s attendant brushed past and apologised to Agrippa by mistake. Octavian kept the mistake quiet. Corrections announced you wanted space you had not earned. A moment later the same attendant returned with a reserved place nearer the rail, meant for Caesar’s kin, and offered it to Agrippa again, eyes skipping Octavian like a cloak-stand. Agrippa didn’t take the place. He tipped his chin a fraction toward Octavian. The attendant’s face flickered annoyance, then blankness, then a bow that arrived late and wrong.

Octavian stepped into the gap anyway, thin shoes on hotter stone. The attendant’s arm stayed a half-breath too long in the way, then withdrew without apology. Octavian counted faces from the new angle: who leaned toward Caesar, who leaned toward money, who leaned only toward their own reflection. When the ceremony ended he was still publicly irrelevant. In his head the reserved place had already been entered beside the wrong chest.

“He looked at you,” Agrippa said, once the crowd had thinned.

“He looked at the steps.”

“He looked at you on the steps.”

“He was checking whether I was ill again.” Octavian had been ill most of his life, coughs and fevers and a stomach that disliked every campaign he had ever approached, and Caesar knew it. That spring, when Caesar had gone to finish the war in Spain, Octavian had followed late, half-wrecked by sea and sickness, and arrived to find the battle already won. His uncle had looked at him as if he were a mildly interesting weather report. Then he had asked, with great politeness, whether the journey had been bad. It had been the longest conversation they had ever had.

“That’s a conversation,” Agrippa had said at the time.

“It was weather.”

The unfinished inventory followed him off the steps. Rome’s dinners did not suddenly learn his name. Packing lists did. Within days the same thin shoes stood on ship plank instead of temple stone, and the wrong chest became a joke Agrippa refused to finish aloud.

# SC-11: Apollonia

POV: Octavian (sole) · Illyrian coast
Date / place: Apollonia drill/study · early 44
Canon refs: FS01–03 · C5 · Parthia prep
Info ENTER: Distance with homework · EXIT: Bond with Agrippa; far from Senate heat; still unfinished
Overhear risk: Low–N/A

---

Apollonia was not a reward. It was distance with homework.

“A Greek town with Italy’s heel for a view,” Agrippa had said from the boat rail, and that was description enough. Caesar had not chosen it for him. Over the hills in Macedonia the army was gathering, and the officers who came down to the coast said the numbers the way other men said prayers: sixteen legions, ten thousand horse, the best of what Spain and Africa had left alive. Then Parthia, beyond Syria, where Crassus’s eagles still hung in some temple. Whoever brought the eagles home, the officers said, would be remembered longer than the man who had only won the war at home, and then they glanced to see whether the boy had heard.

Somewhere in that plan, Caesar had given his great-nephew a place. Octavian was to study with the army and sail for the East beside the general’s staff. He was to learn the work of a master of the horse, the second chair, the man who rode behind the dictator. Nobody at the dinners had been told. If they had been, they would have laughed.

On the Illyrian coast he studied war like homework for a test someone else might take.

Agrippa drilled until sweat made honesty cheaper than speech. Octavian’s body complained; he didn’t narrate the complaint. Weakness spoken aloud became other men’s tool. He asked questions that sounded younger than his calculations, supply, winds, what older officers feared when they joked about the East.

“What do they fear?” Agrippa asked, on the second night, while they scrubbed their gear.

“The bowmen. Mounted archers who shoot backwards while they run. They say you can chase them until you drop and never touch a man.”

“That’s not a fear. That’s a boast about the enemy.”

“It’s both. When a man brags about how clever the other side is, he’s already asking for a bigger army.”

Those officers brightened at Parthia talk, months east, glory, the map widening. One sketched a route in dust with a stick and erased it with his boot, laughing. Octavian copied the erased lines before the boot finished. Then he looked at the sea long enough that Agrippa followed his gaze. Rome’s dinners and this packing list never shared a shore.

“When he calls us,” Agrippa said once, meaning Caesar.

“When he calls,” Octavian agreed. He almost added a colder clause about mouths and mornings, then bit it off. He kept what he could own without saying it: the dust-route, the wind, Agrippa’s stubborn loyalty, and the attendant’s late bow, filed where names that arrive early get spent.

An older officer clapped his shoulder and called him Caesar’s hopeful shadow. Octavian smiled the polite smile and didn’t correct the furniture.

When the officer moved off, Agrippa bumped Octavian’s elbow, barely play, barely warning. “Cheer up, Shadow. At least they remember you have edges.”

Octavian’s polite smile thinned to nothing. “Don’t.”

“Don’t what?”

“That name. Not from you.” He wiped sweat with the wrong wrist, awkward. “They can call me furniture. You don’t.”

Agrippa’s mouth twitched, caught, not cruel. “Noted. No shadows. Only drill.” He paused. “Though you do look like a boy arguing with the sun.”

Octavian almost laughed, then turned it into a cough so the older men would not own the sound. “Keep close,” he said, still awkward with warmth.

Apollodorus found them at the end of the day, grey with dust and distaste, carrying a roll of Greek he had been trying to teach Octavian for a month. “If the young master is finished being a soldier,” he said, “there is a speech of Demosthenes that has been waiting since breakfast.”

“What does he say?” Agrippa asked.

“He says that Athens should have fought Philip while there was still time.”

“Did Athens listen?”

“No.”

“Then he’s no use to us,” said Agrippa, and Octavian laughed in earnest and had to turn it into a second cough. Apollodorus rolled the scroll with the dignity of a man who had heard worse reviews.

They drilled again until the erased dust-route lived in Octavian’s legs as much as in his head. An officer asked whether the boy would last a month east. Agrippa answered with water and another lap, which was clearer than speech. Octavian did not thank him in front of the men. Thanks announced need.

Before sleep they shared a heel of bread Agrippa had bullied from a cook who respected fists more than kin names. No officers. No Parthia sticks in dust. Just the ordinary theft of calories and Agrippa’s rule spoken twice without theatre: no shadows between them. Octavian practised a thank-you and discarded it. He bumped Agrippa’s shoulder back, clumsy, almost play, and lay down sore, young, and unfinished, exactly as far from Senate heat as the sea allowed. He didn’t dream of crowns. He dreamed of the reserved place offered to the wrong chest, then of missing a drill step and being laughed at by men whose names he needed.
