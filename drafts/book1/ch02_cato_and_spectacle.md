# SC-01: Africa Prices

POV: F4 Hordeonius (Cicero fringe in-scene, not a cut) · Forum basilica
Date / place: Late 46 BC · banker stalls / basilica edge
Canon refs: A1 · Chain 1
Info ENTER: Elite soft-chain talk already in the air · EXIT: Named unpaid risk on new-ring client; Thapsus/Cato still moral-economic weather
Overhear risk: High (Forum)

---

Under the basilica’s shade the stalls smelled of wet wool and coin-oil. Africa hit the boards like a price change with blood in it.

“Thapsus,” someone said. Then: “Cato.” The names were not fresh, but the Forum still wanted them loud. Shouts. Bets. Sudden philosophy, months after the runner’s dust should have settled. A man at the next board was still arguing Utica hard enough to reopen a vein. Hordeonius didn't shout. Shouting was for men who sold certainty by the jug.

He knew the shape of it, as any man did who had to price what came after. Thapsus was the battle, the last big one, a spring morning in Africa when the Pompeian armies ran out of men. Utica was the town behind it, where Cato had refused to run, refused to beg, and sat up with a book until his own hand ended the argument. Caesar had been on his way with a pardon. That was the joke the Forum could not stop telling. Cato had died to avoid being forgiven.

“Odds, if you want them,” Sosia said, setting a tablet on the counter. Sosia had been at Hordeonius’s elbow since dawn, collecting opinions along with the coins. “Two to one the physician botched the first stitching and Cato only finished the job. Evens that he tore it open himself. Fifteen to one on the version where he accepted the pardon and was merely rude about it.”

“That last one is not a bet. It is a fairy tale.”

“People pay well for fairy tales at fifteen to one.”

“And the stitches?”

“Everyone is taking the stitches. A man mended, then unmended. It has a shape.”

“It has a bad manager,” said Hordeonius. “Nothing that tidy happens by accident. Put me down for the fairy tale. Small. Someone will want to have been right.”

The next client wore a gold ring so recently acquired it still looked surprised on the finger. Hordeonius had already filed him as *new-ring*.

He adjusted grain expectations with solemn grief. “A man like Cato,” he told him, “is bad for markets and good for sermons. I prefer markets, but I respect sermons that stay out of my ledgers.”

The client wanted a moral. Hordeonius gave him a rate. The client’s mouth opened for philosophy, closed on the number, and managed a thin grateful smile. Then the smile snagged.

“For Cato’s sake,” the new-ring said, louder now, “shave it. Land is coming. Veterans will pay when the colonies open. A patriot’s rate.”

The colonies were Caesar’s promise to his old soldiers: farms, somewhere, eventually, on ground nobody had yet explained how to take from the men currently farming it. A fine promise. It paid nothing until it was kept.

Sosia, at Hordeonius’s elbow, went still in the useful way clerks go still. The cake-seller across the aisle stopped mid-curse to listen. A veteran’s cousin from the knot of land-talkers leaned in.

Hordeonius kept the rate where it was. He tapped the tablet once. Soft. Final.

“I honour the dead with arithmetic,” he said, careful. “I honour the living with invoices. Land that is not yet land does not clear. Pay this, or take your patriotism to a stall that sells wishes.”

The new-ring’s face flushed under the smile. “You price Cato like onions.”

“Onions keep,” Hordeonius answered. “Marble doesn’t.”

The client did not take the tablet. He left the rate hanging, neither paid nor refused cleanly, and walked three stalls away before he spat the smile out to his older companion. “Sermons out of his ledgers, what a patriot.”

The older ring did not bother with courtesy once the banker could not hear. “Pay the rate and stop performing grief for clerks. Or don’t, and let him write you down.”

The cake-seller overheard and laughed once, short. Sosia waited. Hordeonius wrote *new-ring: rate held / unpaid* under *Africa: closed enough*. He left the stylus resting on the name.

Across the way stood Cicero. He had backed Pompey, lost, and been forgiven by Caesar, which in the new Rome was a debt that could never be repaid, only rolled over at a worse rate. He received the same Africa as a bruise that had not faded. Cato dead by his own hand. Caesar’s victory complete enough to make mercy look like a style. Cicero’s mouth shaped a period and lost it. Lesser men converted suicide into slogan. Hordeonius watched the unfinished period. Interesting. Not urgent. Like any account that had not cleared.

A woman selling cakes cursed Cato for spoiling an afternoon’s appetite. The veteran’s cousin asked again whether land would come faster now that the war had a famous corpse and leftover triumph wagons.

“Faster,” he said. “A war with a famous corpse is a finished war. Finished wars hand out farms.”

“Slower,” said a clerk from a nearby stall, with the same confidence. “A finished war is one nobody needs soldiers for. Why pay men you no longer need?”

“Because they have swords.”

“Because they have swords and no war left to spend them on. That is why it is slower.”

Nobody agreed. The cake-seller sold two honey cakes to each side. The Forum only sold noise. Hordeonius let it run. One unfinished account waited on the Kalends, the first of the month, when interest fell due and half of Rome found out who its friends were.

By afternoon the stalls were still selling triumph weather: which streets had choked, which would choke again if Africa’s painted gold returned as rumour, which runners still hugged oil jars like secrets. Hordeonius took those prices down too. He watched a runner from the Domus Publica cut through the press. That was the house beside the Vestals’ temple which Rome lent to its chief priest, and Rome had lent it to Caesar. The victor’s house was still counting eyes. Whether the new-ring would clear was no longer a slogan. It was wax.

# SC-02: Triumph Weather

POV: F1 Nysa · Sacra Via / Domus Publica orbit · FS10
Date / place: Late 46 · street past the Domus Publica (spectacle residue / land hangover)
Canon refs: FS10 · household INF
Info ENTER: Household prep · EXIT: Chooses what to report upstairs; land spit withheld
Overhear risk: Extreme street
**G1:** Second POV: collides with F4’s unfinished question (closed Africa → what victory costs on the street / at the house)

---

Nysa carried bundles through a city that had not finished being drunk on spectacle.

Captives. Gold. Painted lies on wagons. Four triumphs in one month: Gaul, Egypt, Pontus, Africa. Rome had swallowed Caesar’s whole war in four courses, and Africa had been the last, and the taste was still on the street. Phileros had sent her for oil and eyes: watch who lingered near the Domus Publica’s public smile, count which clients arrived early to borrow the house’s favour. She understood tasks. She didn't understand why men needed wagons to prove they had already won.

A veteran along the route spat about land still promised. His spit hit stone and stayed. “They cheer the gold,” he told nobody and everybody. “Gold doesn’t plough.”

Nysa kept the spit with the cheers. Cheers were loud. Spit lasted.

“Africa’s triumph was for the king,” said a second veteran, younger, to the first. “Juba. You can’t triumph over Romans. Bad manners.”

“Then who were the wagons for?”

“Pictures of Romans. Who happened to be dead. Of their own free will.”

A painted board had gone by showing Cato at the end. The street had groaned. Nysa did not know whether groaning was allowed. She kept it with the spit.

Children screamed at elephants, or at the idea of elephants that had already passed. A woman in the crowd named a captive wrongly and was corrected with pride. Rumour walked faster than any procession left in memory. Nysa kept both wrong names. Wrong names were how the street remembered.

Near the Domus steps a banker-looking man paused: ring, stylus, onion-and-money face. He watched the street the way a rate sheet watches numbers. Nysa did not know his name. She knew the look. Men who priced marble before it cooled. Phileros had warned her that such men sometimes bought household gossip by the cup. She kept her face blank and her oil upright.

The clerk at his elbow drifted over, palm open, two copper coins showing. “Oil jars are heavy. Gossip is light. I trade in light things.”

Nysa shifted the jar on her hip. “I carry oil.”

“Everyone carries something. Whose oil?”

“The kind that’s paid for.”

The clerk smiled, unoffended, and pocketed the coins. “Good answer. It will cost someone else more.”

Caesar passed at the edge of sight. Or a litter that might as well have been him. She saw only enough to know the house she served was tied to this noise. He didn't look at her. Great men didn't look at errands. She preferred it. Looking made you responsible for what you saw.

The younger veteran and the older one went back to arguing about colonies and whether Africa’s corpse meant Italy’s farms.

“Which farms?” the older one said. “Every field in Italy already has a man standing on it.”

“Then they will have to move him.”

“You go and ask him.”

Neither had a farm. Both had scars. The younger one glanced at her oil jars, then at the spit still drying on the stone, and shut his mouth.

Nysa walked between their voices and did not answer. Answering was how errands became politics. Politics was how basins got dropped.

Back inside she set down the oil. Dama waited with the inventory face. Phileros would want eyes: who lingered, which clients came early, what the street was selling.

“Early clients?” Dama said, not looking up.

“Three at the Domus smile before the second hour. Two of them carrying nothing.”

“A man who carries nothing wants to be seen carrying nothing. Anyone else?”

“A banker by the steps. His clerk offered me coppers.”

“Did you take them?”

“I am not stupid.”

“Everyone is stupid for two coppers. That is why it is two.”

She reported the early clients at the Domus smile, and the banker by the steps. She did not report the land spit. Carrying it upstairs made her the girl who brought politics in with the jars. Across the river, Phileros had muttered, a foreign household was learning Roman hours. She left that unsaid too. None of her oil. Parade noise still pressed the walls. Closed Africa had not closed the counting.
