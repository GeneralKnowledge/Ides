# SC-68b — War of Words

POV: Cicero · Rome / pamphlet · Chain 6  
Info ENTER: Neutrality impossible · EXIT: Oratory war on Antony declared

---

Cicero stopped pretending the middle existed.

The Philippics—some spoken, some circulated like knives wrapped in paper—named Antony as the disease. Cicero felt the old consulship heat return. Heat was better than Lanuvium’s polite despair.

“The Republic still has a voice,” he told a friend.

“The Republic has your voice,” the friend said carefully. “That is not the same as legions.”

Cicero waved the caution away. Octavian—still not *Caesar* in Cicero’s mouth—could be a sword pointed at Antony. Swords did not need to be loved; they needed to be aimed.

He slept poorly and woke eager. Eager men wrote faster than wise ones.

# SC-69 — East

POV: Cassius (+ Brutus) · Italian ports → Greece · A9 · EXIT-1 late Aug  
Info ENTER: Humiliation appointments · EXIT: Liberty brand + war-chest need

---

Cassius boarded with contempt for the shoreline.

“They offered us corn routes and called it honour,” he told Brutus on a deck that smelled of pitch. “Honour is what we mint when we have silver.”

Brutus watched Italy shrink. “We leave so we do not become the cause of war.”

“We leave,” Cassius said, “so we can afford war if war is honest enough to come.”

Crewmen cared about wind. Cassius cared about Asia’s taxes already rearranging themselves in his head. Liberty would need pay. He did not say *tax terror*. He said *necessity*. Necessity was the republican word for other people’s coin.

Brutus touched a rail. “If we become what we killed—”

“We become victors,” Cassius cut in. “Then we argue about purity.”

They sailed. Behind them, Cicero’s sentences chased Antony through Rome. Ahead of them, cities waited to learn what *libertas* cost per head.
