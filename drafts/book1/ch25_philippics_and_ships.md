# SC-68b — War of Words

POV: Cicero · Rome / pamphlet circulation  
Date / place: Autumn 44 · desk and Curia fringe  
Canon refs: Chain 6 · C7 · B3  
Info ENTER: Neutrality impossible · EXIT: Oratory war on Antony declared  
Overhear risk: Public when delivered

---

Cicero stopped pretending the middle existed.

On the desk lay two drafts: one that named Antony a public enemy in music, and one that named him a mistake in manners. He chose music. Mistakes could be forgiven; music recruited. He sanded a single adjective until it could cut without looking eager—and failed, because eagerness was the point.

The Philippics—some spoken, some circulated like knives wrapped in paper—named Antony as the disease. Ink stained his fingers the colour of old wine. He felt the old consulship heat return. Heat was better than Lanuvium’s polite despair. Heat made sentences arrive before caution could edit them into safety.

“The Republic still has a voice,” he told a friend who had come to warn him about veterans in the streets.

“The Republic has your voice,” the friend said carefully. “That is not the same as legions.”

“Legions listen to pay,” Cicero said. “Pay listens to panic. Panic listens to me if I am loud enough.” He heard how raw that sounded and did not unsay it.

Cicero waved the rest of the caution away with a gesture he had practised on juries. Octavian—still not *Caesar* in Cicero’s private mouth, and *young Caesar* in his public one when the Curia needed a handle—could be a sword pointed at Antony. Swords did not need to be loved; they needed to be aimed. He drafted a period about Antony’s appetite and another about the state’s dignity, then joined them like carpentry.

A courier waited with copies for men who would pretend they had not asked for copies. One copy would reach a senator who hated Antony and feared Octavian equally; Cicero counted that as balance. He sealed the packets with a satisfaction that felt like courage. He slept poorly and woke eager. Eager men wrote faster than wise ones. He congratulated himself for noticing the difference and wrote anyway.

# SC-69 — East

POV: Cassius (+ Brutus) · Italian ports → Greece  
Date / place: Deck / harbour · EXIT-1 (Brutus late Aug; Cassius flexible)  
Canon refs: A9 · Chain 8 seed · C3  
Info ENTER: Humiliation appointments · EXIT: Liberty brand + war-chest need  
Overhear risk: Ship crews

---

Cassius boarded with contempt for the shoreline.

Pitch smelled honest. Italy smelled of appointments that had tried to turn Liberators into corn clerks. A harbour official wished them “favourable gods” in a voice that meant *please leave before you become my problem.* Cassius thanked him with teeth.

“They offered us corn routes and called it honour,” he told Brutus on a deck that flexed under boots. “Honour is what we mint when we have silver.”

Brutus watched Italy shrink. A gull argued with another gull. “We leave so we do not become the cause of war.”

“We leave,” Cassius said, “so we can afford war if war is honest enough to come.”

Crewmen cared about wind and the next meal. One asked if they were fleeing. Cassius said, “We are collecting.” Collecting sounded like bookkeeping. Bookkeeping was how he kept rage useful. He already saw Asia’s taxes rearranging themselves—cities as ledgers, ledgers as legions. Liberty would need pay. He did not say *tax terror*. He said *necessity*. Necessity was the republican word for other people’s coin.

Brutus touched a rail. Salt crusted under his nails. “If we become what we killed—”

“We become victors,” Cassius cut in. “Then we argue about purity. Purity is a peacetime hobby.”

A mate shouted about lines. Brutus answered the mate with courtesy that belonged to a different Republic. Cassius answered with a nod that meant *move*. Brutus watched a crate labelled with someone’s grain promise tip and right itself. He did not take it as omen. He took it as cargo.

They sailed. Behind them, Cicero’s sentences chased Antony through Rome like well-bred dogs. Ahead of them, cities waited to learn what *libertas* cost per head—and whether the brand on a coin could outpray a tax collector.
