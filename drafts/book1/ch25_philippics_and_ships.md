# SC-68b — War of Words

POV: Cicero · Rome / pamphlet circulation  
Date / place: Autumn 44 · desk, Curia fringe, street return  
Canon refs: Chain 6 · C7 · B3  
Info ENTER: Neutrality impossible · EXIT: Oratory war on Antony declared  
Overhear risk: Public when delivered

---

Cicero stopped pretending the middle existed.

On the desk lay two drafts: one that named Antony a public enemy in music, and one that named him a mistake in manners. He chose music. Mistakes could be forgiven; music recruited. He sanded a single adjective until it could cut without looking eager—and failed, because eagerness was the point.

Ink stained his fingers the colour of old wine. He felt the old consulship heat return. Heat was better than Lanuvium’s polite despair. Heat made sentences arrive before caution could edit them into safety.

A friend came to warn him—dust on the man’s sandals, the smell of men who drilled near the temples. “Veterans in the streets,” the friend said carefully. “They drink Antony’s name with their pay. Your periods will not outrun a javelin if you make yourself the loudest man in Rome.”

“The Republic still has a voice,” Cicero said.

“The Republic has your voice. That is not the same as legions.”

“Legions listen to pay. Pay listens to panic. Panic listens to me if I am loud enough.” He heard how raw that sounded and did not unsay it. He waved the rest of the caution away with a gesture practised on juries.

Octavian—still not *Caesar* in Cicero’s private mouth, and *young Caesar* in his public one when the Curia needed a handle—could be a sword pointed at Antony. Swords did not need to be loved; they needed to be aimed. He drafted a period about Antony’s appetite and another about the state’s dignity, then joined them like carpentry.

A courier waited with copies for men who would pretend they had not asked for copies. Cicero sealed the packets with a satisfaction that felt like courage and sent them into the city’s hungry mouths.

One packet came back before dusk.

The seal was broken. The wax had been thumb-smeared and pressed again badly—an insult, not a forgery. Across the top margin someone had scored a short line with a knife-point, deep enough to scar the wood beneath. No sentence. Only the mark, and a smear of something that might have been wine or might have been worse.

The friend, still in the atrium, went pale. “That is not literary criticism.”

Cicero held the scarred tablet until his fingers stopped pretending to be steady. He thought of Atticus, of audiences, of the Capitol’s first day enlarged by memory. Then he ordered fresh copies. “Circulate it wider,” he said. “If they can mark one, let them learn there are many.”

He slept poorly and woke eager. Eager men wrote faster than wise ones. He congratulated himself for noticing the difference and wrote anyway—now with the knife-scar as applause he refused to name fear.

# SC-69 — East

POV: Cassius (+ Brutus) · Italian ports → Greece  
Date / place: Deck / harbour · EXIT-1 (Brutus late Aug; Cassius flexible)  
Canon refs: A9 · Chain 8 seed · C3  
Info ENTER: Humiliation appointments · EXIT: Liberty brand + war-chest need  
Overhear risk: Ship crews

---

Cassius boarded with contempt for the shoreline.

Pitch smelled honest. Italy smelled of appointments that had tried to turn Liberators into corn clerks. A harbour official wished them “favourable gods” in a voice that meant *please leave before you become my problem.* Cassius thanked him with teeth.

“They offered us corn routes and called it honour,” he told Brutus on a deck that flexed under boots. “Honour is what we mint when we have silver.”

Brutus watched Italy shrink. A gull argued with another gull. “We leave so we do not become the cause of war.”

“We leave,” Cassius said, “so we can afford war if war is honest enough to come.”

Crewmen cared about wind and the next meal. One asked if they were fleeing. Cassius said, “We are collecting.” Collecting sounded like bookkeeping. Bookkeeping kept his rage useful. He already saw Asia’s taxes rearranging themselves—cities as ledgers, ledgers as legions. Liberty would need pay. He did not say *tax terror*. He said *necessity*. Necessity was the republican word for other people’s coin.

Brutus touched a rail. Salt crusted under his nails. “If we become what we killed—”

“We become victors,” Cassius cut in. “Then we argue about purity. Purity is a peacetime hobby.”

A mate shouted about lines. Brutus answered the mate with courtesy that belonged to a different Republic. Cassius answered with a nod that meant *move*. Brutus watched a crate labelled with someone’s grain promise tip and right itself. He did not take it as omen. He took it as cargo.

They sailed. Behind them, Cicero’s sentences chased Antony through Rome—marked tablets and unmarked ones alike. Ahead, cities waited to learn what *libertas* cost per head.
