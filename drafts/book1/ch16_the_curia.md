# SC-43a: The Petition

POV: Cassius
Date / place: Curia of Pompey · mid-morning 15 March 44
Canon refs: CHOREO-1.1–4 · A5 · M8
Info ENTER: GO; Caesar seated / arriving among them; Antony outside
Info EXIT: First blows landed; chaos begun
Overhear risk: Extreme inside

---

In Pompey’s Curia the marble held last night’s cold. Cassius counted exits the way other men counted clients.

One door. Too many togae. Benches ran along both sides in tiers. Pompey’s statue at the far end of the room, enemy’s face in marble, watching: the most honest piece of furniture in Rome. Caesar came in with that slight forward lean of a man late for his own power, Decimus’ cheer still faintly on him like perfume. He sat. Or half-sat. The golden chair made its small theatre of precedence: the Senate’s own vote, gilt above their benches, saying *you are a little more than we are*. Caesar had stopped hearing it as a rebuke. Cassius never had.

Tillius Cimber went forward first, as arranged, brother’s exile on his tongue, hands already dishonest with urgency. Cassius watched Caesar’s patience perform itself. The slight turn. The listening face. The clemency brand still wet on the Republic’s memory.

Other senators crowded round the chair with the careful, helpful faces of men who wanted to second the request. It was the natural thing to do. It also put twenty men within arm’s reach.

Now, Cassius thought, not as prayer. Prayer was Brutus’ department.

Tillius’ hands found the toga. Pulled.

The signal was ugly and ordinary: cloth, fist, the insult of touch. Cassius felt the room flinch at that before any blade appeared. In this house you did not lay hands on a senator’s toga; a fist in the cloth was already sacrilege. Caesar’s surprise was real enough to please Cassius and frighten him. Real surprise meant the secret had held. Real surprise also meant a living man’s strength still in the shoulders.

“This is violence,” Caesar said, mild with disbelief, to Tillius.

Publius Casca struck at neck and shoulder, the awkward angle of a first wound made by a man who had practised on air. Caesar caught Casca’s arm. A question burst out of him, sharp with outrage, in the old register of men who still believed Senate rooms were safer than camps. “What is this violence?”

“Brother, help!” Casca said, in Greek, to someone behind him. It came out thin. It was the sound of a man asking for the thing he had come to give.

Cassius moved.

He didn’t see a tyrant in that instant. He saw a body that had walked through Gaul and Alexandria and the stubborn Spanish hills. A body that might yet stand up and make this morning a botched thing. Cassius’ blade went in where flesh allowed it. Heat ran over his knuckles.

Around him the room became teeth.

Senators who were not in it screamed anyway. Benches shrieked on stone. Someone fell without being struck, panic’s own casualty. Cassius smelled blood and dust and the sweet oil in Caesar’s hair. He kept his face tight. Soft faces in a killing lost the killing.

The plan had been simple, and in the room it was not. There were more than sixty of them, and not all of them knew where to stand. Men struck at Caesar and at each other. A blade opened Brutus’ hand. The conspirators had sworn to be a surgery and they were, for a few breaths, a pack.

Caesar fought. Of course he fought. The man had never learned how to be an offering.

Cassius glimpsed Brutus on the far side of the storm, pale, committed, somehow still looking like a statue asked to commit a robbery. Then Caesar’s hand was on a blade and red and the marble of Pompey was closer than it should have been.

Finish, Cassius told the morning. Finish before the door brings Antony.

# SC-43b: At the Base

POV: Brutus
Date / place: Same minutes · Curia Pompeia
Canon refs: CHOREO-1.5–7 · M9 · M5
Info ENTER: Strike begun; his own oath heavy
Info EXIT: Caesar down at Pompey’s statue; Brutus hears a sound he will not agree on later
Overhear risk: Extreme

---

Brutus had told himself it would feel like law.

It felt like a bathhouse brawl with better cloth.

Men he respected shoved. Men he did not respect shoved harder. Caesar’s mouth opened on pain and command at once. Brutus’ ancestors, imagined, arranged on their shelves at home, did not lean in to help. They watched, as masks watch.

He had to step close. Distance was cowardice; closeness was the thing that would be written, if anything was written truly.

His blade found Caesar because the room tilted that way, bodies made a funnel toward the statue’s base. Pompey’s marble face had no advice. Brutus struck as a man strikes when the alternative is being the only clean hand in a pile of red ones, filth either way.

Caesar looked at him.

That was worse than knives.

What Caesar said, if he said, Brutus already knew he would not keep the same as the man at his shoulder. He heard something broken, breath and consonant, perhaps *kai su*, the Greek for *you too*, perhaps only air leaving a throat that had spoken to the whole world. Beside him someone swore he heard *child* after it, the way a father says it to a son. Someone else swore silence was cleaner, that Caesar had said nothing at all, that he had drawn his toga over his face as the last blow came, to die with dignity.

Brutus did not answer. Answers were for courts.

Caesar folded at the base of Pompey’s statue. The old dead rival had finally won a small, filthy trick. The toga drank. The floor drank. Brutus did not count the wounds. Counting would keep him in the room. He needed to leave as liberty, not as butcher.

He found Cicero among the benches, grey, mouth open, already half risen to go, and called the name across the blood like a man calling a witness. “Cicero!” The old man stared at the dagger in his hand and did not come.

“People, Senate…” Brutus’s voice sounded written. He hated that. “The tyrant is dead. There is nothing to fear…”

No one arranged themselves into a people. They ran.

Half the Senate was gone through the doors before he had finished the sentence. Men tangled in their own togas. Some left their shoes. One of the oldest members sat down on his bench and stayed there, white and quiet, as if he had come to observe a game he now realised had stakes.

Cassius’ hand found Brutus’ elbow hard enough to bruise. “Capitol,” Cassius said, practical as salt, station face for anyone still listening.

Brutus understood it a breath late. One steep road up, Jupiter’s walls at the top: a few men could hold it, and the whole city would know where to look.

In the lane of light just beyond the Curia door, before the crowd’s animal closed again, Cassius’s mouth changed. “Capitol,” he repeated, softer, not for the room. “Say it as fear. Antony was held outside, Trebonius’s market day. That gift is already walking toward us with a sword-shaped appetite. Do not stand over the marble arranging vocabulary while the spare learns he is spare.”

Brutus looked once more at the shape on the stones. Softness tried to rise in him. Not the abstract debt: the late hour after forgiveness when Caesar had still wanted his company and made the war feel finished between them. The ordinary talk. The laugh at something small. The hand on his shoulder like kinship politics had not earned. That was the betrayal’s true size. It did not leave when he named the body tyrant. He stood one breath longer than Cassius wanted, long enough to feel the softness as a weight that would follow him up the hill. Then he crushed it the way he crushed unearned tears: privately, incompletely, with the lie that ancestors approved. Under the crush the spare was not mercy’s clean face now. It was a clock. The crush did not empty him. It only made room to walk.

“Capitol,” Brutus said, public word with private cost, and they went out into light that did not know what it was lighting.

# SC-44: The Consul Is Dead (False)

POV: Nysa (F1)
Date / place: Streets from Campus approaches → Forum fringe → Subura edge
Canon refs: Info net · rumour mutation
Info ENTER: Panic wave from Theatre/Campus
Info EXIT: Carries false report that Antony is dead into denser streets
Overhear risk: She is the overhear

---

Nysa hadn’t meant to follow the litter far. Girls who followed too far became bodies in gutters. But the Domus had become unbreathable with Calpurnia’s silence, and Phileros had said, “If you are going to hover, hover where news comes.” News was not a fishmonger’s tray. It still drew a crowd anyway.

She was still on the edge of the Campus crowd when the Curia began to spit men.

Togas with red. Togas without dignity. A senator vomited against a column and wiped his mouth like a boy. Someone screamed Caesar’s name. Names could not reverse blood.

“Knives!”
“Brutus!”
“The consuls, both…”
“Antony…”

The name Antony snagged. Nysa did not see Antony. She saw a gap where a large man might have been and heard, already, two freedmen inventing the rest.

“They killed the consul too,” one said, eyes bright with the pleasure of worst cases. “Had to. He was Caesar’s dog.”

“I heard Trebonius held him and they opened him in the portico,” said the other, not to be outdone.

“Opened him how?” said a third, a baker with flour to the elbows and a morbid appetite.

“Like a fish,” said the first freedman, who had never seen a fish opened but had a gift for anatomy.

Nysa’s mouth moved before her caution did. “Antony is dead?”

“Dead as the dictator,” the first freedman said, happy to be source. “Tell them in the Subura before the bakers’ rumour cleans it.”

“I am the bakers,” said the third man, wounded.

“Then clean it faster,” said the second.

She ran because standing still made you a witness, and witnesses were questioned. The wax fragment under her tunic knocked against her ribs. A second heart that had failed earlier and wanted usefulness now.

In a street that smelled of garum and wet stone she told a woman with bread under her arm, “They have killed Caesar, and Antony.”

The woman clutched the bread. “Both?”

“Both,” Nysa said, and felt the story settle into the city like dust. She didn’t know if it was true. She thought she was being precise. That was how such stories travelled.

By the time she reached the Subura’s first choke-point, the tale had grown a Dolabella. “The young one,” a fishwife told her, “that Caesar was making consul in his place when he went east. Loud. He was in it, or they killed him too, depending who’s selling.” And somehow, a list. Lists always arrived early in panics. Lists made fear feel organised.

Nysa stopped under a balcony and vomited nothing. Then she wiped her mouth, as the senator had, and hated the kinship.

She still had the wax. Incomplete. Unopened by any dictator. Around her, Rome opened every seal at once.
