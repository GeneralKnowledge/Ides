# SC-84: Bononia

POV: Octavian · Bononia bargain
Date / place: Autumn 43 · river island below Bononia
Canon refs: A12 · Chain 7 · Cicero naming = C contested
Info ENTER: Mutual need · EXIT: Triumvirate; lists begun
Overhear risk: Low (guards)

---

They met because none could kill the other two cheaply.

The island sat low in the river below Bononia, a gravel tongue with a tent on it and two camps on the banks watching each other’s fires. Lepidus crossed first over the plank bridge and walked the island with his own eyes before he waved the other two across. On the sand each man let the others’ people pat him down for blades. Nobody pretended it was ceremony.

Antony brought soldier gravity and a hunger that still smelled like Ops unlocked. He looked across the table at the Campania squeak grown into a man who could force a bargain, and whatever joke he had kept since spring did not come out. Octavian had brought the name, and a cold that still cracked sometimes when a room laughed wrong. He felt it crack now, and seal, like wax under a thumb. Lepidus brought the useful talent of being present when maps were drawn, and of surviving other men’s sentences without becoming their subject. He also brought the legions that had waded the river to Antony in the spring, which was why he had a chair. A table held bread nobody ate. Appetite had moved to paper.

Lepidus wanted the law first, because Lepidus always wanted the law first. “A tribune carries it in Rome,” he said. “Three men for setting the state in order. Five years. A consul’s power for each of us, and no veto over any of it.”

“Triumvirs,” Antony said, tasting it. “In Rome a board of three counts copper at the mint and minds the jail.”

“Then this one minds both,” Octavian said.

Nobody laughed. Antony laid his palm on the tablet where the money should have been. Forty-odd legions wanted paying, and the colonies Caesar had promised them were still surveyor’s stakes. “Sulla did this,” Lepidus said quietly, as if the dead could be asked for a precedent.

“Sulla posted names,” Antony said. “That is all a list is. Names, heads, and a sale, and the sale pays the legions.”

Victim lists began like shopping. A name, then a joke, then silence when the joke hit someone each man still half-liked. A guard outside coughed. Conversation became rain and cousins for three breaths. Then it returned to blood accounting. Paper took names faster than mouths could soften them.

Antony’s stylus paused on a lesser enemy first, a man who had borrowed money and returned sermons. Easy. The stylus moved. Then it paused again on a blank that was not blank in anyone’s throat.

“Cicero,” Antony said. He did not look up. Looking would have made it a request.

Lepidus looked at his hands. Hands could not abstain. Octavian kept his face from moving enough to save anyone. For a breath he counted the cost of sparing Cicero: Antony’s favourite wound still open, the lists thinner when the next Kalends asked for blood.

Antony’s finger stayed on the blank. “Say it,” he said, not loud. “Or strike a smaller name in his place. I have lesser enemies enough. Choose.”

A lesser name sat two lines up, a man Octavian half-owed from Apollonia’s edge. Useful. Not loved. Killable without rearranging the map. Striking that name instead would buy a night of quieter conscience and leave Antony’s wound open. Octavian felt the cheaper exit in his hands: the stylus, the wax, the easy swap.

Saving the orator would have cost a different war, one with Antony still armed and the coalition already smelling of unfinished business. Octavian kept that cost. Then he spent it aloud.

“Cicero stays,” he said. Voice thin, audible, irreversible. He did not dress it as law. He set his seal ring on the tablet’s edge, metal clicking wood, so the assent had a body in the room, not only silence. “We do not trade him for a quieter cousin.”

Lepidus cleared his throat. “He still has friends who write.”

“Then they can write his epitaph,” Antony said. “Or they can join him.”

Who had first thought the name did not matter on the wood. The name stood because Octavian had said it stood, and no one struck it out.

“We buy the war with this,” Antony said, tapping the tablet beside the ring.

“The war buys us,” Octavian answered, too soft to be a quip, too true to unsay. He took the ring back. The click leaving wood was quieter than the click arriving.

They swapped one quieter enemy in a whisper: a man each of them half-owed and half-hated, traded so that no single list would look like one man’s revenge. The whisper would become three contradictory stories by morning. They sealed what could be sealed. Seals did not wash.

Antony poured wine that did not taste like celebration. He did not look at Octavian while pouring. Octavian let him not look, and didn’t drink deep. Lepidus drank as men drink when they hope the cup will make them smaller than the map. Outside, a horse screamed at nothing. Inside, the lists grew.

### F2: Seius

Seius found his name absent and felt holy relief, then shame at the holiness. He walked past a posted list twice to be sure, eyes watering from more than wind. Above the names the clerk’s square letters ran: *whoever brings the head… from the treasury… if a slave, his freedom…* and lower, *the goods to be sold*. A neighbour who had once loaned him applause was present in black letters. Seius didn’t stop walking. Stopping looked like guilt.

At the corner Gavius from the next block, petition-tablet man, dinner-circuit memory, stood under the same wood with a woman who had borrowed Seius’s mule last Kalends. “There,” Gavius said, finger on a cognomen that was almost Seius’s cousin’s, almost a stranger’s, wrong by one letter if you wanted it wrong. “Is that your house’s blood?” The woman looked at Seius. Expectation closed like the old petition trap.

“I do not know that spelling,” Seius said. True enough to keep his door; false enough that Gavius’s mouth flattened. The woman stepped back. Kinship itself felt contagious. Seius bought oil he did not need so his hands would look busy. He congratulated no one. He locked doors and checked the locks twice. Seia Prima asked if honesty still mattered. He said honesty was a peacetime crop. She didn’t laugh. “Then harvest carefully,” she said. “Peacetime crops still get taxed when the lists are hungry.” He almost smiled at her, old Seia who audited his atrium smile against paper. He stopped before smiling looked like relief worth posting. In bed he rehearsed congratulations for whoever won the next rumour, and hated the rehearsal even as he perfected it.

### F4: Hordeonius

Hordeonius acquired a neighbour’s leases, and a widow’s garden rights, through channels that smelled like lists. The widow’s steward spat near his shoes and called it accident. Hordeonius called it business and paid anyway. He told himself seals were older than pity. Sleep disagreed. Arrived late. Left early. Charged interest he could not collect from his own nerves.

“I am preserving value,” he informed his empty shelf. The shelf didn’t absolve. Sosia looked at him the way he looked at a bad joke. He had not told one. Truth under proscription sounded like a joke whether you meant one or not. He entered *acquired: unclean* in a private cipher and hated that honesty still felt like bookkeeping virtue. In the dark he smelled Visellius’s old room for no reason and told himself archaeology was closed.

### F1: Nysa

Nysa saw a slave, Festus or a man using Festus’s method, sell a household with a sentence. Informers priced breath. A woman in the street paid with a bracelet and a name; the bracelet was prettier than the mercy it bought. A wrong cognomen nearly got a poet-adjacent man beaten until someone remembered Cinna and laughed wrong. Laughter that sounded like March smoke returning.

Near the Subura corner a boy chalked names from a list onto a wall for men who could not read wood but could read chalk. A woman stopped him mid-letter, pressed a coin into his palm, and whispered a different spelling. The boy hesitated, coin versus chalk. Then he rubbed one stroke out and wrote the lie. Nysa watched the wall become a market. She did not correct him. Correction joined you to the wood.

Phileros drank less now; fear sobered some men and ruined others. He came to the Carinae kitchen door, where Nysa had carried for Antony’s steward since the spring before last, and asked her if the Domus still had a mistress worth fearing. She said fear had moved house. She kept her eyes down and her feet moving. Survival was an unprinted decree. In a side court she found a broken seal stamp and left it; broken seals let new law pretend to be old. She passed a posted list without reading every name. Reading every name invited your own onto the wood. She still felt the ink like heat. She did not look for Calpurnia’s old corridors of dread. Those corridors had been sold with the seals.
