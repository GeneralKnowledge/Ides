# SC-84 — Bononia

POV: Antony / Octavian / Lepidus · Bononia bargain  
Date / place: Bononia / bridge-island tradition  
Canon refs: A12 · Chain 7 · Cicero naming = C contested  
Info ENTER: Mutual need · EXIT: Triumvirate; lists begun  
Overhear risk: Low (guards)

---

They met because none could kill the other two cheaply.

Antony brought soldier gravity and a hunger that still smelled like Ops unlocked. Octavian brought the name and a cold that still cracked sometimes when the room laughed wrong—crack, then seal, like wax under a thumb. Lepidus brought the useful talent of being present when maps were drawn, and of surviving other men’s sentences without becoming their subject. A table held bread nobody ate; appetite had moved to paper.

Victim lists began like shopping. A name, then a joke, then silence when the joke hit someone each man still half-liked. A guard outside coughed; conversation became rain and cousins for three breaths, then returned to blood accounting. Paper took names faster than mouths could soften them.

Antony’s stylus paused. He did not look up when he shaped the next name—old hatred visiting a favourite wound. “Cicero.”

The tent’s air did three things at once. Lepidus looked at his hands as if hands could abstain from history. Octavian’s face did not move enough to save anyone. Saving the orator would have cost a different war—one with Antony still armed and the boy’s own lists thinner. Octavian kept the cost behind his teeth. He corrected nothing. He offered no period.

Who had first thought the name—traditions would disagree even inside this tent’s own air. What happened on the wood was simpler: the name stood, and no one struck it out.

“We buy the war with this,” Antony said, tapping the tablet.

“The war buys us,” Octavian answered, too soft to be a quip, too true to unsay.

They swapped one quieter enemy in a whisper: a man each of them half-owed and half-hated, traded so that no single list would look like one man’s revenge. The whisper would become three contradictory stories by morning. Contested was honest. They sealed what could be sealed. Seals did not wash.

Antony poured wine that did not taste like celebration. Octavian did not drink deep. Lepidus drank as men drink when they hope the cup will make them smaller than the map. Outside, a horse screamed at nothing; inside, the lists grew.

### F2 — Seius

Seius found his name absent and felt holy relief, then shame at the holiness. He walked past a posted list twice to be sure, eyes watering from more than wind. A neighbour who had once loaned him applause was present in black letters. Seius did not stop walking. Stopping looked like guilt. He bought oil he did not need so his hands would look busy. He congratulated no one. He locked doors and checked the locks as if locks were theology. Seia Prima asked if honesty still mattered. He said honesty was a peacetime crop. She did not laugh. Laughter was expensive now. In bed he rehearsed congratulations for whoever won the next rumour, and hated the rehearsal even as he perfected it.

### F4 — Hordeonius

Hordeonius acquired a neighbour’s leases—and a widow’s garden rights—through channels that smelled like lists. The widow’s steward spat near his shoes and called it accident. Hordeonius called it business and paid anyway. He told himself seals were older than pity. Sleep disagreed—arrived late, left early, charged interest he could not collect from his own nerves.

“I am preserving value,” he informed his empty shelf. “A Republic without solvent men is only a riot with better Latin.” The shelf did not absolve. Sosia looked at him as if he had told a joke. He had not. That was the trouble with truth under proscription: it wore humour’s clothes without asking. He entered *acquired: unclean* in a private cipher and hated that honesty still felt like bookkeeping virtue. In the dark he smelled Visellius’ old room for no reason and told himself archaeology was closed.

### F1 — Nysa

Nysa saw a slave—Festus, or a man using Festus’s method—sell a household with a sentence. Informers priced breath. A woman in the street paid with a bracelet and a name; the bracelet was prettier than the mercy it bought. A wrong cognomen nearly got a poet-adjacent man beaten until someone remembered Cinna and laughed wrong—laughter that sounded like March smoke returning.

Phileros drank less now; fear sobered some men and ruined others. He asked Nysa if the Domus still had a mistress worth fearing. She said fear had moved house. She kept her eyes down and her feet moving. Survival was an unprinted decree. In a side court she found a broken seal stamp and left it; broken seals were how new law pretended to be old. She passed a posted list without reading every name—reading every name invited your own onto the wood—and still felt the ink like heat. She did not look for Calpurnia’s old corridors of dread. Those corridors had been sold with the seals.
