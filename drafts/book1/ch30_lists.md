# SC-84 — Bononia

POV: Antony / Octavian / Lepidus · Bononia bargain  
Date / place: Bononia / bridge-island tradition  
Canon refs: A12 · Chain 7 · Cicero naming = C contested  
Info ENTER: Mutual need · EXIT: Triumvirate; lists begun  
Overhear risk: Low (guards)

---

They met because none could kill the other two cheaply.

Antony brought soldier gravity and a hunger that still smelled like Ops unlocked. Octavian brought the name and a cold that still cracked sometimes when the room laughed wrong—crack, then seal, like wax under a thumb. Lepidus brought the useful talent of being present when maps were drawn, and of surviving other men’s sentences without becoming their subject. A table held bread nobody ate; appetite had moved to paper.

Victim lists began like shopping. A name, then a joke, then silence when the joke hit someone each man still half-liked. A guard outside coughed; conversation became rain and cousins for three breaths, then returned to blood accounting. Paper took names faster than mouths could soften them.

Antony’s stylus paused on a lesser enemy first—a man who had borrowed money and returned sermons. Easy. The stylus moved. Then it paused again on a blank that was not blank in anyone’s throat.

“Cicero,” Antony said. He did not look up. Looking would have made it a request.

Lepidus looked at his hands as if hands could abstain from history. Octavian’s face didn't move enough to save anyone. For a breath the boy counted the cost of sparing Cicero—Antony’s favourite wound still open, the lists thinner when the next Kalends asked for blood.

Antony’s finger stayed on the blank. “Say it,” he said—not loud. “Or strike a smaller name in his place. I have lesser enemies enough. Choose.”

A lesser name sat two lines up—a man Octavian half-owed from Apollonia’s edge, useful, not loved, killable without rearranging the map. Striking that name instead would buy a night of quieter conscience and leave Antony’s wound open. Octavian felt the cheaper exit in his hands: the stylus, the wax, the easy swap.

Saving the orator would have cost a different war—one with Antony still armed and the coalition already smelling of unfinished business. Octavian kept that cost, then spent it aloud.

“Cicero stays,” he said. Voice thin, audible, irreversible. He did not dress it as law. He set his seal ring on the tablet’s edge—personal token, metal clicking wood—so the assent had a body in the room, not only silence. “We do not trade him for a quieter cousin.”

Lepidus cleared his throat. “He still has friends who write.”

“Then they can write his epitaph,” Antony said. “Or they can join him.”

Who had first thought the name did not matter on the wood. The name stood because Octavian had said it stood, and no one struck it out. Leaving the first thought contested was still honest; the assent was not.

“We buy the war with this,” Antony said, tapping the tablet beside the ring.

“The war buys us,” Octavian answered, too soft to be a quip, too true to unsay. He took the ring back. The click leaving wood was quieter than the click arriving. Formiae had been purchased in that sound.

They swapped one quieter enemy in a whisper: a man each of them half-owed and half-hated, traded so that no single list would look like one man’s revenge. The whisper would become three contradictory stories by morning. They sealed what could be sealed. Seals did not wash.

Antony poured wine that did not taste like celebration. Octavian didn't drink deep. Lepidus drank as men drink when they hope the cup will make them smaller than the map. Outside, a horse screamed at nothing; inside, the lists grew—and somewhere south of the tent, a road toward Formiae had already begun to matter.

### F2 — Seius

Seius found his name absent and felt holy relief, then shame at the holiness. He walked past a posted list twice to be sure, eyes watering from more than wind. A neighbour who had once loaned him applause was present in black letters. Seius didn't stop walking. Stopping looked like guilt. He bought oil he did not need so his hands would look busy. He congratulated no one. He locked doors and checked the locks as if locks were theology. Seia Prima asked if honesty still mattered. He said honesty was a peacetime crop. She didn't laugh. Laughter was expensive now. In bed he rehearsed congratulations for whoever won the next rumour, and hated the rehearsal even as he perfected it.

### F4 — Hordeonius

Hordeonius acquired a neighbour’s leases—and a widow’s garden rights—through channels that smelled like lists. The widow’s steward spat near his shoes and called it accident. Hordeonius called it business and paid anyway. He told himself seals were older than pity. Sleep disagreed—arrived late, left early, charged interest he could not collect from his own nerves.

“I am preserving value,” he informed his empty shelf. The shelf didn't absolve. Sosia looked at him as if he had told a joke. He had not. Truth under proscription sounded like a joke whether you meant one or not. He entered *acquired: unclean* in a private cipher and hated that honesty still felt like bookkeeping virtue. In the dark he smelled Visellius’ old room for no reason and told himself archaeology was closed.

### F1 — Nysa

Nysa saw a slave—Festus, or a man using Festus’s method—sell a household with a sentence. Informers priced breath. A woman in the street paid with a bracelet and a name; the bracelet was prettier than the mercy it bought. A wrong cognomen nearly got a poet-adjacent man beaten until someone remembered Cinna and laughed wrong—laughter that sounded like March smoke returning.

Near the Subura corner a boy chalked names from a list onto a wall for men who could not read wood but could read chalk. A woman stopped him mid-letter, pressed a coin into his palm, and whispered a different spelling. The boy hesitated—coin versus chalk—then rubbed one stroke out and wrote the lie. Nysa watched the wall become a market. She did not correct him. Correction joined you to the wood.

Phileros drank less now; fear sobered some men and ruined others. He asked Nysa if the Domus still had a mistress worth fearing. She said fear had moved house. She kept her eyes down and her feet moving. Survival was an unprinted decree. In a side court she found a broken seal stamp and left it; broken seals let new law pretend to be old. She passed a posted list without reading every name—reading every name invited your own onto the wood—and still felt the ink like heat. She did not look for Calpurnia’s old corridors of dread. Those corridors had been sold with the seals.
