# SC-07 — Ancestors

POV: Brutus · domus · Porcia pressure  
Date / place: Evening · Brutus’ house  
Canon refs: C2 · soft anchors  
Info ENTER: Clemency beneficiary · EXIT: Ancestral script louder  
Overhear risk: Household high

---

Brutus sat under masks that judged eating.

The older Brutus of the stories—the one boys learned with bread—did not need to speak. Paint and family talk did the speaking. Porcia’s voice stayed low, which made it harder to dodge than shouting.

“Worth is not a dinner conversation,” she said. “It is what you do when dinners become easy.”

Clemency sat in the room like a soft chain. Caesar had spared; Brutus had lived; living under a spared life felt, tonight, less like gratitude and more like a costume that still fit too well. He reached for a philosophical sentence and found only the taste of wine that had gone warm.

“I am not unaware of what the name requires,” Brutus said.

“Then stop practising unawareness as courtesy,” Porcia answered.

A slave’s foot whispered in the corridor—household overhear as ordinary as dust. Brutus lowered his voice anyway.

The foot returned with a tablet: an invitation, seal still soft, from a Caesarian host whose dinners made pardoned men feel forgiven and owned in the same course. Brutus read the names of the promised guests—two new Spain-postings, a man who toasted clemency as if it were sauce—and felt the chain tug toward the couch he was expected to warm.

Porcia watched his hands, not the wax. “You will go,” she said, not as question.

Brutus held the tablet above the lamp until the edge blackened and the host’s compliments curled. He set the ruined courtesy on the side table as carefully as if neatness could make refusal into housekeeping.

“I will send regrets,” he said. “A cousin’s fever. Something that smells like duty.”

“Regrets are still courtesy,” Porcia said. “Burning is nearer the truth.”

He did not decide on knives. He decided, smaller and worse, on a dinner he would not attend and a smile he would not owe. He scraped the blackened tablet into the ash pan so no slave would have to invent a cleaner story for the house.

Porcia did not thank him.

He looked at the masks and stopped looking before the paint could answer.

# SC-08 — Slight

POV: Cassius · elite dinner  
Date / place: Same season · dining room (D host)  
Canon refs: PERS motive · C3 · slight C/D  
Info ENTER: Pardoned, watching · EXIT: Private resentment crystallises  
Overhear risk: Servants

---

Cassius smiled with his teeth and counted who received what commands, what lands, what praise.

The host toasted Caesar’s clemency as if mercy were a spice that made leftover Pompeians edible. Cassius ate. Eating was cover. Across the couches a man newly fat with a Spanish posting laughed too easily. Another name—land near a place Cassius had bled for, or thought he had—floated past like perfume.

Whether the slight was real or polished by pride did not matter by the fruit course. It had found a rib and sat there.

A servant leaned too near with wine. Cassius spoke of rain and cousins until the servant retreated. Then, to the man on his right, lightly: “Some of us are forgiven into irrelevance. It is a delicate art.”

The man chuckled, unsure whether it was a joke. Cassius let him stay unsure.

He left early, courteous, already mapping who else had smiled wrong at the toast. He did not use the word *recruit*. Words like that belonged to later rooms. Tonight he only stored names the way other men stored insults—dry, labelled, waiting for a use.

He paused under a torch and named, inwardly, three men who had laughed at the wrong time. Three was enough to begin a geometry. Geometry was cleaner than rage.

Outside, the street air felt cleaner than the dinner’s gratitude. Clemency, Cassius decided, was a door. Doors could be walked through in either direction.

# SC-09 — Second Line

POV: Decimus · portico / outer rooms · FS05 · FS21  
Date / place: Caesar’s outer orbit · afternoon  
Canon refs: M5 seed · INS  
Info ENTER: Trusted legate glow · EXIT: Hears secondary heir whisper — pride + unease  
Overhear risk: Medium

---

Caesar had kept him half an hour on roads.

Not glory roads—ditch timing, which bridge would take wagons after rain, whether a courier could shave a day between Mutina and the city without killing the horse. Decimus answered with the bluntness Caesar liked: numbers, not compliments. Caesar laughed once at a Spanish memory Decimus had not meant as comedy, and the laugh landed in Decimus’s chest like proof he still belonged in the room where the day was decided.

“Come back after the afternoon clients,” Caesar said. “I want your eye on the Campus route before the games talk turns into a fight.”

Ordinary trust. The kind that made you useful. Decimus left the inner room still tasting that usefulness—warm, specific, undeserved by philosophy.

Someone laughed that Decimus was written into the will’s second line.

The laugh came from a man who collected other men’s secrets like party favours, waiting in the outer portico as if chance were a profession. The whisper hit Decimus’s usefulness like a draft under a door.

“Second line,” the laugher said, soft enough to deny. “After the blood that matters. Still—imagine being loved enough to be ink.”

Decimus felt warmth, then a draft. Being trusted was a kind of height. Heights had edges. He joked once, correctly, about Spanish wine, and the joke bought him exit without looking stabbed.

Alone in the portico shade he tested the thought: *heir* as compliment, *heir* as leash, *heir* as target for other men’s envy. He did not know the wax. He knew the feeling of being indispensable—and how indispensability made betrayal thinkable for men who were not him. Not yet. The “not yet” was quiet and did not introduce itself.

He touched the portico column once—habit of a man checking whether stone still agreed to hold him—and went back toward Caesar’s voice because that was still where his day lived. Caesar was already speaking to a clerk about grain; he glanced up, found Decimus, and tipped his chin as if the afternoon had never been interrupted by other men’s envy.

“Campus,” Caesar said. “Before the light goes dishonest.”

Pride walked with Decimus. Unease walked half a step behind, like a client who had not been dismissed. If the whisper was false, he had been flattered into a shape. If true, he had been loved into a target. Either way, trust had stopped being simple—and he still chose the room that laughed with him about roads.
