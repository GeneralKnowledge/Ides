# SC-22: Access

POV: Decimus · near Domus Publica / Campus
Info ENTER: Caesar’s favour · EXIT: Inside conspiracy as access key (reluctant)
Overhear risk: Street

---

They didn’t recruit Decimus with philosophy. They recruited him with a floor plan.

Cassius and Brutus found him on the Sacra Via, the Sacred Way, where processions walked and the noise of a hundred stalls could drown a dangerous sentence. Cassius spoke first; Brutus stood half a step back, the brand already tired of being used.

“He will not walk into the Curia for a stranger’s summons,” Cassius said. “He will walk if you ask. You know which door the litter uses when the Forum is thick. You have eaten in that atrium. You have joked him past bad omens in Spain.”

The Curia was where the Senate sat, a long hall with benches for six hundred men. The old one had been half torn down for rebuilding, which meant that for the next sitting the Senate would meet in the hall beside Pompey’s theatre, in the shadow of Pompey’s own statue. Decimus had thought that last thing and felt his stomach turn over.

Decimus looked toward the Domus Publica’s public face, the painted smile of a house that still pretended to belong to the city, and felt the warmth of being indispensable curdle. He knew the door. That was the obscenity.

“You are asking me to use the hours he gives me,” Decimus said. “Not a vote. Not a speech. The hours.”

“Yes,” Brutus said, quiet. “If we are monsters, let the city at least see a clean cut. Without you, it becomes a stranger’s ambush, and then we are only a street.”

Decimus’s first answer was not yes. “Find another man he trusts.”

“There isn’t one who will still look him in the face after,” Cassius said. “That is why it has to be you.”

“That is a compliment I would rather have had from a stranger.”

“Strangers do not know which door the litter uses.”

A porter shouted at a mule. Decimus used the noise to breathe. He saw Caesar’s chin-tip from yesterday: Campus, before the light goes dishonest. He hated how ordinary the memory was.

“If I bring him,” Decimus said at last, “you finish clean. No massacre. No Antony first, unless you have already decided otherwise.”

“That is a different argument,” Cassius said.

“Then argue it before I walk him anywhere,” Decimus said. “I can walk him to the right threshold and give the signal. Trebonius can keep him with remounts and Gaul paper until the world ends. I will not supply a butcher’s yard and call it friendship.”

Gaul paper was the dull accounting of ten years of war: horse purchases, requisitions, winter quarters, the paperwork of an army that never stopped needing something. Trebonius had signed half of it. Antony had spent a decade pretending to read it. It was the one subject on which a consul could be held in conversation for an hour without noticing the hour had gone.

He almost smiled at Trebonius’s delay-talk, then didn’t. Smiling made the plan feel like a joke, and jokes were how men talked themselves out of courage.

“I will bring him,” Decimus said. “You do the rest without turning the Senate into meat. Or I am not your summons. I am only another corpse who used to know which jokes Caesar still liked.”

Cassius’ nod was small. Small nods moved armies.

When Decimus turned away, Brutus caught Cassius’s sleeve for half a breath, both faces still public for any watcher.

“He will hate us for using the laugh,” Brutus murmured.

“He already hates the choice,” Cassius said. “Hate is cheaper than finding another friend who still has his laugh.”

“That is a terrible thing to say.”

“It is a very accurate thing to say. Whether it is terrible is a matter for the people who survive to write about us.”

Ten paces on, alone enough, Cassius said to the air what he would not say beside Brutus: “He still smells of Caesar’s wine. Good. Men who smell of nothing are harder to aim.”

# SC-23: The Argument

POV: Brutus (Cassius & Decimus in frame) · closed garden · M6
Info ENTER: Ops planning · EXIT: Spare Antony (Brutus wins); Trebonius tasked in principle
Overhear risk: Low, slave almost hears (**D**)

---

The garden was small enough that anger had nowhere to go but into whispers. Winter herbs still clung in pots. Someone’s cook had left a knife on a stone, and Decimus moved it without thinking, habit of a man who noticed edges.

There were four of them now: Cassius, Brutus, Decimus, and Trebonius, who had come in late and said nothing, which in Trebonius was a form of attendance. They stood in a small square of cold light between two walls, and the argument was one they had been having in different shapes for a week.

Cassius wanted Antony’s morning ended with Caesar’s. “Leave the consul alive and you leave a sword with a mouth,” he said. “He will not write amnesties. He will write funerals. He will lift a bloody toga and make the Forum his client.”

Antony was a big man with a soldier’s laugh and a gambler’s generosity, commander of Caesar’s left wing at Pharsalus and his co-consul now. He could fill a hall with his voice. He had stood at Caesar’s elbow for years, and the Forum knew it, and loved him for it, and followed him.

Brutus shook his head once, the ancestral gesture. “If we kill them in piles, we are not liberators. We are a slaughter. The Senate must see a surgery, not a civil war’s first hour. Spare the consul and we spare the city’s excuse to call us animals.”

“The city will call us animals anyway,” Cassius said.

“Then let them be wrong about the pile,” Brutus answered. His eyes flicked to Decimus, proof the inch from last night still governed him: hearing meant surgery, not cordwood.

“You are very sure of what Antony will do,” said Trebonius, from the wall.

“I am sure of what I would do,” said Cassius, “in his sandals.”

“You would not have the sandals,” Trebonius said. “That is why he is the consul and you are a praetor.”

Decimus counted thresholds in his head: Curia lip, portico shoulder, the place where Trebonius could turn a laugh into a leash. “Antony in the room means noise. Antony outside means a chance to speak after. Trebonius can hold him with business, Gaul talk, debt talk, remounts that never quite arrive. That is his talent.”

“I do have a gift,” said Trebonius.

“And if Trebonius fails?” Cassius asked.

“Then we improvise,” Decimus said, hating the word. Improvisation was what got men killed in Spain when the map lied.

Brutus held the spare. Cassius stored the loss like interest, visible in the jaw, kept for later collection. A slave’s foot scuffed beyond the hedge, too near. Conversation became cover chat: rain, a cousin’s wedding, whether the chickens had been rude. The foot retreated. Almost.

“The chickens,” said Trebonius, as the foot faded, “are the great unsung heroes of the Republic.”

“They will have their day,” said Cassius.

They left with a decision that wore mercy’s face: Antony would live through the knives. Trebonius would be the door that stayed closed on a consul’s back.

In the lane beyond the garden gate, still smiling as if cousins and chickens had satisfied them, Decimus wiped garden-dirt from his palm onto his tunic.

“You got your spare,” he told Brutus, soft.

“You got your clean finish,” Brutus said.

Cassius walked half a step ahead, then without turning: “I got a living consul who will make funerals. Congratulations to both of you.”

Decimus’s mouth twitched despite himself. “Say it louder when we are farther from the hedge.”

“I am saying it now so you remember the price while your hands are still clean,” Cassius said. Then, brighter, for a passerby: “Yes, rain tomorrow, if the chickens agree.”

Decimus washed at a basin later and thought, absurdly, of Spanish wine and Caesar’s laugh, then let the laugh go. Doors didn’t get to keep jokes.
