# SC-22 — Access

POV: Decimus · near Domus Publica / Campus  
Info ENTER: Caesar’s favour · EXIT: Inside conspiracy as access key

---

They did not recruit Decimus with philosophy. They recruited him with a floor plan.

Cassius—or Brutus beside Cassius; Decimus later blurred who spoke first—said the plain thing in a place where the Sacra Via’s noise could drown a dangerous sentence: Caesar would not walk into the Curia for a stranger’s summons. He would walk for Decimus. Friendship was the key. The key was the crime.

Decimus looked toward the Domus Publica’s public face on the Sacra Via—the painted smile of a house that still pretended to belong to the city—and felt the warmth of being indispensable curdle. He had eaten in that atrium. He had joked Caesar past bad omens in Spain. He knew which door the litter used when the Forum was thick.

“If I bring him,” Decimus said, “you finish clean. No massacre. No Antony first—unless you have already decided otherwise.”

“That is a different argument,” Cassius said.

Decimus said yes with logistics eyes. Betrayal, when it wore friendship’s cloak, still needed a schedule, a signal, a man to hold a consul in a colonnade. He could supply two of the three. The third would be Trebonius’ boredom weapon: talk. Decimus almost smiled at that—Trebonius could hold a man with Monday until the world ended—and then did not smile, because smiling at the plan made it feel like a joke, and jokes were how men talked themselves out of courage.

“I will bring him,” Decimus said. “You do the rest without turning the Senate into a butcher’s yard. Or I am not your key. I am only another corpse who used to have friends.”

Cassius’ nod was small. Small nods moved armies.

# SC-23 — The Argument

POV: Brutus (Cassius & Decimus in frame) · closed garden · M6  
Info ENTER: Ops planning · EXIT: Spare Antony (Brutus wins); Trebonius tasked in principle  
Overhear risk: Low — slave almost hears (**D**)

---

The garden was small enough that anger had nowhere to go but into whispers. Winter herbs still clung in pots; someone’s cook had left a knife on a stone and Decimus moved it without thinking—habit of a man who noticed edges.

Cassius wanted Antony’s morning ended with Caesar’s. “Leave the consul alive and you leave a sword with a mouth,” he said. “He will not write amnesties. He will write funerals. He will lift a bloody toga and make the Forum his client.”

Brutus shook his head once, the ancestral gesture. “If we kill them in piles, we are not liberators. We are a slaughter. The Senate must see a surgery, not a civil war’s first hour. Spare the consul and we spare the city’s excuse to call us animals.”

“The city will call us animals anyway,” Cassius said.

“Then let them be wrong about the pile,” Brutus answered.

Decimus counted doors in his head—Curia threshold, portico shoulder, the place where Trebonius could turn a laugh into a leash. “Antony in the room means noise. Antony outside means a chance to speak after. Trebonius can hold him with business—Gaul talk, debt talk, anything that smells like Monday. He is good at Monday.”

“And if Trebonius fails?” Cassius asked.

“Then we improvise,” Decimus said, hating the word. Improvisation was what got men killed in Spain when the map lied.

Brutus held the spare. Cassius stored the loss like interest—visible in the jaw, filed for later collection. A slave’s foot scuffed beyond the hedge—too near. Conversation became weather chat: rain, a cousin’s wedding, whether the chickens had been rude. The foot retreated. Almost. The conspiracy’s favourite weather.

They left with a decision that wore mercy’s face: Antony would live through the knives. Trebonius would be the door that stayed closed on a consul’s back.

Decimus washed the garden-dirt from his hands at a basin and thought, absurdly, of Spanish wine and Caesar’s laugh, and then stopped thinking of laughs. Laughs were for men who were not keys.
