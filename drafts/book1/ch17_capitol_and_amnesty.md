# SC-45: The Joy That Does Not Arrive

POV: Brutus
Date / place: Capitol (N5) · 15 March afternoon
Canon refs: strategic fail · *Att.* 15.1a seed
Info ENTER: Liberty script ready; dagger still metaphorically warm
Info EXIT: Waits for popular joy that does not come clean; begins to understand the city did not read the same text
Overhear risk: Crowd below partial

---

Brutus stood on the Capitol’s rock, above the Forum, where Rome could see him if Rome wished to look up. Some of the Liberators still held their blades up where the Forum could see them. Cassius kept his voice low and watched the paths up the hill. Decimus kept looking north. Casca wiped at a spot that would not leave his knuckle.

Brutus spoke. He heard himself speak. The words were right (*libertas*, tyrant, law, ancestors), each one a stone from a good road. He had learned *libertas* as a schoolboy, in the same lesson as the first Brutus and the last king. Below, the Forum did not cheer. There were cheers in patches, and they died. Someone shouted for Caesar. Someone shouted for the dead man’s veterans. Someone shouted for grain.

“They will come round,” Casca said.

“They will come with torches,” Decimus said, not looking down.

Cassius did not choose between them yet. He was still watching the paths. “Speak again, or go down. One or the other.”

Brutus watched a knot of women shove a knot of men and had his first thought of the day that did not come out of a book: the city had loved the man more than it would ever love the word. Under it there was another thought, smaller and worse: he had killed a man who forgave him and kept him close. He wanted, badly, to wait until the Forum below turned into the crowd he had written the speech for.

“We hold until the reports clarify,” Brutus said, in the voice he used from a tribunal. “Liberty does not run from its own Forum.”

A runner brought nonsense: Antony dead; Antony marching; Lepidus burning houses; Cicero already writing the age.

“Lepidus has a legion on the Tiber island,” Cassius said, without turning round. “Caesar’s master of the horse, with soldiers in reach. Any rumour about him will find a buyer.”

“Cicero is writing the age?” Casca said. “Already?”

He was not. An hour later he was on the steps, out of breath, in a clean toga, as if he had stopped at home to change for dinner. He took Brutus by both hands and said *liberty* twice, the second time to hear how it sounded on the hill. Then he told them to have the praetors summon the Senate up here tonight, while the city’s fear was still in their hands.

“Tonight,” Cassius said, “the Senate is under its beds.”

“Then knock,” said Cicero. He stayed long enough to be seen and not long enough to be counted, and went down again before the light changed.

“He will say he warned us,” Cassius said, when the toga had gone.

“He did not warn us.”

“He will find the letter.”

Toward evening the light went copper. The Capitol’s stone held heat. Brutus’s hands smelled of metal no matter how often he washed them in a basin someone had brought, and each time the cut across his hand opened again. He dried them on his toga and still would not look at his own palms. He began, in his head, the speech as it would be written down.

Cassius said, close enough that only Brutus and Decimus caught it, “We cannot sleep on this rock and call it victory.”

“We can call it a beginning,” Brutus said.

Cassius waited until Casca had turned to the parapet. “Beginning,” he said, not for the crowd. “Decimus wants a road. Casca wants a cheer. You want it written down better than it went.”

Casca’s knuckle still wouldn’t come clean. He followed Cassius’s glance round the faces on the hill. “Galba isn’t here,” Casca said. Brutus remembered the small room, and Cassius saying that he had promised the man nothing. Cassius’s mouth went tight.

“We stay, then,” Cassius said. “But say that it is because we are afraid. Do not make a period of it.”

Brutus did not answer. Below them the Forum was filling again, and nobody in it was looking up.

# SC-46: Force Available

POV: Antony
Date / place: Campus / city troop orbit near Lepidus · late 15 March
Canon refs: M11 · FT14
Info ENTER: Caesar dead; Antony alive; armed force nearby
Info EXIT: Negotiation chosen over instant massacre
Overhear risk: Officers

---

Antony’s first clear desire was to kill everyone in a toga who had ever smiled at Brutus.

His second, slower one, which kept him alive, was to find out who still had swords that would move when he pointed. He had slipped away from Pompey’s portico that morning in a slave’s cloak, and the cloak had been the cleverest thing he had done since dawn.

Lepidus had a legion on the Tiber island, the only organised soldiers close to Rome. They could be in the Forum before dark. Pacuvius, who had carried Antony’s donatives to the legions before, said as much, hopeful as a dog.

“Give the word,” Pacuvius said. “We take the Capitol before dark.”

Antony looked at him. He wanted it too. He could feel in his arms how much he wanted it.

“And then what?” Antony asked. “We kill sixty senators on a temple step, and wake up owning a hungry city that hates us for it?”

“They killed him,” Pacuvius said.

“I noticed.” Antony’s voice came out flatter than grief. Grief would come when he had time to drink it properly. “They also left me alive. That was their first gift. I am not going to repay it by becoming their excuse.”

“They left you alive because they could not hold you,” Pacuvius said.

“They left me alive because Trebonius is a better liar than you are a soldier,” said Antony.

Lepidus had come across from the island with mud to the knee and had not sat down. He spoke carefully, as he always did when he was afraid of being asked for something. “If we talk, we keep our choices.”

“Negotiation preserves my throat,” Antony said. “And yours. And the chance to make the people choose us when they see the body.”

He did not yet know how he would show the body. He knew that he would.

“I can hold my men for a funeral,” Lepidus said. “I cannot hold them for a slaughter that makes every toga in Rome some dead man’s cousin.”

Rumour had him dead in the Subura. He almost liked that.

“Hold the men,” Antony told Pacuvius. “Feed them. Promise them nothing. Tomorrow we talk indoors.”

Antony spat blood from a bitten cheek he did not remember biting and stared toward the Capitol until Lepidus coughed.

# SC-47: Brokerage

POV: Cicero
Date / place: Temple of Tellus / meeting spaces · 16–17 March 44
Canon refs: amnesty trajectory · acts ratified trap · *Att.* retrospective energy without future-wink
Info ENTER: Chaos; Liberators on Capitol; Caesarians armed in potential
Info EXIT: Amnesty path; Caesar’s acts to stand, seed of later trap; Cicero feels useful
Overhear risk: Elite high

---

Cicero was sent for, and for two days he went from house to house.

The Senate met two days later, on the seventeenth, in the temple of Tellus near Antony’s house, at Antony’s call. That was where the bargain would be made. Until then it was made in dining rooms.

“If we do not settle this,” he told a dining room full of men who wanted to hear it, “we will have a second war before we have buried the first man’s meat.”

He enjoyed, privately, shamefully, the return of necessity. Necessity had always been his favourite climate. In necessity, eloquence looked like courage. He had been consul the year Catiline’s men meant to burn the city, and Rome had called him father of his country for stopping them; every crisis since had been, for him, a chance to be that man again.

Brutus’s people wanted praise and protection. Antony’s people wanted the dictator’s papers to remain law so the city did not tip into a void where every debt could be renegotiated with a knife. Cicero heard both and performed the old trick: show each side the part of its face that could still be called Republic.

“Amnesty,” Cicero said, and gave them the Greek on purpose. “Forgetting. Athens once ended a civil war by swearing not to remember it.”

The word went round the room. Amnesty for the killers; validity for Caesar’s acts.

Cicero said, “Peace buys the Republic room to breathe. Law returns in that room, or we burn the house to spite one dead man.”

“You want us to pardon the men who did it,” said a senator.

“I want us to pardon the men who did it so that nobody does it again,” said Cicero. “It is called the Republic. I am told it exists.”

Someone asked if Caesar’s arrangements would stand: colonies, magistracies, the sticky gifts already promised.

“If they fall,” Cicero said, quick, “half of Italy stands up with a claim and a torch. We are not children.”

He heard prudence in it, and liked the sound. He saw Atticus in his mind as audience and felt the old warmth of being the man who could still arrange a city with clauses.

A messenger muttered that Antony had not stormed the Capitol.

“Good,” Cicero said. “Then we still have a consul who prefers a forum to a pyre.”

He left the room with the praise still on his mouth. In the corridor a banker was telling a younger man that the leases on the gardens across the Tiber would move before anyone had opened the will, which was lying with the Vestals, unread. Cicero did not stop for it.

He went home, or to someone’s home that would do, and dictated notes until his hand cramped. Outside, the rumours went on all night. Cicero slept two hours.

On the seventeenth, when the bargain had begun to harden, he allowed himself a thin satisfaction and sanded a period until it shone.
