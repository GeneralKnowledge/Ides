# SC-45: The Joy That Does Not Arrive

POV: Brutus
Date / place: Capitol (N5) · 15 March afternoon
Canon refs: strategic fail · *Att.* 15.1a seed
Info ENTER: Liberty script ready; dagger still metaphorically warm
Info EXIT: Waits for popular joy that does not come clean; begins to understand the city did not read the same text
Overhear risk: Crowd below partial

---

On the Capitol’s rock above the Forum, height was supposed to mean clarity.

Brutus stood where Rome could see him if Rome wished to look up. Some of the Liberators still held their blades openly, theatre of virtue. Cassius kept his voice low and his eyes on approaches, and on faces that should have been there. Decimus was already half elsewhere in his mind: Gaul, legions, the practical after. The northern road was the only plan that did not involve being loved by the Forum. Casca wiped a spot that would not leave his knuckle. The rock held speech, exit, and argument. Height could not choose among them.

Brutus spoke. He heard himself speak. The words were right (libertas, tyrant, law, ancestors), each one a stone from a good road. He had learned *libertas* as a schoolboy, in the same lesson as the first Brutus and the last king. Below, the Forum didn’t become a choir. It became noise with legs. Cheers happened in patches and died. Someone shouted for Caesar. Someone shouted for the dead man’s veterans. Someone shouted for grain. Grain was not a consul.

“They will come round,” Casca said, needing it true enough to keep the blade in the open.

“They will come with torches,” Decimus said, not looking down. “I can hear the road north from here. This rock is not a province.”

“It has a very good view,” Casca said.

“So does a gallows,” said Decimus.

Cassius did not choose between them yet. His eyes stayed on approaches. “Speak again if speech is the plan. Leave if leaving is. Do not stand in the middle and call it philosophy.”

Brutus watched a knot of women shove a knot of men and felt the first cold thought that was not philosophical: we have killed a man the city loved more than it loves our vocabulary. Under it, smaller and worse: I killed a man who forgave me and kept me close. The late hour after the spare returned without asking leave stood beside the Forum noise. Wine gone warm. Ordinary talk. The war treated as a misunderstanding their house could afford. The rock could not make private guilt public enough to share. He wanted, badly, to wait until the Forum rearranged itself into the audience the words deserved. Waiting was the softest cowardice available. It looked like hope.

He almost ordered the linger as policy. Then he saw Decimus’s shoulder already half-turned toward Gaul, and Cassius’s mouth ready to price another hour on stone, and Casca’s open blade becoming theatre the city could hate.

“We hold until the reports clarify,” Brutus said, public face, station calm. “Liberty does not run from its own Forum.”

A runner brought nonsense: Antony dead; Antony marching; Lepidus burning houses; Cicero already writing the age.

“Lepidus has a legion on the Tiber island,” Cassius said, eyes still on the approaches. “Caesar’s master of the horse, with soldiers in reach. Any rumour about him will find a buyer.”

Brutus kept each report as incomplete. He had been trained for incompleteness in books. The street’s incompleteness had teeth. The decision to wait used incompleteness as cover.

“Cicero is writing the age?” Casca said. “Already?”

“He has been writing it since breakfast,” Cassius said. “He is waiting for us to finish so he can say he warned us.”

“He did not warn us.”

“He will find the letter.”

Toward evening the light went copper. The Capitol’s stone held heat. Brutus’ hands smelled of metal no matter how often he washed them in a basin someone provided like a sacrament. He dried them on his toga and still would not look at his own palms. Eyes could not make the spare into something other than blood. He drafted, in his head, the speech he might write down so the day would have a cleaner spine than it deserved. Drafting was another way of not leaving.

Cassius said, close enough that only Brutus and Decimus caught it, “We cannot sleep on this rock and call it victory.”

“We can call it beginning,” Brutus said, still the public sentence.

They moved a few paces off the sight-line, into a corridor of stone where the Forum’s noise became weather. Cassius’s polite face dropped. He had set down a cup. “Beginning,” he said, not for the crowd. “You just spent the afternoon teaching Rome that knives climb hills and wait to be loved. Decimus wants a road. Casca wants a cheer. You want a cleaner text. The city is shopping for who pays. Mark that when your vocabulary comes back stained.”

Casca’s knuckle still wouldn’t come clean. He followed Cassius’s glance along the incomplete ring of faces. “Galba isn’t here,” Casca said. “Brother’s winter came due this morning, patron called the grain hard enough that Galba chose bread over rock. Or the bread chose him.” Cassius did not flinch in public. Brutus remembered the brother’s winter Cassius had promised Galba in February and never paid; absence was the receipt now, standing where a blade should have stood.

Decimus did not soften it. “I will not bless the linger as wisdom. I said so before. Say Capitol if you must, say it as fear, not as a period.”

Brutus took the corridor truth without answering it in kind. Answering would have been another speech. Below them, Rome did not kneel. It shopped, wept, armed, and waited to see who paid. The rock had just taught him that waiting could be a choice with a cost, not only a virtue with a view.

# SC-46: Force Available

POV: Antony
Date / place: Campus / city troop orbit near Lepidus · late 15 March
Canon refs: M11 · FT14
Info ENTER: Caesar dead; Antony alive; armed force nearby
Info EXIT: Negotiation chosen over instant massacre
Overhear risk: Officers

---

Antony’s first clear desire was to kill everyone in a toga who had ever smiled at Brutus.

His second, slower desire, the one that kept him consul instead of a corpse in an alley, was to count who still had swords that would move when he pointed. He had slipped away from Pompey’s portico that morning in a slave’s cloak, and the cloak had been the cleverest thing he had done since dawn.

Lepidus’ men were a fact in the city’s body: the legion on the Tiber island, the only organised soldiers close to Rome. Facts could be marched into the Forum and made into a butcher’s festival. M. Pacuvius (scar through the eyebrow, Antony’s old donative contact) said as much, hopeful as a dog.

“Give the word,” Pacuvius said. “We take the Capitol before dark.”

Antony looked at the man’s hope and found it childish. Childish things started civil wars before you had a will, a funeral, and a story.

“And then what?” Antony asked. “We paint the rock with senators and wake up owning a hungry city that hates us for the paint?”

“They killed him,” Pacuvius said.

“I noticed.” Antony’s voice came out flatter than grief. Grief would come when he had time to drink it properly. “They also left me alive. That was their first gift. I am not going to repay it by becoming their excuse.”

“They left you alive because they could not hold you,” Pacuvius said.

“They left me alive because Trebonius is a better liar than you are a soldier,” said Antony. “I intend to remember which of those is useful.”

Lepidus spoke carefully, as Lepidus always did when weight leaned on him, not weak, not heroic, only present where maps got drawn. “Negotiation preserves options.”

“Negotiation preserves my throat,” Antony said. “And yours. And the chance to make the people choose us when they see the body.”

He didn’t yet know how he would show the body. He knew only that a corpse in the right light was worth more than a thousand virtuous daggers on a hill.

Pacuvius did not wait for poetry. “Massacre first. Funeral after. The rock still has warm knives on it, every hour we feed the men soft is an hour they invent softer orders.”

An officer beside him, young, oil-smell, ambition still unscarred, seconded without looking at Lepidus. “Paint the Capitol before baker-rumour invents a different martyr. Force is a fact. Facts do not keep overnight.”

“Neither does fish,” Antony said. “I have eaten both. The fish was less dangerous.”

Lepidus’s careful mouth found a third pressure: “If you paint senators tonight, tomorrow’s Forum buries you with them. I can hold men for a funeral. I cannot hold them for a butcher’s festival that makes every toga a relative of the dead.”

Three wants, one ditch of snowless March air. Pacuvius’s storm. The officer’s paint. Lepidus’s map that still wanted survivors to bargain with. Antony felt the group lean as a single weight, massacre versus how the body would look tomorrow, and chose the slower blade because the slower blade still had a consul’s name on it.

Rumour had him dead in the Subura. He almost liked that. Let the bakers’ short version bury him; he would arrive at his own funeral as a miracle with a consul’s temper.

“Hold the men,” Antony told Pacuvius. “Feed them. Promise them nothing you can’t spell. Tomorrow we talk in rooms with doors.”

“I can spell,” Pacuvius said, offended.

“Then spell patience,” said Antony.

Pacuvius made the face of a man receiving wisdom, chin tip, eyes grateful, the station grin that kept consuls from smelling mutiny. “As you say. Rooms. Doors. Feed them soft.”

Antony turned toward the Capitol. Behind him Pacuvius’s soft dropped, for Lepidus’s nearest ear and two officers who had wanted dark, and not quite out of a consul’s hearing. “Rooms. While the rock still has senators on it with warm knives. He’ll remember caution more than our hunger, mark that when the Forum wants blood and we offer clauses.”

Antony let it pass without turning. Lepidus did not contradict. Contradiction in open air made you the man who lit the massacre early. Pacuvius looked cheated anyway. Good. Cheated soldiers still listened. Satisfied soldiers invented orders.

Antony spat blood from a bitten cheek he did not remember biting and stared toward the Capitol. Contempt would not collapse it. Pacuvius’s hunger still wanted paint; Antony wanted a funeral that paid.

“You wanted liberty,” he said to the hill, too soft for officers. “Congratulate yourselves. Now learn the price of a crowd.”

# SC-47: Brokerage

POV: Cicero
Date / place: Temple of Tellus / meeting spaces · 16–17 March 44
Canon refs: amnesty trajectory · acts ratified trap · *Att.* retrospective energy without future-wink
Info ENTER: Chaos; Liberators on Capitol; Caesarians armed in potential
Info EXIT: Amnesty path; Caesar’s acts to stand, seed of later trap; Cicero feels useful
Overhear risk: Elite high

---

Cicero arrived at each room. Some had asked for him personally. Panic made men send for words the way fever made them send for doctors.

The Senate met two days later, on the seventeenth, in the temple of Tellus near Antony’s house, at Antony’s call. That was the room in which the bargain would be sold. In the meantime, every evening was a smaller room.

“If we do not settle this,” he told a cluster of faces that wanted settling, “we will have a second war before we have buried the first man’s meat.”

He enjoyed, privately, shamefully, the return of necessity. Necessity had always been his favourite climate. In necessity, eloquence looked like courage. He had been consul the year Catiline’s men meant to burn the city, and Rome had called him father of his country for stopping them; every crisis since had been, for him, a chance to be that man again.

Brutus’ people wanted praise and protection. Antony’s people wanted the dictator’s papers to remain law so the city did not tip into a void where every debt could be renegotiated with a knife. Cicero heard both and performed the old trick: show each side the part of its face that could still be called Republic.

“Amnesty,” Cicero said, and gave them the Greek on purpose. “Forgetting. Athens once ended a civil war by swearing not to remember it.”

The word went around the marble like wine. Amnesty for the killers; validity for Caesar’s acts. A bargain with two handsome hands and a hidden mouth.

Cicero said, “Peace buys the Republic room to breathe. Law returns in that room, or we burn the house to spite one dead man.”

“You want us to pardon the men who did it,” said a senator.

“I want us to pardon the men who did it so that nobody does it again,” said Cicero. “It is called the Republic. I am told it exists.”

Someone asked if Caesar’s arrangements would stand: colonies, magistracies, the sticky gifts already promised.

“If they fall,” Cicero said, quick, “half of Italy stands up with a claim and a torch. We are not children.”

He didn’t hear, in his own sentence, any trap’s hinge. He heard prudence, and liked the sound of himself hearing it. He saw Atticus in his mind as audience and felt the old warmth of being the man who could still arrange a city with clauses.

A messenger muttered that Antony had not stormed the Capitol.

“Good,” Cicero said. “Then we still have a consul who prefers a forum to a pyre.”

He left the bargaining room with praise still arranged on his mouth: peace, breath, law’s return. The smile was sized for men who needed Cicero more than they needed a pyre. In the corridor outside, voices were already pricing what the bargain had not named. A banker-looking man, onion-and-money face, stylus, told a junior that gardens across the Tiber would move leases before anyone opened a will. “It is lying with the Vestals, unread,” he said, “like a sleeping dog. Price it anyway.” A client asked who the paper might wound first. Decimus, if the dead man had loved him in ink, second line, the dinner joke that had once sounded like party favour. The Liberators, if the crowd got three hundred reasons. Or the queen still lodged in Caesar’s green claim, unfinished as a Roman problem.

“The queen is not in the Senate,” the junior said.

“Neither is a will until it walks,” the banker answered. “Acts stand. Gardens walk. Boys inherit. Finish her story or she finishes yours, men are already shopping the options.”

“Which boy?” said the junior.

“That is the entire question, young man,” said the banker. “That is why they are shopping.”

Farther down the same corridor, near the side door servants used when mistresses did not want to be seen pricing, a woman whose messenger wore Antony’s house-ring stopped a clerk mid-bow. “Amnesty is a night’s kindness,” she said, dry. “Paper that stands overnight is the consul’s war-chest. Do not let Cicero’s peace sand the acts into something soft.” Another mouth answered from the opposite wall: an older steward carrying a tablet still soft with Brutus’s mother’s seal. “Then ask whose sons the paper wounds before the seal breaks. Mothers rewrite what rooms praise. Watch when amnesty asks Brutus to thank a consul for surviving.” Neither asked Cicero’s opinion. Both spent the same amnesty he had just sold as breath. Both were already spending someone else’s authority to do it.

Cicero was nearly at the corner when the client’s voice thinned behind him: “He sanded periods while we priced the corpse. Useful man. Expensive illusion.”

Cicero heard enough to file gardens / boy / queen / wound beside amnesty, and Decimus / second line as a debt the dead man still held on the living. He did not enter the corridor argument. Entering would have made him a stall. He preferred rooms that asked for periods. The praise-smile lasted until the temple steps; then it was only a face arranging itself for Atticus.

He went home, or to someone’s home that would do, and dictated notes until his hand cramped. Outside, Rome buried rumour under rumour. Beneath the burial, the city began shopping the dead man’s paper before the seal broke. Cicero slept two hours and dreamed he was consul again: the old habit, not prophecy.

On the seventeenth, when the bargain had begun to harden, he allowed himself a thin satisfaction and sanded a period until it shone. He slept two more hours and didn’t dream of funerals. He dreamed, again, that he was consul. The corridor’s shopping list waited under the dream like interest.
