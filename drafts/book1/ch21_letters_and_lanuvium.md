# SC-60 — Petty News

POV: Cicero · Campania villas  
Date / place: Fundi / Matius orbit · letter desk  
Canon refs: *Att.* 14.1–4 · Chain 4 residual · D3  
Info ENTER: Joy at tyrannicide · EXIT: Needs forces/money; hungers for daily scraps  
Overhear risk: Letter couriers

---

Cicero wrote as other men breathed.

The tablet under his palm was still warm from the last courier’s bag. Wax took fingerprints. He liked that—proof that news had a body before it became opinion. *The deed was fair,* he told Atticus in one of the versions that would not survive intact, *the result is nothing.* He scratched it out. He wrote it again softer. Softness kept invitations open.

From Fundi’s shade he demanded the city’s gossip in teaspoonfuls: who dined with Antony, who smiled at Brutus’ name, what the bakers said about gardens not yet walked. Couriers arrived dusty and left dustier. One boy’s sandals had cracked; Cicero tipped him as if tipping could buy a Republic and then asked, sharply, whether the seal on Antony’s latest posting had looked fresh or tired. Fresh seals meant industry. Tired seals meant theatre.

“If they have no money and no men,” he told a visitor who wanted philosophy, “they have a beautiful murder and a short future.”

The visitor—some neighbour with a taste for Athens—smiled as if force were a vulgar digression. “Surely the Senate—”

“The Senate is a throat,” Cicero said, and hated the sentence as soon as it left him. “Throats need fists behind them, or they only make sounds.”

He hated that he sounded like Cassius. He hated more that Cassius might be right.

Sphaerus brought a note from Hirtius’s orbit—polite, useless, full of men congratulating themselves for surviving March. Cicero answered with courtesy that congratulated itself louder. At night he catalogued his own courage on the Capitol’s first day and found it larger in memory than in the hour. That was allowed. Memory was a kind of consulship.

A second courier brought news that was already half false: Antony generous; Antony monstrous; Brutus worshipped; Brutus finished. Cicero kept both halves. Incomplete information was still information if you labelled it. He asked the boy one more question before dismissing him: whether anyone in the Forum had yet said *Octavius* as if the name were money. The boy looked blank. Blank was an answer. Cicero wrote *not yet* in the margin of a draft and underlined it twice.

He stacked finished letters by the door like spears he could not throw. The stack made him feel armed. Feeling armed was not the same as having an army. He knew the difference and wrote faster anyway—ink as substitute for cohorts, sentences as substitute for pay.

# SC-61 — Cage with a View

POV: Brutus · Lanuvium  
Date / place: Garden / study · A9 seed  
Canon refs: *Att.* 15.1 flavour · C2  
Info ENTER: Hero brand · EXIT: Cannot safely walk Rome; immortality vs peace  
Overhear risk: Household

---

Lanuvium was not exile. Exile would have been cleaner.

Brutus walked the same garden paths until the gravel knew his sandals. Clients came to whisper loyalty and left before dusk—loyalty that evaporated at eighteen miles. Rome was unreachable as Alexandria and louder in rumour than in reach. A steward reported which roads still carried Antonian veterans like dust that refused to settle. Brutus thanked him as if thanks were policy.

“And the boy?” Brutus asked, surprising himself. “The heir talk.”

The steward hesitated. Hesitation was news. “Campania says the name. Rome still laughs at the name. For now.”

Porcia did not waste comfort. “They call you immortal in the same breath they ask you to be quiet.”

“Peace requires quiet,” Brutus said.

“Peace required a plan,” she answered, and left him the word *plan* like a debt.

He drafted speeches for a city that would not let him deliver them. Ink dried on periods that would never meet a Forum. He read Cato into the hedges. He received word that Antony’s veterans drank in the Forum as if the Forum were a camp, and he felt the old ancestral pressure turn sideways into something like nausea.

Immortality, someone had written him, was better than peace. Brutus folded the sentence and did not burn it. Burning would have been a decision. He put the folded scrap under a weight shaped like a tiny Liberty—someone’s gift, someone’s joke—and hated how the joke still worked on him.

Cassius’ letters from elsewhere smelled of ships and contempt. Brutus answered with measure. Measure was what he had left of virtue when the street preferred a corpse’s gardens. At the gate a client offered a purse “for expenses on the road south—if the praetor would only write one introduction.” Brutus returned the purse unopened. “I will not buy quiet with other men’s coin,” he said. The client left offended. Offence was cheaper than a letter that made Brutus Antony’s petitioner. Another client asked when he would return to Rome. Brutus said, “When Rome remembers what a praetor is for.” That one nodded as if it were a date.

He practised a line for a Senate that might never hear him: *We killed a king so that we would not need one.* The hedges did not applaud. Hedges were honest that way.

It was not a date. It was a cage with a view of hills that looked like freedom if you squinted—and beyond the hills, a name war beginning without him.
