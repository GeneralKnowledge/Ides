# SC-64 — Laws for Sale

POV: F4 Hordeonius · basilica / Puteoli paper  
Date / place: Rome stalls · TONE-1 Batiatus medium  
Canon refs: *Att.* 14.12 flavour · SIDE-1 · C9  
Info ENTER: Chaos premiums · EXIT: Citizenship/land “acts” priced; SIDE-1 murmur useless  
Overhear risk: Medium

---

Hordeonius arranged his face into mourning for the Republic and his stylus into appetite.

“Caesar’s acts,” he told a man from Puteoli, “are a market now. A sacred market. Do not blaspheme liquidity.”

Citizenship shortcuts, land whispers, exemptions with seals that looked tired—each had a number. Hordeonius wrote numbers as priests wrote prodigies: neat, certain, slightly insane. Sosia muttered that forged acts smelled like wet leather. Hordeonius priced the smell.

A whisperer tried again to sell him Visellius’ old tablet ghosts—Basilus orbit, an Antonian hand, the Subura stair that still had a rumour attached. He laid a scrap on the stool as if ash could pay.

Hordeonius picked the scrap up with two fingers, the way a man lifts something that might stain, and set it back on the whisperer’s palm. “Archaeology,” he said gently, blessing-flat. “I deal in the living. The living pay interest. The dead pay only in stories, and stories are oversupplied.” He wiped his fingers on the stall-cloth afterward—ego housekeeping, not fear.

The whisperer bowed as if receiving a funeral oration. “Of course. Living seals.” His offense waited for the next pillar: “He’ll price a boy’s name and call a murder tablet poetry. Ask him for Basilus and he sells you piety.” Sosia’s mouth twitched—audible almost—then he drank so the twitch would look like thirst.

A Puteoli man and Sosia argued the next question in the same breath, same stool-space—collision, not correspondence. “Young Caesar” changed land risk, the Puteoli man insisted; Sosia said only veterans changed land risk; a third voice from two stalls down asked whether Servilia’s people were already rewriting clauses in Antium talk, and whether old eagle men would take the boy’s pay or spit it back as wine-shop joke.

“I price living seals,” Hordeonius said. “Mothers rewriting decrees are politics. Politics I charge for seats near. Eagle men who refuse a purse are weather—watch which weather arrives with coin.”

The Puteoli man wiped his brow. “You talk as if mourning were a price list.”

Hordeonius’s stylus paused, then kept writing. “Then don’t mourn on my stool. Watch which veterans take the boy’s pay. Watch which paper arrives from Antium smelling like Servilia’s people.” Sosia’s mouth twitched toward a joke and died there—the Puteoli man was already leaning in, and jokes that named mothers out loud became line items. “I sell you which rumour empties a purse first. Certainty costs extra, and I don’t stock it.”

He priced the Puteoli question twice—once for Antony’s veterans, once for a boy’s name—and entered *boy-Caesar rumour: watch veterans* beside *Antium soft paper: watch Servilia orbit* in a margin. He almost smiled at the neatness of the entry, then stopped. Clerks who smiled at neatness became funny in taverns.

# SC-65 — Empty Seals

POV: F1 Nysa · Domus Publica after masters shift  
Date / place: Service court / tablinum edge · M12  
Canon refs: NOTE-1 residue · HOUSE-1  
Info ENTER: Grief logistics · EXIT: Tablets/seals become others’ weapons  
Overhear risk: Extreme staff

---

The Domus Publica filled with new footsteps wearing old authority.

Nysa packed what Calpurnia ordered packed and watched men she didn’t know break seals she had once guarded with her body. A steward appointed by Antony’s people—or by the house’s new legal fiction—spoke kindly while taking. Phileros hovered with the outer key. Dama watched from the chicken court as if birds were witnesses. Kindness that emptied chests still counted as kindness in the atrium’s mouth.

“These are the people’s now,” he said of a chest. “In a manner of speaking. You will find us careful. Careful is kindness in a house between masters.”

Nysa bowed the bow the atrium taught. “We are grateful for the care.”

Phileros laughed once, short—too short for the steward’s kindness—and then looked at the outer key as if it had changed religion. Drink made him loyal to whoever held that key. Dama claimed the chickens had gone silent out of respect. Nysa put chickens with *noise* and chests with *danger* and kept walking.

When the steward’s kindness had moved to the next chest, Phileros’s short laugh became corridor truth for Nysa and Dama alone. “People’s chest. People’s seals. People’s careful hands in our cupboards. Kindness that empties a house still empties it.”

Dama muttered that the chickens had better taste than the new authority. Nysa did not join the joke loud enough to travel; she let it land as a breath through the nose—enough that Phileros saw he’d been heard. She thought of her burned fragment and touched the empty place in her tunic where it had sat. She hid one blank tablet there out of habit, then put it back on the shelf—blank wax in the wrong hands had already become someone else’s line item. The incomplete spare/stay splinter was ash; the house’s new seals were not. Continuity of danger without the old wax.

Between chests she tried one small want: a cracked oil flask Calpurnia had once filled with her own hands—worth nothing to the people’s inventory, worth a morning to Nysa. She set it behind a water jar as if water could invent a private law. The steward’s clerk found it before the next prayer of inventory. “Careful includes corners,” he said, almost kind, and took the flask for the people’s pile. Nysa’s want failed without a speech. Phileros saw the take and did not laugh. Dama’s chickens made a sound like agreement.

Before dusk the steward sent her across the Sacra Via with a sealed receipt for Antony’s people—kindness that used her legs. The door she reached was not Domus; it smelled of different oil and men who borrowed authority. A clerk there took the seal without looking at her face, then offered, mild as a gift, that a careful girl might stay attached to the new inventory if she learned the new bow. Nysa gave the bow the atrium had taught and walked back while she still could. Attachment was a transfer dressed as safety. She kept her eyes on doors that no longer held her mornings.

# SC-66 — Congratulates, Then Hides

POV: F2 Seius · municipal dinner  
Date / place: Rome fringe · *Att.* 14.14 type  
Canon refs: F2 majority mode  
Info ENTER: Almost-knowledge guilt · EXIT: Private joy; public absence  
Overhear risk: Medium

---

Seius congratulated a Liberator’s cousin with wine-bright eyes and a spine already planning exit routes.

“Liberty,” he said, and meant *I almost stood near the fire and did not burn.*

The cousin clasped his arm too hard. Seius clasped back harder, then softer—etiquette as a vane. At home he told no one he had been sounded for recruitment in the baths weeks ago. He told Seia Prima the city was dangerous for honest men. She believed him because belief was cheaper than questions.

After the second course a neighbour—Quintus from the next block, a man who borrowed tools and returned gossip—leaned in with wine on his breath and a tablet half-hidden under his sleeve. “Sign,” Quintus said softly. “Open support for the Liberators. Three names already. Yours would make four. The cousin saw you clasp. He expects ink.”

Seia Prima’s eyes found Seius across the lamps. Expectation closed like a trap.

Seius felt the old bath-recruitment cold return to his wrists. A signature was a door you could not un-open when Antonian veterans walked the street. Refusal in front of Quintus was another door: the cousin would hear; the dinner circuit would adjust; someone would remember Seius as weather that would not commit.

“My stomach,” Seius began, and hated the cue even as he used it.

“Your stomach was fine for the toast,” Quintus said.

Seius set down his cup carefully, as if care were virtue. “I will not sign tonight. Ask me when the Forum has decided which knives are fashion.”

Quintus’s mouth flattened. “Then you are not with us. Say it plain for the cousin.”

“I am with my household,” Seius said. “That is as plain as I get.”

He left before dessert. Seia Prima followed without speaking until the street. “Quintus will tell them you flinched,” she said.

“Let him,” Seius said. “Flinching keeps the door on its hinges.”

By morning the petition had four names without his, and Quintus had already begun telling the block that Seius congratulated with wine and vanished with excuses. Seius cancelled the near dinners and accepted one far from anyone who could open another tablet under a sleeve. He had not become a hero. He had spent a neighbour’s goodwill to keep his name off a list that could kill him—and the block now had a story about him that was almost true.

On the way to the far dinner he didn’t rehearse congratulations. He counted which faces would still lend him a mule if the winds turned, and found the number smaller by one.
