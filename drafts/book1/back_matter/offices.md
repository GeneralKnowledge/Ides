# Offices and terms

A few words the characters use without explaining, because to them they need no explaining.

## Offices
- **Consul.** Two were elected each year, the highest regular office in the state, with the right to command armies. Romans named the year after its consuls.
- **Praetor.** The rank below consul: judges in the courts, and commanders when needed. In 44 BC there were sixteen. The **urban praetor** heard cases between citizens and ran the city when the consuls were away. The **praetor for foreigners** (*inter peregrinos*) heard cases involving non-citizens. That year Brutus held the first and Cassius the second.
- **Proconsul.** A former consul, or sometimes a former praetor, sent to govern a province, with command of the army there.
- **Tribune of the plebs.** Ten were elected each year. A tribune could propose laws and veto any magistrate, and it was sacrilege to lay a hand on one. A **military tribune** is something else: a senior officer of a legion.
- **Dictator.** An emergency office with power over all the others, held for six months in the old Republic. Caesar was made dictator for life in February 44.
- **Master of the Horse.** The dictator’s deputy. Antony held it in 48–47 BC, and Lepidus in 44.
- **Pontifex Maximus.** The chief priest of Rome. Caesar held it, and so lived in the Domus Publica on the Sacred Way.
- **Triumvirs.** From November 43, Antony, Lepidus and the young Caesar: by law the “three men for setting the state in order”, for five years, each with a consul’s power.

## Law and money
- **Adoption by will.** A Roman could name an heir and ask in the will that the heir take his name. Caesar left three quarters of his estate to his great-nephew Gaius Octavius and adopted him, so he became Gaius Julius Caesar. Historians call him Octavian; in the book most people call him the boy, or the young Caesar.
- **Heirs in the second degree.** Heirs who would inherit only if the first heirs could not or would not. Decimus Brutus was one of them.
- **Proscription.** A posted list of men declared outlaws. Anyone could kill them for a reward, and their property was sold at auction.
- **Freedman, freedwoman.** A freed slave took the family name of the former owner and still owed duties to that house. So Caesar’s freedwoman Nysa is, in law, Julia Nysa.
- **Denarius.** A silver coin worth four sesterces. Under Caesar a legionary was paid 225 denarii a year.
