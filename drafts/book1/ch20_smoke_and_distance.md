# SC-52 — Doors on Fire

POV: Decimus (with Brutus beat at end)  
Date / place: Elite streets → escape prep · post-funeral  
Canon refs: A9–A10 seeds · funeral fallout  
Info ENTER: Riot fallout; houses attacked  
Info EXIT: Gaul / East split begins; Decimus toward Cisalpine logic  
Overhear risk: Clients

---

Decimus smelled his own street before he saw the damage—pitch, cloth, the sweet rot of a smashed oil jar. A client met him with both hands empty and both eyes apologising.

“They came for the door, not even for you,” the client said. “The door was enough.”

Enough was the city’s new measure.

Decimus did not ask who “they” were. Veterans. Debtors. Men who had loved Caesar’s name and needed a wooden face to hit. He sent the household out by the back ways with the calm he had used on the Ides morning, and the calm felt like a borrowed cloak with blood on the hem.

Cisalpine Gaul was no longer a promotion. It was a wall with legions behind it—if he could reach it, if Antony’s hunger for provinces did not become a noose first. Decimus packed maps, not speeches.

Brutus found him in a courtyard that still had one unbroken pot of herbs.

“East,” Brutus said. Not a question.

“You and Cassius,” Decimus said. “I take the nearer knife. Someone has to hold a road in Italy.”

Brutus’ face was pale under soot. “They burned Helvius for a name.”

“They will burn worse for worse names.” Decimus clasped Brutus’ forearm once—friend, co-murderer, stranger. “Do not wait for the Forum to love you. It loved a corpse more.”

He left before philosophy answered.

# SC-53 — Across the Tiber

POV: Cleopatra  
Date / place: Transtiberim / Horti Caesaris working lodging (CLEO-1) · late March 44  
Canon refs: D10 · *Att.* 14.8 queen’s flight energy  
Info ENTER: Protector dead; Rome hostile weather  
Info EXIT: Quiet departure plan; Caesarion packing  
Overhear risk: Court medium

---

Cleopatra did not confuse Rome’s smoke with Alexandria’s. Different ash. Same lesson: a queen without a living Roman guarantor was a story other people finished.

She stood in the garden villa across the Tiber—Caesar’s green claim on the city’s edge—and watched servants pack what could be packed without looking like flight. Flight was what enemies called a scheduled departure. She preferred schedules.

Caesarion asked, in the frank cruelty of children, whether Father would come to the ship.

“Father belongs to their Forum now,” Cleopatra said, and hated the polish of it. Softness later. Ships first.

Ammonius—or a man who did Ammonius’ work—spoke of Cicero’s old rudeness, of books unpaid, of Romans who smiled with inventory in their eyes. Cleopatra filed him under useful irritant.

“We do not beg intermediaries,” she said. “We leave before intermediaries invent a price.”

At night she walked the dark paths once, memorising the smell of Italian wet earth, and thought of the golden statue among Venus’ things in the city—her face in Roman metal—and left it there. Statues were anchors. She needed sails.

# SC-54 — News Late

POV: Octavian  
Date / place: Apollonia area · days after 15 March  
Canon refs: A8 · hour map delayed track · HUMOR-1 off · not-Augustus  
Info ENTER: Unknown; studies / training weather  
Info EXIT: First rumour of Caesar’s death (incomplete); not full will yet  
Overhear risk: Companions (Agrippa orbit)

---

The sea near Apollonia made its ordinary noises. Octavian liked ordinary noises. They told you when the wind would let a ship leave.

He was with men his age and men older who still called him by the soft names of kinship when they forgot to be careful. Agrippa listened more than he spoke, which Octavian already understood as a kind of loyalty.

They had been going in to dinner when the man found them—a freedman with dust that did not match a calm road, and a letter’s weight in his manner even before the wax showed. Dust was the first sentence.

“Caesar,” the man said, and then the rest came broken: knives, Senate, Pompey’s place, Brutus, blood, the city upside down. A mother’s hand in the urgency—come home; she did not know what would happen next.

Octavian’s first feeling was not destiny. It was the childish, humiliating drop of the floor—the sense that the adult arrangement of the world had been a prop. His second feeling was colder: who benefits, who moves, who lies in the first report.

“Say it again,” he said. “Slower. Without the parts you wish were true.”

The courier slower-said a thinner story: Caesar dead; Liberators on a rock; Antony not clearly dead or alive in the first mouthful; no clean word of a will.

Agrippa’s jaw set. Someone cursed. Someone reached for Octavian’s shoulder as if touch could adopt him into safety.

Octavian did not shake the hand off. He also did not lean into it. He asked for ships, for letters, for which road still took a living messenger to Italy. His voice sounded stiff in his own ear—young. He hated that it sounded young.

“We go when going is not suicide,” he said. “First we learn what name I still have.”

He did not say *son*. The word sat unused behind his teeth—kinship soft, political poison until proven. He stood in Apollonia’s wind and practised breathing until breathing looked like command.

Behind his eyes, for one unguarded blink, he saw Caesar’s face as it had been in rarer kindnesses—and shut it away, because kindness was not a plan.
