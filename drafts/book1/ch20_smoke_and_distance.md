# SC-52 — Doors on Fire

POV: Decimus (with Brutus beat at end)  
Date / place: Elite streets → escape prep · post-funeral  
Canon refs: A9–A10 seeds · funeral fallout  
Info ENTER: Riot fallout; houses attacked  
Info EXIT: Gaul / East split begins; Decimus toward Cisalpine logic  
Overhear risk: Clients

---

Decimus smelled his own street before he saw the damage—pitch, cloth, the sweet rot of a smashed oil jar. Beyond the immediate wreck a wider smoke stood toward the Tiber, dirtying the March light as if the river’s green claims were burning on rumour alone. A client met him with both hands empty and both eyes apologising; a steward hovered with a half-burned inventory tablet; a door-slave still held a bucket as if water could renegotiate veterans. The ruin wanted to be a house and would not be one again tonight.

“They came for the door, not even for you,” the client said. “The door was enough.”

Enough was the city’s new measure. The steward added, careful as legality, that the second-line joke from the will had arrived in the street’s mouth before the pitch had—heir enough to hate, not enough to mourn.

Decimus didn’t ask who “they” were. Veterans. Debtors. Men who had loved Caesar’s name and needed a wooden face to hit—ink’s second degree made flesh. He sent the household out by the back ways with the same calm voice he had used on the Ides morning, then wiped pitch from his palm onto his cloak and found he couldn’t get the smell off.

Cisalpine Gaul was no longer a promotion. It was a wall with legions behind it—if he could reach it, if Antony’s hunger for provinces didn’t become a noose first. Decimus packed maps, not speeches.

Brutus found him in a courtyard that still had one unbroken pot of herbs.

“East,” Brutus said. Not a question.

“You and Cassius,” Decimus said. “I take the nearer knife. Someone has to hold a road in Italy.”

Brutus’ face was pale under soot. “They burned Helvius for a name. If you take Cisalpine now, you take Antony’s hunger with it. Come east with us first. Let the nearer knife wait until the Forum’s heat cools.”

“If I wait, the road fills with his veterans,” Decimus said. “You want me safe. I want a gate that still opens for our side. Those are not the same schedule.”

Brutus looked at the broken street as if it were a Senate. “Then do not ask me to bless the nearer knife as wisdom.”

“I am not asking for a blessing.” Decimus clasped Brutus’ forearm once—friend, co-murderer, stranger already arguing about timing. “Do not wait for the Forum to love you. It loved a corpse more.”

He left before philosophy answered—forearm clasp still warm, east already a different country. The question he took north was unfinished on purpose: whose gate would still open when the Forum’s heat found a road. Smoke over the Tiber did not answer. It only dirtied the light toward gardens he could not hold.

On the road north, while that smoke thinned behind him into other men’s problems, he sent two letters: one to Plancus, courteous, asking for a meeting that smelled like alliance and for twenty wagons of grain if the meeting could not be soon; one to a man who owed Lepidus a favour, asking only for a safe night’s roof near Mutina’s approaches. Plancus’s reply arrived soft—praise, delay, no date, and a careful line that grain “could not yet be spared from his own columns.” The Lepidus channel returned nothing but a messenger who wouldn’t meet his eyes and wouldn’t take a second tablet back. Decimus folded both silences into the map-case. Nearer knives, he told himself, were still knives you could see. Unseen friends were already pricing what they wouldn’t send. The will’s second line traveled with him like pitch he could not wipe: loved enough to ink, hated enough to burn.

# SC-53 — Across the Tiber

POV: Cleopatra  
Date / place: Transtiberim / Horti Caesaris working lodging (CLEO-1) · late March 44  
Canon refs: D10 · *Att.* 14.8 queen’s flight energy  
Info ENTER: Protector dead; Rome hostile weather  
Info EXIT: Quiet departure plan; Caesarion packing  
Overhear risk: Court medium

---

Cleopatra didn’t confuse Rome’s smoke with Alexandria’s. Different ash—same dirty March light standing over the Tiber that elite streets were breathing as pitch. Same lesson: a queen without a living Roman guarantor was a story other people finished—and Romans had been finishing her story at dinner long before the knives.

She stood in the garden villa across the Tiber—Caesar’s green claim on the city’s edge, the same gardens bankers had priced as collateral and loungers had joked into wills—and watched servants pack what could be packed without looking like flight. Flight was what enemies called a scheduled departure. She preferred schedules. Schedules were harder to mark up than rumour. The smoke did not ask which side of the river owned it.

A Roman clerk from the house’s new legal weather arrived mid-morning with a polite face and an impolite list. Crates for the river would be “inspected for the people’s interest.” He smiled with inventory in his eyes—the smile she had already met, secondhand, in every Transtiberim joke about queens, children, and half-hours on roads.

“The gardens are being talked as the people’s,” the clerk said. “Best not to look as if you are taking the people’s trees with you.”

“I am taking my household’s linen,” Cleopatra said. “Trees stay. So do statues.”

The clerk lingered on a chest that held nothing but cloth and a child’s spare sandals. Caesarion’s. The inspection became a price without naming one. Ammonius—or a man who did Ammonius’ work—started toward an argument; Cleopatra stopped him with two fingers. Arguing fed the smile.

“Open it,” she said. “Count the sandals. Then leave my schedule alone.”

He counted. He bowed as if the count had been a courtesy exchanged between equals. “The people’s interest is satisfied. Favourable departure.” At the garden’s first turn he stopped long enough for the porter who had let him in: “She counts sandals and calls it a schedule. Rome will finish her story whether the trees stay or not—mark me when the ship wants a second price.”

Ammonius’s jaw worked. Cleopatra paid the hour, not the petition. The porter’s news reached her as wind under the leaves; she did not turn back to answer wind.

Caesarion asked, in the frank cruelty of children, whether Father would come to the ship.

“Father belongs to their Forum now,” Cleopatra said, and hated the polish of it. Softness later. Ships first. The boy’s hours—the ones Romans had gossiped into careers—were now only packing hours, and she intended them to end on water.

Ammonius spoke of Cicero’s old rudeness, of books unpaid, of men who had already decided whether she was a clause or a silence in the dead man’s paper. Cleopatra kept him as useful irritant.

“We do not beg intermediaries,” she said. “We leave before intermediaries invent a second price.”

Ammonius did not stop. He produced a smaller tablet—Cicero’s old account, books still unpaid, rudeness still priced as if a queen’s library were a Roman joke that owed interest. “If we settle this before the Tiber, one mouth in Italy softens. Soft mouths buy fair wind.”

“Soft mouths invent second inspections,” Cleopatra said. “Pay the books from Alexandria if you must. Do not buy Cicero’s courtesy with my schedule. Intermediaries who need my coin to be polite will invent a second price on water.”

Ammonius’s jaw worked again—useful, expensive, already half a sail’s delay. She paid him silence instead of the tablet. The unfinished sail toward open water carried that refusal as ballast—courtesy she would not buy on Italian mud.

At night she walked the dark paths once, memorising the smell of Italian wet earth, and thought of the golden statue among Venus’ things in the city—her face in Roman metal—and left it there. Statues were anchors. She needed sails. The gardens could become the people’s walk; the dinners could keep pricing her child; none of that was her exit. Exit was a schedule that survived inspection—and the unfinished question she took to water was whose mouth in Italy would finish her story first: clerks, Liberators on a rock, or a boy whose name had not yet arrived with a seal.

# SC-54 — News Late

POV: Octavian  
Date / place: Apollonia area · days after 15 March  
Canon refs: A8 · hour map delayed track · HUMOR-1 off · not-Augustus  
Info ENTER: Unknown; studies / training weather  
Info EXIT: First rumour of Caesar’s death (incomplete); not full will yet  
Overhear risk: Companions (Agrippa orbit)

---

The sea near Apollonia made its ordinary noises. Octavian liked ordinary noises. They told you when the wind would let a ship leave.

He was with men his age and men older who still called him by the soft names of kinship when they forgot to be careful. Agrippa listened more than he spoke. Octavian had stopped asking him to fill silences.

They had been going in to dinner when the man found them—a freedman with dust that didn’t match a calm road, and a letter’s weight in his manner even before the wax showed. Agrippa on one side, Maecenas’s quieter sort of attention on the other—companions enough that bad news had to choose a face.

“Caesar,” the man said, and then the rest came broken: knives, Senate, Pompey’s place, Brutus, blood, the city upside down. A mother’s hand in the urgency—come home; she didn’t know what would happen next. Smoke in the report, too—houses, Forum heat—late, incomplete, the same dirty weather Italy was already breathing.

Octavian felt the floor drop—the childish, humiliating sense that the adult arrangement of the world had been a prop. Then the colder part: who benefits, who moves, who lies in the first report.

“Names first,” he said. “Then places. Separate what you saw from what the road invented.”

The courier tried again with a thinner story: Caesar dead; Liberators on a rock; Antony not clearly dead or alive in the first mouthful; no clean word of a will—only gardens and a boy muttered like prices without a seal. Smoke in Italy; a queen’s schedule unfinished in the same breath as the Forum’s heat—road invention or not, the unfinished questions had already boarded the same weather.

Agrippa’s jaw set. Someone cursed. Someone reached for Octavian’s shoulder as if touch could adopt him into safety. Octavian stood inside the silence, the curse, and the hand without choosing comfort.

Octavian didn’t shake the hand off. He also didn’t lean into it. He asked for ships, for letters, for which road still took a living messenger to Italy. His voice sounded stiff in his own ear—young. He hated that it sounded young. He did not reach for the old nickname anyone might offer as softness; hopeful shadows were for men who still thought inventory was a joke.

“We go when going is not suicide,” he said. “First we learn what name I still have—and whether gardens and a boy’s hours are already writing it without us.”

He didn’t say *son*. The word sat unused behind his teeth—kinship soft, political poison until proven. He stood in Apollonia’s wind and practised breathing until breathing looked like command.

Behind his eyes, for one unguarded blink, he saw Caesar’s face as it had been in rarer kindnesses—and shut it away, because kindness wasn’t a plan. The sealed tablet pressed on Caesar in the litter remained unread among other papers when the body was handled; a household fragment burned elsewhere was never that tablet. Dual tracks stood. Names first.
