# SC-52: Doors on Fire

POV: Decimus (with Brutus beat at end)
Date / place: Elite streets → escape prep · post-funeral
Canon refs: A9–A10 seeds · funeral fallout
Info ENTER: Riot fallout; houses attacked
Info EXIT: Gaul / East split begins; Decimus toward Cisalpine logic
Overhear risk: Clients

---

Decimus smelled his own street before he saw the damage: pitch, cloth, the sweet rot of a smashed oil jar. Beyond the immediate wreck a wider smoke stood toward the Tiber and dirtied the March light. Rumour alone could make the river’s green claims look like they were burning. A client met him with both hands empty and both eyes apologising. A steward hovered with a half-burned inventory tablet. A door-slave still held a bucket. Water would not renegotiate veterans. The ruin wanted to be a house and would not be one again tonight.

“They came for the door, not even for you,” the client said. “The door was enough.”

“Is there a door left?” said Decimus.

“There is a doorway, domine. It has an excellent view of the street.”

The steward added, careful as legality, that the second-line joke from the will had arrived in the street’s mouth before the pitch had: heir enough to hate, not enough to mourn. The crowd, which could not follow a sentence about inheritance, could follow a betrayal.

A neighbour’s freedman, helping shift a scorched beam, offered the morning’s other merchandise without being asked. Calpurnia had dreamed the house into her lap, and Decimus had called the dream courage so the knives could keep their schedule. Or the gods had warned and Decimus had mocked the warning. Or there had been no dream, only a story Liberators needed after the Forum chose fire. Three versions. One pitch smell. Decimus filed them without crowning one. Crowning would have made him the dream’s author.

Decimus didn’t ask who “they” were. Veterans. Debtors. Men who had loved Caesar’s name and needed a wooden face to hit. He sent the household out by the back ways with the same calm voice he had used on the Ides morning, then wiped pitch from his palm onto his cloak and found he couldn’t get the smell off.

Decimus packed maps, not speeches. The top one was Cisalpine Gaul, the province Caesar had given him, and it no longer looked like a promotion. It was a wall with legions behind it, if he could reach it before Antony’s hunger for provinces became a noose. Antony had his own eye on those legions, and the Senate, which handed out provinces like favours, was already being asked to hand them elsewhere.

Brutus found him in a courtyard that still had one unbroken pot of herbs.

“East,” Brutus said. Not a question.

“You and Cassius,” Decimus said. “How in Hades do two men stab the giver and keep the gift? Macedonia and Syria, still yours on his paper. Go and sit in them. That is where the legions are, and as far from the Forum as a man can sit. I take the nearer knife. Someone has to hold a road in Italy.”

Brutus’s face was pale under soot. “They burned Helvius for a name. If you take Cisalpine now, you take Antony’s hunger with it. Come east with us first. Let the nearer knife wait until the Forum’s heat cools.”

“If I wait, the road fills with his veterans,” Decimus said. “You want me safe. I want a gate that still opens for our side. Those are not the same schedule.”

“You have never once been on the same schedule as me.”

“I have been on yours since the knives,” said Decimus. “Look where it left my house.”

Brutus looked at the broken street. “Then do not ask me to bless the nearer knife as wisdom.”

“I am not asking for a blessing.” Decimus clasped Brutus’s forearm once: friend, co-murderer, stranger already arguing about timing. “Do not wait for the Forum to love you. It loved a corpse more.”

He left before philosophy answered, forearm clasp still warm. East was already a different country. The question he took north was unfinished on purpose: whose gate would still open when the Forum’s heat found a road. Smoke over the Tiber did not answer. It only dirtied the light toward gardens he could not hold.

On the road north, while that smoke thinned behind him into other men’s problems, he sent two letters. One went to Plancus across the Alps, whose letters were always very good and whose legions were always very close to home: courteous, asking for a meeting that smelled like alliance and for twenty wagons of grain if the meeting could not be soon. One went to a man who owed Lepidus a favour, asking only for a safe night’s roof near Mutina’s approaches. Mutina, on the Via Aemilia, was the fortress he had built in his head a year ago, ditches and all. He counted the twenty in his head the way he counted remounts before rain: numbers that kept men fed. Plancus’s reply arrived soft: praise, delay, no date, and a careful line that grain “could not yet be spared from his own columns.” Decimus read *yet* twice, then looked at the empty stretch of road where wagons should have been and were not. The Lepidus channel returned a messenger who wouldn’t meet his eyes, wouldn’t dismount long enough to take a second tablet back. Decimus held the first letter like a receipt with no goods attached. He folded both silences into the map-case hard enough that the wax edge bit his thumb. Nearer knives, he told himself, were still knives you could see. Friends who would not send grain or a roof had already begun the road that ended without wagons. The will’s second line travelled with him like pitch he could not wipe.

# SC-53: Across the Tiber

POV: Cleopatra
Date / place: Transtiberim / Horti Caesaris working lodging (CLEO-1) · late March 44
Canon refs: D10 · *Att.* 14.8 queen’s flight energy
Info ENTER: Protector dead; Rome hostile weather
Info EXIT: Quiet departure plan; Caesarion packing
Overhear risk: Court medium

---

Cleopatra didn’t confuse Rome’s smoke with Alexandria’s. Different ash, same dirty March light standing over the Tiber that elite streets were breathing as pitch. Same lesson: a queen without a living Roman guarantor was a story other people finished. Romans had been finishing hers at dinner long before the knives.

She stood in the garden villa across the Tiber, Caesar’s green claim on the city’s edge, the same gardens bankers had priced as collateral and loungers had joked into wills. She watched servants pack what could be packed without looking like flight. Flight was what enemies called a scheduled departure. She preferred a departure with an hour attached. The smoke did not ask which side of the river owned it.

A Roman clerk from the house’s new legal weather arrived mid-morning with a polite face and an impolite list. Crates for the river would be “inspected for the people’s interest.” He smiled with inventory in his eyes, the smile she had already met secondhand in every Transtiberim joke about queens, children, and half-hours on roads.

“The gardens are being talked as the people’s,” the clerk said. “Best not to look as if you are taking the people’s trees with you.”

“I am taking my household’s linen,” Cleopatra said. “Trees stay. So do statues.”

“No one has asked about the statues, majesty.”

“Then you may be the first to be disappointed.”

The clerk lingered on a chest that held nothing but cloth and a child’s spare sandals. Caesarion’s. Egypt called the boy Caesar’s heir; Rome had decided to call him nothing at all. The inspection became a price without naming one. Ammonius, who ran her household and spoke for her in the Roman salons, started toward an argument. Cleopatra stopped him with two fingers. Arguing fed the smile.

“Open it,” she said. “Count the sandals. Then leave my schedule alone.”

He counted. He bowed as if the count had been a courtesy between equals. “The people’s interest is satisfied. Favourable departure.” At the garden’s first turn he stopped long enough for the porter who had let him in: “She counts sandals and calls it a schedule. Rome will finish her story whether the trees stay or not.”

Ammonius’s jaw worked. Cleopatra paid the hour, not the petition. The porter’s news reached her as wind under the leaves. She did not turn back to answer wind.

Caesarion asked, in the frank cruelty of children, whether Father would come to the ship.

“Father belongs to their Forum now,” Cleopatra said. Softness later. Ships first. The boy’s hours, the ones Romans had gossiped into careers, were now only packing hours, and she intended them to end on water.

Ammonius spoke of Cicero’s old rudeness, of books promised from the Alexandrian library and never sent, of what Cicero had said about her in Rome since, of men who had already decided whether she was a clause or a silence in the dead man’s paper. Cleopatra kept Ammonius as useful irritant.

“We do not beg intermediaries,” she said. “We leave before they invent another inspection.”

Ammonius did not stop. He produced a smaller tablet, Cicero’s old account: books still unpaid, rudeness still priced like a queen’s library owed interest. “If we settle this before the Tiber, one mouth in Italy softens. Soft mouths buy fair wind.”

“Pay the books from Alexandria if you must,” Cleopatra said. “Do not buy Cicero’s courtesy with my schedule. Soft mouths invent second prices on water.”

“He is a very good writer, majesty.”

“He is a very good writer who thinks I am a bad debt. I would rather be a bad debt than a footnote.”

Ammonius’s jaw worked again: useful, expensive, already half a sail’s delay. She paid him silence instead of the tablet. The unfinished sail toward open water carried that refusal as ballast.

At night she walked the dark paths once, memorising the smell of Italian wet earth, and thought of the golden statue in Venus’ temple, her face in Roman metal beside the mother of the Julii, where Caesar had set it and the whole city had walked past and said nothing. She left it there. Statues were anchors. She needed sails. The gardens could become the people’s walk. The dinners could keep pricing her child. None of that was her exit. Exit was a schedule that survived inspection.

Once she was on the water, someone in Italy would finish her story for her. Clerks. Liberators on a rock. Or a boy whose name had not yet arrived with a seal.

# SC-54: News Late

POV: Octavian
Date / place: Apollonia area · days after 15 March
Canon refs: A8 · hour map delayed track · HUMOR-1 off · not-Augustus
Info ENTER: Unknown; studies / training weather
Info EXIT: First rumour of Caesar’s death (incomplete); not full will yet
Overhear risk: Companions (Agrippa orbit)

---

The sea near Apollonia made its ordinary noises. Octavian liked ordinary noises. They told you when the wind would let a ship leave.

He was with men his age and men older who still called him by the soft names of kinship when they forgot to be careful. Agrippa listened more than he spoke. Octavian had stopped asking him to fill silences.

They had been going in to dinner when the man found them: a freedman with dust that didn’t match a calm road, and a letter’s weight in his manner even before the wax showed. Agrippa on one side; on the other Maecenas, a few years older, Etruscan money and too many books, still only the clever man who happened to be at the table. Companions enough that bad news had to choose a face.

“Caesar,” the man said, and then the rest came broken. Knives. Senate. Pompey’s place. Brutus. Blood. The city upside down. A mother’s hand was in the urgency. Come home. She didn’t know what would happen next. Smoke in the report, too. Houses. Forum heat. Late, incomplete, the same dirty weather Italy was already breathing.

Octavian felt the floor drop, the childish, humiliating sense that the adult arrangement of the world had been a prop. Then the colder part: who benefits, who moves, who lies in the first report.

“Names first,” he said. “Then places. Separate what you saw from what the road invented.”

“He is eighteen,” Maecenas murmured to Agrippa. “He sounds forty.”

“He sounds forty,” said Agrippa. “He is going to be forty by spring.”

The courier tried again with a thinner story. Caesar dead. Liberators on a rock. Antony not clearly dead or alive in the first mouthful. No clean word of a will, only gardens and a boy muttered like prices without a seal. Smoke in Italy. A queen’s schedule unfinished in the same breath as the Forum’s heat. Road invention or not, Octavian thought, all of it was already on the same wind.

Agrippa’s jaw set. Someone cursed. Someone reached for Octavian’s shoulder as if touch could adopt him into safety. Octavian stood inside the silence, the curse, and the hand without choosing comfort.

Octavian didn’t shake the hand off. He also didn’t lean into it. He asked for ships, for letters, for which road still took a living messenger to Italy. His voice sounded stiff in his own ear, young. He hated that it sounded young. He did not reach for the old nickname anyone might offer as softness. Hopeful shadows were for men who still thought inventory was a joke.

“We go when going is not suicide,” he said. “First we learn what name I still have, and whether gardens and a boy’s hours are already writing it without us.”

He didn’t say *son*. The word sat unused behind his teeth: kinship soft, political poison until proven. He stood in Apollonia’s wind and practised breathing until breathing looked like command.

Behind his eyes, for one unguarded blink, he saw Caesar’s face as it had been in rarer kindnesses, and shut it away. Kindness wasn’t a plan. Names first.
