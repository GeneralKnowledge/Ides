---

Hordeonius walked fields that had been other men’s names.

Boundary stones still remembered older owners; his stylus had overwritten them in town with the neatness he once reserved for honest Kalends. Birds argued over seed that was no longer anyone’s virtue. The old Visellius tablets resurfaced in a crate—ash-smudged, worthless, archaeology as promised. SIDE-1b stayed OPEN-0: no courtroom, no crown, only ash under civil war’s larger corpses.

He almost prayed over the tablets. Instead he stacked them for kindling and whispered, “Sacred once,” like a man consoling a cult statue that had failed to keep anyone alive. Sosia asked if the yields would clear. Hordeonius said yields were not the problem. Sleep was. Interest, for the first time, could not purchase it.

He told the empty lane he had preserved value—solvent men, sacred seals, a Republic that still needed warehouses. The lane did not absolve. Owls did not either. A hired man asked whether to pull down a boundary shrine that named the old owner. Hordeonius said leave it. Leaving it was not mercy. Leaving it was how you slept slightly less badly while still owning the dirt.

He walked home counting paces like interest and found that counting no longer calmed him. In the city, someone would call this winning. Hordeonius called it a ledger that had learned how to bite.

# SC-90 — Dust

### Antony

Antony liked the plain’s honesty: kill or be rearranged. Marshes and hills broke sightlines; he liked that too, when it broke the other man’s map. He drove his wing with the old joy that was not joy—competence in blood. Dust coated his teeth. He spat and pointed and the pointing became men moving. Omens were for the tents; on the field he trusted weight and timing. Someone shouted Cassius’ name like weather. Antony did not pause to translate weather into truth. Weather could wait until the counting.

### F3 — Laberius

Laberius saw a signal, or thought he did—flags in dust, an eagle dipping toward a ditch that might have been a marsh yesterday. Vibius yelled that Cassius’ side was broken. Someone else yelled the opposite with equal faith and a different god’s name attached. Laberius believed the yell nearest his ear. That was how plains lied. He kept his century’s shield-line from becoming a rumour of its own and tasted metal that might have been blood or only fear.

A javelin stuck in dirt near his foot like a period. He left it. Taking trophies mid-fight was how men died decorated.

Pay would come later, if later existed—farms promised by dead men and living boys. For now the eagle was the only theology that fit in a hand. He did not know Cassius’ hill. He knew dust, thirst, and the next order that might be wrong. Wrong orders, obeyed together, were still a kind of life.

# SC-91 — Mistaken Loss

POV: Cassius · his hill/camp · M18 information kills  
Date / place: Hill/camp · few witnesses  
Canon refs: M18 · A6  
Info ENTER: Incomplete report · EXIT: Suicide on false belief  
Overhear risk: Few

---

The messenger’s mouth was wrong before the words were.

Dust made the man’s tongue thick; fear made it thicker; distance made the map in Cassius’ head hungry for confirmation. Cassius heard *lost* in a version that matched his worst sketch of the day. Brutus’ success, if it existed beyond the marsh and the lying air, arrived late or not at all in that throat. A second voice tried to amend the first and only added fog.

Cassius, who had trusted numbers, trusted a broken number because broken numbers still looked like numbers if you needed them enough to hate delay. He asked one clarifying question. The answer came incomplete—names missing, directions soft, a standard that might have fallen or only dipped.

Incomplete had always been his enemy in other men’s mouths. Now it lived in his own ears. He looked once toward the dust where Brutus might still be winning and saw only weather. Weather was not a number he could punish.

He made the end himself rather than wait for the plain to invent a worse one. Incomplete information killed him as surely as any blade—his old contempt for delay turned inward and found no exit but steel. The men nearest him would argue later about the exact words. Exact words were another fog.

On another part of the field the day was not finished. Messengers were still lying in good faith. Cassius was finished. Bad news, believed, had been enough.
