# SC-28 — Lesser Nightmares

POV: Calpurnia · Domus Publica cubiculum  
Date / place: Nights approaching mid-March · HOUSE-1  
Canon refs: DREAM-1 approach · E3 prep  
Info ENTER: Anxiety · EXIT: Toward DREAM-1 night  
Overhear risk: Nysa possible

---

Calpurnia had begun to dread sleep the way other women dreaded childbirth—necessary, costly, not optional.

The lesser nightmares did not yet collapse the pediment. They were smaller thefts: Caesar walking into a room that had no floor; a sacrifice animal that stood up after the knife; doors that opened on their own and showed only March cold. She woke with her hands already shaped around his name and found him—alive—sorting tablets as if wax could outrun dread. Once she woke and counted his breaths until the counting itself became a superstition; once she woke and found her own nails cut crescents into her palm. The Domus Publica’s night noises—latches, a dog, a courier’s wrong hour—had started arranging themselves into omens whether she invited them or not.

Pallas dressed her in the mornings with the quiet of someone who had learned not to ask. Nysa brought water and didn't meet her eyes too long. In the dressing-room the three of them made a temporary country: mistress, dresser, water-carrier—no Senate, only cloth and the question nobody said aloud. Pallas’s fingers paused at a pin. Nysa’s jug paused at the lip of the basin. Calpurnia caught both pauses the way she caught March cold through a curtain.

“You may speak in this room,” Calpurnia said, not looking at either face. “Not in the atrium. The atrium invents audiences.”

Pallas chose the pin with unnecessary care. “The kitchen says Spurinna’s *beware* is being sold twice—once as piety, once as sport. The washerwomen have started counting which doors Decimus uses as if doors were weather.”

Nysa set the jug down softer than water required. “The corridor says cheerful visitors. Cheerful men always mean schedules.”

Calpurnia let both sentences sit. She did not thank them. Thanks made servants into prophets, and prophets got punished when mornings stayed ordinary. “Dress me for a city that still pretends mornings are safe,” she said. “Then go back to looking like furniture.”

They did. After the door-latch, in the service slip beside the cubiculum, Pallas’s voice dropped its station softness. “She knows we know. That makes us dangerous and useful in the same breath.” Nysa did not answer with comfort. Comfort was a lie with good manners. She carried the empty jug toward the court and kept *schedules* and *doors* in different pockets of her mind.

Later Caesar found Calpurnia at the tablinum edge. “You are not usually like this,” he said once, almost mildly, and she hated how often mildness was his kindness.

“The house listens,” she said. “So do I.”

She didn't have Senate words. She had body words—the ache behind the eyes, the taste of metal before dawn, the Domus Publica’s public smile on the Sacra Via looking like a painted lie about safety. She stopped pretending the dread was private. Privacy was a luxury the pontifex maximus’s wife didn't keep when the city hung whispers on their bed-curtains. Spurinna’s professional caution, Decimus’s open hand, the staff’s sudden piety: she stacked them beside the lesser nightmares and found the stack heavier than any one omen.

# SC-29 — Spare the Consul

POV: F1 Nysa · Domus Publica corridor / door  
Date / place: Daytime, days before Ides · peristyle edge  
Canon refs: NOTE-1 prep · hour map  
Info ENTER: Partial conspiracy noise from visitor · EXIT: Holds dangerous half-truth  
Overhear risk: She *is* the overhear

---

Nysa was resetting a latch when the visitor’s attendant muttered.

The visitor himself—someone Decimus-shaped, or Trebonius-shaped; she never got the face clean—was in the atrium speaking cheerfully about schedules. Cheerful men were furniture in this house. He praised the household’s order loud enough for any door-slave to sell later as loyalty. The attendant, waiting with a cloak, answered with the smile attendants wore when masters performed friendship—then, to another cloak as if stone could not hear:

“—not the consul—spare—no—friends first—danger—if he stays—”

The words arrived wrong-ended, emphasis on *stays* or *spare* or *friends*—she couldn't tell which noun owned the verb. The second cloak hissed that Monday talk was getting expensive. Phileros barked for mats. The attendant shut his mouth so hard Nysa heard teeth, then—once the visitor’s cheerful back had turned toward the tablinum—muttered to the cloak-friend, exit-soft and venom-clear: “Spare, stay, friends—pick a noun before the house does. I’m tired of smiling at schedules that smell like knives.”

She walked on with empty hands and a full head. In the side room she pressed what she had into wax with a stylus that shook once and then obeyed: *friends* · *spare?* · *stay / danger* · *consul?* Incomplete. Ugly. Misfiled even as she wrote it—she put *friends* largest because the voice had bitten that word, and *consul* small because it might have been the thing spared or the thing ignored. Hers either way.

She wrapped the fragment in linen and set it against her ribs. She didn't understand Senate shapes. She understood that half-truths killed slower than whole ones only if you never delivered them to the wrong door—and that she might already have the wrong half.

She had almost made the linen disappear under her tunic when Phileros turned the corner with a basket of mats and the look of a man hunting unfinished work.

“What are you holding against your ribs?” he asked, not kindly.

“A strap-end,” Nysa said, and opened her empty hand before the linen could speak. The fragment stayed pressed higher, under the fold, a hard grain against bone. Phileros stared at the empty palm, then at the latch she had been resetting, then grunted as if latches were the only crime worth pricing.

“Mats,” he said. “Before the atrium invents another omen.”

She went. The near-catch sat hotter than the muttered words. She had meant to leave the splinter somewhere safer before night—under a loose tile, behind a water jar—and Phileros’s basket had stolen the minute. Delayed handoff; hunted feeling. In the court she passed Pallas again—two servants, one dread—and neither named Calpurnia’s morning pauses. Naming made pauses into evidence. Medullina, if Nysa saw her in the Subura later, would want a trade. Nysa would give birds and chickens. Not this. The fragment was still only a splinter—mercy or trap depending on which word you stressed—and it hadn't yet grown legs as street rumour. It had only almost become a door-search. The attendant’s muttered truth stuck beside it: schedules that smelled like knives. Cheer in the atrium; truth in the cloak-space. The house already knew that engine.

# SC-30 — Weather

POV: Caesar (sparse) · Domus Publica tablinum  
Date / place: Same days · Parthia logistics under lamp  
Canon refs: M1 · AMB-1 · Spurinna joke flex  
Info ENTER: Parthia logistics · EXIT: Aware of danger-as-weather; not ops list  
Overhear risk: Medium

---

Caesar didn't keep an enemies list. He kept work.

He kept ships, grain, maps, the stubborn arithmetic of an eastern war, and the smaller arithmetic of which men still laughed when he cut a joke. Calpurnia’s hands, the staff’s sudden piety, Spurinna’s professional *beware*—he treated the fear in the house like March weather: real, inconvenient, not a reason to cancel the city’s work.

Decimus came and went with the ease of a man who had earned doors. Caesar trusted that ease the way he trusted a bridge he had watched built. Trust was habit. Habit had won Gaul more often than omens had.

“They say the Ides are rude,” Caesar remarked once, dry, when a secretary mentioned Spurinna.

The secretary did not know whether to smile. Caesar let him escape into tablets.

In the tablinum stack sat unfinished business and the eternal temptation to believe unfinished business was immortality. He didn't feel a plot as a plot. He felt the city's nerves. Cities got nerves before campaigns. He'd lived through nerves before.

If someone had handed him a clean list of knives, he might have read it. Nobody did. Weather does not arrive as a list.
