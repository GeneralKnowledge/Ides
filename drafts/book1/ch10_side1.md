# SC-24: Almost

POV: F2 Seius · baths side-room
Date / place: Early Feb 44 · quiet room off the palaestra
Canon refs: F2 · Plut. Favonius/Statilius parallel
Info ENTER: Grievance ready · EXIT: Flinches; knows *something* hangs
Overhear risk: Medium

---

Marcus Seius Vestalis had come to the baths with a grievance polished like a shoe.

Kingship talk at dinners. Offices promised years ahead while men like him still counted aedileship like a ladder with missing rungs. Seia Prima had said that morning, sweet as vinegar, that a husband who only admired liberty would die admired and poor.

In the side-room the air was wet wool and oil. L. Minucius Basilus’s voice (or a man who spoke Basilus’s careful pitch) kept soft: not knives, never knives, only *friends who refuse to watch Rome become a household*.

“You have been wronged by the calendar,” the man said. “So have others. When the moment comes, men of standing will be remembered.”

Seius’s heart performed a small treason of excitement. He saw himself named later in a clean story. Then he saw Antony’s face in some remembered dinner, and Cassius’s dryness, and his own body’s talent for sweating through tunic wool.

“What moment?” Seius asked, and hated that his voice cracked like a boy’s.

“You will know if you are meant to know,” the man said. “For now, listen harder. Speak less. If you cannot bear weight, do not pick it up.”

A laugh burst from the main hall, someone’s joke about a wrestler. Seius laughed too late, then too loud. The recruiter’s eyes cooled.

“I am with the Republic,” Seius said, which was what men said when they meant *I am with my skin*.

“Of course,” the man answered, already done with him.

Seius left with oil still on his shoulders and a knowledge like a splinter: something hung in the city, and he had flinched at the height where hanging became holding. He congratulated himself for prudence all the way toward the street.

On the oil benches a client of his (soft-eyed, useful at dinners) pretended to adjust a strap and spoke without looking up. “Soft rooms are for men who finish sentences, Marcus. You left mid-breath. The palaestra notices who gets invited into quiet and who exits empty.”

Seius smiled the smile of a man who had paid for privacy and bought a notice instead. By the time he reached home Seia Prima had already heard, from someone who sold oil and opinions, that her husband had been tapped for a quiet hour and come out without a name. She did not ask which room. She only said, sweet as vinegar again, that prudence was expensive when it looked like flinching in public.

Prudence tasted like relief and cowardice mixed, and he couldn't tell which spice was stronger. The city, he noted, was catching a cold: doors tapped after soft hours, and men who flinched paid in gossip before anyone admitted knives.

# SC-25: Soften the Debt

POV: F4 Hordeonius · taberna near Janus medius
Date / place: Same week · afternoon ledgers
Canon refs: SIDE-1 overlap · Basilus orbit · TONE-1
Info ENTER: Normal ledgers · EXIT: Asked to ease Basilus-orbit debt for “a friend of liberty”
Overhear risk: Low–Medium

---

L. Hordeonius Felix preferred numbers that arrived on the Kalends.

A. Visellius Ralla’s tablet sat among the honest ones, interest tidy, excuses few. Hordeonius respected a freedman’s son who paid. Respect, in his religion, was a rate.

L. Statius Paullus arrived smelling of someone else’s perfume and spoke like a man borrowing virtue. “There is a friend of liberty,” Paullus said, “who would take it kindly if a certain Basilus-adjacent obligation breathed easier for a month. Soften the debt. Consider it piety toward the Republic.”

Hordeonius didn't laugh. Bankers who laughed at piety ended as tavern stories. He slid Visellius’s tablet an inch closer to himself (possession as theology) and let Paullus watch the wax refuse to travel.

“I am a friend to the Republic in the only way that matters,” he said, arranging his face into temple seriousness. “I keep its ledgers honest. Softness is how cities drown. Who guarantees the softness?”

Paullus named no guarantor worth the wax. He named atmosphere. Atmosphere did not seal. He reached as if to touch the tablet’s rim; Sosia’s hand was already on the stool-leg, ready to pull it. Hordeonius closed the tablet with a soft click that sounded like a door in a better house.

“Seals are older than liberty,” he continued, gentle, as if instructing a nephew who had tracked mud onto marble. “Seals will be here when liberty is a word sold by the jug. Bring me a name that can bleed money if the month goes wrong. Otherwise Visellius pays as written, and Basilus (if Basilus is in this bargain) pays as written too.”

Paullus left offended in the expensive way. Hordeonius blotted nothing. He only entered *inquiry, political softness, refuse* in a hand so neat it looked like virtue, then checked his ring as if the refuse might have dirtied him.

Sosia, his collector, muttered that philosophers were bad for cash flow. Hordeonius agreed solemnly, which made Sosia look at him as if he had told a joke. He hadn't. That was the trouble with truth: it sometimes wore humour's clothes without asking.

When the perfume had cleared, Sosia jerked a chin at the empty stool. “So if someone softens Basilus for liberty…”

“Then bring me a name that bleeds,” Hordeonius said, already blotting. “Otherwise Visellius pays as written.” He entered *soft hours, storm weather* without looking up. A consul walking free of a bad morning was the kind of morning he would not underwrite without an invoice. “I sell umbrellas. I do not join the rain.”

# SC-25b: Body

POV: F4 Hordeonius · Subura-edge insula
Date / place: Two nights later · stair-stink, cheap lamp
Canon refs: SIDE-1 · OPEN-0 · D5 rhyme seed
Info ENTER: Client late on interest · EXIT: Body; tablets name Basilus orbit + Antonian agent
Overhear risk: High (neighbours → rumour)

---

Visellius didn't arrive on the Kalends.

Hordeonius went himself, because a banker who only sent Sosia learned too late when a ledger had become a corpse. The room smelled of cheap oil, old wine, and the metal tang men pretend is only coins.

Visellius Ralla was on the floor with his throat opened by someone who hadn't cared about neatness. Hordeonius washed his hands in a basin that wasn't clean enough and still washed them.

The tablets weren't hidden. That was either stupidity or theatre. One line of marks pulled toward Minucius Basilus’s orbit, Paullus’s perfume talk made suddenly concrete. Another mark, different hand, tugged toward C. Septimius Bucco, an Antonian cash-rat Hordeonius knew from uglier markets.

Neighbours already filled the stair with theories. Hordeonius filed them without choosing one. Seals were his trade, and a seal had been broken with a knife.

“He was,” Hordeonius told the silent mess, “a credit to his father’s manumission. Paid on the Kalends. Do you know how rare that is in this city of philosophers?”

Nobody answered. The dead never did. That was why ledgers needed the living.

He took copies, left the originals for whoever played magistrate this week, and entered *Visellius, A.: doubtful* with a care that felt like closing a child’s eyes. On the street he heard his own name already mutating into rumour, interest you paid without a contract. Tablets, he noted, could kill as quietly as speeches.

A water-carrier at the stair mouth (jug dripping, no stake in Basilus or Bucco) told a neighbour loud enough for Hordeonius’s ear: “Paid on the Kalends, throat opened for paper. Someone wanted the debt soft. Someone else wanted the soft man quiet. Ash will remember the smell longer than the Senate remembers the name.” Hordeonius did not correct her. Street mouths were how SIDE-1 would travel when larger corpses later taught the city to forget on purpose. He filed her sentence beside the three stair theories and kept OPEN-0: killer still unknown; ash pressure already planted.
