### Between the lines

They met where the ditch stank of melt and horse and the kind of truce that lasted only as long as both sides wanted a witness.

Snow threatened without committing. A herald’s voice cracked on a title. Someone on Antony’s side laughed and was glared quiet. Pacuvius waited two spear-lengths back with the patience of a man who preferred storm to grammar. On Decimus’s side an aide held a tablet of Antony’s demands that had already been refused twice in writing.

Antony came on foot the last stretch, body first, cloak open enough to show he was not hiding a sword he intended to use *yet*. Decimus came with clean hands and dirty boots, the old habit of looking like the honest party. Cheer might still open a morning. It might not.

“Last talk,” Antony said, not loud. Loud was for walls. “Yield Mutina. Keep your skin. I take the province the consul needs. You take a road south that isn’t a grave.”

“I hold a legal command,” Decimus said. “The Senate…”

“The Senate writes music,” Antony said. “I write pay. Your grain weeks are shorter than your letters to Cicero admit. I have counted your smoke. Cooking smoke, not forge smoke. You are eating carefully. Careful men surrender while they can still walk.”

Decimus felt the hit. He had not known Antony’s count was that close. He kept his face officer-flat. “Hirtius will come.”

“Hirtius will come when he can sit a horse,” Antony said. “Pansa will come when he has taught enough farm boys which end of a pilum goes forward. Neither of them is consul until the Kalends, and neither of them is here. Until then you have hinges and hope, and hope won’t fill a granary.” He glanced once at Mutina’s wall. “You killed a man who trusted your cheer. Do not ask me to trust your legality.”

Decimus’s throat tightened, the old key turning. He didn’t climb down. Climbing down would’ve looked like confession.

“No,” Decimus said. “Not for breakfast. Not for your count of my smoke.”

Antony’s mouth did something almost like a smile. “Then we introduce you to winter.”

They parted without clasping. The truce ended when the first boot left the ditch mud. Behind Decimus, the aide whispered, “He bluffed the grain.”

“He might have,” Decimus said. “I cannot un-eat what we have already not eaten.” The aide’s mouth twitched. “At least he didn’t offer you a chair. Chairs make threats look like visits.” Decimus almost laughed, then didn’t. Laughing in a ditch after refusing winter was how men became other men’s stories.

That night a courier brought Plancus’s newest courtesy: admiration for Mutina’s hinges, regret that his own columns were “not yet free to join,” a promise dated after a thaw that had not begun. Decimus set the tablet under the same stone as Antony’s old threats. Plancus’s courtesy weighed like another empty sack. The smoke count was already short.

### Antony

He rode back smelling snow and horse and impatience. Pacuvius wanted storm now. Antony held the storm one more day, not mercy, timing. Timing kept soldiers paid long enough to hate the right wall.

A scout reported smoke from the town’s edge, cooking, not surrender. Antony nodded. “He thinks a province is a personality,” he told Pacuvius. “Let him eat posture.”

### Decimus

He watched the lines tighten and wrote fewer letters that pretended optimism.

“Hirtius will come,” the aide said again, needing it.

“After the Kalends,” Decimus said. “Him and Pansa both. Until then we eat salt ox and count hinges.”

He walked the walls at night with a cloak that did not keep the Ides out. The deed felt far and stupidly intimate, a debt owed a corpse who had trusted his cheer. An aide offered wine. Decimus refused, then took half a cup anyway. He checked a gate twice and didn’t tell anyone why the second check felt like apology. Below, Antony’s fires multiplied. The siege had a face now. The face that had counted his smoke.

# SC-74: The Senate’s Sword

POV: Octavian · Rome (compressed)
Date / place: Kalends of January 43 · Curia fringe / after-session
Canon refs: Chain 6 tragic misread · C5 · C7
Info ENTER: Fear of Antony · EXIT: Octavian instrumentalised
Overhear risk: Curia

---

Cicero argued Octavian into usefulness the way he argued clauses into laws. A sweating senator on the end bench, near the doors, feared Antony more than boys. Octavian stood on the threshold among the men who listened through the open doors: not a senator, so not inside, and present enough to be aimed.

The consuls’ chairs had new men in them. Antony’s year was over; Antony was in a ditch outside Mutina. Hirtius sat wrapped too warmly, the spring’s sickness still in his cheekbones. Octavian had read his last book of the Gallic war at Apollonia and privately thought it better than the dead man’s. Pansa sat beside him, broad and plain, a list of recruits and pay open on his knee before the auspices were cold. Both had been Caesar’s men. Caesar had named both to this year himself. Cicero called them the Senate’s swords. Octavian counted it plainer from the doorway: the only two men in the room with the right to raise legions and lead them, and the fathers meant to send them north to break the ring around Decimus. Cicero wanted a third blade to go with them.

Cicero read the motion in his court voice. Thanks to the Martian legion and the Fourth, “who left the consul Antony and came over to the Senate and to Gaius Caesar.” Thanks to the veterans “whom Gaius Caesar raised at his own cost.” And for Gaius Caesar himself a praetor’s command and a seat among the fathers, so that the legions he already had would at last belong to a law. Octavian heard *at his own cost* and thought of the actual cost: five hundred denarii a man through the Campanian colonies in the autumn, Philippus’s face when the chests went out, Antony at Brundisium offering his own soldiers a hundred apiece and hearing the ranks laugh. The Senate was thanking him for a crime it had decided to need.

“The young Caesar,” Cicero said, and the name seemed to catch in his throat on the way out, “will march with the consuls and defend the state against Antony’s madness.” The senator muttered *adoption* and *armies* in one breath, hoping grammar could make them one animal, then closed his mouth into agreement. Octavian watched Cicero take the closing for virtue’s return.

Octavian listened to himself become a tool. When Cicero’s gaze sought approval, he gave a nod calibrated to obedience and nothing else. He didn’t think *empire*. He thought *survive the week without looking grateful for their fear.* A clerk miswrote his age. He corrected the number and left the tone alone. Hirtius looked at him once, the look of a man who had copied out Caesar’s sentences for years and now found the name signed in a boy’s hand. Octavian held the look until Hirtius let it go. Pansa did not look up. He was counting.

Agrippa stood where the Curia fringe still smelled of wet wool and ambition. Close enough to hear Pacuvius’s name travel in a courier’s mutter, Antony’s storm-man still pricing timing at Mutina. Far enough that senators could pretend the boy arrived alone. A sweating senator wanted Octavian to smile gratitude. Agrippa did not move. Octavian kept his own nod thin.

Afterward a senator clasped his arm and called him the Republic’s luck. Octavian returned the clasp at half strength. Half strength still looked like gratitude if you did not measure. Agrippa’s stillness held until the clasp ended. Only then did he match pace toward the vendor street. A northern courier’s fragments stayed unmerged in Octavian’s hand: Decimus holding, Antony digging, Pacuvius still hungry for storm. Cicero left looking like a man certain he had saved the Republic. Outside, a vendor sold hot food and called no one Caesar. Octavian heard him and didn’t correct him.
