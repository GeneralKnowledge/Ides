# SC-73 — Walls

POV: Antony / Decimus (split) · Mutina · siege winter  
Info ENTER: Negotiation failed · EXIT: Siege lit

---

### Antony
Antony looked at Mutina’s walls and saw a stubborn man inside them wearing Decimus’ face.

“Last talk,” he told a tribune. “Then we teach masonry who the consul is.”

The parley failed in the usual way: law citations, insults dressed as courtesy, neither side able to climb down without looking climbable. Antony rode back smelling snow and horse and impatience.

### Decimus
Decimus watched the lines tighten and wrote fewer letters that pretended optimism.

“Hirtius will come,” an aide said.

“Hirtius will come if Cicero’s Rome remembers how to send men,” Decimus said. “Until then we eat carefully.”

He walked the walls at night. The Ides felt far and stupidly intimate—like a debt he still owed a corpse.

# SC-74 — The Senate’s Sword

POV: Cicero / Octavian · Rome · Chain 6 tragic misread  
Info ENTER: Fear of Antony · EXIT: Octavian instrumentalised

---

Cicero argued Octavian into usefulness the way he argued clauses into laws.

“The young Caesar”—he still choked on the name and used it anyway when the Curia needed a handle—“will defend the state against Antony’s madness.”

Senators who feared Antony more than they feared boys voted with their sweat. Octavian, present enough to be seen, young enough to be underestimated twice, listened to himself become a tool and filed the sensation for later.

Privately he thought: *They think they hold the sword. They hold the hilt. I am learning the edge.*

He did not smile. Smiling would have looked like a man who already owned the morning, and he did not—only a boy with legions beginning to smell him as paymaster.

Cicero went home certain he had saved the Republic with a clever adoption of force. Certainty was his favourite mistake.
