# SC-01 — Africa Arrives

POV: F4 Hordeonius (+ Cicero fringe) · Forum basilica  
Date / place: 46 BC · banker stalls / basilica edge  
Canon refs: A1 · Chain 1  
Info ENTER: War “ongoing” in minds · EXIT: Thapsus won; Cato dead — moral shock  
Overhear risk: High (Forum)

---

The news hit the stalls like a price change with blood in it.

“Thapsus,” someone said. Then: “Cato.” Then the Forum did what it did with greatness—turned it into shouts, bets, and sudden philosophy.

A runner still smelled of horse and dust. Half the knot insisted Utica was rumour dressed as fact; the other half was already pricing grain as if Africa had finished dying. Hordeonius did not shout. Shouting was for men who sold certainty by the jug.

He adjusted grain expectations with solemn grief. “A man like Cato,” he told a client whose ring was too new, “is bad for markets and good for sermons. I prefer markets, but I respect sermons that stay out of my ledgers.”

The client wanted a moral. Hordeonius gave him a rate.

Across the way Cicero received the same fact as a bruise to the Republic’s imagination. Cato dead by his own hand—Caesar’s victory complete enough to make mercy look like a style. Cicero’s mouth shaped a period he would later write better; for now he only stood among men who were already converting a suicide into a slogan.

“They will make him marble before the week is out,” someone muttered.

“Marble does not clear,” Hordeonius answered, not to Cicero, to the air that smelled of money and onion. He entered *Africa: closed enough* and felt the city’s mood shift under his stylus like a floorboard.

A woman selling cakes cursed Cato for spoiling an afternoon’s appetite. A veteran’s cousin asked whether land would come faster now that the war had a famous corpse. Nobody agreed. Agreement was not the Forum’s product. Noise was.

# SC-02 — Triumph Weather

POV: F1 Nysa · Sacra Via / Domus Publica orbit · FS10  
Date / place: Triumph season · street past the Domus Publica  
Canon refs: FS10 · household INF  
Info ENTER: Household prep · EXIT: Booty/slave flood; veterans expect land  
Overhear risk: Extreme street

---

Nysa carried bundles through a city drunk on spectacle.

Captives, gold, painted lies on wagons—Rome swallowing Africa as parade. Phileros had sent her for oil and eyes: watch who lingered near the Domus Publica’s public smile, count which clients arrived early to borrow the house’s weather. She understood tasks. She did not understand why men needed wagons to prove they had already won.

A veteran along the route spat about land still promised. His spit hit stone and stayed. “They cheer the gold,” he told nobody and everybody. “Gold doesn’t plough.”

Nysa filed the spit beside the cheers. Cheers were loud; spit lasted.

Children screamed at elephants or at the idea of elephants. A woman in the crowd named a captive wrongly and was corrected with pride. Rumour walked faster than the procession. Nysa kept both wrong names; wrong names were how the street remembered.

Caesar passed as weather. She saw only the edge of him—enough to know the house she served was tied to this noise. He did not look at her. Great men did not look at errands. She preferred it. Looking made you responsible for what you saw.

Back in the service court she set down the oil and told Dama nothing about land. Land talk belonged to men with scars and empty hands. She belonged to doors, basins, and the habit of listening until listening became a kind of armour.
