# SC-01 — Africa Arrives

POV: F4 Hordeonius (Cicero fringe in-scene, not a cut) · Forum basilica  
Date / place: 46 BC · banker stalls / basilica edge  
Canon refs: A1 · Chain 1  
Info ENTER: War “ongoing” in minds · EXIT: Thapsus won; Cato dead — moral shock; city already turning victory into parade weather  
Overhear risk: High (Forum)

---

The news hit the stalls like a price change with blood in it.

“Thapsus,” someone said. Then: “Cato.” Then the Forum did what it did with greatness—turned it into shouts, bets, and sudden philosophy.

A runner still smelled of horse and dust. Half the knot insisted Utica was rumour dressed as fact; the other half was already pricing grain as if Africa had finished dying. Hordeonius didn't shout. Shouting was for men who sold certainty by the jug.

He adjusted grain expectations with solemn grief. “A man like Cato,” he told a client whose ring was too new, “is bad for markets and good for sermons. I prefer markets, but I respect sermons that stay out of my ledgers.”

The client wanted a moral. Hordeonius gave him a rate. The client’s mouth opened for philosophy, closed on the number, and produced a thin grateful smile suitable for patrons and priests alike.

“You honour the dead with arithmetic,” the client said, careful.

“I honour the living with invoices,” Hordeonius answered, equally careful.

Across the way Cicero received the same fact as a bruise to the Republic’s imagination. Cato dead by his own hand—Caesar’s victory complete enough to make mercy look like a style. Cicero’s mouth shaped a period and lost it—the right clause refusing to arrive while lesser men were already converting a suicide into a slogan. Hordeonius watched the lost period the way he watched a late payment: interesting, not urgent. Eloquence without a ledger still moved air; air did not clear.

“They will make him marble before the week is out,” someone muttered at Hordeonius’s elbow—Sosia, collecting opinions with the coins.

“Marble does not clear,” Hordeonius answered, not to Cicero, to the air that smelled of money and onion. He entered *Africa: closed enough* and felt the city’s mood shift under his stylus like a floorboard.

A woman selling cakes cursed Cato for spoiling an afternoon’s appetite. A veteran’s cousin asked whether land would come faster now that the war had a famous corpse. A clerk from a nearby stall argued that famous corpses were inflation for patriotism and deflation for grain. Nobody agreed; the Forum only sold noise.

Hordeonius let the noise run until it sorted itself into prices. Thapsus as fact. Utica as theatre. Cato as a sermon that would outlive the men who priced him. He did not need Cicero’s unfinished period to know the Republic had been bruised; he needed to know whether bruised men still paid on the Kalends.

When the new-ring client was three stalls away, he spat the smile out. “Sermons out of his ledgers—what a patriot. He priced Cato like onions.”

His companion—another ring, older—did not bother with courtesy once the banker could not hear. “Onions keep. Marble doesn’t. Pay the rate and stop performing grief for clerks.”

The cake-seller overheard and laughed once, short, the way street laughter admits a hit.

By afternoon the same stalls were already selling the next weather: triumph talk, wagon talk, which streets would choke first when Africa arrived as painted gold. Hordeonius priced that too—spectacle was a tax on patience—and watched a Domus Publica runner cut through the press with oil jars hugged like secrets. The house that served the victor was already counting eyes. The unfinished question under *Africa: closed enough* was not whether Caesar had won. It was what the city would do with a victory loud enough to drown land promises.

# SC-02 — Triumph Weather

POV: F1 Nysa · Sacra Via / Domus Publica orbit · FS10  
Date / place: Triumph season · street past the Domus Publica  
Canon refs: FS10 · household INF  
Info ENTER: Household prep · EXIT: Booty/slave flood; veterans expect land  
Overhear risk: Extreme street  
**G1:** Second POV — collides with F4’s unfinished question (closed Africa → what victory costs on the street / at the house)

---

Nysa carried bundles through a city drunk on spectacle.

Captives, gold, painted lies on wagons—Rome swallowing Africa as parade. Phileros had sent her for oil and eyes: watch who lingered near the Domus Publica’s public smile, count which clients arrived early to borrow the house’s favour. She understood tasks. She didn't understand why men needed wagons to prove they had already won.

A veteran along the route spat about land still promised. His spit hit stone and stayed. “They cheer the gold,” he told nobody and everybody. “Gold doesn’t plough.”

Nysa filed the spit beside the cheers. Cheers were loud; spit lasted.

Children screamed at elephants or at the idea of elephants. A woman in the crowd named a captive wrongly and was corrected with pride. Rumour walked faster than the procession. Nysa kept both wrong names; wrong names were how the street remembered.

Near the Domus steps a banker-looking man—ring, stylus, onion-and-money face—paused just long enough to watch the wagons as if they were a rate sheet. Nysa did not know his name. She knew the look: men who priced marble before it cooled. Phileros had warned her that such men sometimes bought household gossip by the cup. She kept her face blank and her oil upright.

Caesar passed at the edge of sight. She saw only enough to know the house she served was tied to this noise. He didn't look at her. Great men didn't look at errands. She preferred it—looking made you responsible for what you saw.

A second veteran, younger, argued with the first about colonies and whether Africa’s corpse meant Italy’s farms. Neither had a farm. Both had scars. Nysa walked between their voices and did not choose a side. Choosing sides was how errands became politics, and politics was how basins got dropped.

Back in the service court she set down the oil and told Dama nothing about land. Land talk belonged to men with scars and empty hands. She belonged to doors, basins, and listening hard enough that her face stayed blank while her hands kept moving. The parade noise still pressed the house’s walls. Closed Africa, she noted, had not closed the counting.
