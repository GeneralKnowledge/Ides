# SC-67 — Which Caesar Pays

POV: F3 Laberius · Italian veteran talk · FS10  
Info ENTER: Murder rage · EXIT: Listens for paymaster of the name

---

In a colony’s dusty forum Laberius heard two agents in one afternoon.

Antony’s man spoke of continuity, of the consul who still drank with soldiers, of land that would come if they held steady. Octavian’s man—younger, sharper, almost embarrassed by his own nerve—spoke the name *Caesar* as if it were a donative already weighed.

“Which one buried him?” a corporal asked.

“Neither,” Laberius said. “Philosophers buried him. These two are arguing over the will.”

He did not commit. Commitment was how you got the wrong eagle. He listened. Listening was a veteran’s first weapon after the gladius.

# SC-68a — The Room Full of Women and Ruin

POV: Cicero · Antium · 8 June 44 · Canon: digest C · Servilia soft power  
Info ENTER: Brutus asks advice · EXIT: Cassius rejects corn insult; Brutus abandons Rome return; Servilia will cut decree; Cicero feels wrecked

---

Antium’s light was pretty. Pretty light made disasters look arranged.

Brutus asked Cicero to speak as if speech could still be strategy. The room held Porcia’s severity, Tertulla’s stillness, Favonius’ moral cough—and Servilia, who listened the way bankers listened to interest.

Cicero began well. He always began well. Return to Rome under arms? He painted the danger without naming cowardice. Stay in Italy as moral monuments? He praised the idea just enough to bury it. Accept the Senate’s insulting commissions—Asia’s scraps, Sicily’s corn—as tools rather than humiliations? Better tools than open war with empty hands. He heard himself navigating and mistook navigation for statesmanship.

Porcia’s mouth tightened at every compromise. Tertulla watched Cassius’ empty place as if absence were an argument. Favonius coughed morality into the pauses.

Cassius arrived mid-flow—late on purpose or by fate, either way a blade. Dust on him. Impatience sharper than dust.

Cicero tried to resume. Cassius did not resume; he cut.

“Sicily,” Cassius said, when the corn commission surfaced. “They want me to feed the city while Antony feeds on it. I will not carry grain like a freedman while men who spared him call it peace.”

Brutus’ face did the old slow collapse into resolve. The Rome-return balloon—Cicero’s prettiest paragraph—died in Brutus’ throat. East became less a choice than a harbour with a direction.

Servilia cut in—not loud. Loud was for men who needed to be seen cutting. “That clause in the decree,” she said, naming the thorn without asking permission. “It can be managed. I will see it managed.” No vote. No request. A mother’s firm rewriting the state’s paper through private channels.

Cicero felt the ship take water. He had been invited to advise and had become furniture in a family firm’s emergency meeting. Soft power was not soft when it rearranged your sentences after you spoke them.

Favonius muttered about civil war’s cost. No one appointed him captain. Porcia looked at Brutus as if measuring how much of Cato remained.

Outside, the sea kept being the sea. Inside, Cicero tasted the day’s fact without future knowledge: the Liberators would not recover the capital by cleverness alone, and his cleverness had just been politely shelved.

He left praising everyone. Praise was how you exited rooms that had already decided without you. On the road he began composing, already, the letter to Atticus that would make him central again on the page.
