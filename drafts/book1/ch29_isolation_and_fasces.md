# SC-82 — Allies Evaporate

---

Victory tasted like abandonment.

Decimus left Mutina’s relief into a map where Plancus hesitated, Lepidus smiled wrong, and Antony—bleeding but unbroken—slid toward friends Decimus could not buy. The siege broken should have felt like breath. Instead it felt like being handed a road with no destination stamped on it.

Cicero’s praise arrived like perfume on a corpse: sweet, late, useless for horses. Decimus read one line of congratulation twice, then folded it into a pack already too light on grain. He wrote one more letter that tried to sound steady—gates held, honour intact, the Republic still a word that could travel. His hand knew better than his sentences.

Then he rode with dwindling staff into country that did not care who freed the Republic. An aide counted purses. Another counted betrayals and got a higher number. A third asked whether they should wait for Cassius’ weather from the East. Decimus said waiting was how traps learned your name.

“They will say I did not pursue,” the first aide muttered.

“They will say many things,” Decimus said. “Say them after we find a road that isn’t a trap.”

He thought, briefly, of Caesar’s atrium and the cheer he had worn like a clean cloak on a March morning. Trust had been the key. Keys, on a bad road, looked like jokes told by dead men. He checked his horse’s mouth for sores and found none. The sores were elsewhere.

At a crossroads shrine someone had left a cheap wreath for “the liberators.” Rain had ruined it. Decimus did not stop to fix symbolism. Symbolism did not shoe horses.

The trap’s final shape found him where the map promised Cassius and delivered strangers with a price for a famous head. Decimus—who had opened Caesar’s morning with a clean hand—ended in a ditch’s logic: abandoned by alliances, useful only as proof that liberty’s insider could die as meat. He did not make a speech. Speeches had been other men’s luxury. If he thought of Caesar’s laugh one last time, the thought did not become a line anyone else could steal. Word would reach Rome late and wrong in places. The fact would be simple enough for even bakers: Decimus Albinus was gone.

# SC-83 — Consul

POV: Octavian · approach to Rome  
Date / place: Field of Mars politics / army weather  
Canon refs: A12 prep · C5 — learning, not serene  
Info ENTER: Armies after Mutina · EXIT: Consul; boy with fasces  
Overhear risk: Soldiers + Senate terror

---

Octavian entered the politics of force with hands that still remembered shaking in Apollonia.

Soldiers wanted a consul who was theirs—paymaster, name, a face young enough to insult the old seats. Senate wanted a miracle that obeyed. Octavian offered them a nineteen-year-old with Caesar’s name and enough armed insistence to make elections look like weather. Agrippa’s silence beside him was heavier than speeches; Apollodorus adjusted cloaks as if cloth could negotiate.

He felt sick after the first hard bargain—stomach, not omen. Sickness was private: file under body. A taste of metal rose; he swallowed it like bad advice. Publicly he accepted fasces as if they were owed homework. A clerk miswrote his age; Octavian corrected the number and left the tone alone. Tone was how men revealed hunger.

“They will hate this,” Agrippa said.

“They hated the knives more,” Octavian said. “Hate is a kind of attention. We will spend it carefully.”

A senator called him the Republic’s luck and waited for a smile of destiny. Octavian returned a nod calibrated to obedience. A soldier shouted the name Caesar as paymaster weather; Octavian filed the shout under *useful* and did not bow to it like a hymn.

Still not serene. Still learning where ruthlessness lived in the body—usually behind the teeth, never in a speech about forever, never in a title he did not yet have language for.
