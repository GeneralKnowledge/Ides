# SC-82 — Allies Evaporate

POV: Decimus · roads after Mutina  
Date / place: North Italian roads · dwindling staff  
Canon refs: A10 end · death path fog OK  
Info ENTER: Siege broken · EXIT: Flight; death-on-road  
Overhear risk: Dwindling staff

---

Victory tasted like abandonment.

Decimus left Mutina’s relief into a map where Plancus hesitated, Lepidus smiled wrong, and Antony—bleeding but unbroken—slid toward friends Decimus could not buy. The siege broken should have felt like breath. Instead it felt like being handed a road with no destination stamped on it.

Cicero’s praise arrived like perfume on a corpse: sweet, late, useless for horses. Decimus read one line of congratulation twice, then folded it into a pack already too light on grain. He wrote one more letter that tried to sound steady—gates held, honour intact. His hand knew better than his sentences. He did not send a second copy. Second copies were for men who still believed paper multiplied safety.

Then he rode with dwindling staff into country that did not care who freed the Republic. Rain made the road a trough. An aide counted purses and got a number that made him quiet. Another counted betrayals and got a higher number. A third asked whether they should wait for Cassius from the East.

“Waiting is how traps learn your name,” Decimus said.

“They will say I did not pursue,” the first aide muttered.

“They will say many things. Say them after we find a road that isn’t a snare.”

At a crossroads shrine someone had left a cheap wreath for “the liberators.” Rain had ruined it. Decimus did not stop to fix it. He checked his horse’s mouth for sores and found none. The sores were in the map: every promised friend a thinner line than yesterday.

They slept once in a barn that smelled of wet hay and someone else’s fear. In the dark Decimus thought—briefly, without speech—of Caesar’s atrium and the cheer he had worn like a clean cloak on a March morning. Trust had been the key. He put the thought away the way he put away a broken strap: later, if later existed.

On the second day the staff was smaller. One man had “gone for water” and not returned. Decimus did not send a search. Searches taught the road your remaining number.

Near a river ford the map promised a contact who could pass them toward the mountains and, beyond rumour, toward Cassius. The contact was three men with ordinary faces and a mule. One smiled too quickly. Decimus’s remaining aide whispered, “Lepidus’s smile.”

“Or anybody’s price,” Decimus said.

He still dismounted. Refusing the ford meant the open road behind, where Antony’s friends were thicker. He kept his hands visible. Optics, even here.

They asked for his name as if they did not know it. He gave it because lying would have bought minutes, not miles. A purse changed hands—not his purse. Someone else’s coin for his head. He saw the metal. He understood the arithmetic before the knives moved.

There was no speech. Speeches had been other men’s luxury. He went for the horse because horses were logistics, and logistics was the last language he trusted. A hand caught his cloak. Mud took his knee. The ditch at the ford’s edge was shallow and insultingly ordinary—meltwater, a broken rein, the smell of wet wool.

He did not make a line about the Republic. If he thought of Caesar’s laugh one last time, the thought stayed inside where no one could steal it. Pain was brief and specific and unpaid by meaning.

Word would reach Rome late and wrong in places. The fact would be simple enough for bakers: Decimus Albinus was gone. The man who had opened a dictator’s morning with a clean hand had ended as meat on a priced road.

# SC-83 — Consul

POV: Octavian · approach to Rome  
Date / place: Field of Mars politics / army pressure  
Canon refs: A12 prep · C5 — learning, not serene  
Info ENTER: Armies after Mutina; Decimus death as incomplete rumour · EXIT: Consul; boy with fasces  
Overhear risk: Soldiers + Senate terror

---

Octavian entered the politics of force with hands that still remembered shaking in Apollonia.

A courier brought Decimus’s end as three incompatible stories: ditch, Alps, Lepidus’s smile, Antony’s purse, no purse, a speech, no speech. Octavian kept all three. Incomplete was safer than a single neat corpse. What the stories shared was enough: the Liberator who had held Mutina was no longer a piece on the board. Soldiers heard that shared core before senators finished arguing which version was virtuous.

“They will say you failed to save him,” Agrippa said.

“They will say many things,” Octavian said. “Say them after the men are paid.”

The vacuum mattered as pressure, not triumph. With Decimus gone, the Senate had one fewer excuse to pretend Mutina had settled the year. Octavian’s own ranks smelled the empty square and wanted a consul who was *theirs*—paymaster, name, a face young enough to insult the old seats. Senate wanted a miracle that obeyed. Octavian offered them a nineteen-year-old with Caesar’s name and enough armed insistence to make elections look like something you survived rather than chose. Agrippa’s silence beside him was heavier than speeches; Apollodorus adjusted cloaks as if cloth could negotiate.

He felt sick after the first hard bargain—stomach, not omen. A taste of metal rose; he swallowed it. Publicly he accepted fasces as if they were owed homework. A clerk miswrote his age; Octavian corrected the number and left the tone alone.

“They will hate this,” Agrippa said.

“They hated the knives more,” Octavian said. “Hate is attention. We will spend it carefully.”

A senator called him the Republic’s luck and waited for a smile of destiny. Octavian returned a nod calibrated to obedience. A soldier shouted the name Caesar as paymaster; Octavian did not bow to it like a hymn. He let the shout stand because paymaster was a job he understood better than luck.

Still not serene. Still learning where ruthlessness lived in the body—usually behind the teeth, never in a speech about forever, never in a title he did not yet have language for. Decimus’s absence did not make him wise. It made the road to Bononia shorter, and the men behind him louder.
