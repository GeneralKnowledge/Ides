# SC-70 — Cisalpine

POV: Decimus · Cisalpine HQ  
Date / place: Late 44 · command rooms / gates  
Canon refs: A10 · C1 residual  
Info ENTER: Legal command · EXIT: Antony wants swap; war geometry forms  
Overhear risk: Officers

---

Decimus read Antony’s pressure in dispatches before he heard it in hoofbeats.

The tablets arrived with seals that looked like courtesy and read like a hand on a throat. One letter offered friendship in the same sentence as a deadline. Decimus set that letter under a stone so it could not blow into the yard and become gossip.

Cisalpine was walls, levies, the cold arithmetic of holding a road into Italy. He wrote to Cicero with the stiff pride of a man who still believed praise from Rome was supply—and knew, privately, that praise was thinner than grain.

“He wants the province,” an officer said. “He will call it law.”

“He can call it breakfast,” Decimus said. “I call it mine until the Senate says otherwise—or until he brings enough men to rename facts.”

He inspected gates. Hinges took oil. Oil was easier than ideology. Ideology had not kept his street from burning in March. A courier asked whether to answer Antony’s latest “request” with softness. Decimus chose a sentence that sounded like law and felt like a door barred from inside.

He inspected a levy roll and found names that still loved Caesar’s pay more than Decimus’ legality. He did not punish the love. He scheduled the pay talk for morning.

At night he walked the wall-walk and tasted snow early. The Ides sat in his chest like a key he could not return. Keys, he knew, cut both ways.

# SC-71 — Sea Road

POV: Cleopatra · sea toward Alexandria  
Date / place: Open water · CLEO-1 exit  
Canon refs: FS06 · *Att.* 14.8 energy · C8  
Info ENTER: Rome unsafe · EXIT: Offstage; Caesarion fuse remains  
Overhear risk: Court

---

The ship took Italian earth-smell out of their clothes by the second day.

Cleopatra watched Caesarion sleep and refused nostalgia as policy. Rome had been a useful garden. Gardens burned. A nurse hummed a Greek line; Cleopatra cut the hum with a look that meant *not here—not where sailors can sell melodies.* Alexandria still needed a queen who arrived looking inevitable, not chased. Salt dried on her lips like a seal.

“When we land,” she told Ammonius’ stand-in, “we speak of continuity. Not of flight. Flight is what clients do.”

“And of the boy in Italy who took the name?” he asked.

“We speak of him when he becomes weather. Not before. Weather is public. Gossip is private inventory.” She watched Caesarion’s sleeping fist and added, almost to herself, “Names travel faster than ships. We will not race a name we do not yet need.”

A sailor cursed a rope. Cleopatra did not translate the curse into omen. Omens were a Roman hobby she could rent without converting. She turned her face into salt wind and let Rome become a rumour with good furniture—and a child ashore who still carried a fuse she would not light from a deck.

# SC-72 — Sextus

POV: Cicero · letter desk  
Date / place: Villa / Rome fringe · *Att.* 16.4 energy  
Canon refs: FS11 · secondhand only  
Info ENTER: Western rumour · EXIT: Sextus unresolved on the board  
Overhear risk: N/A

---

Cicero wrote: Sextus holds the sea’s western knife; do not invent a Republic that ignores fleets.

The report under his hand was secondhand twice—someone who knew someone who had seen sails. A different report claimed Sextus was already finished. Cicero kept the contradiction in the margin like a live coal. He did not promote either claim to fact. He promoted both to unease. Unease was his most honest genre. He sanded a sentence until it could travel to Atticus without pretending omniscience, then added a postscript about Antony’s hunger for provinces as if the western knife and the northern road were the same problem wearing different coats.

On the same tablet he almost added a joke about philosophers ignoring docks. He deleted the joke. Jokes travelled badly in wartime.

He sealed the letter. Sealing still felt like action. Outside, a thrush made a sound like a hinge. Cicero took it for autumn, not omen.
