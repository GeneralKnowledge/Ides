Decimus read Antony’s pressure in dispatches before he heard it in hoofbeats.

The tablets arrived with seals that looked like courtesy and read like a hand on a throat. One letter offered friendship in the same sentence as a deadline. Decimus set that letter under a stone so it couldn’t blow into the yard and become gossip.

At mid-morning an envoy came anyway, cloak clean, smile practiced, the sort of man who carried other men’s threats as if they were invitations to dinner. He stood in the command room where the wall map of the Via Aemilia still showed Decimus’s pins as facts. An aide hovered with a wax roll. A levy clerk waited at the door with pay marks still wet. The room wanted to be polite. It was already a siege rehearsal.

“The consul asks only what the law already leans toward,” the envoy said. “Hand him Cisalpine in good order. Keep your honour. Keep your skin. Rome will find you another usefulness.”

“Rome found me this one,” Decimus said. He didn’t offer a chair. Chairs made threats look like visits.

“He will call it a swap,” the envoy went on, mild. “You will call it wisdom. The Senate will call it clarification, when clarification arrives.”

Decimus knew the swap because it had been done to him while he was on the road. In early summer Antony had walked a law through an assembly crowded with his veterans: Cisalpine to Antony for five years, and the Macedonian legions brought home across the sea to go with him. Decimus held the province on Caesar’s grant. The Senate had never voted to take it from him. It had never voted to keep it for him either, and he had stopped waiting for that letter.

Decimus heard the gap in that sentence: *when*. Clarification that might never leave the stable. “Tell him I call it mine until the Senate says otherwise, or until he brings enough men to rename facts. He can call the renaming breakfast if he likes.”

The envoy’s eyes flicked once to the levy roll on the table: names, marks for pay, a smudge where someone’s thumb had been nervous. “Your men still love Caesar’s pay more than your legality. My principal has a longer memory for that love than you do.”

That was the first true thing said. Decimus felt it land under the sternum where the Ides still sat like a key he couldn’t return. “Get out,” he said. “If you return with an army, return with an army. Do not return with grammar.”


The envoy paused at the threshold long enough for the room to hear the complaint under the courtesy: “He will remember the refusal more than the wit.” Then he left.

After the man was gone, Decimus walked the gates. Hinges took oil. Oil was easier than ideology. Ideology had not kept his street from burning in March.

In the yard he found the levy spokesman, an older miles with a Caesarian scar and a mouth that had learned to ask for bread without kneeling. “We heard the cloak,” the man said. “If Antony comes, do we fight men who still drink to the name we buried with?”

“You fight the men who try to take the road,” Decimus said.

“And pay?”

Decimus looked at the chest. Coin still rattled when the lid shifted, but shallowly, honest about the bottom. He ordered a partial donative for the gate cohorts, coin he’d meant to hold for spring, and watched the spokesman’s shoulders drop half an inch. Loyalty bought today might still open a gate tomorrow.

The spokesman weighed the purse with his eyes, not his hands. “Partial,” he said. “That’s a word that tastes like next week’s apology.”

Decimus almost grinned, the old cheer that had opened mornings, and stopped it before it became a performance. “Then taste it and still hold the hinge. Next week’s apology is still coin I have to find.” The spokesman barked once, short, surprised, and the yard heard it as weather, not mutiny. Decimus scheduled the rest of the pay talk for morning. He hated that mornings now had prices.

He wrote to Cicero with the stiff pride of a man who still believed praise from Rome was supply, and knew privately that praise was thinner than grain. The same week he wrote Plancus again, shorter, less perfume, asking for two cohorts’ worth of grain and a date, any date, for a meeting on the road.

The answer did not arrive alone. A courier who had eaten at Plancus’s table stood in the yard with mud on one boot and perfume still clinging to the other, Plancus’s weather made flesh. “He admires your hinges,” the man said, practising the pretty line. “He understands Antony’s appetite. His own theatre is unsettled. Grain and cohorts cannot be spared.” Decimus waited for a date. The courier’s eyes found the gate-hinge instead. “He told me to say *yet*, if you pressed. *Yet* is not a day on a road.” Soft refusal with a human face stacked the same height as Antony’s hard threats on the table. Decimus dismissed the man before admiration could invent another perfume. The Lepidus channel he had seeded on the road north, a roof near Mutina’s approaches, a messenger who would not meet his eyes, still returned nothing. He did not send a second tablet to ask why.

The aide asked anyway, one finger holding open the empty slot in the letter-case. “Lepidus has more legions over the mountains than Plancus has. The whole coast from the Alps to Spain, and the chief priest’s robe on top of it. One word from him and Antony has friends at his back as well as a wall in front.”

“One word from Lepidus goes to whoever is standing nearest when he has to say it,” Decimus said. “Pray it’s us.” He did not pray. He sent the aide to count the grain.

A southern courier arrived near dusk with dust on his cloak and a packet that smelled of harbour pitch. Cleopatra’s people had sailed, queen and boy both, talking continuity to anyone who asked, not flight. The courier said it like marketplace weather: ships that left looking inevitable. Decimus did not chase the rumour into Alexandria. He had hinges. He had Antony’s hunger. A queen on salt water was confirmation that one fuse had left Italy without needing his map to light it. He set the packet under the stone with Antony’s courtesy-threats. He did not invent a mosaic for a court he could not hear.

Cicero’s latest letter, opened on the same stone, carried a margin note in Cicero’s own hand, unease, not fact. Sextus still held, or seemed to hold, the western sea’s knife; do not invent a Republic that ignores fleets. A second report under the note claimed Sextus finished. Cicero had kept both coals. Decimus kept the contradiction as one more unfinished edge on a board already loud with Antony. He almost smiled at philosophers discovering docks. The smile died when he remembered his own thinning chest.

A different courier asked whether to answer Antony’s latest written “request” with softness. Decimus chose a sentence that sounded like law and felt like a door barred from inside.

The sentence did not need an answer. A rider came up the Via Aemilia from Ariminum with his horse lathered to the girth: Antony had left Rome with the Macedonian legions behind him and was on the road north. Not hurrying. That was worse.

Decimus did not wait to be caught on the open plain. He took his legions into Mutina, because Mutina had walls, a granary, and the road running past its gate. In the yard, while carts were loading, the levy spokesman asked what came next.

“He comes up the road,” Decimus said. “He can’t march on into Gaul with a town full of legions at his back, so he stops. He digs ditches round us and waits for us to eat the last ox. We wait for an army from Rome to come and break his ring. Whoever’s count runs out first loses.”

“Which army?” the spokesman said. “Rome’s busy writing.”

“Next year’s,” Decimus said. “On the Kalends the consulship leaves Antony’s hands. Caesar named the next two before he died. Aulus Hirtius, who writes better than he rides and has been sick since spring. Gaius Vibius Pansa, who governed provinces for Caesar and can get farm boys into armour faster than most men get senators into a vote. Letters can’t raise legions. Consuls can, and lead them. Those two are the only swords the Senate owns.”

“Hirtius and Pansa.” The spokesman worked it through aloud. “Caesar’s men. Coming to get Caesar’s…” He stopped. “Coming to get you out.”

“Yes.” Decimus did not help him finish the first sentence. It would only be funny if they arrived.

Inside Mutina he had the draught oxen killed and salted in the granary yard, every beast that could pull a cart, because a shut town had no use for carts and a great deal of use for meat. The butchers worked on past dark by torchlight. Brine and blood ran together in the gutter. The spokesman watched with his arms folded and did not say *partial*.

Later he walked Mutina’s wall-walk and tasted snow early. The key in his chest cut both ways. It had opened Caesar’s morning. It might yet open his own throat. He checked a hinge twice and didn’t tell the sentry why.
