### Brutus

Brutus pressed two fingers under his ribs where the reports had lodged and found no relief there.

He had the versions in incompatible shapes. Cassius broken. Cassius mistaken. Cassius ended by his own hand on a lie the plain told. Brutus kept all three without choosing. What they shared was enough: Cassius was gone. The weight of that sat in the body first, breath short, ribs tight, the old courtesy habit wanting a sentence that would arrange grief into something useful. He had no sentence. He had a line that still needed bodies.

He didn't deliver a speech about it. This was weight and breath and mud that took boots the way grief took time.

He fought like a man hoping philosophy could stiffen a shield-wall. For a while it did. Faces that had minted *EID MAR* with him held because holding was what they still knew how to do. He moved among them with the old etiquette of a man who still believed posture mattered: nod, correct a gap, refuse the luxury of sitting when the men stood. Mud took his boots. Dust took his mouth. He tasted metal and did not ask whether it was blood or only fear. An aide offered water. Brutus took it, swallowed wrong, coughed once, ugly, human, and went back to the gap.

Then mud and numbers undid the stiffening. He saw men who wanted tomorrow’s grain more than yesterday’s brand. Both kinds broke when the plain insisted. An officer, face known from the minting days, caught his sleeve and begged him to pull back to a cleaner hill. The cleaner hill was visible. Higher ground. Fresher air. A place where the day might be lost slower and with more witnesses who could later invent dignity.

Brutus looked at it. The cost of taking it was clear in the body. Abandon the knot that still held. Leave the men who still answered *EID MAR* with their shoulders. Trade the day’s shape for a cleaner story about retreat. Cassius would have called delay contemptible. Cassius was not here to call anything.

“Hold what you can,” Brutus said. Not a period. Not an antithesis. Only an order with the paint worn off.

He refused the cleaner hill. The refusal lived as lived cost. The knot shrank. The breath got shorter. The officer’s face went from hope to something flatter. Brutus felt the choice settle behind the sternum where the reports already sat, grief and stubbornness sharing the same muscle. He held what he could until what he could was a shrinking knot that no philosophy could stiffen again.

He ended without arranging his last posture for witnesses. The men nearest him already disagreed with their eyes about what they were seeing. On the ground the restoration army was broken into names and baggage. An *EID MAR* coin lay in the dust with the rest of the metal. If he thought of Cassius’ contempt for delay one last time, he did not say it. Saying it would have been arranging. He had refused arranging once already today.

### Octavian

Octavian was ill enough that credit became argument among healthier men.

Fever made the tent canvas pulse. Sweat ran into his eyes and made the map swim. A courier asked for orders. Octavian gave two that mattered: hold the camp ditch; do not chase beyond the markers Agrippa had set. And one that only sounded like command, a sentence about courage he regretted as soon as it left his mouth. He didn't invent himself as genius of the plain. He survived noise. He survived other men’s versions of his usefulness.

Agrippa’s reports arrived clearer than his own pulse. “Brutus is breaking,” Agrippa said, or something close enough that Octavian could act without pretending he had seen the break.

“Then do not let our men become a rumour of victory before they are paid,” Octavian said. His voice cracked on *paid*. He hated the crack. Hating it did not stop it.

When someone tried to crown him with the day’s glory in a sentence, Octavian kept breathing and didn't bow. Sweat ran into the corner of his mouth; he swallowed it and asked Agrippa for the next count of the ditch.

Fever eased, later, into a thin clarity on the road-edge of the camp. “They will want farms,” Agrippa said, Italian soil, promises already louder than the plain’s dust.

“They will want farms that belong to someone else,” Octavian said. He didn't dress it. He kept one hand flat on the list of promised acres until the paper stopped fluttering. Paper would not hold the soil still. That was the next war’s seed, held in the same breath as survival. He did not invent a destiny for it. He named the want. He kept breathing.

# SC-93: Peak

POV: Antony · post-battle tent · Chain 9
Date / place: Tent · officers
Canon refs: underestimates Italy land rage
Info ENTER: Military prestige · EXIT: Settlement talk; underestimates land crisis
Overhear risk: Officers

---

Antony drank the win without sweetness.

Dust still lived in his teeth; wine only moved it around. “We divide,” he said. “We settle veterans. We keep the East from coughing blood.” Officers congratulated him in the register soldiers used for men who had just rearranged a map with bodies.

A tribune mentioned Italian land assignments, farms, promises, men who would want soil that already had owners. Antony waved it toward tomorrow with the same hand he had used to point cohorts. “Italy will wait. Italy always complains. The East is bleeding now.”

Outside the tent-flap, while congratulations still filled the wine, a junior officer muttered to the horse-holder: “Italy always complains, until it bills you in veterans who already know which farms have owners. Capua dates. Mutina mud. Same unpaid dirt, new maps. When the East stops bleeding, the soil still owes.” The horse stamped. Inside, Antony heard only the congratulations. He laughed once, short, and went back to the map.

On the plain a clerk tried to enter victory in a clean column and stopped. The stylus had nothing honest to finish with. He left the wax open to the weather, and to the next man who would have to price Italian soil that still carried other men’s names. The East pulled one victor. A list of promised acres fluttered in Antony’s unread tomorrow. In Rome, someone found a charred debt mark in a shop that no longer had an owner. No one solved Visellius. Civil war did not keep cold cases for bankers. The open wax and the ash tablet were the same unfinished question: who pays for dirt that already has owners.
