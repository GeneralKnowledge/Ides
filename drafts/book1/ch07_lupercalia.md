# SC-16 — Diadem

POV: Antony · Lupercal / Forum · A4  
Info ENTER: Festival heat · EXIT: Diadem offered & refused; monarchy panic Public

---

Antony liked the Lupercalia because it let a consul sweat in public without looking weak.

He ran, joked, slapped backs, played the old fertility chaos that Romans pretended was holy. The Lupercus strips stuck to his thighs; goat-smell and winter sweat made a single perfume. Caesar sat above it in the special chair—half god, half tired general—watching the city perform itself. Gold caught the February sun and threw it back into the crowd’s eyes until looking up hurt in a useful way.

When Antony came forward with the diadem—call it wreath, call it crown’s cousin, call it a test—the Forum’s noise changed pitch. Offer. The gesture hung. Wool and metal. A child’s cry somewhere under the press. Antony’s grin was wide enough to hide whether he had been told to offer or had offered to see what broke.

Caesar refused.

Not once only—refused in a way that let every watcher invent a script: humility, theatre, trap, warning, joke gone wrong. Antony lowered the thing as if lowering a cup he had not meant to empty. The Forum exhaled wrong—half relief, half hunger.

“He will not be king!” someone shouted, relieved.

“He already is!” someone else shouted, not relieved.

In the press, Seius (F2) felt his stomach drop—the sensation of a door opening onto a stair he did not want to climb. He told himself it was only festival. Festival was how politics practised in costume. Beside him a freedman muttered about Parthia and crowns in the same breath, as if the east were a wardrobe.

Afterward Antony drank and laughed and said the symbol was temporary. Fulvia, hearing the laugh later, did not correct him. Temporary symbols were the ones that started wars.

By night the city’s elite rooms had a new oxygen: fear of *regnum*, whether or not a diadem had ever truly fit.

# SC-15 — Veiled Talk

POV: Cassius (Brutus in frame) · portico stroll · App. 2.113 energy  
Info ENTER: Separate private thoughts; diadem still sticky in the city · EXIT: Mutual disclosure begins  
Overhear risk: Medium-High

---

They chose a colonnade where shadows made faces honest and mouths careful. The festival was still sticky in the city’s teeth—the diadem’s ghost, the crowd’s half-cheer, Caesar’s refusal that somehow made kingship louder.

Cassius did not begin with murder. He began with the difference between name and power, because that was how you invited a careful man to stop being careful.

“If he refuses the name and keeps the power,” Cassius said, almost idle, “what do we call the difference? Latin?”

Brutus walked half a step slower. Ancestors walked with him whether he invited them or not—imagined shelves, wax faces, the dull pressure of being someone’s correct grandson.

“We call it the Republic enduring,” Brutus said.

“We call it a man packing for Parthia with the Senate in his purse.” Cassius glanced once—no lingering—at a slave too near, then at a water-seller’s boy whose ears were the wrong age for politics. “I have spoken to men who are tired of enduring. I am asking if you are one of them, or if you prefer to write essays about Cato while the rest of us choose a morning.”

Brutus’ silence lasted long enough to be an answer forming. A cart rattled past on the street beyond the columns; someone cursed a mule as if the mule were a tribune. Brutus waited for the noise to cover him.

“If it were done,” he said at last, “it would have to be law’s face, not a gang’s. The Senate must see a surgery. Not a street.”

“Law’s face,” Cassius agreed, and tasted the first shared permission. No daggers yet. Only contempt with a schedule beginning to grow underneath—men to speak to, rooms to choose, a friend of Caesar’s still unnamed who could open a door.

They walked on as if they had been talking about the weather. In Rome, that was often the same conversation.
