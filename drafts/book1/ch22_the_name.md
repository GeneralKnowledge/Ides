# SC-62 — Caesar

POV: Octavian (+ Cicero glimpse) · Naples / Cumae / Philippus villa · Canon: A8 · Apollonia pack  
Info ENTER: Heir rumour thickening · EXIT: Accepts inheritance; followers say “Caesar”; elders refuse the name

---

Italy smelled different when you arrived as a claim.

Octavian stood in Philippus’ villa light—sea glare, careful furniture, advice thick as oil. Atia’s fear sat in the room without needing a chair: she had already rearranged the cushions twice, as if cushions could refuse a will. Philippus spoke of safety, of refusal, of not taking a dead man’s quarrel into a living throat. Outside, a gardener clipped something that did not need clipping. Noise for courage.

“Refuse the inheritance,” Philippus urged, not looking at Atia when he said it. Looking would have made it a conspiracy between parents. “Refuse the name. Live. Rome will invent another quarrel without you.”

Atia’s hands found the cushion seam and worried it open a thread. “They will kill you for the name,” she said. “Or use you until the name is empty and then kill what is left.”

Octavius—he still heard himself as Octavius in the bones—looked at Agrippa. Agrippa did not nod. He waited. Waiting was a vote. On the terrace rail a ring left a pale band on Octavian’s finger where he had twisted it too hard; he stopped twisting because that, too, was information.

“If I refuse,” Octavian said, stiff with the effort of not sounding eighteen, “I refuse what he wrote when he still breathed. That is a kind of theft from the dead.” He hated the polish. Polish was how you survived rooms like this. “I will take what is mine. The name included.”

Atia’s mouth opened and closed. Philippus looked toward the sea as if the sea might offer a legal opinion. The gardener’s shears stopped. Silence was worse than clipping.

A follower came to the terrace door—young, dusty from the road, eyes already rehearsing loyalty. He bowed wrong, then corrected the bow, then spoke the word as if it were a password he had paid for:

“Caesar.”

Not under the breath. Aloud. The room flinched as if the air had changed temperature. Atia’s face did something private and then went blank. Philippus’s jaw set. Agrippa’s stillness deepened—approval without theatre.

Octavian held the greeting without smiling. Smiling would have made him a boy delighted by a toy. “Tell the others,” he said. “The name stands. So does the will.”

The follower left as if dismissed from a temple. Cicero, visiting the neighbourhood of villas like a man touring symptoms, heard the followers before he saw the boy: *Caesar* said in the courtyard as if the word were already a consul. Cicero’s smile performed courtesy and privately stored a future headache. The boy’s face was still young enough to look temporary. Names were not.

“Philippus will not call him that,” someone murmured.

“Neither will I,” Cicero said lightly, and meant it as taste, not prophecy.

Octavian, alone for a stolen minute on the terrace, shook—once, hard, the body arguing with the decision. Then he stopped shaking because shaking was information enemies could use. He practised the name under his breath until it felt less like costume and more like a weapon he was not yet strong enough to lift cleanly. Agrippa found him there and said nothing useful—only stood where a friend stood when a friend had decided something irreversible. That was enough. Decisions needed witnesses more than they needed applause.

# SC-63 — The Boy

POV: Antony · Rome / Ops orbit · Canon: M13 · Fulvia  
Info ENTER: Consul; veterans near · EXIT: Posts Caesar’s acts; underestimates Octavian

---

Antony counted money the way he counted cohorts: roughly, with threats for arithmetic.

Around the Temple of Ops the city’s paper heart still beat in Caesar’s rhythm—acts, grants, promises. Clerks moved tablets like bricks. A veteran waited with a petition that smelled of wine and land hunger. Antony meant to keep the rhythm. Rhythm kept veterans fed. Fed veterans kept consuls alive.

Fulvia listened to a client’s petition and priced it with her eyes before Antony opened his mouth—how much loyalty, how much cash, how much theatre. He almost smiled. Almost was marriage.

“The boy is in Campania,” an agent said. “Calling himself Caesar. Followers thick as flies on a sweet stall. Some of ours asked whether his purse travels with the name.”

Antony laughed, genuine. The laugh bounced off Ops’ stone and came back sounding larger than the boy. “Let him. Rome has many Caesars in wine shops. When he wants money, he will learn who holds the keys.”

The waiting veteran shifted. “If the name pays land,” he said carefully, “some will listen. Names do not fill bellies. Pay does. But names open doors to pay.”

Fulvia’s eyes flicked to Antony—price, risk, theatre—then away. Antony waved the veteran toward a clerk with a smaller petition and a larger chance of being forgotten.

He posted continuity like piety: Caesar’s acts stand. Ink dried on decrees that kept the dead man’s calendar breathing. The crowd heard stability. Liberators heard a noose made of paperwork. Antony heard himself buying time—and time, in April, still felt cheaper than blood.

“If the boy becomes loud,” Fulvia said, “buy his friends or break them. Do not wait until the wine shops stop laughing.”

“He is a squeak,” Antony said. “I have Decimus to cage and a province to take. I don’t hunt squeaks.”

A clerk asked whether the Campania rumour should be answered in writing. Antony waved him off. Writing gave squeaks a shape. He preferred them as jokes told over wine, already half forgotten by morning.

The underestimation sat in the room like an unopened letter. The veteran’s careful sentence sat beside it. Antony did not open either. He had a city to drink into loyalty, and a name he still thought belonged to a corpse and a consul’s posting board—not to a thin boy practising syllables by the sea. The cost of that thought would not arrive as a letter. It would arrive later, as men who had already chosen which Caesar paid.
