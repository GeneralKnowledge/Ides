**Scenes:** SC-62, SC-63, SC-63b · mid-April–May 44

---

# SC-62: Caesar

POV: Octavian (+ Cicero glimpse) · Naples / Cumae / Philippus villa · Canon: A8 · Apollonia pack
Date / place: Mid-April 44 · Philippus’ villa, Bay of Naples
Info ENTER: Heir rumour thickening · EXIT: Accepts inheritance; followers say “Caesar”; elders refuse the name

---

Italy smelled different when you arrived as a claim.

Octavian stood in Philippus’s villa light: sea glare, careful chairs, advice thick as oil. Atia’s fear sat in the room without needing a seat. She had already rearranged the cushions twice. Cushions could not refuse a will. Philippus spoke of safety, of refusal, of not taking a dead man’s quarrel into a living throat. Outside, a gardener clipped something that didn’t need clipping. Noise for courage.

Below the terrace the villas ran one after another down the Campanian shore, every one of them a senator’s house with a senator’s ears. Philippus had been lifting Octavius onto this same rail since the boy was small enough to be lifted. In the war he had asked Caesar’s leave to stand near no one, got it, and lived. He was handing the boy that trick now as if it were an heirloom.

“Refuse the inheritance,” Philippus urged, not looking at Atia when he said it. Looking would have made it a conspiracy between parents. “Refuse the name. Live. Rome will invent another quarrel without you.”

Atia’s hands found the cushion seam and worried it open a thread. “They will kill you for the name,” she said. “Or use you until the name is empty and then kill what is left.”

Octavius, he still heard himself as Octavius in the bones, looked at Agrippa. Agrippa didn’t nod. He waited, still as a vote cast without theatre. On the terrace rail a ring left a pale band on Octavian’s finger where he had twisted it too hard. He stopped twisting because that, too, was information.

“If I refuse,” Octavian said, stiff with the effort of not sounding eighteen, “I refuse what he wrote when he still breathed. That is theft from the dead.” He hated the polish. Rooms like this required it. “I will take what is mine. The name included.”


Atia’s mouth opened and closed. Philippus looked toward the sea. The gardener’s shears stopped mid-clip.

A follower came to the terrace door, young, dusty from the road, eyes already rehearsing loyalty. He bowed wrong, then corrected the bow, then spoke the word as if it were a password he had paid for:

“Caesar.”

Not under the breath. Aloud. The room flinched. Atia’s face did something private and then went blank. Philippus’s jaw set. Agrippa’s stillness deepened, approval without theatre.

Octavian held the greeting without smiling. Smiling would have made him a boy delighted by a toy. “Tell the others,” he said. “The name stands. So does the will.”

The follower, emboldened, added with a grin meant as gift: “Not merely the hopeful shadow anymore, then…”

Agrippa’s head turned a fraction. Octavian’s voice stayed flat. “The name. Only the name.”

The follower flushed, bowed too deep, and fled.

Cicero had walked over from his own house along the shore to return the boy’s visit of two days before, a dutiful call, all respect and no opinions. He reached the courtyard in time to hear the follower’s *Caesar* going round it as if the word were already a consul. Octavian watched the old man’s smile hold its courtesy while something behind it took a note.

“Philippus will not call him that,” someone murmured.

“Neither will I,” Cicero said lightly, as if it were a matter of taste. Behind him two elders from a neighbouring villa were already converting the courtyard chant into wine-shop sport updated. Not *whether* the boy would squeak, but *how many weeks* until Antony’s laugh bought him cheap. Underestimation had changed costume. It had not retired.

Cicero’s smile held. “They are still exercising,” he murmured, to no one in particular. “Tell me when the exercise stops amusing them.”

Octavian, alone for a stolen minute on the terrace, shook once, hard, the body arguing with the decision. Then he stopped shaking and flattened both palms on the rail until the pale band on his finger stopped moving. He practised the name under his breath, wrong once, then closer. *Gaius Julius Caesar.* A lawyer would tack the old family on the end, *Octavianus*, so the papers showed which house the son had come out of. He left it off. Nobody calling him Caesar from a doorway was going to add it. Agrippa found him there.

“I almost said it first,” Agrippa murmured, not apology, inventory. “Shadow. Old habit.”

“Break it,” Octavian said. Then, quieter, the rare awkward warmth: “You may call me late to drill. Not that.”

Agrippa’s mouth twitched. “Late to drill, then, Caesar.” He stood within reach and didn’t look away.

That evening they walked the villa’s outer path where gardeners had stopped inventing courage with shears. Ordinary noises: sea, a dog, someone’s dinner argument about fish. Octavian counted them the way he counted incompatible rumours, separate, useful. Agrippa kicked a loose stone and watched it roll.

“Philippus still won’t say it,” Agrippa said. “The name.”

“He can keep Octavius for his cushions,” Octavian answered. “The road has already chosen.” He hated how young the sentence sounded. Agrippa did not rescue him with softness. He only matched pace. That was the older friendship: not shadow, legs that kept the same count without announcing need.

# SC-63: The Boy

POV: Antony · Rome / Ops orbit · Canon: M13 · Fulvia
Date / place: April 44 · Temple of Ops, Rome
Info ENTER: Consul; veterans near · EXIT: Posts Caesar’s acts; underestimates Octavian

---

Antony counted money the way he counted cohorts: roughly, with threats for arithmetic.

The keys to the Temple of Ops hung on a clerk’s cord, and the clerk stood where Antony could see him. “Parthia’s money,” the clerk had said the first morning, “under the harvest goddess.” Since the Ides it had answered to the consul. Antony liked the sound the keys made.

Around the temple the city’s paper heart still beat in Caesar’s rhythm: acts, grants, promises. Clerks moved tablets like bricks. A veteran waited with a petition that smelled of wine and land hunger. Antony meant to keep the rhythm. Rhythm kept veterans fed. Fed veterans kept consuls alive.

Fulvia listened to a client’s petition and priced it with her eyes before Antony opened his mouth: how much loyalty, how much cash, how much theatre. He almost smiled. Almost was marriage.

“The boy is in Campania,” an agent said. “Calling himself Caesar. Followers thick as flies on a sweet stall. Some of ours asked whether his purse travels with the name.”

Antony laughed, genuine. The laugh bounced off Ops’ stone and came back sounding larger than the boy. He had met the nephew when the nephew was still inventory. Great-nephew, strictly; Antony never spent the extra word. Too careful with his face. Too hungry for older men’s approval. A chest that looked wrong under a reserved place. That boy did not hold Italy. “Let him. Rome has many Caesars in wine shops. When he wants money, he will learn who holds the keys.”

The waiting veteran shifted. “If the name pays land,” he said carefully, “some will listen. Names do not fill bellies. Pay does. But names open doors to pay.”

Fulvia’s eyes flicked to Antony, price and risk and theatre, then away. Antony waved the veteran toward a clerk with a smaller petition and a larger chance of being forgotten. For a breath the veteran’s sentence almost interested him. Then Decimus’s Cisalpine hunger filled the interest the way a real war fills a map.

He posted continuity like piety: Caesar’s acts stand. Ink dried on decrees that kept the dead man’s calendar breathing. The crowd heard stability. Liberators heard a noose made of paperwork. Antony heard himself buying time. Time, in April, still felt cheaper than blood.

“If the boy becomes loud,” Fulvia said, “buy his friends or break them. Do not wait until the wine shops stop laughing.”

“He is a squeak,” Antony said. “I have Decimus to cage and a province to take. I don’t hunt squeaks.” The word was easy because it kept the boy small enough to mock. Mockery cost less than another front.

Fulvia’s mouth almost smiled. “Squeaks grow teeth if you feed the wine shops. Call him shadow if it comforts you. Campania already stopped.”

“Shadow,” Antony repeated, amused, tasting an officer’s old joke secondhand. “Even better. Shadows don’t hold fasces.”

A clerk asked whether the Campania rumour should be answered in writing. Antony waved him off. Writing gave squeaks a shape. He preferred them as jokes told over wine, already half forgotten by morning.

The veteran’s careful sentence sat in the room like an unopened letter. Antony didn’t open it. He had a city to drink into loyalty, and a name he still thought belonged to a corpse and a posting board.

# SC-63b: Pompey’s Gardens

POV: Octavian · Rome · Canon: T9 plant · App. *BC* 3.14–23
Date / place: May 44 · praetor’s tribunal in the Forum; Antony’s gardens; Philippus’s town house
Info ENTER: Name taken; elders bet weeks · EXIT: Accepts the inheritance in law before Antony’s brother; Antony refuses Caesar’s money; Octavian sells his own land to pay the legacy
Overhear risk: Forum crowd / Antony’s guests

---

The praetor who heard him was an Antonius.

Brutus’s chair had stood empty since April, and wills still had to be heard, so the consul’s younger brother sat in it. Gaius Antonius had the family jaw and less behind it. He looked like a man who had been told the family joke and was waiting for his turn to tell it. Octavian stood below the tribunal in a toga Atia had had brushed twice on the road. Six witnesses stood at his back, Philippus’s choosing, men with good ears and no opinions. Agrippa stood further off by a fruit seller, where a friend could pass for a customer.

“Gaius Octavius, son of Gaius,” the praetor read, and looked up. “You know the form.”

“I accept the inheritance of Gaius Julius Caesar,” Octavian said, “and his name with it.”

He had said the sentence on every stage of the Appian Way until the mule-driver could say it with him. He said it now without the road in it. The clerk wrote. A man in the crowd called *Caesar* a little too loud and a second man hushed him, and the hush carried further than the name.

Gaius Antonius leaned on one elbow, friendly. Octavian had heard that lean described in wine shops; it belonged to the brother. “Mine will want to know you said it clearly.”

“Tell him I did.”

“He’ll laugh.”

“Then he will have heard it,” Octavian said.

The praetor’s eyes went over the crowd the way a steward’s go over a store-room before the master asks. Octavian watched him count. He noted which faces the man stopped on, and that he stopped on the loud one twice.

Antony received him three days later in Pompey’s gardens, out past the theatre on the Campus side, and made him wait at the gate long enough for the waiting to say what Antony would not.

“Pompey’s gardens,” Agrippa said at the gate, looking at the gilded finials. “Pompey’s statue in the Curia. Pompey’s house on the Carinae.” He kicked a pebble off the step. “He collects Pompey.”

Octavian did not answer. Gate slaves had ears and wanted supper.

When the gate opened, the consul was lying on a couch in a garden room that smelled of bath oil and cut box, with a dish of early figs and two friends who did not get up. Antony’s hair was still wet. He looked bigger lying down than most men looked standing. He waved the boy toward a stool. Octavian stood.

“The will leaves three hundred sesterces to every citizen in Rome,” Octavian said. “The heir pays the legacies. The money he left is in the Temple of Ops. I have come for it.”

Antony ate a fig. He took his time over it, as if the boy might change his mind before he swallowed.

“The money he left,” he said, “was the state’s money, and the state has spent it. Your great-uncle ran the treasury like a camp chest. Half of what was in it was promised before you were off the boat.” He wiped his fingers on a napkin a slave held out. “And while you were learning Greek at Apollonia, boy, I stood in that Senate the day after the Ides and kept his acts alive. If I had not, they would have thrown him in the Tiber like a dead tyrant, and his will after him, and his name with it. You are standing in my garden on a name I saved. You might call that a legacy.”

One of the friends laughed. Antony let him.

Behind the couch a clerk went past on some errand, a cord at his belt with keys on it. Octavian saw the keys and did not look at them twice.

“Then the people will be paid from my own estate,” he said.

Antony’s eyebrows went up. He had good eyebrows for it. “With what?”

“With what is mine.”

“Your father’s farms at Velitrae?” Antony laughed, generous with it. “Sell them, then. Sell your mother’s jewels. Rome will drink your health for a month and forget the face. Go home to Philippus. He has the right idea. He sat out the whole war and he is the best-fed man in Campania.”

Octavian found that he was standing with his weight on one hip and a hand loose at his side, as the consul stood when he stood, and he set both feet square again. He did not know how long he had been copying the man. Long enough for the friends to see it, perhaps. He bowed the exact depth owed a consul by a private citizen, no more, and went out past the gate slaves into the evening.

“That went well,” Agrippa said on the road back, which was the nearest Agrippa came to a joke.

“It went,” Octavian said. “He told me what he has. A treasury and a laugh. Now I know what I have.”

At Philippus’s town house that night the lamps burned late. A banker’s freedman sat at the table with tablets: the Velitrae land, a house, a share in a Campanian vineyard that had come down through the Octavii, a list of slaves with their trades beside their names. Atia came in with a box under her arm and set it on the table without a word, and Octavian understood that the box was her jewels and that she had brought them herself so that no slave could be blamed for carrying them.

Philippus looked at the list the way he had looked at the sea at Puteoli, as a thing that would go on with or without his leave. “You are selling your father’s land,” he said, “to pay a dead man’s promise to strangers.”

“To every citizen in the city,” Octavian said. “Strangers vote. Strangers stand in the Forum when the consul speaks.” He pushed the box back toward his mother. “Not these. Not yet.”

Atia did not take it. She left it on the table and went out.

The freedman held the first sale tablet ready. Octavian took off the ring, warmed the wax over the lamp, and pressed the seal. The pale band on his finger showed for the time it took, and then the ring was back on it.
