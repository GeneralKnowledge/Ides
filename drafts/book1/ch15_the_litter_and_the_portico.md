# SC-40 — Sour Birds

POV: Calpurnia  
Date / place: Morning 15 March 44 · Domus Publica after litter departs  
Canon refs: M2 · M4 · Suet. 81 · DIO-OMEN-1  
Info ENTER: DREAM-1 spent; Caesar gone with Decimus  
Info EXIT: Stay lost; bad sacrifice talk floods the house; she waits without power  
Overhear risk: Extreme

---

The vestibule still held the smell of the litter—wool, leather, the oil they rubbed into poles so the wood would not raise blisters on other men’s shoulders. Calpurnia stood in it like a woman who had missed a boat by the width of a hand.

Phileros talked too much, which meant he was afraid. “The chickens were sullen, domina. The boy said so. And the exta—someone said the liver looked insulted.”

“Someone,” Calpurnia said.

“A man from the college. Or a man who knows a man from the college.” Phileros’ shoulders performed helplessness. “They always look insulted if you want them to.”

Nysa passed with an armful of yesterday’s tablets, eyes down. Calpurnia almost asked what the girl had tried to do at the door. She did not. Questions made servants invent answers.

In the atrium the painted pediment—still whole, still smiling its public smile—seemed to lean. Calpurnia knew that was her mind. She also knew minds could be right while looking foolish.

A physician’s slave arrived with a message no one had requested: if Caesar felt the old swimming in the skull, rest was piety. The message was late on purpose. Late advice was how careful men stayed employed after disasters.

Calpurnia sent the slave away with courtesy sharp enough to cut.

She walked to the cubiculum and closed the door that had opened itself in the night. The latch was ordinary. Ordinary things had begun to feel like insults.

“He will come back,” she told the empty bed.

The bed did not argue. That was its only virtue.

From the street rose the ordinary roar of the Ides—markets, oaths, a woman laughing as if laughter were still allowed. Calpurnia listened for a different sound and hated herself for inventing it in advance.

# SC-41 — Among Other Papers

POV: Decimus  
Date / place: Litter route Domus Publica → Campus Martius / Curia Pompeia approach  
Canon refs: A5 · M5 · NOTE-1 (Artemidorus-tradition) · MAP-1 travel 20–35 min  
Info ENTER: Caesar in litter; attendance secured  
Info EXIT: Delivered to Senate threshold; unread warning among papers; Decimus peels toward role  
Overhear risk: Medium (crowd; note-hander)

---

The litter swayed. Caesar sat as he sat in triumphs and in mud—half present, half already in the next room. Decimus walked beside the near pole when the street narrowed, close enough to speak without shouting.

“Short session,” he said again, because repetition was a kind of rope. “You dismiss them if the birds offend you. You will have been seen. That is what matters.”

Caesar made a small sound that might have been assent. His hands rested on a stack of tablets and rolls someone had thrust in at the vestibule—petitions, lists, the city’s endless paper hunger. Decimus did not look too long at the stack. Looking too long was how men with knives under togas started sweating through wool.

Near the turning toward the Campus, a man pressed in—Greek face, urgent manners, the particular sweat of someone who believed information could still alter a morning. He pushed a small sealed tablet toward Caesar’s hands.

“Read this,” the man said, or something near it. “Read it now. Alone.”

Caesar took it. Of course he took it. He took everything. Taking was cheaper than arguing in a crowd. He slid the tablet into the stack with the others, among other papers, and the litter moved on because litters moved on.

Decimus felt a cold stitch under the ribs. He did not know what the tablet said. He knew only that mornings were full of tablets, and that if he snatched at this one he would look like a thief in front of the man whose trust he was currently wearing like a clean cloak.

“Friends everywhere,” Decimus said lightly. “Even the street writes to you.”

Caesar did not open it.

At the portico of Pompey the air changed—shade, columns, the smell of stone that had watched other men’s ambitions. Senators clustered in the false casualness of men who had arrived early on purpose. Decimus saw Casca’s shoulder, Tillius’ mouth already shaped for petition, Cassius too still. Brutus stood as if carved for a different century.

Antony’s laugh carried from farther along the portico—big, soldierly, not yet afraid. Trebonius was already angling toward that laugh with the patience of a door.

Decimus helped Caesar down. Their hands met once more. Caesar’s palm was dry.

“Go in,” Decimus said, and smiled the smile that had opened Spanish gates. “I will see to the edges.”

He did not go in at once. Edges meant Antony. Edges meant keeping the consul’s body in the colonnade while the room inside did what rooms did when enough men had decided.

Caesar entered the Curia of Pompey among men who loved him, hated him, owed him, and—Decimus knew with a clarity that felt like nausea—needed him stopped before the Parthian clocks started again.

Decimus wiped his clean hands on his tunic, once, where no one would see, and went to find Trebonius’ eye.

# SC-42 — Hold Him with Talk

POV: Antony  
Date / place: Porticus of Pompey · morning 15 March 44  
Canon refs: M6 · CHOREO-1.8 · Trebonius  
Info ENTER: Consul arriving for Senate business  
Info EXIT: Held in conversation by Trebonius; hears disturbance late  
Overhear risk: Medium

---

Antony liked the Theatre of Pompey complex the way soldiers liked any place with shade and a crowd to read. Columns made people smaller. Smaller people told the truth faster, or lied worse—either was useful.

He arrived with the loose stride of a man who had drunk enough last night to prove he was alive and not enough to prove he was soft. The Ides were a workday. Caesar had called them; Caesar would talk ships and provinces and the endless eastern itch; Antony would agree where agreement bought him quiet and argue where argument bought him men.

Trebonius found him before he reached the Curia door.

“Marcus,” Trebonius said, warm as old campaign wine. “A word. Not even a word—three. You’ll thank me for keeping you from the first hour’s boredom.”

Antony grinned. “If it’s boredom, send a tribune. If it’s money, speak.”

“It’s a man in my debt who swears he has news from Gaul that won’t keep.” Trebonius’ hand on Antony’s elbow was friendly. Friendly hands steered. Antony allowed himself to be steered a few paces into the portico’s shoulder, where the Senate’s murmur became a wall.

They spoke of nothing that mattered: a cavalry remount, a joke about a legate’s mistress, whether Hirtius wrote better than he fought. Trebonius was good at nothing-that-mattered. Antony found himself laughing, then found the laugh lasting a little too long, as if the morning wanted him facing the wrong way.

A thin suspicion crossed him—not plot, not dagger, only the soldier’s sense that a door had been closed behind his back. He glanced toward the Curia.

“Caesar’s gone in,” Antony said.

“Caesar’s always gone in,” Trebonius answered. “That’s why we have to catch our business in the colonnade like tradesmen.”

The suspicion sat down and became patience. Antony was consul. Consuls were not dragged by the sleeve unless they chose to be. He chose, for three more breaths, to be polite to a colleague.

Then the sound changed.

Not a speech-sound. Not a vote-sound. A human noise wrong in the throat—shout stacked on shout, the scrape of benches, something like a scream cut short and replaced by many feet.

Antony’s body understood before his politics did. He shook Trebonius’ hand off without thinking it rude.

“What in—”

Trebonius’ face was still warm. That was the obscenity. Warmth, while the Curia poured men out like a broken jar—togas, bare heads, eyes too white.

“Caesar,” someone yelled, and then the name became a dozen other words: blood, knives, Brutus, liberty, murder, the consul, where is the consul—

Antony felt for a weapon he had not brought into a Senate morning. His palm found only the cloth of his tunic.

Trebonius said something mild. Antony did not keep it. Mildness was a kind of knife too.

He ran—not toward philosophy, toward the door—and the crowd ran with him and against him, a single animal with too many mouths.
