# SC-87 — Necessity

POV: Cassius · Asia / Syria orbit  
Date / place: City audience / interpreters  
Canon refs: Chain 8 · C3  
Info ENTER: Need pay · EXIT: Liberty felt as extraction  
Overhear risk: Interpreters

---

Cassius listened to a city’s elders explain poverty with practiced dignity.

The hall smelled of oil and fear-sweat dressed as perfume. Outside, harbour noise argued with temple bells. A fly walked the rim of a cup as if auditing the room. “Liberty,” he said, “is not free. Rome’s disease is expensive. You will help cure it.” He did not raise his voice. Mathematics did not need volume. Volume was for men who still believed Forum weather could be shouted down.

He let silence work after the sentence—Brutus’ tool, borrowed without credit. Silence made elders fill the air with offers.

Interpreters softened the edges—turned *pay* into *contribute*, *now* into *in due season*. Softening did not soften the silver leaving. A chest lid knocked like a bad omen; Cassius filed it under *hinge*. He did not flinch. Flinching was Brutus’ private hobby. Publicly Cassius was mathematics in a cuirass—the recruitment engine turned tax engine without changing the face that had once counted dinner slight and dinner power.

A young local clerk’s face did the arithmetic of hatred. An elder’s hand trembled on a staff that had seen other conquerors use prettier nouns. Cassius filed both under *later problem* and signed for ships. Later problems, he told himself, were what victory was for. Philippi was a place on a road, not a prophecy—only a field where numbers would have to stand up and fight.

An officer asked if they were becoming what they killed. Cassius said, “We are becoming solvent. Solvency is a kind of liberty the Forum never priced honestly.” He meant it. That was the danger. He dismissed the elders with courtesy sharp enough to cut. Courtesy, like taxes, could be a blade if held correctly.

On the steps, wind off the harbour tried to steal a paper. Cassius pinned it with a thumb and smiled without kindness. Papers that escaped became rumours. Rumours became prices. He preferred prices he set himself.

# SC-88 — EID MAR

POV: Brutus · East camp / mint  
Date / place: Mint tent / camp edge · coin pack  
Canon refs: *EID MAR* · Chain 8 · C2  
Info ENTER: Philosophical self-image · EXIT: Coin brand; crack vs exaction  
Overhear risk: Officers

---

The dies showed daggers and the pileus—*EID MAR*—as if a date could be a sacrament.

Brutus held a fresh coin. Pretty. Clean. Lies were often clean. The metal still held the heat of striking. Around him, officers argued yields and marches with the same mouths that praised ancestors. One man weighed a sack and called the weight patriotic. Brutus did not correct the adjective. Correction would have required a philosophy the sack could not carry. He thought of Porcia, of masks at home, of soft chains that had once felt like clemency, of Lanuvium’s gravel knowing his sandals—and shut the thoughts before they became speeches no army would wait for.

“We are not pirates,” an officer said, too loud, as if volume could wash the tax roads clean.

“We are paying an army,” Brutus answered. “History can argue the nouns. The army argues in grain and miles.”

One honest doubt crossed him: *If Cato could see the ledgers—* He shut it down. Doubt before the plain was a luxury tax. He kept the coin anyway—brand as comfort, brand as trap—and walked out where the camp smelled of men who would spend *libertas* as wages without reading the Latin on the metal.

A mint worker burned his finger and cursed without poetry. Brutus gave him a nod that was almost kindness and walked on before kindness became a speech.

Cassius, somewhere else on the same war, would call this necessity. Brutus called it necessity too, and hated that their mouths agreed while their sleep did not. Sleep, unlike coinage, still refused to clear. In the dark he held the warm coin until it cooled, then put it away as if putting away could stop a crack from spreading.
