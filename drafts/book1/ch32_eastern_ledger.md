# SC-87 — Necessity

POV: Cassius · Asia / Syria orbit  
Date / place: City audience / interpreters  
Canon refs: Chain 8 · C3  
Info ENTER: Need pay · EXIT: Liberty felt as extraction  
Overhear risk: Interpreters

---

Cassius listened to a city’s elders explain poverty with practiced dignity.

The hall smelled of oil and fear-sweat dressed as perfume. Outside, harbour noise argued with temple bells. A fly walked the rim of a cup as if auditing the room. “Liberty,” he said, “is not free. Rome’s disease is expensive. You will help cure it.” He did not raise his voice. Mathematics did not need volume.

He let silence work after the sentence—Brutus’ tool, borrowed without credit. Silence made elders fill the air with offers.

Interpreters softened the edges—turned *pay* into *contribute*, *now* into *in due season*. Softening did not soften the silver leaving. A chest lid knocked; Cassius noted the hinge and did not flinch. Publicly he was mathematics in a cuirass—the recruitment engine turned tax engine without changing the face that had once counted dinner slight and dinner power.

A young local clerk’s face did the arithmetic of hatred. An elder’s hand trembled on a staff that had seen other conquerors use prettier nouns. Cassius signed for ships anyway.

Before the audience closed, a courier from the west—dust, salt, a seal cracked by too many hands—brought news that arrived as three incompatible tablets: Mutina won and lost; consuls dead; lists in Italy; Decimus gone; the boy with fasces; Antony still breathing. Cassius kept all three. Incomplete west-news was still useful if you labelled it. He did not halt the extraction for a rumour’s sake. Philippi was a place on a road, not a prophecy—only a field where numbers would have to stand up and fight.

An officer asked if they were becoming what they killed. Cassius said, “We are becoming solvent. Solvency is liberty the Forum never priced honestly.” He meant it. That was the danger. He dismissed the elders with courtesy sharp enough to cut.

On the steps, wind off the harbour tried to steal a paper. Cassius pinned it with a thumb and smiled without kindness. Papers that escaped became rumours. Rumours became prices. He preferred prices he set himself.

# SC-88 — EID MAR

POV: Brutus · East camp / mint  
Date / place: Mint tent / camp edge · coin pack  
Canon refs: *EID MAR* · Chain 8 · C2  
Info ENTER: Philosophical self-image · EXIT: Coin brand; crack vs exaction  
Overhear risk: Officers

---

The dies showed daggers and the pileus—*EID MAR*—as if a date could be a sacrament.

Brutus held a fresh coin. Pretty. Clean. Lies were often clean. The metal still held the heat of striking. Around him, officers argued yields and marches with the same mouths that praised ancestors. One man weighed a sack and called the weight patriotic. Brutus did not correct the adjective. Correction would have required a philosophy the sack could not carry. He thought of Porcia, of masks at home, of soft chains that had once felt like clemency, of Lanuvium’s gravel—and shut the thoughts before they became speeches no army would wait for.

“We are not pirates,” an officer said, too loud, as if volume could wash the tax roads clean.

“We are paying an army,” Brutus answered. “History can argue the nouns. The army argues in grain and miles.”

News from Italy had reached the mint tent as gossip: lists, fasces, a boy paid in fear. Brutus did not ask which version was true. Truth west of the water had become a peacetime hobby. One honest doubt crossed him: *If Cato could see the ledgers—* He shut it down. Doubt before the plain was a luxury tax. He kept the coin anyway—brand as comfort, brand as trap—and walked out where the camp smelled of men who would spend *libertas* as wages without reading the Latin on the metal.

A mint worker burned his finger and cursed without poetry. Brutus gave him a nod that was almost kindness and walked on before kindness became a speech.

Cassius, somewhere else on the same war, would call this necessity. Brutus called it necessity too, and hated that their mouths agreed while their sleep did not. Sleep, unlike coinage, still refused to clear. In the dark he held the warm coin until it cooled, then put it away as if putting away could stop a crack from spreading.
