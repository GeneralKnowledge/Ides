# SC-87 — Necessity

POV: Cassius · Asia / Syria orbit  
Date / place: City audience / interpreters  
Canon refs: Chain 8 · C3  
Info ENTER: Need pay · EXIT: Liberty felt as extraction  
Overhear risk: Interpreters

---

Cassius listened to a city’s elders explain poverty with practiced dignity.

The hall smelled of oil and fear-sweat dressed as perfume. Outside, harbour noise argued with temple bells. A fly walked the rim of a cup as if auditing the room. “Liberty costs,” he said. “Rome’s disease is expensive. You will help cure it.” He didn't raise his voice.

He let silence work after the sentence—Brutus’ tool, borrowed without credit. Silence made elders fill the air with offers—most of them dates that were not dates.

Interpreters softened the edges—turned *pay* into *contribute*, *now* into *in due season*. Softening did not soften the silver leaving. A chest lid knocked; Cassius noted the hinge and did not flinch. Publicly he was mathematics in a cuirass—the recruitment engine turned tax engine without changing the face that had once counted dinner slight and dinner power.

An elder with a staff stepped forward before the interpreters could varnish him. “We can send half by the Kalends,” he said in Greek plain enough that Cassius needed no cushion. “The other half after harvest. Take half now and we remain a city. Take all now and you march past hungry walls that will not open for your return.”

The interpreter began, “What he means—”

“I heard what he means,” Cassius said. He had an officer weigh the nearest sack on the spot—grain, not rhetoric. The weight came short of the tablet’s claim by enough to insult geometry. The elder did not look away. Cassius let three breaths pass without raising his voice—long enough for a shout that did not come. He gave them a number instead: half now, sealed; the rest in thirty days under hostage sureties from two merchant houses already in the hall. “Refuse the sureties,” he added, still quiet, “and I take the full claim today and leave your walls the problem of explaining hunger to your own people.”

The elder’s mouth worked. He took the half and the sureties. Hatred stayed on the young clerk’s face; survival stayed on the elder’s. Cassius signed for ships on that arithmetic.

Before the audience closed, a courier from the west—dust, salt, a seal cracked by too many hands—brought news that arrived as three incompatible tablets: Mutina won and lost; consuls dead; lists in Italy; Decimus gone; the boy with fasces; Antony still breathing. Cassius kept all three and didn't merge them. He didn't halt the extraction for a rumour’s sake. Philippi was a place on a road, not a prophecy.

An officer asked if they were becoming what they killed. Cassius watched the short-weight sack being restacked and said only, “We are becoming paid.” He didn't offer the Forum a prettier noun. He dismissed the elders with a bow that meant *paid*.

On the steps, wind off the harbour tried to steal a paper. Cassius pinned it with a thumb and didn't smile. Escaped papers became other men’s geometry. He preferred his own.

# SC-88 — EID MAR

POV: Brutus · East camp / mint  
Date / place: Mint tent / camp edge · coin pack  
Canon refs: *EID MAR* · Chain 8 · C2  
Info ENTER: Philosophical self-image · EXIT: Coin brand; crack vs exaction  
Overhear risk: Officers

---

The dies showed daggers and the pileus—*EID MAR*—as if a date could be a sacrament.

Brutus held a fresh coin. The metal still held the heat of striking. Around him, officers argued yields and marches with the same mouths that praised ancestors. One man weighed a sack and called the weight patriotic. Brutus’s thumb stayed on the *EID MAR* lettering and did not lift to correct the adjective. He thought of Porcia, of masks at home, of Lanuvium’s gravel—and shut the thoughts before they became speeches no army would wait for.

“We are not pirates,” an officer said, too loud, as if volume could wash the tax roads clean.

“We are paying an army,” Brutus answered. “Call it what you must. The men still need grain and miles.”

News from Italy had reached the mint tent as gossip: lists, fasces, a boy paid in fear. Brutus didn't ask which version was true—asking would've required a Forum he no longer owned. One honest doubt crossed him: *If Cato could see the ledgers—* He shut it down and felt the shut as shame, not cleverness. He kept the coin anyway—brand as comfort, brand as trap—and walked out where the camp smelled of men who would spend *libertas* as wages without reading the Latin on the metal.

A mint worker burned his finger and cursed without poetry. Brutus gave him a nod that was almost kindness and walked on before kindness became a speech.

Cassius, somewhere else on the same war, would call this necessity. Brutus called it necessity too, and hated that their mouths agreed while their sleep did not. Sleep, unlike coinage, still refused to clear. In the dark he held the warm coin until it cooled, then put it away as if putting away could stop a crack from spreading.
