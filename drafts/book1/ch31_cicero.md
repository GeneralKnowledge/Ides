# SC-86 — The Voice Ends

POV: Cicero · Formiae coast road  
Info ENTER: Flight from proscription · EXIT: Murder — oratory loses to arms

---

Cicero had always believed a sentence could outrun a sword if the sentence were good enough.

The litter swayed on the Formiae road. Sea air lifted the curtain edges. He had left too late and too early—late for safety, early for the dignity of a man who still thought negotiation might grow legs. Proscription had turned his name into a price. He tried to treat the price as a rhetorical problem and failed.

Atticus lived in his head as audience. So did the Philippics—pages that had felt like legions when he wrote them. He saw, with a clarity that arrived like nausea, that paper had never been a cohort. He had aimed a boy at Antony and called it statecraft. The boy had learned. Antony had remembered.

“Faster,” he told the bearers, then hated the undignified word.

When the killers found the road, his attendants made the noises attendants make—loyalty, panic, the math of their own skins. Cicero put his head out enough to be seen. He had looked at juries this way: measuring, already composing.

He did not deliver a final Philippic. Rhetoric needed breath and an hour. He had seconds and a neck.

He offered them the stillness he had practised in courts when the verdict was already known. If composure was vanity, let vanity be his last client.

The blow came. Pain was brief and total and insultingly ordinary. Rome’s most expensive voice became silence with blood in it.

He did not learn what they would do with head and hands. That knowledge belonged to other rooms.
