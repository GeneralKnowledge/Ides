# SC-86 — The Voice Ends

Cicero had always believed a sentence could outrun a sword if the sentence were good enough.

The litter swayed on the Formiae road. Sea air lifted the curtain edges and brought salt that tasted like unfinished letters—Atticus unanswered, Philippics still warm in memory like a fever that thought it was an army. He had left too late and too early: late for safety, early for the dignity of a man who still thought negotiation might grow legs if fed enough clauses.

Proscription had turned his name into a price. He tried to treat the price as a rhetorical problem and failed. Rhetoric needed an audience that was not hunting. He catalogued, helplessly, the old skills: period, antithesis, the pause that made juries lean. None of them lengthened a road.

Atticus lived in his head as audience. So did the Capitol’s first day after the knives, enlarged by memory into courage. So did the boy he had aimed at Antony and called statecraft. The boy had learned. Antony had remembered. Somewhere between them, lists had become the Republic’s new literature. Lanuvium’s polite despair felt, suddenly, like a kinder country—cages with views, not prices with blades.

He remembered telling a friend that the Republic still had a voice. The friend had said the Republic had Cicero’s voice. The distinction, once clever, now walked beside the litter like a creditor. He had waved caution away with a jury gesture once; the gesture had not grown a cohort.

A boy selling fish by the verge stared at the litter as if fame were a colour. Cicero almost shaped a greeting and then did not. Greeting belonged to mornings when names were not prices. He counted the poles’ rhythm and tried to turn it into a period; the period refused to close.

“Faster,” he told the bearers, then hated the undignified word. Speed was not eloquence. Speed was what animals did when the forest closed. He tried a mental period about courage and found only the rhythm of poles on stone.

When the killers found the road, his attendants made the noises attendants make—loyalty, panic, the math of their own skins. Someone begged; someone ran; someone stayed because staying was also a calculation, and calculations had always been Cicero’s neighbours if not his friends. He put his head out enough to be seen. He had looked at juries this way: measuring, already composing, already knowing the verdict lived outside the speech.

He did not deliver a final Philippic. Rhetoric needed breath and an hour. He had seconds and a neck. He offered them the stillness he had practised in courts when the verdict was already known. If composure was vanity, let vanity be his last client. Vanity, at least, had always answered his letters. He almost shaped a line about the Republic and let it die unspoken—mercy to the air.

He thought, absurdly, of a softened sentence he had once drafted for Atticus about extraordinary medicine. Medicine had become the list. The list had become this road.

The blow came. Pain was brief and total and insultingly ordinary—no period, no antithesis, no applause. Rome’s most expensive voice became silence with blood in it.

The sea kept sounding like itself. That offended him more than the blades—nature’s refusal to annotate.

He did not learn what they would do with head and hands. That knowledge belonged to other rooms, other markets, other men who priced trophies. His last clear thought was not a slogan. It was the petty wish that Atticus would understand the silence without needing a courier to explain it—and that the boy would not mistake silence for permission.
