## A. Executive assessment

**Craft strengths.** The manuscript already possesses a recognisable artistic identity: incomplete information as drama; money/land/pay as moral weather; plural motives; humour that does not wink; a gold Ides spine (esp. Ch 14–16, 19) that achieves Gardner’s “vivid continuous dream.” F4 Hordeonius, F1 Nysa, and F3 Laberius are not decorative — they supply social sensors historical principals cannot. Sentence-level control in the gold spine is often excellent: concrete nouns, earned metaphors, restraint about theme.

**Largest craft weaknesses (distinct from mere shortness).**
1. **House-style aphorism engine overused.** Patterns such as *X was how…*, *a kind of…*, *filed under…*, and *weather* as metaphor (~60 body uses) create voice identity — and, outside the characters for whom they are dictionary-true, flatten POV distinction into one witty narrator.
2. **Lean chapters substitute epigram for ordeal.** Mid-tier ~600-word chapters often *name* a McKee value change in a closing aphorism rather than *dramatising* the struggle that produces it (esp. Ch 25–29, 33–34).
3. **Emotional aftermath underfed relative to dread.** The book invests densest craft in pre-Ides dread; mourning, betrayal hangover, and Philippi suffering remain comparatively schematic (Cron: external event without enough internal causality on the page).

**Most promising artistic qualities to protect.** The novel’s argument is not “republic vs tyranny” as slogan but that **paper, pay, rumour, and unfinished sentences** decide empires before battle standards do. SIDE-1 OPEN-0, NOTE-1 dual tracks, disputed last words, and Octavian-not-Augustus are craft choices, not leftovers. Future revision must deepen scenes *inside* that identity — not sand it into generic prestige historical fiction.

## B. The twenty-book synthesis

Books are grouped into five practical lenses. Conflicts between approaches are resolved by the governing philosophy in the mission brief (canon and voice first; dramatisation before polish).

| Lens | Books | Core principles used here |
|------|-------|---------------------------|
| **A — Voice, prose, readability** | King; Strunk & White; Zinsser; Pinker; Browne/King; Handley; Dillard; Goldberg; Lamott | Cut throat-clearing and duplicate explanation; prefer concrete perception; one idea per beat; do not kill rhythm for false brevity; revise in passes (Lamott); clarity over ornamental period pastiche |
| **B — Scenes, action, tension** | Gardner; McKee; Bell; Kiteley; Lamott; Goldberg | Scene = want + resistance + change; maintain continuous dream; specificity serves pressure; constraints can unlock stuck scenes (Kiteley) without gimmickry |
| **C — Psychology & emotion** | Maass; Cron (*Wired*, *Story Genius*); Gardner; King | External event must hit an assumption; moral dilemma with cost; reader asks “what happens next *to this person*?”; no manufactured trauma |
| **D — Plot, structure, momentum** | Snyder; Bell; McKee; Vogler; Maass; Cron | Causality over coincidence; turning points change available choices; use Save the Cat / Journey only as diagnostics — do not force Roman history into midpoints or hero’s journeys |
| **E — Originality & discipline** | Pressfield; Kleon; Dillard; Goldberg; Lamott | Steal craft, not plot/voice; distinguish real problems from Resistance tinkering; protect what is already distinctive |

**Conflicts resolved in favour of this manuscript:**
- **King “2nd draft = less” vs LEN-1 / Maass depth:** Do not compress the gold spine to “fix” leanness. Expand underfed consequence scenes first; apply King cuts *inside* overwritten micro-passages and template aphorisms later.
- **Snyder beat sheet vs historical chronology:** Missing “Fun & Games” or “Dark Night” labels are not defects if causality and cost are on the page.
- **Vogler hero’s journey vs ensemble:** Octavian is not Luke Skywalker; Decimus is not a redeemed antihero arc on schedule. Refuse forced journeys.
- **Strunk brevity vs Gardner richness:** Long sentences in Ch 14 dream prose that carry perception are assets; long sentences that only nest abstractions are liabilities.

## F. Prose and voice audit

### Exemplary passages worth preserving (do not “normalize”)
- **Ch 14 SC-35a:** pediment as face-muscles; blood as cut-finger heat; “weather of a man who had survived by not answering too early.”
- **Ch 2 SC-04:** “Virtue does not clear”; warehouse Republic — F4 identity peak.

- **Ch 31:** ordinary pain; Atticus silence wish — vanity without sermon.
- **Ch 33 SC-91:** incomplete messenger as murder weapon.

### Recurrent sentence-level patterns (genuine, not isolated)

| Pattern | Approx. scale | When it works | When it fails |
|---------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| *weather* as metaphor | ~60 body hits | Caesar opacity; Forum climate; F1 filing | Every chapter’s default noun for politics |
| *filed / entered under* | ~21 | Octavian inventory; F4 ledgers | Non-clerks “filing” feelings |
| *was how…* | ~29 | Character thesis after earned beat | Replaces the beat itself |
| *a kind of…* | ~15 | Tentative perception | Softens every abstraction |
| Aphoristic closer | Frequent in lean chs | F4 sincerity comedy | Same cadence from Brutus, Antony, Laberius, OCT |
| Image then gloss | Common | Rarely needed | “He aimed it” after aiming already shown (some Ch 19 compress already helped) |

### Over-explanation / theme announcement
- Mild narrator future-leans: Ch 1 “would later write better”; Ch 28 “plains would later”; Ch 34 “officers would later swear.”
- Ch 34 SC-95 risks essay (“Hooks only…”) — continuity voice leaking into story voice.
- Ch 27 italic thought *hilt/metal* is sharp but borders on thesis card — keep if embodied in gesture.

### Voice inconsistencies
- **Ch 7 SC-16:** Antony-tagged scene carries Seius gut + Fulvia prophecy-flavored aside.
- Cross-POV witty negation (“He did not X. X was for men who…”) has become a **project dialect**. Native for Cassius/F4; corrosive when universal.
- Mid-tier expansions sometimes sound like polished planning notes with metaphors attached (esp. Ch 26, 27 parley).

### Highest-value sentence-level improvements (when touching a file)
1. Cut one *weather/filed/was how* per page unless POV-native.
2. Delete gloss sentences that restate the prior image.
3. Replace abstract Brutus/Cicero hinges with one observable behaviour (Maass/King).
4. Soften future-leans to present inadequacy.
5. Do **not** run a global adverb purge — adverbs are not the disease here; **template profundity** is.

## K. Final editorial verdict

1. **What is the novel doing particularly well?**  
   It has a rare thematic identity — power as incomplete information, money, and unfinished paper — and a gold Ides sequence whose prose already meets a serious literary standard while holding hard historical locks.

2. **What most prevents it from reaching its potential?**  
   Not lack of clever sentences — **lack of ordeal time and emotional deposit** outside the Ides spine, plus a spreading **aphorism template** that can make dissimilar minds sound like one editor.

3. **What should be fixed first?**  
   Phase **1D** consequence ordeal (Ch 25–29, 33–34): dramatise Mutina→Philippi costs with scene want/resistance/change, without breaking canon.

4. **What should deliberately be left alone?**  
   Ch 14–16 core; NOTE-1 / OPEN-0 / AMB-2 / disputed last words; F4 comedy engine; SERIES-1 ending boundary; conspirator plurality.

5. **What would constitute a successful next revision?**  
   A cold reader finishing Ch 34 feels they read **one novel** whose second half hurts; Decimus’s end and Philippi register as lived consequences of Ch 9 and Ch 16; new prose deepens POV difference rather than cloning *weather/filed/was how*; locks still hold.
