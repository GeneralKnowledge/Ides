## 1. Executive assessment

**Does the manuscript currently feel compelling as a complete novel?**  
**Yes, with a soft mid-band.** A cold reader who continues for *these people* will get: Decimus’s friendship-as-key; Brutus’s brand colliding with creditors and mud; Cassius’s mathematics failing in fog; Antony’s charm and underestimation; Octavian learning without destiny music; Cicero’s almost-courage and Formiae silence; F-tier ledger/street/eagle truth. The Ides (14–16) and funeral (19) remain the highest engagement peaks. Mutina→lists→Philippi now carry ordeal enough that history alone is not why you turn pages.

**Strongest dramatic qualities**

1. **Unequal knowledge as engine** — NOTE-1 dual, false Antony-dead, Cassius’s incomplete INF, Plancus soft silence.  
2. **Spoken subject ≠ real subject** — Galba’s patron note; spare-Antony garden; Antium “advice”; Bononia shopping lists.  
3. **Cost attached to choice** — burned courtesy; brother’s winter; GO under Kalends; priced ford; chalk-wall spelling.  
4. **Controlled dark humour** where earned — Hordeonius refuse-sale; lists-as-shopping; wine-shop Caesars — without joking Formiae or Curia knives.

**Most important engagement risks**

1. **Ch 20–23 trough** — necessary logistics/letters/flinch with too little same-room opposition after funeral heat.  
2. **Epigram convergence under pressure** — especially Decimus/Octavian “They will say many things…” mirror (Ch 29); mild Cassius↔Hordeonius solvency wit.  
3. **Static or essay-adjacent connective** — Ch 5, 11, 32; Ch 34 hooks that summarise the sequel.  
4. **Forcing humour** into grief/terror scenes would damage the book — restraint is already correct there.

**Overall:** The novel holds a reader who wants character consequence. It does **not** need another expansion wave or a joke quota. The smallest high-value set is listed in §6; **implement nothing before the author’s cold read**.
