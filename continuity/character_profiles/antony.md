# M. Antonius

**Type:** Historical · Regular POV  
**Function:** Body/charm; aftermath engine; underestimation; soldiers’ pay; funeral aim

## Identity
Consul; Caesarian heir-apparent of force; Ops paper; Mutina besieger; Philippi victor who misreads Italy.

## Core drive
Keep rhythm — pay, veterans, continuity of Caesar’s acts — while buying time with charm and contempt.

## Soft spots / fears
Soft hands / Liberators’ legality; being left alive as their “gift”; the boy becoming loud (dismissed too long).

## Perception filter
Bodies, wine, smoke counts, posting boards, who still drinks to which Caesar.

## Voice & humour
Swagger on purpose; never permanent clown. Jokes that price rooms. Dangerous underestimation as laugh.

## Loyalty map
Caesar’s name as feeding tool · Fulvia as co-pricer · soldiers · self-survival · Lepidus as useful weight.

## Information boundaries
Does not know Octavian’s endgame; hears Decimus’s end late/wrong; Italy land rage underestimated after Philippi.

## Behavioral tells
Body first; laughs at Ops; waves clerks off writing squeaks; counts cooking smoke; divides maps with wine still in teeth.

## Book 1 arc
Lupercalia (Ch 7) → delayed outside Curia (Ch 15) → Capitol (Ch 17) → will/funeral (Ch 18–19) → Ops underestimate (Ch 22) → Mutina (Ch 27) → Bononia (Ch 30) → Philippi peak (Ch 33–34).

## Must
Charm and calculation hard to separate; underestimation carries later cost without narrator spoiler.

## Must not
Permanent drunken buffoon; Shakespeare parody; theatrical villain only; prophetic fear of Actium.

## Consistency questions
- Is the joke serving appetite/control, not “being clever”?
- Does he still dismiss the boy with body-language, not thesis?
- Post-Philippi: Italy waved to tomorrow?
