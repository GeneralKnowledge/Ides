# Places and travel times

**Status:** Canon pack, stage 2. The storyboard uses this to test whether a person or a piece of news can be where a scene puts it. Distances are modern estimates along the ancient roads or sea lanes, rounded; they are **estimate**, not source, unless a source is given. A Roman mile is about 1.5 km.

## Speeds the book allows

| Who or what | Usual pace | Fast | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| An army with baggage | 20–25 km a day | 30–35 km for a few days | Slower in mountains, marsh and winter. |
| A forced march, light troops | 40–50 km a day | Nero's march north in 207 is the book's fastest (Liv. 27.43–50, to check for days) | |
| A rider carrying letters, changing horses | 60–80 km a day | 100+ km in an emergency | Nobody in the book outruns this. |
| A merchant or war ship, summer | 100–150 km a day | | Sailing season roughly March to October. In winter, news by sea mostly stops. |
| News of a battle reaching Rome | survivors and messengers first, then official letters | | Trasimene: rumour before the praetor's announcement (Liv. 22.7, text). |

## Rules for the storyboard

1. A character may not learn of an event sooner than the fastest plausible time above.
2. News reaching Carthage or Sicily in winter waits for the sailing season unless the storyboard names a reason.
3. The Alpine pass is never named; the book shows snow, rockfall, the descent and the Taurini.
