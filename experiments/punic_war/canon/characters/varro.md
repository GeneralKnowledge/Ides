## What he knows and when

- Knows Fabius's strategy from the inside of the Senate and despises its cost to the farms of Italy.
- After Cannae he does not know the size of the loss for days; numbers come in from Venusia and Canusium.
- In Etruria in 207 he is far from the Metaurus: he hears of it as Rome does, by courier.

## How he talks

Livy's Varro is a demagogue speaking against the nobles (Liv. 22.34, 22.38); Polybius is cooler. The book's Varro is a capable, quick, popular man who believes in fighting because the farms of his voters are burning, and who says so plainly. He is not a fool. After Cannae he talks less and works more.

## Invention zone

His private life is unknown: wife, children, house. The years 212–209 are empty. Any scene there is invention and should stay inside what a propraetor or senator does.
