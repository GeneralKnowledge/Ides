# Hanno the Great

**POV.** Carthage. The peace party at home; right about the cost, wrong about Rome.

**Name:** there are several Hannos in this war. In the book, plain "Hanno" means this man; everyone else called Hanno gets a handle (see [names](../names.md)).

## Where he is

Carthage throughout, 218–207. No recorded journey.

## What he knows and when

- News from Italy reaches Carthage by sea, weeks late, and often first through Mago or Hannibal's own letters. Hanno hears victories before he hears costs.
- Knows the treasury and the fleet; knows what Spain and Sardinia need, and that the council sends men to Spain more readily than to Italy (Liv. 23.13, 23.32).

## How he talks

Livy gives him set speeches; their argument is consistent: the war was a mistake, Rome will not break, every victory asks for more. The book's Hanno is old, rich, sarcastic and frightened of the Barcids' army more than of Rome.

## Invention zone

The council's day-to-day politics, his household and his allies are unrecorded. Scenes may show council debates on recorded decisions (reinforcements to Spain, Sardinia 215, the fleet for Syracuse), not new ones.
