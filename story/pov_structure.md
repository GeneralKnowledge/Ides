# POV Architecture

Ensemble political fiction needs **incomplete vantage points**. No omniscient “history teacher” narrator required (style decision later); structurally, knowledge must stay fractured.

---

## 1. Design goals

- Multiple social altitudes (Senate / household / army / money)  
- Conflicting interpretations of same events  
- At least one channel for class-crossing rumour  
- Historical figures remain themselves; POVs don’t replace them as secret protagonists of history  
- Reader assembles truth like a Roman hearing five rumours  

---

## 2. Historical POV candidates

| Candidate | Strengths | Weaknesses | Recommend |
|-----------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| **Caesar** | Intimacy with power; irony of limited knowledge | Over-centrality; hard to hide conspiracy; death ends arc early | **Limited / intermittent** — not sole spine |
| **Brutus** | Moral conflict; tyranny themes | Over-familiar; risk of modern liberal projection | **Yes**, carefully Romanised |
| **Cassius** | Operational conspiracy; sharper edge | Can flatten to “villain” | **Yes** as counterweight to Brutus |
| **Decimus** | Best betrayal mystery; Caesarian insider | Less famous → good; needs research discipline | **Strong yes** |
| **Antony** | Aftermath engine; soldiers; funeral | Can eat story post-Ides | **Yes** for Acts 3–5 |
| **Octavian** | Long consequence payoff | Must stay young/uncertain early | **Yes**, delayed weight |
| **Cicero** | Letters = built-in INF; wit | Talk > action; elite bubble | **Yes** as intermittent |
| **Calpurnia** | Private dread; omens; household | Limited institutional access | **Supporting** |
| **Cleopatra** | External empire lens; grain/dynasty | Away from Curia mechanics | **Supporting / arc segments** |
| **Servilia** | Crosses Caesar/Brutus; soft power | Easy to over-soap | **Select scenes** |
| **Fulvia** | Street + elite; later war | Later peak 43–40 | **Growing presence** |
| **Lepidus** | Nearby force mystery | Risk of dullness | **Sparse** |
| **Hirtius / Pansa** | Mutina bridge | Die 43 | **Campaign POVs** |
| **Sextus Pompey** | Parallel board | Off-centre from Ides | **Satellite** |

## 3. Structural options

### Option A — “Wheel around the Ides” (recommended baseline)
- **6–9 rotating POVs** across Acts  
- Fixed historical spine: Decimus, Brutus, Cassius, Antony, Cicero, Octavian  
- **2–3 fictional POVs** for INF / army / money  
- Caesar as rare POV early (to establish opacity) then withdrawn  

**Pros:** Ensemble GoT energy; history intact  
**Cons:** Needs strict scene discipline  

### Option B — “Two altitudes”
- Elite strand (Brutus/Decimus/Antony)  
- Below-stairs / street strand (fictional)  
- Meet at funeral & riots  

**Pros:** Murder-mystery street engine  
**Cons:** May under-serve East/Philippi unless expanded later  

### Option C — “Relay baton”
- Each act primary POV shifts: Cicero (46–45) → Decimus (early 44) → Antony (Ides aftermath) → Octavian (43) → Brutus (42)  

**Pros:** Clear act identity  
**Cons:** Drops ensemble intimacy  

### Option D — Hybrid (recommended for multi-book)
- Option A inside books  
- Option C emphasis shift across books

## 4. What historical cast cannot supply (gaps)

| Missing experiential zone | Why historical POVs fail |
|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| Continuous slave/freed INF | Elites don’t see their own walls |
| Ordinary Roman fear of hunger/rent | Senators theorise; don’t live it |
| Mid-tier officer logistics | Generals see maps not mud |
| Municipal Italy land panic | Rome-centred sources |
| Concurrent false rumour life | Need a character who *is* the rumour path |
| Investigation of a side-murder | No detective office — need busybody with access |

---

## 5. POV rules

1. A POV only knows their information state.  
2. No accidental future spill (“this would lead to empire”).  
3. Personality claims for historical POVs need source tags or clear **D** invention.  
4. Fictional POVs must not solve Ides better than history did.  
5. Death of POV is allowed and productive.
