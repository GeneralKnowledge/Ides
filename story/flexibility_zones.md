# Historical Flexibility Zones

Places where the record is thin enough for invention **without** moving anchors.

**Rule:** Invent freely here; then lock inventions in `continuity/canon.md`.

---

## Zone F1 — Private hours of documented days
**Examples:** Nights of 14–15 March 44; dawn before Lupercalia; hours after Senate rises.  
**Allowed:** Conversations, sex, quarrels, prayer, drafting tablets, slave punishments, delayed messages.  
**Not allowed:** Changing the public outcome of the day.

## Zone F2 — Unrecorded meetings
Elite politics ran on dinners (*convivia*), bath encounters, portico strolls, garden talks, litter side-chats.  
**Allowed:** Any pairing who could geographically co-locate.  
**Check:** `people.md` movements + `locations.md` travel times.

## Zone F3 — Household interiors
Almost all domestic dialogue is **D**.  
**Goldmines:** Caesar–Calpurnia house; Servilia’s circle; Antony–Fulvia; Cicero’s Tusculum/Formiae; Cleopatra’s Roman residence (details **C/D**).

## Zone F4 — Enslaved / freed intelligence work
Names mostly lost → create fictional INF agents.  
**Allowed:** Theft of tablets; listening at doors; selling rumours; loyalty conflicts.  
**Constraint:** Don’t give them impossible Latin literacy rates without reason; some *were* highly literate (Tiro type).

## Zone F5 — Conspiracy recruitment process
We know *that* recruitment happened, not full transcripts.  
**Allowed:** Failed approaches; almost-recruited senators; decoy grievances; arguments about killing Antony.  
**Constraint:** Keep core known conspirators involved; don’t “reveal” Cicero as sword-hand (**B** he wasn’t).

## Zone F6 — Omen manufacture & sincere misreading
Religion is practiced; meanings contested.  
**Allowed:** Human-faked signs; genuine odd events read wrong; competing priestly interpretations.  
**Constraint:** No confirmed gods-on-stage unless later canon changes.

## Zone F7 — Street & market Rome
Taverns, cookshops, fulleries, fire-brigade politics, graffiti, brothels, collegia.  
**Allowed:** Entire fictional micro-societies.  
**Payoff:** Rumour cascades; mistaken identity (prep for Cinna the poet lynching).

## Zone F8 — Financial shadowplot
Debts, dowries, bribes, grain futures, forged accounts.  
**Allowed:** Parallel “murder mystery” about money that *intersects* politics.  
**Constraint:** Don’t invent a second treasury system that replaces Roman institutions.

## Zone F9 — Provincial roads & sea voyages
Weeks in transit: storms, pirates, delayed dispatches, affairs, illness.  
**Allowed:** Compress minor journeys slightly; invent travel companions.  
**Constraint:** Don’t teleport armies.

## Zone F10 — Spanish campaign (45) camp life
Battle fixed; camp society open.  
**Allowed:** Octavian as overlooked youth scenes; Pompeian despair; messenger races.

## Zone F11 — Mutina winter (44/43)
Siege operations documented broadly; personal arcs open.  
**Allowed:** Truce talks fiction; desertion; forged orders; Decimus’ private fear.

## Zone F12 — Proscription economy (43)
Lists exist as phenomenon; individual bargains often **C/D**.  
**Allowed:** Trading names; hiding enemies; slaves betraying masters (historical pattern **A**); fictional victims.  
**Constraint:** Cicero still dies.

## Zone F13 — Eastern extraction (43–42)
Liberators’ fundraising brutal; local detail sparse in popular memory.  
**Allowed:** City hostage plots; Jewish/Asian elites as POV; “liberty” hypocrisy scenes.

## Zone F14 — Women’s political rooms
Sources male-filtered.  
**Allowed:** Servilia, Fulvia, Porcia, Calpurnia, Atia as strategic actors in **D** scenes consistent with **A** agency patterns.  
**Constraint:** Don’t turn them into modern activists; keep Roman leverage forms (marriage, dowry, clients, ritual, soft intel).

## Zone F15 — Parallel crimes
Unrecorded murders, blackmail, theft of wills, kidnapping of witnesses.  
**Allowed:** Fully fictional cases that *thematically* rhyme with tyrannicide / inheritance.  
**Constraint:** Must not overwrite A-events; ideally feed INF into known crises.

## Zone F16 — Almost-history (counterfactual temptation)
Characters may *plan* things that fail.  
**Allowed:** Abortive coups; letters not sent; murders attempted and missed.  
**Constraint:** Failed plans cannot succeed if they would erase an anchor.
