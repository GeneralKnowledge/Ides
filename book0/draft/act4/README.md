## Self-audit (three tests and the watchlist)

- **Action:** Cicero hides his lictors and is named in Antony's edict; the statues come down and the boy goes up to the Capitol in a toga too big; Antony adds a name to the edict and does the sum on another; Bucco's stool appears with Hercules on the seal, and Visellius pays down principal early; Tullia tells her father about the third instalment; Brutus wins Deiotarus his title and hears his own name; Antony's soldiers clear the Forum and Nysa marks Chreste in her bed; Caesar gets down off his horse; the legions mutiny and Laberius prices the cup in fields; Antony bids alone; Oppius asks for the money and Fulvia tells Antony he has shown Rome he is frightened; *Quirites*, the knees, the roll, no day.
- **Point of view:** Cicero calls waiting decency; Nysa reads Pompey's death as the end of the war; Antony takes the dinner's laughter for loyalty and does not believe what he thinks about his wife; Hordeonius reads Bucco's seal as Antony's being a creditor, and later reads Antony's bid as Caesar's credit; Cicero does not tell Tullia he thought of asking her to ask Dolabella; Brutus takes being heard for being equal; Nysa takes soldiers for protection until the rock; Cicero calls the walk friendship; Laberius hides that he looked at the land and that he knelt.
- **Pilot note (Antony):** the calculating man under the soldier shows in IV-03 (what a line on the edict is worth) and IV-11 (the sum that does not come out).
- **Explanation:** trimmed before commit: IV-04 Visellius's "the kind of man who owes as little as possible" (repeated III-04); IV-11's "he found that she was right". IV-07's count was simplified to one false mark, not two.
- **Endings:** varied: a game of knucklebones (IV-01), a laugh held in (IV-02), a song (IV-03), a soldier counting his money (IV-04), a letter about a loan (IV-05), a letter to his mother (IV-06), waiting for the cook (IV-07), "I'll see to it" (IV-08), the pack against his back (IV-09), "what I am going to write" (IV-10), Fulvia going out (IV-11), the field he can see (IV-12).
- **Walk-ons:** the silversmith says "It'll be less tomorrow"; the auctioneer's clerk says nothing when asked when Antony will pay; the roll clerk says "Next". Oppius is allowed courtesy and no speech.
- **Rooms going silent:** "nobody" is high in IV-02 and IV-12 (6, 7), mostly "nobody said when"; no change.
- **Hindsight:** none intended. The scar-lipped man is unnamed and only spits. The boy in the toga is not looked at again. Nobody mentions the Ides.

## Cast-check additions

Vatinius at Brundisium (IV-01), Oppius asking for the price (IV-11), Bucco's first stool by the Janus (IV-04, consistent with Book 1's "kept a stool by the Janus"), Tiro back with Cicero (IV-08), Lepidus to hold Rome during Africa (IV-11).

## History still marked (check)

- IV-01: Cicero's lictors hidden at Brundisium (*Att.* 11.6, check); Vatinius in command at Brundisium and kind (check); Corcyra, young Pompey's sword, Cato's rescue (Plut. *Cic.* 39, check); the edict naming Cicero and Laelius (*Att.* 11.7, checked).
- IV-02: Pompey's death in the boat in front of Cornelia (Plut. *Pomp.* 79 to 80, check); statues of Pompey and Sulla thrown down (Suet. *Jul.* 75; Dio 42.18, check); Octavian's toga of manhood, October 48 (Nic. Dam. *Aug.* 4, check); the linen and the joke about the head are invented.
- IV-03: Antony with lictors and a sword in the city (Dio 42.27, check); Cytheris in a litter (Plut. *Ant.* 9, read); Antonia his cousin and the suspicion about Dolabella (Plut. *Ant.* 9, read). Antony adding Laelius at his own choice is invented.
- IV-04: Dolabella's law cancelling debts and rents; Trebellius's veto (Dio 42.29 to 32, checked). Bucco's arrival in 47 and the Hercules seal are invented (Antony's descent from Hercules, Plut. *Ant.* 4). Dolabella's debt to Hordeonius is invented.
- IV-05: Tullia arrives 12 June (*Att.* 11.17, checked); the third instalment of the dowry due in July 47 (*Att.* 11.2, 11.25, check); Quintus's letters to Caesar blaming his brother (check).
- IV-06: Deiotarus at Nicaea, Brutus speaking for him "most freely" (Cic. *Brut.* 21; Bell. Alex. 67 to 68, check); Caesar's remark is not heard by Brutus, as Book 1 Ch 6 requires. Storyboard date moved from July to August 47 (after Zela).
- IV-07: barricades and wooden towers; Antony's soldiers from the Capitol at dawn; tablets cut down; men thrown from the rock (Dio 42.32 to 33, checked). Chreste's soldier is invented.
- IV-08: Plut. *Cic.* 39.4 to 5 (checked); Caesar's generous letter in the summer (*Fam.* 14.23, check); Tiro's return from Patrae (check).
- IV-09: Sallust promising a thousand more and nearly killed; two senators killed (Dio 42.52, checked; App. *BC* 2.92, check); the march to Rome.
- IV-10: Antony the only bidder (Cic. *Phil.* 2.64, check); the beaks on the house (*Phil.* 2.68, check); the spear (standard practice).
- IV-11: Antony "indignant" when asked to pay, and his complaint of no reward for Pharsalus (Plut. *Ant.* 10, checked); Oppius as the man who asks is invented; Dolabella pardoned and on Caesar's staff.
- IV-12: Dio 42.53 to 55 and Suet. *Jul.* 70, 38.1 (checked): *Quirites*, the begging, the land from public holdings and his own, settled apart, money part now and the rest with interest. "Not like Sulla" is the soldiers' gloss; Appian's own words still unread.
