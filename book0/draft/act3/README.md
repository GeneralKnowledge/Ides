## Self-audit (three tests and the watchlist)

- **Action:** Cato tells Cicero he should not have come; Antony takes a quadrireme with rowing boats, crosses, and pays a fisherman for news of the recruits killed at Lissus; Laberius's bread goes over the valley and he gives out one loaf fewer than there are men; Caelius's six years without interest, and Visellius says he would have paid anyway; the house hears Caesar is dead and then that he is not; Laberius runs; Brutus returns a book and says nothing else; Antony comes up the slope first; Laberius takes a silver cup against orders and finds Domitius in the senator's shoes from Corfinium; Larissa.
- **Point of view:** Cicero calls the coldness envy and makes an inventory of Formiae; Antony lets the army cheer him for the wind; Laberius takes hunger as a race he is winning; Hordeonius calls Caelius a fool with debts and is caught out by his own relief; Nysa's relief is about not being sold, and she is ashamed of it; Laberius reads the general's calm as anger held back; Brutus calls his silence keeping clean; Antony feels the battle as his own (the "he knew it was not" sentences were cut; the cavalry on the right is told as what he heard later).
- **Explanation:** cut before commit: III-02's closing "nobody who saw him would have known"; III-08's paragraph explaining that the battle was decided on the right; III-07's Cannae summary in Polybius, which pointed at the battle (now the sixth book, on how the state is made, which is Brutus's own subject).
- **Endings:** varied: a sentence in speech (III-04 Hordeonius on Visellius; III-08 "Go and see to your horse"), plain business (III-03 back to the tower, III-07 the wax smoothed, III-06 Rufrius telling a man to pick his feet up), an object put away (III-09 the cup in the pack), a lamp handed over (III-05), Brutus's honey (III-01).
- **Walk-ons:** the decent officer with the eagles; the fisherman paid for news; the man behind Laberius at Scaeva's shield ("two hundred thousand for a shield"); the new man at Domitius's body. Labienus is allowed his hard sentence; it is from Caesar.
- **Rooms going silent:** "nobody" is high in III-08 (6), mostly "nobody had told them to"; no change.
- **Hindsight:** none intended. Pompey's officers divide the chief priesthood and the houses as the sources say; Brutus does not judge them aloud.

## Cast-check additions

Scaeva's shield (III-03), Crastinus among the dead (III-08), Libo's blockade at Brundisium (III-02), Calenus with Antony (III-02), Labienus before Pharsalus (III-07), Cato and Cicero's son at Dyrrachium (III-01).

## New people

No new named invented characters. The Firmum recruit from Corfinium recurs, unnamed. The decent officer of the eagles joke is Nonius in Plutarch; he is unnamed here because Book 2 uses Nonius for the gatekeeper.

## Objects to carry into Act IV

## History still marked (check)

- III-01: Plut. *Cic.* 38 for the jokes (eagles and jackdaws, "nothing ready", the Gaul's citizenship, "with your father-in-law") and Cato's reproach (check wording); young Marcus commanding a squadron of horse.
- III-02: Caes. *BC* 3.23 to 29: Libo at Brundisium and the boats under hurdles; Bibulus burning thirty ships; Coponius's sixteen Rhodian ships wrecked; Nymphaeum; Otacilius killing 220 recruits at Lissus after a promise of safety (3.28). Caesar's letters threatening to come in a fishing boat are invented (Plut. *Caes.* 38 has him try to cross in a small boat).
- III-03: chara with milk (Caes. *BC* 3.48, checked); loaves thrown, Pompey hiding them, "wild beasts" (Plut. *Caes.* 39; Suet. *Jul.* 68, check); the old Greek woman's method is invented; Scaeva's shield with 120 holes, 200,000 sesterces and promotion (Caes. *BC* 3.53, check); six fights in one day.
- III-04: Caelius's chair beside Trebonius's, six years without interest, the riot, the consul breaking his chair (Caes. *BC* 3.20 to 21; Dio 42.22 to 23, check). Caelius borrowing from Hordeonius for his games is invented.
- III-05: Milo killed by a stone, Caelius killed at Thurii by Gallic and Spanish horse (Caes. *BC* 3.22, checked). The false report of Caesar's death is invented, placed before the real defeat at Dyrrachium.
- III-06: Caes. *BC* 3.62 to 74: the old camp, thirty-three cohorts, the right column following a wall, thirty-two standards lost, the men asking to be punished, Caesar's speech (3.73). The standard-bearer striking Caesar (Plut. *Caes.* 39; App. *BC* 2.62, check).
- III-07: Caes. *BC* 3.82 to 83 (quarrels over the priesthood, Domitius's three tablets, check); Labienus at the council, "not the army that beat the Gauls" and the oath (3.87, check).
- III-08: Caes. *BC* 3.85 to 99: "Now's the time for battle", the halt in the middle of the plain, the fourth line against the cavalry, the two weak legions joined on the left (3.89), Crastinus (3.91, 3.99); "They would have it so" (Suet. *Jul.* 30, check).
- III-09: tables laid, silver, myrtle, a turf bank with ivy (Caes. *BC* 3.96, check); Caesar forbidding plunder (3.97); Domitius killed by the cavalry in flight (3.99).
