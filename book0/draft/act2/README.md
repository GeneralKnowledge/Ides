## Self-audit (three tests and the watchlist)

- **Action:** Antony sees Fulvia send creditors to the Ides and is asked to "look in"; Cicero sails with the money short; the century eats millet and Laberius shows the engineers how to sew hide; Rufrius takes the next tent; Nysa reads a letter she was not meant to and the house buys for the year; Pompey rises for Brutus; Antony sits in Fulvia's office until the bankers see him there; twelve die by lot and Laberius has Vibius write down where every man has been; Hordeonius takes a house at the arbitrator's figure; Caesar asks Nysa's name twice and forgets it.
- **Point of view:** Antony asks who Curio owed before he can stop himself (the pilot note's calculating Antony, twice in the act); Cicero hears Tullia's "wait for Spain" as courage and does not say why he goes; Laberius hides that he cannot swim and finds work on the bank; Nysa works out a reason for the buying that Chreste takes apart; Brutus calls his tiredness principle; Hordeonius writes *valued*, not *sold*; Nysa keeps the name to herself and then he does not look at her.
- **Explanation:** cut before commit: II-02's "He had not chosen the side that would win..." (the scene already shows it); II-06's hand on his own shoulder, which pointed at Larissa (same fix as the pilot's III-10 ending); II-09's second "Exact" ending, which repeated I-03.
- **Endings:** varied: plain business (II-03 the awl, II-05 the long word, II-06 the cake to the slave, II-09 Visellius paying), a homecoming line (II-07), a list written (II-08), a sum (II-10), a door shut (II-01), a sea-sickness (II-02).
- **Walk-ons:** the creditors' boy asks "Before the Kalends?"; the arbitrator worries about his knees; Opimius talks about his cobbler; the Greek sutler names a price and says nothing else.
- **Motifs:** wiping cloth used once (II-09). Lamps in II-02 and II-10, separate houses.
- **Hindsight:** none intended. II-10's boy at the dinner is a thin boy whose shoes are too thin; the narration does not look at him again. II-10 no longer mentions Caesar's face on coins (none before 44).

## Cast-check additions

Gaius, Vibius and Rufrius in II-03 and II-08. Fulvia's daughter Claudia, about seven, in II-01 (Book 2's Claudia). Atia and Philippus at the family dinner in II-10, unnamed in Nysa's view.

## New people

Opimius, a hide-dealer of the Velabrum (II-09), invented and named because Hordeonius would name him. The arbitrator is "Gnaeus", praenomen only (Plotius was dropped: Book 1 uses it for Plancus's brother). Unnamed: the creditors' boy, Fulvia's steward, the Greek sutler, the centurion of the Ninth, the absent man at Cremona.

## History still marked (check)

- II-01: Curio's surety for Antony's debt (Plut. *Ant.* 2, 250 talents, read in an earlier session); Caesar paying Curio's debts (sixty million, Val. Max. 9.1.6, check); Curio's law against Juba (Caes. *BC* 2.25, check); Cato leaving Sicily; four legions to Sicily.
- II-02: Tullia's premature son, May 49 (*Att.* 10.18, check); her advice to wait for Spain (*Att.* 10.8, check); sailing from Caieta 7 June (*Fam.* 14.7, check: the bile); Dolabella in the Adriatic.
- II-03: floods, both bridges lost, the convoy cut off, fifty denarii the modius, the hide boats (Caes. *BC* 1.48 to 54); the Lusitanians on skins (1.48).
- II-05: Afranius's house thronged in Rome on the news of Caesar's trouble (Caes. *BC* 1.53); Decimus's first sea battle off Massilia (1.56 to 58, nine ships, check).
- II-06: Pompey rises and embraces Brutus (Plut. *Brut.* 4, read earlier, check the wording); the camp's talk of punishing neutrals (Suet. *Jul.* 75; Cic. *Att.* 11.6).
- II-07: Curio's death (Caes. *BC* 2.38 to 42, read in an earlier session); Gaius Antonius's surrender at Curicta with fifteen cohorts (check); Curio's son by Fulvia, about a year old (check).
- II-08: App. *BC* 2.47 (read): five minae promised at Brundisium, the threat of decimation, 120, twelve by lot, the absent man and the centurion. The Cremona mule convoy is invented.
- II-09: Caes. *BC* 3.1 (read): arbitrators, pre-war valuation. Opimius's leather contract is invented.
- II-10: Caesar's eleven days, elected consul for 48 (Caes. *BC* 3.1 to 2); Atia, Philippus and Octavian at a family dinner is invented; Octavian's age (fourteen, born September 63).
