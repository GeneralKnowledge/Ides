# Mind sheets: Book 0

**Status:** First version, 11 Nov 2026. Descriptive, not prescriptive.

The Book 1 sheets in [`writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md`](../../writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md) are the authority for all six Book 0 POVs; every one of them already has a sheet there. This file adds a short "before" note for each: what is different about this person five years younger, before the thing Book 1 knows about them has happened. The three tests (action, point of view, explanation) and the two tests for a scene's ending apply unchanged, and so does the style watchlist (`book2/voice/WATCHLIST.md`).

The risk common to all six is hindsight. Each of them is a person to whom the Ides has not happened. Nobody in Book 0 is shaped for a later book: Brutus is not yet the man who will kill Caesar, Antony is not yet the man who will lose to Octavian, and the reader who knows should feel it without any sentence leaning on it.

---

## Laberius (about 37)

**What is different:** he is in the middle of his life as a soldier, not at the end of it. Gaius is alive and talks. There is no land in his head yet, only pay and the next dry place. Caesar is the general, seen at a distance, and the general is winning.

**Notices:** the same things: feet, straps, ground, who has eaten, who is on his left. In Act I he notices his own country: Picene hill towns, the way the women there carry water, a dialect word he has not heard in years. Men from his valley are on the other side at Corfinium.

**What he misunderstands:** that the war is between generals and has nothing to do with him. He thinks pardons are for gentlemen. He thinks the fraternising at Ilerda is the war ending.

**Particulars available (one per scene, not a catalogue, as the Book 2 pilot asked):** a Picene food he finds again in Act I and misses after; he cannot swim (the Sicoris floods); a mother or sister in a hill town he does not visit when he passes (check against Book 1 before using); the chara root bread, which he is good at making and proud of.

**The risk:** making him a witness to every famous moment. He sees what a centurion sees. At Pharsalus he sees a slope and a camp with the tables laid.

## Nysa (about 23)

**What is different:** she is a slave in the house, below Chreste, and the house is the one thing she is sure of. She has not yet been sent to count people. She is not yet anyone's source.

**Notices:** the household's things: oil, lamps, linen, the storeroom, which door Phileros has barred and at what hour. Calpurnia's moods by what she asks for. The master is a name, a seal and a long absence; when he is home for eleven days the house doubles its lamps.

**What she misunderstands:** that what happens in Spain and Greece cannot come into a kitchen. In 47, when soldiers clear the Forum, it does.

**The risk:** making her the series' narrator in waiting. She is twenty-three and mostly busy. Her counting is a habit, not yet a use. She must not see Cato or learn his face, and she must not enter Pompey's house.

## Hordeonius (about 45)

**What is different:** he is at the top of his trade, and his trade, lending to men who stand for office, stops: there are no proper elections when the consuls have left Rome. For the first time he has to lend on something other than ambition.

**Notices:** the same: the term on the paper, the security, who stands behind a borrower. New in these years: the laws. A debt valued by arbitrators at pre-war prices, a praetor offering six years without interest, a tribune offering to cancel debts altogether. He reads each one as a man reads a change in the weather that is aimed at him.

**What he misunderstands:** that the law of debt is a matter of law. Each proposal in Book 0 is politics, and its authors die or win by the sword.

**The risk:** the ledger jokes in the narration. He gets his maxims in speech. Visellius pays on the Kalends through all of it, and Hordeonius does not say what he thinks of that.

## Cicero (57 to 59)

**What is different:** he is not yet pardoned and not yet needed. He has lictors with laurelled fasces and no triumph, a daughter in a bad marriage to a man on Caesar's side, and a decision he takes eight months to make.

**Notices:** sentences, audiences, precedents, as in Book 1. In Pompey's camp he notices that nobody wants his sentences, and makes jokes they do not forgive.

**What he misunderstands:** that the side he joins wants him. That Caesar's courtesy at Formiae is a request he can decline without cost. That being exempted by name from Antony's edict is a favour.

**The risk:** the letters. They are the best source in the period and they will tempt the scene into quotation. Use what he would say, not what he wrote to Atticus about it later.

## Brutus (about 36)

**What is different:** he has not yet been given dinner. He joins the man who had his father killed because it is right, and is courteous to him. He reads in camp, and makes an epitome of Polybius (Plut. *Brut.* 4, checked: during the campaign, not on the eve).

**Notices:** what is fitting, as always. At Larissa, the thing that is not fitting: that he is glad to be alive and glad to be liked.

**What he misunderstands:** that answering a question about where Pompey has gone is only answering a question.

**The risk:** writing Larissa toward the Ides. Book 1 remembers four things about that tent (handover). The scene puts them on the page as they happen and ends without a sentence about what they will cost.

## Antony (about 34 to 36)

**What is different:** he is Caesar's man and has nothing of his own: no house of his own standing, no army of his own, a wife he suspects, debts. By the end of the book he has Pompey's house and has not paid for it.

**Notices:** bodies and noise, as in Book 1. At Ariminum, the ranks looking at a tribune in a slave's tunic; he feels them decide on his skin. In Rome in 47 the Forum turns on him and he does not feel it in time.

**What he misunderstands:** that being liked by the soldiers is the same as being able to stop them. The mutiny of 47 is his failure before it is Caesar's success.

**The risk:** making him the drunk of the anecdotes. He is the best field officer in the book at Pharsalus and the worst governor of Rome in it a year later, and both are him.

---

## Not POVs

Caesar has no scene from inside in Book 0. He is seen by Antony at Ariminum, Laberius on the march, Cicero at Formiae and on the road, Brutus at Larissa, Nysa in the house. Pompey is seen only from outside. Octavian appears two or three times through Nysa, a thin boy of fourteen or fifteen in his grandmother's family; nobody notices him and the narration does not either.
