# Handover: what Books 1 and 2 have already fixed

Book 0 is written after the books that follow it, so this handover runs backwards. Everything here is already on the page in Book 1 (`drafts/book1/`) or fixed by Book 2's contract and handover (`book2/`). Book 0 may not contradict it without changing the later book first, and the series rule is that Book 1, now going to print, is not reopened for Book 0's convenience.

Lines marked **(check)** come from planning files or from inference, not from a sentence in the text, and are confirmed before the act that uses them.

## Where Book 0 must leave things for Book 1

Book 1 opens with the prologue at Utica (April 46, Cleanthes) and Ch 1, a dinner of pardoned men in late 46. By then:

- Cassius "had fought for Pompey, and lost, and had lived since Pharsalus on Caesar's pardon" (Ch 1).
- Brutus has been pardoned at Larissa; Cicero has been pardoned and has written his thanks "a hundred times since Pharsalus, to a hundred men" (Ch 3); his public smile has been "doing that for three years" by February 44 (Ch 6).
- Ligarius is still in Africa (Ch 3). Book 0 does not use him.
- Pompey is dead; Labienus is alive and with the Pompeians (he dies at Munda, Ch 4); Gnaeus and Sextus Pompey are alive (Ch 4).
- Antony owns Pompey's house in the Carinae, "bought at Caesar's auction, never paid for" (Ch 23).
- Calpurnia's household is in the house on the Sacred Way, with Phileros holding the keys, a steward, Chreste and Nysa (Ch 2).
- Laberius, Gaius and Vibius are together; Rufrius is "next door since Ilerda" (Ch 4).
- Visellius has been paying Hordeonius on the Kalends since about 50 (Ch 10: "six years" in March 44).

## Brutus

Book 1 remembers Larissa four times. Book 0's scene must hold all of them:

- "It had the same weight it had had at Larissa after Pharsalus, when Brutus came in out of the mud expecting chains and was given dinner" (Ch 6; Caesar's hand on his shoulder).
- "At Larissa he gave you dinner," Servilia says. "He wrote to me that night" (Ch 8).
- "The quick warmth in his chest he had felt as a young man when Caesar's hand fell on his shoulder after Pharsalus: the warmth of being chosen" (Ch 26).
- "He had stood in a tent at Larissa after Pharsalus with mud to his knees and heard Caesar be kind to him, and had felt himself feel grateful, and had hated it" (Ch 29).

So: a tent; mud to the knees; expecting chains; dinner; Caesar kind; a hand on the shoulder; gratitude and hatred together; Caesar writes to Servilia the same night.

Also: Brutus governed Cisalpine Gaul in 46 (Ch 6). Pompey had his father killed (Plut. *Brut.* 4, checked).

## Antony

- "Caesar's left wing at Pharsalus" (Ch 9).
- Owns Pompey's house in the Carinae, "bought at Caesar's auction, never paid for" (Ch 23).
- Trebonius has "ten years of Gaul accounts ... Antony put his seal to half of them without reading" (Ch 9).
- Mind sheet: quick with his body, slow with consequences; afraid of his wife's opinion. In 47 that wife is Antonia; Fulvia comes later **(check date)**.

## Cicero

- Pleads for Ligarius before Caesar in late 46 and "took them to Pharsalus" (Ch 3).
- Tullia married Dolabella "when she was twenty-eight and still hopeful", he was "unfaithful to her before the wedding wreaths were dry", divorced her when she was carrying his child; the dowry came back in instalments, the third overdue (Ch 21). Book 0 is inside that marriage.

## Others

- Trebonius "held Massilia for him and governed Spain for him and took the consul's chair from him for three months" (Ch 21). He was also urban praetor in 48 **(check)**; Book 0 can use that without contradiction.
- Decimus's own record: "the ships he had built against the Veneti, and the fleet he had taken in against Massilia" (Ch 4).
- Labienus "had been ten years at Caesar's right hand in Gaul" (Ch 4). He goes over to Pompey in January 49.
- Cato after Pharsalus "sat up to his meals. He never lay down to dinner again" (Porcia, Ch 4). If Cato is seen in Book 0 after August 48, he sits.
- Statilius lived by "live unnoticed ... through Pharsalus and Africa and the pardons" (Ch 23).
- Cleopatra's claim "kept in Alexandria for three years before any Roman wrote it down" (Ch 20, spoken in 44): Alexandria stays rumour in Book 0.
- Octavian: "the boy", unnoticed; ill as a child at Velitrae, his mother's hand on his forehead (Ch 38); Atia touches his cheek "as she had when he was eight" (Ch 32).

## What Book 2 needs left alone

- Fulvia's history: Clodius, then Curio, both dead by violence. Curio dies in Book 0's span (Caes. *BC* 2.42, checked).
- The scar-lipped man stays unnamed in Book 0. Book 2 may name him once; if it does, Book 0 (written later) may use that name.
- Rufrius's fate after Philippi is Book 2's to decide.
- Lucius Antonius has no Book 0 role.
- Calenus is Caesar's legate in Greece in 48 **(check)**; he dies in Gaul in 40.
