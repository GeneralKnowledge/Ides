## Story need
Cicero’s death in Ch 31 is bought at Bononia by a cost count. The real history leaves a joke that travelled from his dinner table to Octavian’s ears. The reader had no sight of it, and so no reason to feel the count might not be only arithmetic.

## Added (about 1,800 words)
- **Ch 28, SC-81b (Cicero, late April 43, new scene).** The crowd carries Cicero to the Capitol. The Senate snubs the boy. At dinner Cicero says *laudandum, ornandum, tollendum*, Labeo pockets it, and Tiro warns him. Sources: *Ad Brut.* 1.3.2; *Fam.* 11.20–21.
- **Ch 29, SC-82 (Decimus).** One block: Labeo at Eporedia, and Decimus writes to Cicero (*Fam.* 11.20).
- **Ch 29, SC-83 (Octavian).** One block: Labeo tells the saying at the camp dinner. Octavian: “no intention of being lifted.”
- **Ch 30, SC-84 (Octavian).** Two sentences: the verbs pass under the count and he decides not to let them in. This is not the cause, and nothing else in the Bononia scene changes.
