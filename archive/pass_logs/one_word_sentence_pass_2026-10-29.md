# One-word sentence pass — 2026-10-29

**Purpose:** Reuben, reading Book 1, found one-word sentences used as a habit (“This is an example. Here.”). Find them, fix them in one pass, and add a rule so they do not come back.
**Scope:** Narration in the reading text of the prologue and Ch 1–36 (what the DOCX export prints; scene headers, continuity notes and source notes excluded). Speech is out of scope: people clip when they talk.
**Kind of pass:** Style-only, one pass. Each change folds a fragment into its sentence or gives it a subject and verb. Wording otherwise kept; no fact, sequence or knowledge changes.

**Pattern:** a full narrative sentence followed by one or more one-word or verbless fragments that label it: an adjective verdict (“He tapped the tablet once. Soft. Final.”), a *Not X. Y.* correction (“Not mirth. Recognition.”), a list broken into one-word sentences (“Shouts. Bets.”), or a name dropped as its own sentence after the sentence that set it up (“Servilia.”). Two- and three-word verbless captions of the same shape (“No vote. No request.”, “Not an insult. Not an honour.”) were fixed with them.

## Kept on purpose

- **Ch 20, the courier to Octavian:** “Knives. Senate. Pompey’s place. Brutus. Blood.” The news arrives broken, as heard; the fragments are the event.
- **Ch 30, Laberius sees Rufrius** across the line (“Rufrius. From the next tent at Munda.”) and **Ch 33, Cicero sees Philologus** pointing the soldiers down the walk. Recognitions where the stop is the point. Ch 33's second recognition, Popillius, was folded so the two do not compete.
- **Ch 32, *Clean.*** The one word Hordeonius writes in his cipher; it is quoted, not a narrator caption.
- **Ch 4, “Not glory roads. Ditch timing.”** Held brand (HUMAN_VOICE_RULES §4.3).
- **Ch 16 Curia:** “One door. Too many togas.”, “He sat. Or half-sat.”, “Togas with red.” are the killing's propulsion (protected core, §1); only “Pulled.” was folded into its sentence, which keeps the beat.
- **Ch 15**, the noise from the Curia heard in the portico (“Not a speech-sound. Not a vote-sound.”), and **Ch 35**, the apparition (“Very tall.”) and Cassius misreading the plain (“A man pulled from a horse. A shout.”): held.
- **Ch 30,** the translation of Cicero's pun (“To lift. To lift away. To remove.”).
- Subject-and-verb short sentences (“Cassius ate.”, “Nobody did.”, “He drank.”) were not touched.
