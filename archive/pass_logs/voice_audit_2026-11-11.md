# Voice pass: scene audit, 2026-11-11

**What this is.** Every scene of Book 1 read against the three tests in [`writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md`](../../writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md) (action, point of view, explanation) and the five patterns an outside reader found in Ch 1–6. Each scene gets one verdict. Text audited: main at 225cefa plus the three pilot scenes.

**Verdicts.**
- **Leave:** the scene already does what the pilot scenes do. At most a sentence to test.
- **Line edits, light:** the scene works. A handful of explaining or captioning sentences come out.
- **Line edits, heavy:** the event is sound and stays. A stretch of it (often the opening, a chorus of bystanders, or the last paragraph) is rewritten.
- **Rewrite:** nothing much happens, or what happens is buried under verdicts, or the viewpoint thinks like someone else. Rebuilt from the mind sheet round one event, keeping the facts and the lines named in the notes.

## What the audit found

1. **The book already contains its own model, and most of it is in the second half.** Thirty-five of the 39 scenes to leave are in Ch 20–38. They are the scenes added or rebuilt in late October: Statilius, the column, Trebonius at the posting station, Pompey’s gardens, the hair of the star, Athens, Brundisium, Casilinum, Smyrna, Forum Gallorum, the three verbs, Porcia’s room, Julia in her doorway, Calenus and Varro, Sardis, Abydos. The pilot scenes were written toward that standard without knowing it.
2. **The old voice is concentrated in Ch 1–13 and in a scatter of short scenes later on.** Those later ones are mostly under 600 words, were never rebuilt, and now sit beside scenes that show them up: Ch 22 SC-63 beside Pompey’s gardens, Ch 30 SC-80 after Forum Gallorum, Ch 36 SC-87 before Sardis, Ch 33 SC-85 before Julia.
3. **One mind in every head has a signature.** Laberius, Antony, Decimus, Octavian and Nysa all “file”, “price” and “keep” what they see. That is Hordeonius’s mind, and it is the first thing to take out of the others.
4. **A chorus keeps turning up.** Anonymous freedmen, clerks, veterans and paired officers (one grey, one young and ambitious) arrive to trade epigrams and deliver the next chapter’s set-up. The scar-lipped veteran does it four times. In the scenes to leave, bystanders are particular: the thread woman’s son, the porter who scratches his ear, the chickpea seller.
5. **Early Nysa and late Nysa are two people.** In Ch 11–19 she speaks in finished sayings (“Cheerful men always mean schedules”). From Ch 21 on she is the woman of the pilot scene. The early scenes move to meet the late ones.
6. **The Ides chapters need more than was assumed, but not in their core beats.** The petition, the base of the statue, the hand at the door, the unread note and the funeral are sound. The trouble is in the aftermath scenes (Ch 17–19), which were written in the caption voice. They stay last and are handled lightly: line edits throughout, and one rewrite proposed (Ch 18 SC-48, Hordeonius).

## Things for Reuben to decide

- **Ch 22 SC-63, “The Boy” (Antony, 437 words).** It does badly what Pompey’s gardens then does well. Cut it, or reduce it to one beat (Antony hears “Caesar” from Campania and laughs; the keys of Ops).
- **Ch 30 SC-80, “Mud”.** Gaius dies in half a sentence (“He saw Gaius open at the neck”) one scene after Forum Gallorum spent 2,000 words on him. Proposed: rewrite to Forum Gallorum’s standard. This adds perhaps 500 words.
- **Ch 12 SC-29, the wax fragment.** The attendant mutters an impossible finished speech. The rewrite has Nysa catch four or five words she cannot place. Check on the way: how much can she write in early 44? Ch 35 says she reads “slowly”.
- **Ch 2 SC-01 and SC-02.** The new SC-02 puts Hordeonius and Sosia on Caesar’s steps watching who comes out. The SC-01 rewrite should give him a reason to be there.
- **Length.** The 21 rewrites total 12,500 words. At the pilot’s rate (1.6 to 2 times) they come back at 20,000 to 25,000, less what the heavy edits cut. Expect the book to grow by roughly 8,000 to 10,000 words, to about 104,000.
- **The parked factual fixes** from the chapter reviews go in when their scenes are handled: Sextilius “from headquarters” (Ch 4), “that morning” (Ch 4), the future winks and the bow in Ch 5, and the hand-check on where Ligarius was taken (Ch 3).
