## Section B — Executive assessment

**What the book is now:** A canon-disciplined Book 1 that can be read as **one novel** from Thapsus through Philippi. The Ides sequence (Ch 14–16) and funeral (Ch 19) remain the dramatic peak. Phase 1D made Mutina, Decimus’s death-road, name-war, Bononia/lists, and Philippi into **lived consequence** rather than postcard timeline. Phase 1G reduced house-style voice convergence and seeded Decimus/Brutus mid-band costs that 1D.6 still flagged.

**Principal strengths**

1. **Gold spine intact** — dream/fetch/Curia/false Antony-dead; funeral as aiming; disputed last words; NOTE-1 dual.  
2. **Incomplete-information engine** — still the book’s argument: paper, pay, rumour, and unfinished sentences decide empires before standards do.  
3. **Causal chain Ides → Philippi** — spare Antony → funeral → name → Mutina → lists → East tax → info-death / second-day weight is now experiential enough to carry a cold read.  
4. **Locks held** — OCT≠Augustus; AMB-2; SERIES hooks-only; SIDE-1 subordinate; no Actium plotting.

**Most consequential remaining risks (not blockers for an author cold read)**

1. **Mid-band energy trough (Ch 20–23)** — still the softest stretch relative to Ides and Mutina; improved by 1G seeds but not “solved.”  
2. **Lean length (~30k)** — concise by design after mid-tier 1A; will feel short next to conventional historical novels. That is a **form choice**, not proof of missing plot.  
3. **Residual craft tics** — fewer after 1G; still some *weather* (mostly Ch 12/14, intentional) and aphoristic closers.  
4. **Open VERIFY items** — Appian funeral gesture; Latin letter lines (P2).

**Frank readiness:** Further agent development cycles have diminishing returns. The highest-value next act is **your** uninterrupted read with the guide in §D. This audit selects **Gate C** (see §H): freeze prose until you return with notes.

## Section D — Six narrative-section review guide

Use these while reading. Record reactions **before** inventing fixes. Chapter ranges verified against current `CHAPTER_MAP` / draft order.

### 1. Opening — Republic’s fractures (Ch 1–6)

**Understand by end:** Civil war’s moral/market shock; debt/patronage machinery; clemency as soft chain; Octavian as irrelevant inventory; *perpetuo* and Parthia packing as pressure.

**Character focus:** Hordeonius (rates), Nysa (listening), Seius (debt ladder), Brutus/Cassius/Decimus seeds (Ch 4), Octavian edge (Ch 5).

**Keep uncertain:** How much clemency “means”; whether heir-whisper about Decimus is true.

**Propels next:** Clemency beneficiaries + perpetual offices make conspiracy *thinkable*.

**Ask yourself:**  
1. Do I care about anyone before Lupercalia, or only about atmosphere?  
2. Does Ch 4’s Decimus road-trust stick so Ch 14 will hurt?

### 2. Escalation toward the Ides (Ch 7–13)

**Understand by end:** Diadem panic; disclosure; recruitment with price; spare-Antony decision; SIDE-1 as parallel blood not Ides cause; omens manufactured + ambiguous; GO chosen.

**Character focus:** Antony (public body), Cassius (engine), Brutus (brand/mercy), Decimus (key), Cicero (blind calm), Laberius (land powder).

**Keep uncertain:** Caesar’s knowledge (AMB-1); NOTE-1 meaning; omen causation (AMB-3).

**Propels next:** Attendance + delay + trust locked for 15 March.

**Ask yourself:**  
1. Is spare-Antony a moral victory or a strategic mistake *as the characters see it*?  
2. Does GO feel irreversible, or administrative?

### 3. Assassination and immediate aftermath (Ch 14–20)

**Understand by end:** Dream/dread; Decimus fetch; Curia chaos; false Antony-dead; amnesty trap; will/heir; funeral street vote; Liberators split (near knife vs East).

**Character focus:** Calpurnia, Decimus, Caesar (sparse), Brutus/Cassius, Antony, Nysa, Laberius, Octavian (delayed news).

**Keep uncertain:** Last words; NOTE-1 dual tracks; how much Antony “planned” the funeral.

**Propels next:** Name, money, and veterans — not philosophy — will decide who owns the corpse’s power.

**Ask yourself:**  
1. Does anything after Ch 16 feel like comedown rather than consequence?  
2. At Ch 20, do I still feel Decimus’s guilt as a live fuse?

### 4. Name, legacy, inheritance (Ch 21–25)

**Understand by end:** Force-gap (letters ≠ armies); Octavian accepts *Caesar*; Antony underestimates; street flinch economy; Antium locks East; Philippics as body risk.

**Character focus:** Cicero, Brutus (cage + purse refuse), Octavian/Atia/Philippus, Antony/Fulvia, Laberius (which purse), Servilia, Cassius.

**Keep uncertain:** Whether the boy’s name can buy land before Antony notices; how much East is necessity vs purity.

**Propels next:** Provinces and armies — Cisalpine as Decimus’s nearer knife.

**Ask yourself:**  
1. Where does my attention drop (honestly)?  
2. Does Antony’s “squeak” line make me dread Mutina, or only nod at irony?

### 5. Military–political consequences incl. Mutina (Ch 26–31)

**Understand by end:** Cisalpine refusal; Mutina siege/field; Decimus abandoned and killed; Octavian consular pressure without Augustus mind; Bononia lists; Cicero’s Formiae end.

**Character focus:** Decimus, Antony, Octavian, Laberius, Hordeonius, Seius/Nysa (lists), Cicero.

**Keep uncertain:** Pansa’s cause (AMB-2); who “first named” Cicero; exact ally betrayals’ private motives.

**Propels next:** Eastern extraction and Philippi as liberty’s bill coming due.

**Ask yourself:**  
1. Is Decimus’s death prepared enough that it feels inevitable *and* wasteful?  
2. Does Bononia make Formiae feel purchased?

### 6. Road to Philippi and ending (Ch 32–34)

**Understand by end:** Liberty as tax; Cassius killed by incomplete information; Brutus’s second-day weight; Antony peak + Italy blind; OCT survives ill/not genius; SERIES hooks only.

**Character focus:** Cassius, Brutus, Antony, Octavian, Laberius, Hordeonius (OPEN-0 ash).

**Keep uncertain:** SIDE-1b solution; next war’s shape (hooks, not plot).

**Propels next (series):** Land · sea · Eastern weight · cold between victors — **not** written here.

**Ask yourself:**  
1. Does Philippi feel like the cost of Ch 9’s mercy and Ch 16’s knives?  
2. Does the ending close *this* book without pretending the history is finished?
