# The Ides of March, Book One: reader questionnaire

Thank you for reading the book. This questionnaire is for someone who has read all of it: the prologue at Utica and the thirty-eight chapters that follow. Think of it as a long conversation with an editor who wants to know what actually happened to you as you read, not what you think the author would like to hear.

## Before you start

**Honesty is the most useful thing you can give.** Praise helps when it is specific, because it tells the author what to protect. Criticism helps more. If something bored you, confused you, annoyed you or failed to convince you, please say so plainly. Nothing you write will be argued with.

**There are no right answers.** This is not a test of whether you noticed things. If you did not notice something, that is information too. If you are unsure, say so, and say what you think anyway.

**Three kinds of answer are all welcome, and it helps to know which one you are giving:**

- **Felt:** what the book did to you (gripped, moved, bored, uneasy, amused).
- **Understood:** what you took to be happening, and why, and where you lost the thread.
- **Judged:** what you think works or does not work as writing, and why.

Each question is marked with the kind it mostly asks for, but answer in whatever way is true for you.

**Evidence makes an answer usable.** Wherever you can, give a chapter number, a few words quoted from the page, or a short description of the scene (“the scene where…”, “the conversation just after…”). Do not reread to hunt for references unless you want to. What stuck in your memory is itself useful.

**Skip freely.** Leave out any question where you have nothing to add. “Nothing to say here” is a perfectly good answer. A few full answers are worth more than many thin ones.

**Please answer on your own.** Fill this in before you discuss the book with anyone else who has read it, and before you read anyone else’s comments on it.

A chapter list is at the end, if it helps you place things, along with an optional chapter-by-chapter tracker.

### For any answer, you might add

- Where was it? (chapter, scene, or a few words from the page)
- What specifically caused the reaction?
- What did you expect to happen?
- What actually happened?
- How did it affect your understanding or your enjoyment?

## About your reading

Short answers are fine here. These help put everything else in context.

- How did you read it (Word document, printed pages, tablet, phone), and over how long?
- Did you read every page, or did you skim or skip anywhere? (Saying where is very helpful and will not offend anyone.)
- Did you ever put it down for more than a few days? If so, roughly where?
- What do you usually read? Do you read historical fiction?
- How much did you know about this period of Roman history before you started?
- Have you read any earlier version of this book, or seen anyone else’s comments on it?

## 1. Overall reading experience

**1. (Judged)** If a friend asked you what kind of book this is, what would you tell them? How would you describe it in two or three sentences?

**2. (Understood)** What do you think the story is ultimately about? Not the events, but the thing underneath them. It is fine if your answer is “I’m not sure”.

**3. (Felt)** Now that you have finished, what has stayed with you? An image, a line, a person, a moment, a mood?

**4. (Felt)** What was your strongest positive reaction anywhere in the book, and your strongest negative one?

**5. (Judged)** Would you recommend it? To whom? Is there anything you would warn them about first, or anyone you would not recommend it to?

*Optional prompts:* Did your view of the book change as you went on? Was there a point where you decided what you thought of it?

---

## 2. Opening and engagement

**6. (Understood / Felt)** What did you make of the prologue when you read it? Once you had read on, did it feel connected to the rest of the book? How?

**7. (Understood)** Over the first few chapters, did you understand what was going on and why it mattered? Where did you feel oriented, and where did you feel lost or held at arm’s length?

**8. (Felt)** When, if at all, did you become invested in the book, and what did it? Did that investment hold to the end, or did it come and go? Was there a point where you nearly stopped reading?

*Optional prompts:* What were you curious about early on? Was that curiosity answered, kept alive, or forgotten? If you used the chapter tracker at the end, you can point to it here.

---

## 3. Characters

**9. (Felt / Judged)** Which characters did you find most interesting, and which did you care about emotionally? These may not be the same people. What made the difference?

**10. (Felt)** Which characters did you struggle to connect with, keep track of, or care about? What got in the way?

**11. (Understood)** Were there moments when a character’s motives or decisions did not make sense to you? What did you expect them to do, and what did they do?

**12. (Judged)** Whose development over the book did you believe? Was there anyone whose change felt sudden, unearned, or missing altogether?

**13. (Judged)** Could you tell characters apart by the way they speak and think? Did anyone sound too much like someone else, or like the narrator?

**14. (Judged)** Did any character feel underused, overused, or unnecessary? Was there anyone you wanted more of, or anyone you would have been happy to see less of?

**15. (Felt / Judged)** Did the historical figures feel like people, with private lives and bad days, or did any of them feel like a name from a textbook walking through the scene?

*Optional prompts:* Was there a character you changed your mind about? Where did that happen? Was there anyone you were surprised to find yourself liking, or disliking?

---

## 4. Viewpoints and structure

The book moves between many people, often within a single chapter.

**16. (Felt / Judged)** How did the different viewpoints add up for you: as one story told from many sides, as several stories running alongside each other, or as something else? Did they form a whole?

**17. (Judged)** Did each viewpoint give you something the others could not? Were there any that seemed to be doing the same job as another? Whose sections did you look forward to returning to, and whose did you find yourself hurrying through?

**18. (Understood)** Were the changes of viewpoint, place and time clear? Were there moments when you had to go back to work out who you were with, where, or when? Did the number of people feel manageable?

**19. (Understood / Judged)** The viewpoints come from very different places in society. Did that give you a convincing picture of Roman society as a whole? Was there any part of that world you felt was missing?

*Optional prompts:* Was there a chapter where the switching felt especially effective, or especially disorienting? Did the five acts the book is divided into mean anything to you as you read?

---

## 5. Plot, pacing and intrigue

**20. (Felt)** Did the story keep moving? Where, if anywhere, did it drag, rush, or seem to repeat itself? Where did you skim?

**21. (Understood / Felt)** When characters were talking politics or manoeuvring, could you follow who wanted what and why? Did those scenes hold your interest? Point to any you found especially clear or especially hard going.

**22. (Judged)** Did the major events feel properly prepared for? Did anything come out of nowhere, or arrive so clearly signalled that it lost its force?

**23. (Judged)** Were the consequences of events followed through? Did anything happen that seemed important and then stopped mattering?

**24. (Understood / Judged)** Which side stories or threads did you notice running through the book? Did they feel connected to the main story? Did any thread disappear, or end without enough payoff?

*Optional prompts:* Was there a moment of real suspense for you, even where you knew the history? Was there a moment you guessed what was coming? Were you ever told something you would rather have found out for yourself?

---

## 6. The historical world and its politics

**25. (Understood)** At each stage of the book, could you follow the political situation: who held power, who wanted it, and what was at stake? Where did your grip on it slip?

**26. (Judged)** Did people’s decisions seem believable given the circumstances they were in, even when you disagreed with them? Did anything strike you as historically or logically implausible?

**27. (Felt / Judged)** Did Rome, and the other places the book goes, feel lived in? Which details made them real for you? Did the ordinary people feel connected to the great events, or kept apart from them?

**28. (Judged)** How did the balance between history and story feel? Were there places where you wanted more explanation, and places where you were given more than you wanted?

*Optional prompts:* Did you use the cast list, the page of offices and terms, the historical note or the timeline? When, and did they help? Were there words, titles or customs you had to guess at?

---

## 7. Writing, dialogue and atmosphere

**29. (Judged)** How did the prose read for you: easy, demanding, uneven? Can you point to a passage you thought especially well written, and one you thought weak?

**30. (Felt / Judged)** How did the dialogue sound? Did people talk like people? Where did the humour land for you, and where did it fall flat or feel out of place?

**31. (Felt)** What could you see, hear and smell as you read? Where did the atmosphere work, and where was the description thin or overdone?

**32. (Judged)** Did you notice any repeated words, images, sentence shapes or habits of style? Did anything pull you out of the book, such as a phrase that sounded too modern or a line that did not fit its speaker?

**33. (Judged)** Did the writing trust you? Were there places where it explained something you had already understood, or left you without something you needed?

*Optional prompts:* If you marked passages as you read, which ones, and why? Was there a page you read twice for pleasure, or twice because you could not follow it?

---

## 8. Emotional and thematic impact

**34. (Felt)** Which moments moved you most? Which moments seemed to be meant to move you but did not, and why do you think they missed?

**35. (Felt / Judged)** Did the deaths, betrayals and losses carry their proper weight? Did any pass too quickly, or linger too long?

**36. (Felt)** Did you find yourself sympathising with people who do hard or terrible things? Whom, and how did that feel?

**37. (Understood / Judged)** What ideas or questions has the book left you thinking about? Did they seem to grow out of the story, or did they feel placed on top of it?

*Optional prompts:* Was there a moment that changed how you felt about the whole book? Was there anything you found distasteful or hard to read, and did it feel justified?

## 10. The ending, and what comes next

This is the first book of a longer story. It ends at Philippi.

**39. (Felt / Judged)** Did the ending feel earned? What did the final chapters do to you, emotionally and as a close to the book’s ideas?

**40. (Judged)** As a single book, did it give you a satisfying experience on its own terms, even though the wider story continues?

**41. (Understood / Felt)** Which threads felt resolved by the end, and which felt deliberately left open? Did the open ones leave you intrigued or frustrated?

**42. (Felt)** Do you want to read the next book? Why, or why not? Whose story would you most want to follow?

*Optional prompts:* Did you expect the book to end where it did? Was there a final image or line that stayed with you, or one you wish had been different?

---

## 11. Editorial priorities

**43. (Judged)** In your view, what is the book’s greatest strength, and what is its greatest weakness?

**44. (Judged)** If the author could make only one improvement, what should it be? Then, if you like, name up to three changes you would prioritise, in order. And is there anything the author should absolutely keep, whatever else changes?

## Quick reactions

Finish each sentence with the first thing that comes to mind. Do not think too hard about these.

- I was confused when…
- I stopped believing the book when…
- I wanted more of…
- I could have done with less of…
- The character I think about most is…
- The line I remember is…
- This book is really about…
- When I closed the book I felt…
- If I could change one thing, it would be…
