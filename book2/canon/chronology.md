# Chronology, 221–207 BC

**Status:** Canon pack, stage 2. Every line carries its source. The **Check** column says how it was verified: **text** means the passage was read in the source and says what the line says; **flag** means there is a problem, explained in the note below the table.

**Calendar warning.** The Roman calendar of these years ran ahead of the seasons, by weeks and later by months. A Roman date (2 August for Cannae) is the date Romans wrote, not the solar season. Where Polybius gives a season (harvest, the setting of the Pleiades, midwinter) the storyboard uses his season for weather and light, and the Roman date for anything a Roman character would say or write.

Abbreviations: Pol. = Polybius, *Histories*; Liv. = Livy, *Ab Urbe Condita*.

## Flags

**A. The envoy at Carthage (218).** Livy names Q. Fabius among the envoys and gives the toga-fold words ("here we bring you war and peace; take which you please") to "the Roman" (Liv. 21.18). Whether this Fabius is Fabius Maximus is disputed. The book uses the scene only as something people say about Fabius, never as his memory, unless Reuben chooses otherwise.

**B. Who saved the consul (218).** Polybius has it from Laelius that the seventeen-year-old son did it, near the Po (Pol. 10.3). Livy prefers the son but reports Coelius's version that a Ligurian slave did (Liv. 21.46). The book tells it as the son, and lets one voice in the camp say it was a slave.

**C. The day of Trasimene.** Ovid puts it on the fifth day from the end of June, in a line whose "bis" is unclear (*Fasti* 6.767–8). The book says only "late June"; nobody names the day.

**D. Dictator or prodictator.** Polybius and Livy 22.8 both call Fabius dictator, chosen by the people because the surviving consul was away. Livy elsewhere calls him prodictator for that reason. Characters call him dictator.

**E. The date of Cannae.** Gellius, citing the annalist Q. Claudius, gives the fourth day before the Nones of Sextilis, that is 2 August by the Roman calendar (Gellius 5.17.5). The calendar was out of step with the sun, and Polybius sets the campaign at harvest. Romans in the book say "before the Nones of Sextilis"; the weather is high summer.

**F. The captive girl (209).** Polybius has Scipio give her back to her father (Pol. 10.19.6); Livy to her betrothed, Allucius (Liv. 26.50). The book follows Polybius, who knew Laelius; Livy's version can be the story the army tells.
