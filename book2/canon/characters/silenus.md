# Silenus of Caleacte

**POV.** Hannibal's camp. A Greek historian writing the war while it happens, from beside the general.

## Facts

| Fact | Source | Check |
|---|---|---|
| A Greek from Caleacte in Sicily who wrote Hannibal's history | Nepos, *Hann.* 13.3 | text |
| Was with Hannibal in his camp "as long as fortune allowed", with Sosylus of Sparta | Nepos, *Hann.* 13.3 | text |
| Tells the story of Hannibal's dream after Saguntum: called by Jupiter to the council of the gods, given a guide, the serpent of devastation behind him. Cicero says Coelius follows Silenus here | Cic. *Div.* 1.49 | text |
| Cited by Livy for a figure at New Carthage, 209 | Liv. 26.49.3 | to check |
| Nothing known of his age, family or death; his work is lost | | |

## Where he is

With Hannibal's army, 218 onwards. If the storyboard puts him somewhere Hannibal is not, it is invention and must be marked.

## What he knows and when

- Sees what Hannibal shows the camp and what the camp says about him. He does not hear Hannibal's private counsel unless Hannibal chooses to say it to him.
- Hears Spain and Carthage news when Hannibal does.

## How he talks

A Greek among Carthaginians, Iberians, Gauls and Numidians. Educated, curious, slightly vain about his prose; collects omens and numbers. Sosylus is his rival in the same tent.

## Invention zone

Almost everything except his job. He is the book's window on Hannibal, so he carries the "seen from outside" rule: he never knows what Hannibal thinks, only what he says and does.
