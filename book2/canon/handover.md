# Handover from Book 1

Where everyone stands when Book 2 opens, in spring 41 BC. Book 2 may not contradict anything here without changing Book 1 first. Sources are Book 1's text (`drafts/book1/`), `continuity/canon.md`, the character profiles and `writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md`.

Lines marked **(check)** were taken from planning files rather than the final text and must be confirmed against the chapter before the act that uses them is drafted.

## How Book 1 ends

- **Ch 38 SC-95a (winter 42–41, Rome).** Octavian comes home up the Appian Way, ill, curtains open. Nysa counts the crowd at the Capena gate and gives the steward of Antony's Carinae house the true number. He turns the tablet face down. An Octavian freedman at the back door has a list of Caesar's freedmen still owing duty; she says her legal name, Julia Nysa, aloud for the first time and leaves for the young Caesar's house. Lucius Antonius (consul) and Fulvia are heard through the walls talking about the veterans and the land "in a voice that was going up". Fulvia's woman stands aside.
- **Ch 38 SC-95c (spring 41).** In the young Caesar's house the women sew by one lamp. Nysa mends his torn tunic so nobody can see the tear.
- **Ch 38 SC-95b (spring 41, last scene).** North of Capua, the old twine from after Munda, "fourth line from the Volturnus road". Surveyors with groma and chain, an Octavian freedman with a list, the triumvirs' eighteen cities, Capua among them. The farmer, Gnaeus Aulius, with his father's purchase tablet, a wife, three children and an old man on a bench. A clean white stake through the beans. "There'll be compensation." "When?" The scar-lipped veteran: "Nobody asked the vine." Laberius makes his mark, then steps over the twine and walks toward the farmer. What he says or does is not on the page.
- **Ch 38 SC-95 (Nov 42, Rome).** Hordeonius burns the Visellius copies and closes the account without a line. Bucco is back at the Janus, lending to veterans against their acres.

## Carried POVs

### Laberius
- **Name:** Sextus Laberius Cordus, centurion, discharged after Philippi. Picene. About 45 in 41 **(check age)**.
- **Called:** "Laberius" by the narration and his men. Only Rufrius calls him "Cordus" (and "Mule"). Vibius does not.
- **Where:** On his allotment north of Capua, fourth line from the Volturnus road, from the boundary stone to the second stake, along the line to the ditch. He has made his mark on the freedman's list.
- **Has:** His discharge tablet. Gaius's boots: newly stitched at the left heel in Aug 43, resoled twice by Ch 35, last resoled at Thessalonica.
- **Lost:** Gaius (killed at Mutina, javelin above the mail, "It knew you walk like a mule").
- **With him:** Vibius, his optio, alive. The scar-lipped veteran (funeral torch Ch 19, colony forum Ch 24, Casilinum bridge Ch 27) has the plot two fields up with the old vine. Laberius does not know his name. Book 2 may give it, once, and it then goes in the names list.
- **Rufrius:** spared at Forum Gallorum (Ch 30), stood on Laberius's left at Philippi and held him at the ditch (Ch 37). Whether he is alive and settled is open **(check Ch 38)**.
- **His question:** who is the man on his left, now that the man is a farmer.

### Nysa
- **Name:** Julia Nysa in law, freed by Caesar's will (Ch 25; Phileros first used the name to her in Ch 18). About 31 in 41.
- **Where:** The young Caesar's household, from the day after the Capena gate. She sews in the evenings with the other women. "They'll want to know who carries."
- **Patron:** Octavian, as Caesar's heir; she owes him *obsequium*.
- **Knows:** The Carinae house from the inside (Fulvia, Lucius, the steward, Fulvia's woman who counts the silver). She gave Fulvia's woman the true count once (Ch 35). She undercounted the games crowd for Antony (Ch 25) and the steward knows it.
- **Habits:** Counts in Greek, in rows then tens. Reports objects, not meanings. Keeps a book (Phileros, Ch 33).
- **Medullina:** her Subura contact, left unexplained by Reuben's decision (11 Nov). Book 2 does not explain her either unless Reuben says so.

### Octavian
- **Called:** "the young Caesar" in the Carinae kitchen and by Nysa; not "the boy" any more.
- **Body:** Ill at Philippi and again at Brundisium in the winter; rumoured dead. Thin, pale, cloak to the chin. Dislikes oil burned for nothing; wears clothes his household makes (Suet. *Aug.* 73).
- **Position:** Triumvir. Holds Italy and the job of settling the veterans. Not a genius; healthier men argue about what he did at Philippi.
- **Around him:** Agrippa (set the markers at Philippi). Maecenas (Book 1 cast). Apollodorus of Pergamon, his teacher.
- **Not yet in Book 1, true by 41 and available:** married since 43 to Claudia, Fulvia's daughter by Clodius, whom he sends back to her mother in 41 (Suet. *Aug.* 62) **(check source wording)**.

### Hordeonius
- **Name:** Lucius Hordeonius Felix, equestrian moneylender, stall near the Janus. About 53 in 41 **(check age)**.
- **With him:** Sosia, his collector.
- **Last act:** burned the two Visellius tablets; still does not know whose hand killed Visellius (OPEN-0 stays open). Closed the account without a line.
- **Ledger facts:** Calenus's steward borrowed (Ch 34). Fulvia's silver-woman asked his month rate and did not borrow. Bought a proscribed garden and was spat at (Ch 33).
- **Rival:** Gaius Septimius Bucco, Antony's cash agent, back at the Janus, lending to veterans against their acres.

## Promises Book 1 made

1. **The unpaid acres** (FS10). The land is assigned and the compensation is not. This is Book 2's engine.
2. **Sextus at sea** (FS11). Grain and the coast.
3. **What Laberius did at the twine.** Book 2 opens on its consequence the next morning.
4. **Nysa in the heir's house.** She counted for Antony's house; now she counts for the young Caesar's, while Fulvia, whom she has heard through the walls, becomes his enemy.
5. **Bucco and the veterans' acres.** Money lent against land that is still somebody else's.

## Must not (carried from Book 1)

- No Actium, and no hindsight about it.
- Octavian ill and not a genius.
- No stacked sequel thesis, and no narrator explaining what the land war means.
