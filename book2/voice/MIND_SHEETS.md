# Mind sheets: Book 2

**Status:** First version, 11 Nov 2026. Descriptive, not prescriptive, as in Book 1.

The Book 1 sheets in [`writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md`](../../writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md) remain the authority for the four carried POVs. This file adds what has changed in each of them since Philippi, and full sheets for the two new POVs. The three tests (action, point of view, explanation) and the two tests for a scene's ending apply unchanged.

Facts used here are in the [handover](../canon/handover.md) and the [chronology](../canon/chronology.md). Anything marked **(check)** is confirmed before the scene that uses it.

## Carried POVs: since Philippi

### Laberius

**What has changed:** for twenty years the man on his left was a soldier and the answer was to stand. Now he has stepped over the twine toward a farmer standing in beans, and the land he was promised turns out to have a family on it. Gaius is dead and he walks in Gaius's boots.

**Notices now:** the same things, feet and ground and who has eaten, but he has started noticing them on civilians: the farmer's hands, the old man on the bench who does not get up, the children held by the shoulders. He does not have a word for what he is doing when he looks at them that way.

**What he still misunderstands:** he thinks the land question is between him and Gnaeus Aulius. It is between Octavian and Lucius Antonius, and the two of them are going to make it a war. He does not believe that until soldiers he knows are on the other side.

**The risk in his scenes:** making him a man of conscience who explains his conscience. He does not. He checks the farmer's fence instead, or he does not, and the reader sees which.

**From the pilot read (11 Nov):** of the six blind scenes he read most like a type, the taciturn veteran. Give him things only he would have: a Picene way of saying something, a food he misses, a thing he is bad at (he cannot swim; he is no good with animals; he cannot judge soil, only ground to stand on), a memory that is not about soldiering. One such particular per scene, not a catalogue. His friendship with Vibius worked and should carry more of it.

### Nysa

**What has changed:** she is in the young Caesar's house under her own name. She counts for him now. She has heard Fulvia and Lucius through the walls of the Carinae house all winter, and she knows Fulvia's woman.

**Notices now:** the new house's economies (one lamp, plain wool, clothes made at home), who comes to the young Caesar's door, which of them he sees and how long they wait. Claudia, Fulvia's daughter, is in this house as his wife until he sends her back. Nysa is the one person in it who has seen the mother's house from the inside.

**What she still misunderstands:** she thinks that carrying for one house and not the other keeps her out of the war between them. Both houses know where she has been.

**The risk in her scenes:** making her a spy. She is not one. She is a woman who counts, in a house that has found out it needs counting.

### Octavian

**What has changed:** he is triumvir, in charge of Italy and of paying the army in land, and almost everyone in Italy hates him for one half of that or the other. He has been ill again and rumoured dead.

**Notices now:** lists, still, but now they are lists of farms, and the names on them are people who will curse him at the Forum. He notices which of Antony's people are polite to him and which are only careful.

**What he still misunderstands:** he believes that if he pays the soldiers, everything else can be survived. That turns out to be true, which is the worst thing that happens to him in this book.

**The risk in his scenes:** the genius. He is not one. He is ill, frightened of the veterans, and right more often than he deserves. The Perusine altar story is told about him; he never confirms it to the reader.

### Hordeonius

**What has changed:** he burned the Visellius copies and closed an account without a line, for the first time in thirty years. Bucco is back at the Janus lending to veterans against acres that are still somebody else's.

**Notices now:** the paper the land war makes. Allotment tablets used as security. Compensation promised and not dated. Farmers selling before they are evicted and veterans selling before they have farmed. He reads the whole war as a market in land titles, which is wrong about the war and right about the land.

**What he still misunderstands:** he thinks he burned the Visellius tablets out of decency. He did, partly. He also burned the one asset that tied him to anyone.

**The risk in his scenes:** the narration doing ledger jokes for him. He gets his maxims, in speech, once or twice a scene.

## New POVs

### Fulvia

**Who:** Antony's wife; earlier the wife of Clodius and of Curio, both dead by violence. Mother of Claudia (by Clodius) and of Antony's sons. In Rome in 41 with Lucius, while Antony is in the East. Real, and one of the few women of the period the historians give actions to rather than only influence (Dio 48.10.4).

**Notices first:** men's loyalty, measured by what they do when Antony is not in the room. Money: who has been paid and who is waiting. Her children's faces when soldiers are in the house. Insults, and who repeats them.

**How a thought moves:** fast, to the next step, as someone who has buried two husbands and run a household through a proscription. She does not look back unless someone makes her. When she reasons, she reasons from what was done to her and hers.

**When she generalises:** about men, aloud, cutting, to her women or to Lucius. She is allowed to be right and unfair in the same sentence.

**What she cannot or will not say:** that she is afraid Antony will not come back. That she is jealous (Appian's reading of her, which she would deny). That Claudia's return is a humiliation she feels as a mother and not only as a politician.

**What she misunderstands:** she thinks the veterans can be held by the name of Antony while Antony is in Alexandria. She thinks Plancus, Pollio and Ventidius will come. She believes Octavian is a sick boy who can be frightened. Each of these was reasonable in the summer of 41.

**In talk:** direct, quick, unafraid of soldiers. She gives orders and does not soften them. With her children she is short and close.

**Body:** a household's keys; the weight of a sword she has seen men wear and decided to wear herself, at Praeneste (Dio 48.10.4). Older than the women around her; the sources do not give her age **(check)**.

**Sounds like her:** "He has my daughter back to me untouched, he says. He wants it known he never touched anything of ours. Tell the cook we eat at the ninth hour."
**Does not:** a speech about the condition of Italy. She talks about who has been paid.

### Lucius Antonius

**Who:** Antony's youngest brother. Tribune in 44 (his land law), consul in 41 with Servilius Isauricus **(check)**. Takes up the cause of the dispossessed against Octavian, enters Rome by night, then is shut in Perusia and starved into surrender. Spared. Real.

**Notices first:** crowds, and whether they are with him. Petitioners and their papers. The difference between what his brother's letters say and what they do not say.

**How a thought moves:** in public terms: the consul, the law, the Republic's dispossessed. He believes in this language more than his brother does. Underneath it, the younger brother: what Marcus will think, whether this is what Marcus wants, whether Marcus will come.

**When he generalises:** in speeches, sincerely, and his speeches are better than his plans. In private he asks questions.

**What he cannot or will not say:** that he is doing this partly to matter to his brother. That he does not know whether Antony wants him to do it.

**What he misunderstands:** that Antony's generals will march on his word. That a consul's dignity is protection. That the farmers who cheer him in Rome are an army.

**In talk:** warm, eager, a little too long. Fulvia cuts him off and he lets her.

**Body:** the consul's chair and lictors in the first acts; in Perusia, hunger, cold, the ditch and wall he can see from the town and cannot cross.

**Sounds like him:** "They have the decree. I have the men whose fields are in it. Ask Marcus which of us he'd rather have at his back."
**Does not:** a soliloquy on the doom of the Republic. He thinks he can still win until the day he surrenders.

**To check before drafting:** the cognomen *Pietas* Dio says he took for his loyalty to his brother **(check Dio 48.5)**; his fate after Perusia (sent to govern Spain) **(check)**.
