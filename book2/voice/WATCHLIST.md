# Drafting watchlist

From the outside editorial review of Book 1, Chapters 1–6 (11 Nov 2026). The reviewer found a strong opening with no rewrite needed, and named five habits of the house style to manage, not eliminate. They are questions for the three-tests self-audit at the end of each act, read alongside `writing/voice/MIND_SHEETS.md`. They are not bans or quotas, and none of them is a reason to make the prose less polished, less articulate or less literary.

## Five questions

1. **Spacing of good lines.** Read a scene's dialogue aloud. If every speaker lands a quotable line, which one is this person's and which are the book's? Keep the best one. Let the others misunderstand, repeat themselves, trail off or say the ordinary thing, where that is what this person would do. Do not manufacture awkwardness.
2. **Reversals.** Look for the turn "not this, but that", the thing that turns out to mean its opposite, the statement privately recognised as false. Once in a scene is thought. Twice is a shape. Change how the second thought moves (a question, an interruption, an action, a plain statement) and keep the insight.
3. **Scene endings.** The ending tests in the mind sheets push endings toward a physical action. The review adds the other half: if every scene ends on a gesture that carries the theme, readers start waiting for it. Across an act, let some scenes stop when the business is done: someone leaves, the door shuts, the next thing on the list. Keep the gesture where it is earned.
4. **Minor characters.** A walk-on gets eloquence only when the person would have it: education, rank, drink, nerve, a reason to show off. The test case is the former praetor in Book 1 Ch 6 ("fear when it has learned Latin"), which stays because the line is the event: he is a senator, he is drinking, the room goes quiet, he takes it back. A muleteer, a clerk or a soldier in Book 2 talks like a muleteer, a clerk or a soldier.
5. **Visible design.** Echoes, parallels and rising consequences are the book's structure and stay. Each scene must also work as an encounter between people who want something from each other, with no knowledge of the scenes around it. If a scene is there only to complete a parallel, give it its own trouble or cut it.

## How to use it

At the act self-audit, read each scene once for these five, after the three tests. Note what you change in the changelog line for that act. If a question turns up nothing, move on.
